	1
	Jia Xu
	The Chief-Controller and the main strategist of Cao Cao after Guo Jia unfortunately passed away from his illness.
	* The main objective of Jia Xu in this battle was to acquire land for Cao Cao.
* His strategic aim was to employ the Easterly Wind that he anticipated Zhou Yu to use, as a bait to lure him into falling for his ambushes and traps.
* His contingency aim for failure was to immediately fight for Jiangling so that he could prevent Zhou Yu from acquiring a base for himself in the south.
	2
	Zhuge Liang
	The main strategist of Liu Bei. The orchestrator of Red Cliffs.
	* The main objective of Zhuge Liang was to prevent Zhou Yu from crushing Cao Cao in the battle, because Zhuge knew that once Cao Cao is no more, he would become the target of opposition and Sun Quan’s much more influential and larger forces would mean he would be no more soon after as well.
* Zhuge isolated Zhou Yu away from crushing Cao Cao by planning and orchestrating extensively for the Huarong flood where he predicted a flood to exploit, so that he could prevent the scenario of Zhou Yu executing the aftermath strategy.
	3
	Zhou Yu
	The Chief-Controller of Sun Quan’s forces. The victor of Red Cliffs.
	* Zhou Yu’s main objective of the battle was to humiliate and crush Cao Cao down. Because he wanted to prove to the world that Cao Cao’s forces were not invincible forces, but rather forces who managed to lose the battle even though they predicted his bait far ahead.
* Zhou Yu’s strategic aim was to bait Jia Xu into thinking his Easterly Wind bait was to be executed against Wen Ping’s forces, and to lure Jia Xu’s forces as well as Cao Ren’s and Zhang Liao’s forces deep inside his territory, then to lure away Cao Cao’s forces using Zhuge Liang’s forces to infiltrate their main base.
	4
	Guo Jia
	The former main strategist of Cao Cao. The contingency planner of Red Cliffs.
	* Guo Jia’s main goal was to unite China under Cao Cao’s rule, because he found Cao Cao’s rule to be the most potent one out of all. Since he was going to die, he wanted to contribute to Cao Cao even more by planning several contingencies ahead.
* Guo Jia’s contingency for Red Cliffs was to assign Zhu Ling as a backup reinforcement to aid Xun You and Cao Cao’s forces, neutralise Xun Yu from taking any actions or suppressing Sima Yi and then promote Sima Yi to be the successor of the battle.
	5
	Sima Yi
	The successor of Guo Jia. Converted into the Chief-Controller after Cao Cao was entrapped in Huarong.
	* Sima Yi’s main goal in general was to usurp Cao Cao’s throne by climbing the power ladder so as to avenge his clan’s unfortunate execution. But the main objective of this battle was to retrieve the lost leverages that he had in the faction.
* Sima Yi’s strategic aim was to neutralise Xun Yu and Jia Xu in the battle, collude with Zhuge Liang, collude with Zuo Ci, save Cao Cao in a key moment and establish tripartite with Zhuge Liang so as to create a narrative that he was the one who saved Cao Cao.
	6
	Zuo Ci
	The immortal of the north, a religious leader. A loyal supporter of Yuan Fang. He was vengeful for Yuan Fang’s death and served as an assassin to strangle Cao Cao in the faction.
	* Zuo Ci’s main goal was to avenge his dead master, Yuan Fang and the entire Yuan clan which was destroyed by Cao Cao’s schemes in Guandu.
* Zuo Ci’s strategy was to enter Cao Cao’s faction through Sima Yi, gain the trust of Cao Cao, assassinate all the head doctors of Cao Cao to pose as the only doctor for Cao Cao and then assassinate Cao Cao while pretending to treat him.

Jia Xu’s main strategy (Including Cao Cao faction’s strategy as a whole, I call it Jia Xu’s strategy because he was basically the main strategist in this battle):
- Jia Xu (or Guo Jia probably) foresaw that a massive flood would occur on the banks of the great Han river. Why does this piece of information matter you may ask? The banks of Han river, as stated, was an area which was heavily, heavily guarded by Liu Biao of the south's armies. The armies who are actively attacking the Han river would be easily defeated. Doesn’t Cao Cao have a better army and more resources? Yes, but to attack the Han river, it would mean to come and attack through ships on the Han river. The Han river was stated to be usually shallow. So it would be unrealistic to attack the south by arriving through the shallow Han river, it would be suicidal as combined with the fact that Liu Biao already extensively guarded the area with his ship. But as Zhuge Liang explained, there would be a flood that would occur each year. The flood would fill up the water, but it still wouldn’t be enough for ships to come through. But as stated, the Han river flood was especially voluminous and big at the year of the battle, which was predicted by the geniuses (Zhuge Liang, Jia Xu, Guo Jia, Zhou Yu, etc…).

- So Jia Xu (and Guo Jia) first launched an attack on Liu Biao’s territory by taking advantage of the flood which would have filled the river and successfully used the massive flood to wash away the guarding soldiers as well. Jia Xu’s first motion of trap succeeded successfully. This led to the next phase of his strategy. Jia Xu intimidated Liu Cong (the promoted heir of Liu Biao’s land) and all of his troops enough by deliberately launching an attack with 200,000 men. 200,000 men would obviously intimidate the forces, as Liu Biao remotely did not have a comparable amount of forces to Cao Cao’s 200,000 men forces. This prompted a surrender and Jia Xu successfully subjugated Liu Cong and Jingzhou under his control without much casualties on his forces. Jia Xu then proceeded to his next phase of his strategy.
- There was a problem in Jingzhou. Zhuge (I think it would be revealed later in the story) spread widespread rumors on Liu Bei’s greatness and generosity, so most people supported Liu Bei. People supported Liu Bei so much so that even Liu Biao supported Liu Bei as his influence on people was simply too much. The incredible rise of support from the people also negatively damaged Cao Cao’s image for people. So people of Jingzhou extensively hated Cao Cao and his forces, so much so that such hate could be affiliated with possible betrayals, uprisings or assassination from people. So Jia Xu opted to solve this situation with his ‘usual trick’. His usual trick was to kill the heir of Jingzhou, Liu Cong. Why? Because regardless of his surrender, he is still a man of the Liu family and Liu family were known for royalty and were greatly affiliated with the Han dynasty, so they could revolt at any time against Cao Cao. So Jia Xu solved both the problems regarding Liu Bei’s influence and Liu Cong’s affiliation, by eliminating Liu Cong altogether. By eliminating Liu Cong, he had set another trap. He spread rumors that Liu Cong, the heir of Jingzhou, was unjustifiably and brutally killed by Liu Bei. This was to fix an antagonist in the eyes of a confused masses who didn’t have a clear antagonist in their eyes at those times of immense conflict and chaos. So by this trap setting, he had effectively neutralised the problems.

- Jia Xu’s strategy began with a bang. Jia Xu’s next death trap to engulf Sun faction was to use Xiangyang as bait. He deliberately stationed a few hundred thousand men of forces on Xiangyang precisely to create a facade of importance on land. What is the bait here exactly? Jia Xu fabricated a facade that Xiangyang was a place of importance to misdirect Zhou Yu. But Xiangyang had already been subjugated and occupied by Cao Cao’s forces. So there would be no need to occupy any troops over there, but Jia Xu did to lure Zhou Yu and his men away into thinking that Cao Cao’s forces were trying to capture Liu Bei’s men’s march to Xinye (or Xiakou??? Fact-check me if I was wrong). But in truth, the forces in Xiangyang would board the navy when the navy would resupply at Xiangyang and would launch an attack on South by travelling towards South with the combined forces. But unfortunately for Jia Xu, Zhou Yu easily saw through the facade and countered the trap as he traveled to battle against the main forces of Cao Cao on the Han river instead of engaging on Xiangyang as he knew Xiangyang was a bait to lure them away.

- Jia Xu’s strategy to win in Red Cliffs probably covered Liu Bei’s forces’ march towards Xiakou. Jia Xu probably ordered to infiltrate the masses of the crowd that Zhuge advised Liu Bei to take to Xiakou. A really simple yet a very effective tactic. What does this do? It allowed minimal casualties or losses for Cao Cao and would allow them to eliminate one key player (Liu Bei of south) from the long game. But Zhuge long predicted this tactic, I mean who wouldn’t?

- Jia Xu laid the next trap for Zhou Yu to fall into. The forces of Xiahou Yuan and Dun who were stationed at Xiangyang, had been marching towards Liu Bei’s forces. But unfortunately, a ‘nameless’ advisor (probably Xu Shu) used a large smoke/fire to block the way and blocked the Cao Cao’s forces. This was a perfect opportunity for the Sun faction to strike. Cao Cao’s forces had been forced to fight on land, they had been widely scattered by the constant march and battle, their troops had been forced out and the morale of the Cao Cao forces were down and crumbled. So Zhou Yu’s men then begged Gongjin (or Zhou Yu) to march their troops over to the scattered troops of Cao Cao’s faction and finish them all at once as their morale and position was terrible.

He knew that his burning leather-made Jingzhou ships would flawlessly cover the woods in Red Cliffs combined with the burning of Wulin through Jiaoqui’s team, which would cut all of Cao Cao’s retreat routes to just Huarong path. He knew that Cao Cao’s forces were worn down and taking them down wouldn’t be hard. But assuming a contingency situation where Zhuge Liang managed to isolate Zhou Yu away which did happen through Zhuge’s Huarong flood. So Zhou Yu planned to immediately conquer Jiangling, send forces to apply pressure and eventually take over Yizhou through Yiling, conquer the long river and then establish the southern territories as his. All of this was under the scenario that Zhuge Liang managed to capture Cao Cao for himself, which would mean Zhuge Liang would conquer northern territories in Cao Cao’s place. But taking Cao Cao’s place would mean he would be the possessor of the Han emperor, Liu Xie, which would mean Zhuge Liang would be deemed the Emperor Kidnapper. Not only that, Zhuge Liang would have to unite the northern territories together as well, which would minimalistically take years. Zhou Yu, by that time, would invade the Shu territories through Jiangling and then the three southern commanderies, and would possess Yizhou (Yi province) by that time as well. Which would mean Zhuge Liang and Zhou Yu split the world into two: North (Shu/Zhuge Liang) and South (Wu/Zhou Yu). By that time, Zhou Yu and Zhuge Liang would clash for a showdown between the territories. Supported by the Long river and defended by Yizhou’s defenses, it would be easy for Zhou Yu to defeat someone as territorially and diplomatically troubled in the north. However this trap was under the contingency that Zhuge Liang would try to conquer the north for himself, but Zhuge Liang prevented the trap from sprouting into reality.
- I am not too sure about this trap, if it does exist, then this trap would be a follow up to the former trap. Zhou Yu possibly planned the threat of conflict between Cao Cao’s sons??? It was mentioned that if Cao Cao were to die in the Huarong path, the next immediate problem for Wei would be for them to choose their next ruler. It was directly stated that this exact situation would then lead to the conflict between Cao Cao’s sons: Cao Pi and Cao Zhi who were supported by different groups within the faction. This would force Jia Xu to take action and abandon Cao Cao in the Huarong as well as convince Cao Ren to defend Yiling for future purposes, as well as give complete support over to Cao Pi to promote him into the next ruler of Wei. Possibly this was a two-layered trap. So if Jia Xu did abandon Cao Cao and solved the sons conflict matter, then Cao Cao, who would be isolated in the mountains, would die. Zhou Yu already planned to force Jia Xu back due to his inferior military power as well as defend Yiling from Cao Ren who had not much backing him up as well as his fewer forces who were already shaken by Zhou Yu’s earlier strategy in Red Cliffs. So abandonment of Cao Cao would lead to wider consequences. Real big-brain trap. If Jia Xu abandoned the sons and internal conflict to save Cao Cao instead, the conflict of the sons would ensue wider resulting in wider consequences and the self-destruction of Wei. But if Jia Xu abandoned Cao Cao and solved the internal conflict instead, then Zhou Yu’s contingencies from Red Cliffs would result in several consequences as well (If we think logically, the best scenario for Jia Xu to choose here would be the latter one). Zhou Yu possibly planned for both of these scenarios, however we all know that even if Zhou Yu did plan this trap to happen, it wouldn’t sprout into reality anyways as this trap would only happen if Cao Cao was captured and executed by Zhuge Liang’s forces in Huarong, which didn’t happen. However his secondary contingency of conquering Jiangling and Yiling as a base would still pose an incredible threat as that would mean Zhou Yu could conquer from strategically advantageous positions in the future. If we delve even deeper and think logically about the situation, Zhou Yu could still make the trap work, because information from Huarong route was still blocked due to Zhuge Liang’s doings, which Zhou Yu was explicitly aware of. So that would mean Jia Xu would still be unsure whether or not to save Cao Cao or solve the internal conflicts and prevent Zhou Yu from acquiring strategically advantageous positions. Of course, Zhou Yu could easily anticipate that Jia Xu would choose the most logical choice of the situation which is the latter one, but Zhou Yu would still have his contingency backing him up anyways.
- Zhou Yu also ordered forces to pursue Yiling and conquer them. The reason was to set up a base for themselves as well as be used as an intimidation tactic to outmaneuver the warlords of Yizhou. The tactic worked and Gong Su of Yizhou also agreed to surrender under Zhou Yu’s forces through a letter. Cao Ren’s forces who Jia Xu planned to take on Yiling, would also be blocked by Zhou Yu’s forces whom he sent to conquer Yiling as they were much faster than Cao Cao’s forces who were still troubled from Zhou Yu’s easterly wind bait and other shenanigans. The reason Zhou Yu’s campaign felt intimidating was because Zhou Yu single handedly defeated Cao Cao and his entire forces who had much better forces and much better resources, and since news of Cao Cao withdrawing was spreading, it would intimidate them even more as that would mean Zhou Yu would shift his target to Yizhou. So through intimidation, Zhou Yu successfully submitted multiple surrenders.
- Zhou Yu misdirected everyone into thinking he conquered Jiangling to conquer Jingzhou back. But nobody (except Sima Yi, Zhuge Liang and Jia Xu) anticipated that Zhou Yu’s main goal was Yizhou all along. Zhou Yu could simply take Yiling by blocking off Jiangling. Cao Ren was also not able to see Jiangling’s forces since the latter had to dig in its heels. Zhou Yu anticipated that Jia Xu would anticipate Zhou Yu using Jiangling as a strategic base for his future conquerings and operations. So Zhou Yu’s main strategy already crippled Cao Cao’s forces enough. So Zhou Yu had a contingency to block Jia Xu if he anticipated Zhou Yu’s contingency strategy, which was to use Jia Xu’s numerical inferiority and inferior military power to block any of his strategies. So unfortunately Jia Xu was unable to execute his foresight due to Zhou Yu’s contingency. Zhou Yu’s Cao Cao forces crippling strategy is the real problem, which meant that Zhou Yu planned this secondary strategist way prior.
- Zhou Yu had another contingency for the next scenario: Zhuge Liang would let Cao Cao pass Huarong path through the south of the route so as to prevent his trap from working. This wasn’t as favourable to Zhou Yu as Zhuge Liang conquering north due to the fact that he would possess all of the south and its geography while Zhuge Liang would be riddled with a lot of internal and external problems in the scenario. But this contingency was to prevent Liu Bei from being in the tripartite at all. Zhou Yu executed this contingency by overturning many warlords against Zhuge Liang, through some shenanigans in the 4 commanderies arc. Zhou Yu also planned to conquer Yizhou through Jiangling and Yizhou as his strategic base, then conquer the Shu territories through Jiangling and the 3 southern commanderies as well. In this scenario, Liu Bei might be able to conquer a few territories, however Zhuge Liang would still need to maintain the tripartite as well as the warlords as well as Zhou Yu’s campaigns. Zhou Yu would still have the support of the Long river as well as the defence of Yizhou’s geography. This contingency would also eliminate Liu Bei from the game. However, unfortunately for Zhou Yu, he did not see Zhuge Liang’s barrage of long-term schemes coming in the 4 commanderies arc. To further understand this part of Zhou Yu’s strategy, just read the 4 commanderies arc, the reason I did not elaborate further on this is because this crosses over to the next arc which I did not plan to analyse.
