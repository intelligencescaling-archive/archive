Introduction to Uminekos Terminology

I have already mentioned this in the preface, but Umineko is a very complex piece of media. Therefore, I will need to explain some terms and give some information necessary to understand Beatrice's feats.
If you are already familiar with Umineko and its concepts/terms, then feel free to skip this chapter.
I highly recommend reading this otherwise.

The world in Umineko has multiple layers; We will take a closer look at the most important of them here.

The Witches Game Board:
The Witches Game Board is the place on which the supposedly “real world” seems to take place. The Territory Lord/Game Master sets the scene and tells a story here. They give their opponent, the Human Side, mysteries that are supposedly only possible due to the use of magic. It is the Human Sides objective to prove those mysteries possible without the use of
magic.

The Meta World:
The Meta World is the world in which the two players of the Witches Game confront each other.  The Game Master sets the scene and controls the events of the Game Board through here, while the Human Side throws their theories out in an attempt to solve the mysteries presented.

The Real World:
The truest of all words, in which Ange lives. It is not really easy to explain the real world without knowledge about Umineko. Just treat it as the truest of all worlds, away from any Meta World or Game Board.
Let’s take a closer look at some terms now;

Red Truth:
Whenever something is said in red like this, it is guaranteed to be 100% the truth. It is primarily used to strike down the Human Sides theory in the Witches Game by denying them through the red truth.

Blue Truth:
Whenever something is said in blue like this, it is a theory that tries to explain a mystery. If a blue truth cannot be cut down by the Witches side, the mystery at which the Blue Truth was aimed counts as defeated. The Witches side can however take its sweet time to counter them.

How to win the Witches Game:
The Witch Side has no real way to win the game, placing it at a clear disadvantage. If a whole Game Board is finished and the Human Side did not solve each and every mystery present, the Witch's Side did postpone their defeat and a new Game Board will be started.
Only by making the Human Side completely surrender and accept the witch's existence can the Witch Side win.

The Human Side can clear the game by solving each and every mystery present, including those of previous Game Boards. When solving the Game, a Human has the chance to become a witch.

Game Master:
Someone who has created a Game Board. They reign freely over their respective Game Board and try to challenge the Human Side with their respective mysteries. They have complete control over the gameboard and know everything that happens on it.

Fragment:
A Fragment shows possible or alternate scenarios for a certain event. Each Fragment is as valid as the original reality.

Logic Error:
When the Witches Side presents a mystery which is unsolvable/contradicts itself, a Logic Error occurs. When that happens, the Game Master will be unable to do anything else until the Logic Error is solved. This can be seen as some sort of eternal torture.

Knox’s Decalogue:
A set of 10 commandments (the fifth is not present in Umineko) created by Ronald Knox. Each of them holds the power of a red truth. Umineko uses a slight variation of the original Decalogue, however the core thought of each commandment is still present.
Introduction to Beatrice’s Character

Beatrice is a very complex character and I will leave out a lot in this short introduction to her. This is not a writing doc, therefore I will not fully analyze everything about her

Beatrice serves as Umineko's main antagonist for the first 4 Episodes, also called the Questions Arc. In the Answers Arc, which covers Episode 5-8, she holds a more supportive role and can not be considered an antagonist anymore.
Beatrice also serves as the Game Master for the first 4 Episodes, making all of the mysteries presented there hers (the EVA-Beatrice mysteries are a special case that will need some more specific explanations). She is Battlers opponent and tries to make him submit to her, at least on the surface.

True Identity:
Beato's “True Identity” is “Sayo Yasuda”, who has taken on the role of both Shannon and Kanon. Feats of Piece-Yasu will be documented in this doc as well.

True Intentions:
To keep it brief, Beatrice's true goal is to make Battler remember his promise to her, make him understand her fully, make him remember the original events of Rokkenjima and make him solve the mysteries. After all, a mystery does need to be solved in the end.
“Comp” Beatrice
The Comp (short for Composite) version of a character is a version which includes every official version of said character.

In the case of Beatrice, we cannot only say “VN, Manga and Spin-Off Version”, since Beatrice is such a multi-layered character.

I will not actually take a look at each spin-off version of Beatrice but only focus on “manga” and “VN” most of the time. The term “Comp” is just fitting for Beatrice as she has many multiple versions of herself. Keep this in mind.

Here is a short overview of all major versions of Beatrice and which of those will be looked at in this documentation.
