Honestly- D.W. from Arthur sometimes feels like an SCD Character. (Consider this kinda a doc)

Various spoilers for Arthur Episode ahead!

Was just watching the Arthur Episode: "Prove it," and it probably shows D.W. at her best in terms of foresight and charisma.

I remember as a kid I didn't even understand what was happening in this Episode, Because it is honestly a little bit complicated for a 5-year-old to understand.

It is quite amazing how well written of a show Arthur is. Arthur and D.W. are siblings, and the show makes you really believe it. In episodes that are focused on Arthur's perspective, DW looks like the bad guy, but in episodes of where DW is the focus, Arthur looks like the bad guy.

In the episode Prove It, Honestly, Arthur and DW both kind of look like bad people, or at least annoying people.

Spoilers ahead in case if you care lol -

Alan comes over to Arthur's house for a visit. DW wants to be entertained, but Arthur tells her to go away. Alan however finishes his homework way before Arthur, and says he's fine with trying to entertain DW. Alan and DW actually get along quite well, as DW is honestly interested in science, which Alan is more than happy to explain. He teaches her how to make a compass by putting a needle and cork in a bowl of water, and how to make a sundial as well.

By the time Arthur comes back, he tells DW to shoo away.

Dw later hears Arthur talking about going to a science museum, and asks if she can go. Arthur however is entirely against having her come, and their parents agree that DW might find the museum a bit boring.

This frustrates DW, and she walks away, calmly declaring that she believes Arthur will soon be begging to take her to the museum.

Arthur comes home from school the next day, baffled to find their backyard has been turned into a science museum by DW single-handedly. After manipulating Arthur into paying everything he has in his pockets, which granted is not much, DW shows various kids from her preschool, different science experiments she's conducted. However, the science experiments are on another level of flawed. DW claims that turning on a hose will cause water to come out. Not because the water is coming from the hose, but because simply turning on a hose when it is in contact the bucket will cause water to appear.

Dwu also claims that wind is created by trees blowing, that the tides are created by the sand running away from the water so fast that it turns to liquid, and that the sky is blue because the other main colors have been taken from other various objects, like dirt, grass, and various fruits.

This all is too much for Arthur, who is frustrated by DW's complete lack of scientific knowledge, as well as her unwillingness to listen to him when he attempts to explain that she is wrong. Arthur resorts to bringing Alan to tell DW that she's wrong, But she refuses to back down even to him.

This finally pushes Arthur over the edge, and he rushes to his parents, begging to let DW come with them to the Science museum. This confuses their parents, but Arthur insists.

At the end of the episode, Arthur is relieved to have finally been able to prove DW was wrong with all of her experiments, however, DW leaves simply claiming that all of her experiments were fake, and the only true one she had was proving the she could make Arthur bag their parents to let her come to the museum. Dw walks away, claiming she's finally proved. She's smarter than Arthur, and Alan tells Arthur that maybe he does actually underestimate his little sister, to which Arthur responds:

"A smart DW? (Shutter) I don't know if the world is ready for that!"

I bothered to give a rather detailed summary of this episode, because I honestly believe that this strategy is kind of cool. DW had the charisma to round up most of her preschool classmates to come over to her backyard. It's unknown whether her classmates were in on the joke, but regardless, DW either Has the charisma and leadership able to convince her classmates that all of her experiments are valid, and yes, these kids are preschoolers, but so is herself- OR She has the charisma, leadership, and/or Manipulation tactics to be able to convince her classmates to put on this whole show for her to convince Arthur to take her to the museum.

The entire backyard scenario is also obviously situations of strategy, planning, and foresight.

Now sure, if you were to really look at: "Composite D.W. " Then yeah, the show would kind of be riddled with anti-feats for her. A lot of episodes she's featured in, as well as lines of dialogue, seem rather stupid, and are probably meant to showcase exactly how differently preschoolers see the world to adults.

However, it is somewhat of a running gag in the show for DW to prove that she's a lot smarter than she seems. In an early episode, DW copies Arthur in everything that he does in order to get him to admit that he likes her being different from him, and also convinces their mom to let her watch her favorite show for every single day that she let herself watch Arthur's show while she copied him. It makes little sense for her mom to agree to this, so this is probably another manipulation feat for DW.

There are also later episodes where DW showcases her intelligence. There's an episode where DW has to put on a play, and DW wants Arthur to participate in it, however he refuses. When the play actually has to go on, DW runs away crying. When Arthur goes to comfort her, she claims that absolutely no one from her class offered to play the missing role in her play. Seeing her in a distressed state, and knowing that if he doesn't help, the show will most likely be canceled, ndw would be an emotional wreck for a while, which would probably cause Arthur a lot of problems of his own. Anyway, Arthur volunteers to play their missing role. The rule turns out to be very silly, however, Arthur plays it to the letter, and does a decent job, only to find out after the play has ended that DW actually never asked any of her classmates to play the missing role, and betting on Arthur giving in at the very last second the whole time.

There's also an episode where DW wants to watch an episode of: "Dr. Whozit." (A parody of Doctor Who), purely because everyone around her seems to have already watched it. However, her parents claim that it's probably a show that's a little bit too old for her. As in, she should wait until she's a little bit older before watching it.

That doesn't stop DW from figuring it out though. She tries to find the recording on the TV, but finds it has a parent's lock. She tries various tactics to get her parents, or even Arthur, to say what the code might be, however they all fail.

When their dad struggles to remember the internet password, he asks their mom what the password might be, and their mom tells him to check "the book." DW then realizes the password must be in that book as well.

DW gets her friend, Bud, to distract her Dad by asking a recipe for food. While Bud does this, DW manages to get the book down, and even though she can't read, she is able to figure out the password simply by noticing a picture that most looks like a television, noticing the password next to it has four shapes, the same number of letters needed for the password, and then she simply presses the buttons that most fit the shapes written in the book.

I remember when I watched this, I was kind of blown away by DW's intelligence. Sure, she can't read, but that didn't stop her from figuring out what the password was. In a way, this was almost as if she was deciphering an alien language, since she literally figured out what the password was, not by actually knowing what the letters were, but by simply copying their shapes.

I dunno, Maybe I'm completely out of it, but you can't tell me that DW doesn't at least deserve this post of recognition for her efforts. 🙃
