Beginning:
The story begins with the ancient origins of Halloween and Samhain in Celtic Ireland, where Samhain is portrayed as a festival associated with the dead, spirits, supernatural forces, sacrifices, and fears of evil returning during the coming winter. During one such Samhain, a fifteen-year-old boy named Enda becomes obsessed with Deirdre, the youngest daughter of the Druid king Gwynnwyll. Enda is mocked by his family because of his physical deformities and because Deirdre is already associated with Cullain. Enda eventually follows Deirdre to a stream and attempts to approach her, but she becomes frightened and believes he intends to attack her. Humiliated by her rejection, Enda becomes consumed by resentment. Later, during the Samhain celebration, Enda attacks Deirdre and Cullain and kills them. The tribe then kills Enda in retaliation, preserves his head and heart, and takes them to the Hill of Fiends. Deirdre and Cullain are buried, while the Druid king's shaman curses Enda's soul to wander and repeatedly experience his crime and punishment. Immediately afterward, the weather suddenly becomes unnaturally dark and cold, with snow appearing unusually early, which the tribe interprets as evidence that their deity has heard the curse. The narrative then explains how Samhain's traditions eventually evolved through Roman and Christian influence into Hallowmas, All Saints' Day, and eventually Halloween. Although the supernatural meaning of the holiday fades over time, the story establishes that inexplicable violent crimes occasionally occur on Halloween, suggesting that the ancient evil has never completely disappeared.
The story then jumps to Haddonfield, Illinois, on Halloween night in 1963. Six-year-old Michael Myers is preparing to go trick-or-treating in a clown costume. His grandmother criticizes modern Halloween traditions and tells Michael about the older, darker traditions surrounding the holiday. She recalls stories from her childhood about the Bogeyman, including animals being killed and a barn being burned. She also tells Michael about a strange Halloween incident in which a large dead hog was somehow placed on top of a chimney, causing smoke to fill a house. Michael's mother Edith becomes uncomfortable because she knows her son has already been experiencing disturbing problems. Edith explains that Michael has been getting into fights at school and at home, has begun wetting his bed again, hears voices telling him to hate people, and has violent dreams. Edith and her mother discuss the possibility that these problems resemble an incident involving Michael's grandfather, although the details are not explained yet. Michael then returns wearing his clown costume, and his grandmother encourages him to go out for Halloween while reminding Edith to call her later.
That same Halloween evening, Michael's older sister Judy is at home because her parents are out. Her boyfriend Danny arrives, and they spend time together. Before anything else happens, a group of children comes to the door trick-or-treating. Judy briefly jokes with them by asking what they will do if she refuses to give them candy, and one of the children says they will kill her. The children then receive candy and leave. Danny arrives shortly afterward, and he and Judy joke around and flirt. Judy pretends to threaten Danny with a carving knife, but it turns out she is only using it to carve a jack-o'-lantern. They finish the pumpkin, place it outside, and talk about the Halloween traditions surrounding jack-o'-lanterns, costumes, ghosts, goblins, and trick-or-treating. Danny then frightens Judy while wearing a Frankenstein mask. The two eventually become intimate upstairs while believing that Michael is still out trick-or-treating.
Meanwhile, Michael has returned home and watches Judy and Danny from outside. He hears them together and experiences intense anger because of a voice in his head that has been influencing him. The voice has appeared in his dreams and waking life and has increasingly ordered him to stop couples who resemble the lovers from his strange recurring visions. Michael remembers images of people in ancient-looking costumes dancing around a huge bonfire, with a couple resembling Judy and Danny while he stands among the crowd hating them. The voice tells him to return home, so Michael sneaks into the house without them noticing, enters the kitchen, takes a butcher knife, and waits for Judy and Danny to finish. Danny eventually leaves, promising Judy that he will call her the next day. Michael then follows Judy upstairs. Judy recognizes him and initially thinks his appearance is some kind of joke, but Michael attacks her and kills her. Afterward, he goes downstairs, eats a cookie, drinks milk, leaves the house carrying the knife, and waits outside. A dark sedan arrives carrying Michael's parents, and his father removes the clown mask and realizes what has happened.
Michael is then taken before a magistrate. Because he is only six years old, the court struggles with how to handle the case. Michael openly maintains that he committed the killing, while investigators have difficulty finding evidence against anyone else. Danny is questioned aggressively but is ultimately not established as the killer. The magistrate decides that Michael's actions were the result of madness and orders him committed to Smith's Grove Sanitarium rather than treated as an ordinary criminal. The court establishes that Michael's case must be reviewed periodically and that, if he remains institutionalized until age twenty-one, he would eventually be tried as an adult. Michael's parents are devastated as he is taken away. One person who remains deeply focused on him is psychiatrist Dr. Sam Loomis, who immediately senses that something is seriously wrong with the boy despite Michael's calm and seemingly innocent appearance.
Six months later, Loomis reports to Judge Christopher about Michael's behavior at Smith's Grove. Michael has not openly committed any misconduct that can be directly proven, but the other juvenile patients are strangely afraid of him. Loomis explains that Michael is the youngest person in the ward yet nobody bullies him because they seem terrified of him. Loomis also describes an incident involving a trustee named Gilden, who played a harmless prank on Michael by causing him to accidentally over-salt his food. Michael showed no reaction at the time, but Gilden later became severely ill from what was diagnosed as food poisoning. Loomis suspects Michael was responsible but cannot prove it. Loomis also tells the judge that the other children behave as though they instinctively sense danger around Michael.
Over the following months, more strange incidents occur whenever Michael feels insulted or mistreated. A boy who repeatedly changes the television volume later gets burned by scalding shower water even though Michael apparently never leaves his room. A nurse who argues with Michael later falls down the stairs and is seriously injured, while another boy who borrows one of Michael's games and fails to return it develops a severe illness. Loomis becomes increasingly convinced that Michael is somehow responsible, but nobody ever actually sees him commit these acts. At the same time, Michael gradually gains control over the juvenile ward because everyone is afraid to challenge him. Loomis realizes that Michael might potentially escape whenever he wants because of the influence he has over the other patients and staff, but Michael refuses to acknowledge this when Loomis confronts him. Loomis believes that Michael remains because he recognizes that Loomis is genuinely trying to help him.
As another review approaches, Loomis worries that Judge Christopher will eventually release Michael because there is still no direct evidence connecting him to the strange incidents. In mid-October, Michael unexpectedly asks Loomis whether the hospital can have a Halloween party. Loomis initially finds the request disturbing but realizes that the party could provide an opportunity to observe Michael closely. The hospital approves the event, and the patients become excited as they create costumes and decorations. As Halloween approaches, Michael becomes increasingly restless and irritable. Loomis recognizes what he considers an anniversary reaction to Michael's original Halloween crime and places the staff on heightened alert. He specifically wants multiple people watching Michael so that any suspicious behavior can be witnessed.
The Halloween party begins with Michael dressed as a clown. The children eat cake and drink soda before playing games. During musical chairs, a sixteen-year-old girl named Sophie defeats Michael for the last chair, which immediately makes Loomis pay close attention to Michael. The next game is apple-bobbing. Sophie is participating when the lights suddenly go out even though there is no obvious reason for a power failure. Loomis rushes toward the vat and the emergency generators restore the lights. Sophie is found beside the vat, soaked and apparently having fallen into the water. Loomis immediately searches for Michael and discovers him standing some distance away beneath a basketball backboard. Michael's costume and hands are completely dry. Loomis and a nurse revive Sophie, and the party is immediately ended. Loomis's attempt to catch Michael in an obvious act fails because there is still no direct proof that Michael caused the incident.
Loomis nevertheless eventually succeeds in keeping Michael institutionalized. On the day Loomis is supposed to go before Judge Christopher and argue that Michael remains dangerous, he learns that Judge Christopher suffered a fatal heart attack the previous night. A new judge takes over the case and is much less sympathetic toward Michael. Loomis gives the new judge a forty-five-page report detailing Michael's personality and the strange incidents that occurred during his years at Smith's Grove. Although there is still no direct evidence proving Michael caused the incidents, the new judge accepts Loomis's recommendation that Michael remain institutionalized. Fifteen years then pass.
The story next moves to October 30, 1978. Dr. Loomis and nurse Marion Treadwell travel through a rainy night toward Smith's Grove to deal with Michael, who is now an adult. Loomis explains that Michael is supposed to undergo a physical examination and appear before a judge, after which he is expected to be heavily sedated before being transported. When they arrive near Smith's Grove, they immediately notice that something is wrong. Several patients are wandering outside the institution despite the fact that they are not supposed to be there. One escaped patient approaches their vehicle. Loomis speaks with him and learns that the man says Michael is gone and that the evil is gone. Loomis becomes extremely concerned and orders Marion to drive toward the entrance.
Loomis reaches the guard booth and discovers that the guard has been killed. While Loomis contacts the main building, Marion remains inside the station wagon as escaped patients move around the parking lot. One of them climbs onto the roof of the car and attacks Marion through the window. She manages to escape the immediate danger and drives away in panic while the attacker clings to the vehicle. Marion loses control in the rain, crashes into curbs, and is thrown across the vehicle. Loomis finds her afterward and determines that she is badly shaken but alive. He then sees someone inside the station wagon and realizes that the vehicle is moving away. The mysterious driver manages to get the car onto the highway and disappears into the darkness. Loomis tells Marion that the evil is gone, although she is not comforted by his statement.

Prison Escape:
To first explain Michael’s strategy we first need to cover Michael’s strategy shielding.
1. When Michael killed both Judy and Danny there wouldn’t be any consequences for him → to begin with, Michael at the time was of young age. His age was crucial because the juvenile justice system generally treats very young children differently from adults, focusing more on their age, capacity, and circumstances than imposing ordinary adult criminal punishment. This by itself would significantly reduce the likelihood of him receiving ordinary punishment. Michael's mental condition could also have played a role → Michael's family was well aware of Michael’s disturbing dreams and the voices inside his head. Although, it wasn’t intentional the condition itself reinforced the narrative that Michael wasn’t a hard cold killer—but instead a misunderstood mentally ill kid. This worked so well in fact, even though Michael himself affirmed to killing them, the court still doesn’t find him “guilty” in a regular sense.
 (Strategy Shielding)
2. Because the other patients fear Michael, this makes it easier for Michael to formulate his strategy as there would be fewer obstacles and fewer variables he would have to account for. However, it also serves as a way for Michael to psychologically dominate the ward. Because everyone is so afraid to challenge him, he gains influence without needing to overt force. As Loomis commented, with this, he could escape the prison any time he wants.

3. Regarding the incidents with Michael, he could freely kill anyone he wants for a couple reasons → Michael has already established the narrative that he’s naturally composed and poker face which hides any behavioral evidence. Even when accused he doesn’t openly reveal what he’s doing which makes it harder for Loomis to become certain about his motives which in turn, keeps him guessing. Michael’s murders also couldn’t legally be proven → Despite the obvious connections between the victims’ interactions with Michael and their subsequent deaths, this would not constitute sufficient legal evidence to convict Michael of anything. This becomes even more apparent when considering that there were no witnesses to the incidents, leaving Loomis’s accusations as little more than unsubstantiated claims.
(Acting Skills + Concealment, Strategy Shielding + Structure)

Michael’s unsupported murders works well in Michael’s favor → because of Michael’s personality he hasn’t exhibited any potential for trouble making and the fact that Loomis’s convictions were baseless would lead to Christophoer releasing Michael. However, I assume Michael did not intend to escape around that time, especially considering that Halloween was still months away. Even so, his previous actions had already laid the groundwork, ensuring that when Halloween eventually arrived, he would be more than prepared to escape and return to Haddonfield. (H2 confirms that Michael waited for Halloween to come)

I won’t go over Michael’s previous murders because to be quite frank nothing special happens. Despite Loomis having a crowd of people watching over him Michael still somehow finds a way to turn the generator off → this allows Michael to kill discreetly, whilst freeing him of any future convictions. Michael then predicts that Loomis would take this opportunity to catch Michael in the act, so Michael hurries to eliminate any evidence by drying his clothes and body.

Regarding the new judge, there is also a possibility that Michael anticipated a stricter ruling and therefore recognized that he could eventually remain confined to Smith’s Grove. If Michael genuinely knew that the new judge was going to release him, then confidently killing the old judge would make more strategic sense.

Adversities: It’s also important to recognize that, while executing these schemes, Michael was operating under significant adversity—most notably, Dr. Loomis. To begin with, Loomis constantly monitored Michael’s movements and was the only individual capable of recognizing his true capabilities. This obviously made Loomis an ongoing threat  as Loomis would very often formulate strategies or use tactics to try and catch Michael in the act. However, of course Michael usually anticipated these tactics and handled them accordingly. Furthermore, Michael was institutionalized, meaning his movements were heavily restricted by the constant presence of staff and the reputation he had established within the institution. Despite these constraints, Michael was able to reduce many of these adversities through his influence alone, using the fear and reputation he had cultivated to reduce interference and make his plans considerably easier to execute.
(Risk Management)
Note
The rest of the events in the novels are essentially identical to those in the original film, with the main difference being the additional context and internal monologue provided by the novels. Because of this, I won’t be going over Michael’s strategy for these events.
Conclusion
Surprisingly, the novelization doesn’t provide many buffs. For the most part, it enhances Michael’s social intelligence, manipulation, adversity capacity—particularly his impulse control—and his foresight.

  [ NOTES ]
In a novelization for the film, it was stated Myers had foreseen the events of 1978 around 15 years in advance. This by itself is vague so we’re going to take the most literal and high ball interpretation—he predicted everything and formulated this plan in advance.
[ THE DATE ]
Notably, Michael’s scheme occurred on Halloween. This specific date benefits his strategy greatly, as it introduces a layer of assurance in the strategy (all of which act as natural micro-contingencies for Michael’s scheme) —>
1. Any calls made to law enforcement about overly violent action (especially in such a small town) may simply be casted away as pranks within the inevitable sea of prank calls the station will receive at this time of year, concealing any real violent actions entirely.
2. Any calls made to friends will also inevitably look as though they are simply playful pranks, making the action of actively calling the police as a result of any of these calls seem completely illogical.
3. Even if someone did call the police due to a friend’s call, it defaults back to the first point - The station likely gets these calls all night, and hence will discard it.
4. Neighbors likely won’t take any plea for help at their door seriously (especially from a teenager). Why is this? Well, Halloween itself has a plethora of teenagers playing pranks (especially in such a small town), it only stands to reason that many incidents will go ignored.
5. Even if the neighbors did believe what they are seeing is true and called the police, it again defaults to the first point - The station simply has no way of confirming the story and is forced to write it off as a prank, especially if a teenager is involved somehow.
6. Naturally, plenty of individuals will be out and dressed in costumes. This conceals Michael’s odd outfit and appearance, completely obscuring him from the public eye while he enacts his plan.
[ CONTINGENCY’S ]
Prior to his murder spree, Michael escaped the mental institution by instigating a mass breakout of inmates. Although the events leading up to the escape are never shown, the aftermath reveals extensive preparation. After stealing Loomis's car, Michael had apparently fabricated false IDs to maintain a concealed identity before and after his scheme, compiled notes regarding commercial flight schedules, and acquired or possibly created maps of Haddonfield to aid his navigation.

 Myers possessed multiple escape plans meaning he had contingencies for potential setbacks. Michael also planned his scheme on exactly Halloween which adds extra strategy shielding and contingencies. For example, a lot of individuals will be out and dressed in costumes. This conceals Michael’s odd outfit and appearance, completely obscuring him from the public eye while he enacts his plan. This actually helps later down the line in misdirecting Loomis with Bennett Tramer.

[ 15 YEARS LATER ]
Prior to Halloween night and the murder spree, Michael stalks these notable characters:

1. Laurie Strode
2. Annie Brackett
3. Lynda Van Der Klok
4. Bob Simms
5. Tommy Doyle
6. Lindsy Wallace
Throughout Michael's escape, his psychiatrist, Dr. Sam Loomis, engages in a relentless cat-and-mouse pursuit across Haddonfield, determined to stop him before he kills again. As Halloween night unfolds, Michael carefully orchestrates his strategy around Annie babysitting Lindsey at the Wallace house, while Laurie Strode babysits Tommy Doyle at the Doyle house. All the while, Loomis is trying to catch him.
Prior to his first encounter with Laurie, Annie, and Lynda, Michael Myers breaks into a hardware store and grabs his costume, a knife, and rope. All attention is diverted from Michael as the police chalk it up to just kids (Halloween date coming into play) and don’t take it seriously as the severity of the items aren’t a high priority.

[ LOOMIS SHIELDING ]
* Myers steals Judith’s tombstone, influencing Loomis into believing Myers is simply planning on returning to his original home as a result.
* Myers abandons the stolen car near the original Myers house, furthering his misdirection.
* Myers kills two dogs (one of which being the Wallace’s pet), each serving two purposes —> 1) It grants him a tool of misdirection to use against Loomis and 2) It causes Lindsey (the little girl Annie is babysitting) to become increasingly concerned and possibly hectic, ensuring Annie relocates her to the Doyle house where Laurie is babysitting Tommy.
* Myers places the stray dog’s corpse in the original Myers’ home (and ate a part of it - Which establishes an action that is needed for survival, planting the idea in Loomis that Myers utilizes the house as a base of sorts), manipulating Loomis into believing the house is Michae’s base for the night and that he will inevitably return with the tombstone (misdirecting him into believing his original prediction was completely accurate).
The end result of this portion of the strategy contains multiple purposes & elements, all crucial aspects Myers utilizes later on within the scheme.
1. By fabricating a false plan of action concerning Michael’s childhood home, Loomis (and any other bodies of law enforcement accompanying him) is misdirected into staying at the Myers house, looking out for Michael’s eventual return. This naturally works in Michael’s favor, as it shields Laurie or the other victims from receiving aid at any point in the strategy - Scattering law enforcement’s time and resources via misdirection, layering his scheme with a fabricated strategy to ensure his main strategy goes unhindered.
2. This misdirection notably spreads law movement’s resources thin, forcing Loomis and likely other officers to keep watch over the Myers house. This spread of resources compliments the aforementioned factor the date - The station itself already is unlikely to dispatch resources on any calls revolving around teenagers, this outcome is further strengthened by Myers indirectly spreading their already conservative resources across town.
3. By specifically killing the Wallace’s dog, it may result in Lindsey becoming discomforted with the dog’s disappearance. This heavily increases the likelihood of Lindsey arguing with Annie, which strengthens Annie’s decision to inevitably relocate Annie to her nearest friend, who is also babysitting (Laurie). This leaves the Wallace house empty, granting Michael the perfect opportunity to continue his strategy - As well as building the Doyle house (where Laurie is babysitting) as an area of high value to protect due to the fact there are now two children present (which will become important for the strategy overall). [NOTE — Annie’s primary reason for abandoning Lindsey in the film is wanting to see her boyfriend, Michael simply has this tactic prepared to ensure these events would play out with higher likelihood].
[ WALLACE HOUSE MURDERS ]
Now that Myers had enacted his first act of strategic shielding and had granted himself the necessary opportunity to further his actions (via killing the Wallace’s dog), he enacts the main murders that take place —>
* Annie returns to the Wallace house after dropping Lindsey at the Doyle house, where she is ambushed and killed by Myers before she can make contact with anyone.
* Laurie’s friends, Lynda and Bob, arrive at the Wallace house knowing their close friend (Annie) is there (Myers ensured they would still hold this notion until they arrived there, by swiftly eliminating Annie before she could make contact). They enter the house but can’t see to locate Lindsey, so they decide to play around for a little. Myers ambushes Bob and kills him, before draping a sheet over himself and wearing Bob’s glasses to disguise himself as him. He goes back over to Lynda in the disguise, causing her discomfort by staying silent. She calls Laurie as a result (as Myers had anticipated) and Myers swiftly kills her as well, over the phone.
* A bit after this, Myers cuts the phone line to the Doyle house (likely directly after the call - To ensure any calls being made from the Doyle house as a result would be cut off).
These three murders all served strategic purposes, mainly revolving around a double-layered trap complete with a contingency Myers had implemented into the events to lure Laurie to the Wallace house.
1. From Laurie’s perspective, her friend had called her and suddenly disappeared from the call - Appearing to have been physically forced away from the phone. However, it could easily be a Halloween prank, so calling the police would be a complete overreaction. This naturally establishes a sense of worry into Laurie (without compromising the strategy), encouraging her to investigate the house.
2. If Laurie simply assumes it’s a Halloween prank or decides to not investigate given the circumstances, she’ll inevitably realize the phone line has been cut. She will recall, however, that Lynda had called her from the Wallace house - Implying the phone there is still functional. This is the trap’s second layer, ensuring Lourie must still enter the Wallace house due to the fact that the Doyle house isn’t able to make or receive calls (a huge negative impact for Laurie, as she is currently babysitting two children at the Doyle house - Indirectly orchestrated by Myers, ensuring she has a high-priority area to protect).
3. If Laurie does call law enforcement and is genuinely concerned by the phone call, she will discover the line had been cut. This fundamentally acts as a contingency to ensure Michael’s strategy isn’t hindered, trapping Laurie further (which even if the call does go through, even for a little, the likelihood it’s simply written off as a prank call is extremely high given the aforementioned date contingencies and the law enforcement resource spreading, all calculated by Myers).
4. By ensuring Lynda and Bob arrive at the Wallace house, it allows for the neighbors surrounding the house to witness two teenagers walk into the building. This plants the idea in their heads that a prank could be in play, a crucial contingency Myers naturally implements his strategy once Laurie is successfully lured into the house.
5. By attacking the Wallace house instead of the Doyle house (where Laurie is staying) or the Strode’s official residence, it ensures that even if Loomis sees through the initial misdirection and somehow knows about Laurie’s connection to Michael (them being siblings) and thus locates her to preclude Michael, he finds no trace of Michael due to him precisely targeting an area (the Wallace house) that would bypass any reasoning process Loomis would generate.
[ LAURIE TRAP ]
Inevitably, Laurie is forced to investigate the Wallace house. As she walks in, the house is completely devoid of lighting. She eventually stumbles upon a bed, with a body resting on it complete with Judith’s tombstone. From here, we see Michael had implemented several immaculate forecasts and micro-contingencies —>
1. As Laurie stands back in shock, she comes into immediate contact with another body. Laurie runs through the house and each way she turns she finds another body, until she has discovered each and every one of her friend’s corpses. This implies Myers had predicted her precise pathway and movements throughout the house, and had prepared the bodies in such a manner to align with her actions.
2. When she reaches the hallway, Myers is there standing waiting for her - He slashes her, which causes her to fall off the balcony and onto the lower level of the floor. Michael had purposefully waited in this area of the house, anticipating they’d eventually cross paths here and ensured they would via scattering the bodies in precise locations that would result in Laurie turning away from them (in this case, each body’s position can be counted as a micro-contingency to ensure this alignment).
3. Laurie observes a nearby door and attempts to open it, only to discover it’s been locked by Myers beforehand (showcasing a micro-contingency he had prepared in foresight of his initial attack failing).
4. Then, Laurie attempts to flee to the back door - Where she notices it’s been jammed shut via a rake. Myers had enacted this micro-contingency as well, anticipating her actions perfectly.
5. Eventually, Laurie still manages to escape the house via breaking the door’s window. She runs over to the nearest neighbor’s house first, but the neighbor refuses to open the door in face of Laurie’s pleas. Myers had predicted this outcome and accounted for it as a contingency, given the aforementioned assurances of the date (Halloween night obviously makes these types of pranks more abundant, making neighbors untrusting of such events). This contingency is strengthened by the fact Lynda and Bob had been publicly lured to the Wallace house beforehand, allowing any nearby neighbor to witness two teenagers enter the house - Increasing the likelihood they’d simply discard Laurie’s pleas as a simple prank. Even if the neighbors did let Laurie in, they knew the likelihood of the police actually responding was low due to the fact it’s Halloween and plenty of prank calls are made - In this case, they’d rather not put themselves in the line of fire, the danger would far outweigh the reward (if they take Laurie’s words as true).
With this, Laurie returns to the Doyle house. This finalizes Michael’s strategy, as it focuses the trio (Laurie, Tommy, & Lindsey) into one location that notably doesn’t have any form of communication (due to the phone line being cut) with law enforcement, as well as misdirecting Loomis from investigating any time soon due to Michael’s fabrication plan of action revolving around his childhood home. These shieldings converge into Michael having the perfect opportunity to eliminate all three, successfully completing his goal with no method of being counteracted.
[ ADAPTABILITIES ]
Despite Michael’s strategy having numerous micro-contingencies, assurances, and shielding tactics - It still, notably, had immaculate potential for adaptation —>
1. If the Wallace’s neighbor was to let Laurie in, it would leave Tommy and Lindsey isolated in the Doyle home without phone line communication. This grants Myers the perfect opportunity to eliminate them, as all parties (including Laurie) will have been removed from the equation. Not only this, the timeframe he’d have to complete this would be sizable due to him indirectly spreading law enforcement resources thin - Prolonging the time in which it’d take them to arrive at the Wallace house, which is near the Doyle house, in the first place. If Laurie noticed this, she’d have to return to the Doyle house regardless as to save Tommy and Lindsey - If not, they’d both be killed and it’d be her own fault.
2. Even if Lynda and Bob never arrived at the Wallace house, Michael’s strategy would be unhindered. He could simply make the phone call himself, not saying a word - Which would cause intrigue in Laurie regardless due to the fact she believes Annie is still at the Wallace house by this time.
3. If Annie never chooses to bring Lindsey to the Doyle house, Myers could simply kill them both and make the phone call himself as well. This would still leave at least one child in the hands of Laurie’s care whilst luring her, keeping the overall value of the strategy intact.
4. On the same note as point three, Myers could simply kill Annie and allow Lindsey to flee. Here, there’s two outcomes —> 1) If Lindsey flees to the Doyle house, she’d alert Laurie of Annie’s murder. Upon attempting to call law enforcement, Myers would’ve already or be in the process of cutting the phone line, intercepting her entirely. This would force her into investigating the Wallace house, resulting in the same outcome of her being lured. 2) Lindsey runs to a neighbor’s house and alerts them of Annie’s death (if the neighbor doesn’t listen to Lindsey and assumes it’s a prank, she’d eventually end up at the Doyle house and it defaults to the first outcome). Given law enforcement having spread resources due to Michael’s indirect manipulation, it would likely take them a while to approach the Wallace house in the first place (assuming it isn’t taken as a prank call, in which case it’d prolong the timeframe even more). Now, with the Wallace house under focus, it leaves the Doyle house completely isolated. In this outcome, Michael would have completely shifted the focus from the Doyle house to the Wallace house, isolating Laurie and Tommy. With this, Myers has the opportunity to kill both Laurie and Tommy.
