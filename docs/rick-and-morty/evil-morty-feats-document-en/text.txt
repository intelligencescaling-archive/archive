OVERVIEW
Evil Morty is a Morty who, over multiple seasons, goes from being a clandestine operator who controls a Rick through brain transmission technology to becoming the president of the Citadel of Ricks, destroying said Citadel, and breaking the Central Finite Curve. His arc culminates with the capture of Rick Prime, obtaining the blueprints of the Omega Device, and later, a defeat caused by overconfidence.
This document covers exclusively the canonical material of the animated series Rick and Morty (Seasons 1 to 9). Comics, video games, or unaired material are not included.
CRITICAL SCOPE NOTE
Evil Morty is not born as an innate genius. His technical knowledge is acquired cumulatively: he adapts already existing technologies and scans the brains of the Ricks he eliminates. His main cognitive advantage is not pure inventiveness nor absolute superiority over the Ricks on their own turf (physics, invention, direct combat), but a very consistent reading of the structural blind spot of the Ricks —the systematic underestimation of Mortys and excessive ego— combined with extreme patience and the ability to use institutions, infrastructure, and others' arrogance as weapons. Feats that depend heavily on the exclusive physics or technology of the verse are recorded, but they are separated from pure reasoning, planning, and insight feats.
I. CONDITIONS AND CONTEXT OF THE UNIVERSE
To correctly evaluate Evil Morty's feats it is essential to understand the rules of the environment in which he operates.
The Central Finite Curve
Rick C-137 created the Central Finite Curve: a filter that separates the infinite universes where Rick is the most intelligent being from the rest of the multiverse. Inside the Curve, the narrative presents the Ricks as the cognitive peak. Outside of it there exist beings capable of inventing interdimensional travel and multiversal-scale threats. Evil Morty identifies the Curve as a prison and dedicates years to escaping it.
Nature of the Ricks
All Ricks are essentially the same egocentric and arrogant individual across different timelines, with variations. They possess extreme intelligence in technological inventiveness and understanding of physics/multiverse, but they fail repeatedly and systematically in:
• Social and institutional reasoning
• Self-criticism
• Detection of their own biases
• Evaluation of threats that do not resemble them (especially threats coming from Mortys)
The Council of Ricks, in S1E10, accuses C-137 of the murders based almost exclusively on his history of non-cooperation. Many Ricks die in trivial ways (they run in a straight line toward portals opened by C-137, insist on using hacked portals, lack Phoenix Protocol in critical situations). Underestimation is the most constant and exploitable Achilles' heel of the species.
Nature of the Mortys and the Citadel
The Mortys function as shields: the “incompetent” brain waves of a Morty cancel the genius waves of a Rick, making him harder to track. The Citadel, created by C-137 and rebuilt after its destruction, operates as a complete capitalist city with structural inequality between Ricks and Mortys, and also among the Ricks themselves (charisma and connections generate real hierarchies). After the pain matrix method demonstrated its effectiveness, the Citadel became an industrial-scale market of cloned Mortys.
Methodological principle of this document
Context is always prioritized over the “clean” or inflated version of each feat. What at first glance may seem like massive manipulation of the most intelligent beings in the universe, upon examining the Citadel and the actual behavior of the Ricks, turns out to be largely the deliberate exploitation of a structural blind spot that they themselves gift.
II. ORIGIN AND FORMATION
Episode S7E5 reveals Evil Morty's starting point.
His original Rick was toxic and gave him an ultimatum (“either you're with me or you leave”). Evil Morty fakes reconciliation, gets him drunk, builds that same night the brain control patch and takes control. The patch technology already existed (confirmed in S1E10 by a Rick who had seen it before). It is most likely that he had observed it or obtained the blueprints through his own Rick.
He erases his data from the Morty Agency directory so as not to be trackable. He begins killing Ricks and scanning their brains. This accumulation of technical knowledge —not an extraordinary innate intellect— is the main source of his later technological competence.
The controlled Rick retained enough consciousness to desire death; his final “kill me” before the freed Mortys seems genuine. After the failure of S1E10, Evil Morty lives as just another Morty in the Morty District until he sees the news of the Citadel elections and decides to run.
NOTE ON ACQUIRED KNOWLEDGE
Evil Morty does not demonstrate an innate cognitive capacity that breaks the human ceiling in a general way. His trajectory is that of a resentful Morty who adapts existing technology, accumulates knowledge systematically by scanning the brains of Ricks, and combines that knowledge with a very consistent reading of the Ricks' blind spot and with extreme patience. This distinction is central in order not to inflate his profile.
III. S1E10 — CLOSE RICK-COUNTERS OF THE RICK KIND
First appearance. Evil Morty operates from the shadows. The “Evil Rick” with the scar is a remotely controlled puppet.
FEAT 1 — Remote control of a Rick + operation of the Morty Matrix
He completely controls a Rick through brain transmission technology. Through him he orders and supervises the murder of at least 27 Ricks in their own dimensions and maintains a secret facility with a matrix of tortured Mortys whose pain generates a brain-wave cancellation field powerful enough to hide from other Ricks.
ANALYSIS
It is not just “using a puppet.” It is operating a complex infrastructure (mass capture of Mortys, maintenance of the pain field, logistics of interdimensional assassinations) while remaining hidden. The level of control is total: the Rick speaks, acts and jokes according to Evil Morty's orders.

IV. S3E7 — THE RICKLANTIS MIXUP / TALES FROM THE CITADEL
Evil Morty goes from clandestine operative to political actor. The rebuilt Citadel functions as a capitalist city with deep inequality.
FEAT 6 — Correct diagnosis of the Citadel's contradictions + effective political speech
He identifies and verbalizes with precision the real structural flaws: the Ricks/Mortys division is functional to power; the schools indoctrinate; the streets keep the Mortys fighting among themselves; the factories exploit Ricks of identical IQ for a fraction of the boss's salary. The speech connects both with marginalized Mortys and with lower-class Ricks and allows him to rise significantly in the polls.
ANALYSIS
The merit is not in inventing a new or especially sophisticated rhetoric. It is in correctly reading a system that he knows firsthand (as a Morty who has lived in the Citadel) and in choosing the message that best exploits the already existing cracks. It is competent social and institutional reading, not a cognitive tour de force. Influencing marginalized Mortys who are already discontented does not require genius; channeling also the resentment of exploited Ricks does raise the level of the diagnosis.

FEAT 9 — Elimination of the Shadow Council
Once in the presidency, the Shadow Council (the Ricks who really controlled the Citadel from before the formal Council) appears and makes it clear to him that the chair is irrelevant: they still rule. Evil Morty asks “Who's with me?” and, with a snap, the guards execute them. Afterwards he consciously discards the grandiloquent speech (“speeches are for campaigns”) and moves to action.
ANALYSIS
This is the most spectacular moment of the episode, but its real difficulty must be measured with the full context. The Ricks of the Shadow Council suffer the most constant structural defect of their species: they systematically underestimate anyone who is not a Rick. Even after Evil Morty won the elections, they continue treating him as a temporary pawn. They do not activate serious security protocols, they have no Phoenix Protocol or evident contingencies, and they die from ordinary gunshots. Evil Morty does not need to “lower their guard” through especially deep manipulation. They themselves gift him the opening out of ego. His intelligence here consists of correctly identifying that blind spot and betting on it at the exact moment when he has formal power. It is a sharp reading of the Ricks' Achilles' heel, not an intellectual overcoming in open field.

V. S5E10 — RICKMURAI JACK
Real objective of the entire arc: to get out of the Central Finite Curve. Everything previous were means.
FEAT 10 — Partial brain scan + completion during dinner
He had already scanned most of C-137's brain years earlier (S1E10). At dinner he only needs to complete the remaining part. He controls the pace of the conversation just long enough to finish the scan without C-137 realizing until it is already too late. He uses a device; he does not absorb the memories directly into his mind.
ANALYSIS
It is not an improvised full scan. It is the continuation and closing of a process begun years earlier. The merit is in the long-term patience and in knowing exactly how much time he needed to prolong the conversation. C-137 does not detect the scan in progress.

FEAT 22 — Vulnerability due to overconfidence (defeat)
After many victories, he lowers his guard: he takes C-137 to his lair and shows off. C-137 takes exact advantage of that opening, introduces the Omega Device blueprints into the machine itself and erases it from all realities. In the final combat he possesses very advanced technology (moldable force field, high-power ray, reverse-engineered temporal freeze device), but without the shield he is physically vulnerable. C-137 had anticipated the use of the temporal freezer and had already called the Time Police. Evil Morty is forced to surrender.
ANALYSIS
The clearest demonstration of his limit. The same pattern that he exploited for years in the Ricks (underestimation + ego) ends up applying to him. He got used to winning, lowered his guard, showed his base and lost the only existential weapon that guaranteed his security. The defeat is not due to lack of technical intelligence, but to a failure of psychological discipline: overconfidence after a streak of victories.

IX. CONCLUSION
Evil Morty is not a genius who surpasses the Ricks on their own turf (inventing, physics, direct combat). He is someone who:
1. Identifies in a consistent way the biggest blind spot of the Ricks (underestimation + ego).
2. Plans on extremely long timeframes.
3. Uses institutions, infrastructure and the collective stupidity of the Ricks themselves as weapons.
4. Is willing to burn entire civilizations of Ricks and Mortys to achieve a single objective: to get out.
5. Accumulates technical knowledge in an acquired way (brain scanning), not innate.
His greatest demonstrated weakness is the same one he exploited in others: overconfidence after a streak of victories. When he felt superior, he lowered his guard, showed his base and lost the Omega Device.
Context matters more than the “clean” description of each feat. Without the context of the Citadel and the real behavior of the Ricks, his achievements inflate until they seem like massive manipulation of the most intelligent beings in the universe. With the context, he is revealed as a sharp and reiterated reading of a defective system, executed with exceptional patience and coldness from the weakest possible position on the board.
