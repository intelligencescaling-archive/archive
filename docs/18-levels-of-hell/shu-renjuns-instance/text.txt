Shu Renjun’s Instance

Context: True Self Players | The 18 Levels of Hell: Lying is Forbidden Here Wiki | Fandom
The Dungeon Structure: This arc takes place across a series of player-created dungeons inside the Hell instance system. The six players involved are each required to design one dungeon for the others to survive. The dungeons are played sequentially, and each player's dungeon is cleared in turn. The final, third secret chamber of the Hell instance contains the main plot — and crucially, it is where the Dungeon Boss (Hua Changsheng) operates.
The Scoring System: Each dungeon cleared earns a player 1 point. The player with the highest score at the end of all six dungeons survives. If two or more players tie for highest, additional rules apply. Critically: Hua Changsheng's skill can tamper with the score data directly, allowing her to override the legitimate results.
Player-Designed Dungeons: In the third secret chamber, players are given the unique ability to design their own dungeons for the others to navigate. These dungeons must follow the "principle of puzzle-solving" — they cannot be designed as guaranteed death traps with no solution. However, the difficulty has no upper limit, and players can exploit this to create near-impossible scenarios.
The Six Players:
Chen Ran — The protagonist. Three-Star player with Military Form ideology. Possesses "Eternal Kingdom" (called "Word Weaver: Eternal Realm" or "Eternal Dominion" in this arc) — a True Self-level skill that destroys all active player-created skills within range, can penetrate dimensional boundaries, and prohibits skill use for one hour afterward. Requires receiving a Lie before activation. Also possesses nascent "Highest Good is Like Water" Heart Cultivation — a chaotic, intuitive counter-system to standard rational thinking.
Qiu Yinong — Chen Ran's partner. Three-Star Weird Speaker. Possesses "Wheel of Time" — a skill that resets time by up to five minutes, pulling all affected players back to an earlier state and causing memory loss of the intervening period. Can be used repeatedly as long as a Ghost Language (Guǐ Yǔ) exists in the dungeon. The Ninth True Self Player is believed to reside within her.
Lin Xuan — Three-Star player with Space Skill "Magic Cube Universe" (also called "Cube World"). Creates up to six parallel dimensions simultaneously, transfers players between spaces, and includes dimensions with faster or slower time flow than the original. His skill is bound by the dungeon's rule system — a fatal flaw discovered in this arc. A gambling addict whose greatest weakness is over-reliance on his skill at the expense of independent thinking.
Shu Renjun — Five-Star player. Possesses "Forbidden Bookstore" — a Weird Speaker skill that can record events in a supernatural book and then force players to re-enact those recorded events in a scripted performance. Has achieved "Unity of Heaven and Man" Heart Cultivation — a rare, rule-transcending system that makes him resistant to standard psychological manipulation.
Hua Changsheng — The arc's true hidden antagonist and Dungeon Boss. Three-Star Weird Speaker. Possesses "Super Energy Modeling" — a Superego-level skill that can tamper with virtual world data, including score statistics. Can force a dungeon settlement at will, overriding the legitimate results. Has been concealing this skill throughout the entire dungeon. Previously killed a Five-Star player using this same method.
Big Gold Tooth (Cui Yu) — Four-Star player from the Underworld resistance organization. Possesses "Golden Mouth, Jade Words" — a skill with a trigger mechanism: if he breaks a chain of consecutive affirmative statements, his subsequent words are heard as their opposite by affected targets. A true believer in the Underworld's cause, willing to sacrifice himself strategically.
Stars are basically levels. The higher the Star, the more intelligent and powerful someone is.
True Self Players are 10 individuals who have achieved an advanced state of psychological and metaphysical integration within the game system, representing a higher tier of Player evolution beyond standard Player capabilities. The concept is closely tied to ego structure, self-awareness, and the ability to operate across different states of consciousness. To achieve this particular level, one has to reach 10 Stars and meet with the 1st True Self Player on the Eternal Land, a dimension separated from Hell.
True Self Players have achieved a level of psychological wholeness that manifests as enhanced capabilities within the game system. The distinction appears to be related to Freudian psychoanalytic structures (Id, Ego, Superego) but transcends simple psychological categorization into something metaphysically substantive within the game's reality. They have not yet reached True Self in Heart Cultivation, but all of them are Near True Self.
In short, True Self would be the state of complete detachment from all laws, emotions and concepts of the universe, while also simultaneously affecting the universe on the whims of your thought. A state of Godhood that Omniscient (a True Self Player watching over Hell) accomplishes.
Heart Cultivation Systems:
* Supreme Perfect Enlightenment (Buddhist): Standard system. Suppresses emotion, produces formulaic rational thinking. Weakness: predictable to sufficiently powerful No-Distance users like Omniscient.
* Highest Good is Like Water (Taoist): Integrates emotion into thinking, produces unpredictable intuitive leaps. Chen Ran is in the early stages of developing this.
* Unity of Heaven and Man (Confucian): Transcends the rules and social order that constrain all players. Shu Renjun has achieved this but is not fully immune to moral coercion.
The Liar Killer and the No-Lying Rule: Every player carries a Liar Killer weapon. If a player states something objectively false in a provable way, the weapon fires autonomously. Players use careful language ("perhaps," "likely," "should") to avoid lying. "Future Lies" are binding oaths — if violated, the weapon fires. "Ghost Language" (Guǐ Yǔ) is a false premise established as objective truth; breaking it causes those who contradict it to technically lie.
No-Distance: A high-level mental combat technique. At the Superego level (the third realm of the Spiritual Platform), it can project real players into a mental world and simulate their future behavior with near-perfect accuracy. "No-Distance Kill" refers to the combination of Qiu Yinong's Wheel of Time and repeated No-Distance attacks — the Wheel of Time pulls a target back before they can recover from No-Distance exhaustion, allowing indefinite mental assault.
Weird Speakers vs. Liar Killers: Weird Speakers can activate skills directly without needing to receive a Lie first. Standard players (Liar Killers) must receive a Lie before using a skill, which charges the weapon. This makes Weird Speakers more flexible but also more vulnerable to certain counters.

On one side stands Omniscient, a True Self Player operating outside the Instance, who uses No-Distance to access all knowledge within Hell’s environment. Through this ability, she constructs a perfect cognitive replica of Chen Ran, a projection that mirrors his state of mind, reproduces his reasoning patterns, and predicts his future decisions. This allows her to pre-counter every move he makes before he even executes it. On the other side are high-tier players such as Shu Renjun, who possess overwhelming experience, refined structured reasoning, and near immunity to manipulation due to Superego or Unity of Heaven and Man cultivation.
This creates a paradox with no apparent solution. If Chen Ran relies on structured reasoning, his thoughts become perfectly predictable to Omniscient. If he abandons structure, he is simply outclassed by veteran players. In either case, there is no viable decision-making framework that leads to victory.
Chen Ran does not attempt to solve this paradox directly. Instead, he invalidates it by removing the condition that makes it possible: continuity of cognition. His reinterpretation of Eternal Kingdom is central to this shift. While others view it as a one-hour defensive nullification, Chen Ran treats it as a rupture in causality. During this period, Omniscient’s No-Distance loses its data stream, all skills are disabled, and the continuous observation required for prediction collapses. The result is a closed, unobservable decision space in which Chen Ran can think freely without being modeled.
Outside of Eternal Kingdom, however, he enforces a strict behavioral constraint on himself. He refuses to think strategically, avoids forming conclusions, and does not interpret information. This is not passivity but deliberate information control. Any thought he finalizes becomes data that Omniscient can replicate. To prevent this, he splits his cognition into two modes: inside Eternal Kingdom, he acts as an active strategist; outside of it, he becomes non-committal and cognitively restrained.
Because he cannot safely think, Chen Ran shifts the burden of reasoning onto others. He forces his allies to generate the insights he needs while keeping himself insulated from the informational consequences. His treatment of Lin Xuan exemplifies this. By openly mocking him, questioning his value, and excluding him from discussions, Chen Ran creates a high-pressure environment designed to test whether Lin Xuan can function without relying on his Space Skill and whether he can deduce under stress. The result is successful: Lin Xuan independently realizes that Hua Changsheng’s plan relies on the interaction between Eternal Kingdom’s one-hour skill restriction and the Red Light, Green Light immobilization combined with time dilation. This would ensure that Chen Ran is unable to use his skill at the exact moment Hua Changsheng activates hers. Crucially, Chen Ran never reaches this conclusion himself—he needs Lin Xuan to do it so that the information does not become visible to Omniscient. Lin Xuan is thus elevated into an essential execution node in the strategy.
At the same time, Chen Ran conducts precise psychological probing of his opponents. With Hua Changsheng, he targets the concept of paternal love, triggering an immediate and violent emotional reaction. From this, he deduces that Superego cultivation does not eliminate emotional vulnerability; it merely suppresses it. Under the right stimulus, emotional instability still surfaces, providing a potential attack vector. With Shu Renjun, Chen Ran constructs a highly specific narrative: a child raised under oppressive control, who kills his mother to gain freedom, only to find himself constrained again by societal rules. This profile is not arbitrary but derived from the typical background associated with Unity of Heaven and Man cultivation, combined with observed behavior and dungeon context. Shu Renjun’s loss of composure and the manifestation of chains confirm the accuracy of the deduction—his immunity is not absolute, but maintained through control.
When analyzing the dungeon itself, Chen Ran applies strict logical reconstruction. The setup presents two statues requiring simultaneous backstabs, yet one of the supposed targets—the Third Person—has already died from a fall, making it impossible to stab them physically. This contradiction leads to the only viable resolution: the Adult and the Third Person share a single body as a chimera. From this, Chen Ran derives three conclusions: the Adult cannot kill the Third Person because they are physically unified; the Adult is aware of the Third Person while the latter remains unaware; and stabbing the Adult is equivalent to stabbing both. However, Chen Ran presents these conclusions as merely “possible,” because in a player-designed dungeon, an incorrect assertion could be treated as a lie and result in death.
Hua Changsheng then reveals her endgame. Her backstory involves a child who allowed two adults to die by untying their ropes, and her ability, Super Energy Modeling, enables her to directly manipulate score data. She sets all other players’ scores to zero and her own to four, triggering a forced settlement that would instantly eliminate everyone else. Her hidden advantage lies in timing: the combination of immobilization and time dilation ensures that Eternal Kingdom expires before the critical moment, leaving Chen Ran unable to respond. Once again, this trap is not deduced by Chen Ran himself, but by Lin Xuan, fulfilling the role Chen Ran assigned to him.
Cui Yu introduces another layer of complexity through his strategic sacrifice. Recognizing that survival is impossible and that his greatest value lies in eliminating Shu Renjun, he uses the Future Lie mechanism to bind himself to Shu Renjun in an unavoidable death pairing. His speech, while partially ideological, is also calculated to create a momentary hesitation in others, allowing him to secure the necessary position to enforce the binding.
Before his death, Shu Renjun makes a final move by using No-Distance to reveal Chen Ran’s operational plan to Hua Changsheng, including Lin Xuan’s spatial manipulation, Qiu Yinong’s memory resets, and the timing of Eternal Kingdom. His objective is not immediate trust but delayed validation. After memory resets, Hua Changsheng will eventually recover this information and confirm its accuracy, retroactively granting credibility to Shu Renjun even after his death.
Following this, Chen Ran faces Shu Renjun’s psychological dungeon, a simulated psychiatric scenario designed to collapse his perception of reality. He anchors himself to a specific inconsistency—his parents’ white hair, which contradicts deeply ingrained memories of their behavior. This anomaly allows him to reject the illusion. Across the three layers—family, love, and friendship—he consistently prioritizes logical inconsistency over emotional appeal. In the third layer, he identifies Chen Yang’s behavior as unreliable and refuses the offered pill, instead waiting for confirmation from a more consistent source. His key adaptation here is recognizing that his own emotional desire for the scenario to be real makes his perception inherently unreliable.
A hidden fourth layer follows, in which Chen Ran realizes that identity verification has not been completed because the expected confirmation phrase has not appeared. He is subjected to extreme physical and psychological torment, including dismemberment and interrogation, yet he does not react in any way. He understands that the trap is designed to catch those who revert to normal human responses prematurely. Only by maintaining complete non-response does he pass the test.
The final execution phase relies on a complex multi-space architecture orchestrated by Lin Xuan. At the critical moment, players are distributed across spaces with differing time flows. Hua Changsheng is delayed through repeated spatial transfers, while Qiu Yinong resets time, erasing memory of these actions. Lin Xuan then swaps the real players into a separate space and leaves copies behind. When Hua Changsheng activates her forced settlement, she kills only Shu Renjun and the copies, leaving the real team unharmed.
The true killing move, however, lies in Chen Ran’s exploitation of repetition. He recognizes that Shu Renjun will consistently use No-Distance in every loop created by Qiu Yinong’s Wheel of Time. Although memory is erased with each reset, the mental cost of using No-Distance accumulates. After nineteen loops, the total strain is applied simultaneously, causing both Shu Renjun and Hua Changsheng to collapse. This victory is achieved without direct confrontation—only by allowing consistent behavior under memory erasure to accumulate into self-destruction.
The accompanying chess match between Chen Ran and Shu Renjun mirrors this process. Each move corresponds to a known strategic element, each reset to a loop, and each replay to repeated execution. Shu Renjun anticipates the first eighteen iterations, but the nineteenth introduces a deviation—a move outside the predictable structure, symbolized by placing a piece at the center. This is the decisive break from determinism.
The entire plan operates across five layers simultaneously: physical (real versus copies), informational (delegated cognition), psychological (false perception of alliances), temporal (loop accumulation), and strategic (indirect action by Chen Ran). Shu Renjun ultimately realizes that he has lost not to Chen Ran, but to his own consistent behavior, stating, “I lost to myself.”
After the conflict, Chen Ran identifies a final meta-layer: the apparent weakness of prior opponents indicates a deliberate confidence-building phase. He anticipates stronger, coordinated threats ahead and responds by refusing to advance in rank, avoiding triggers that would escalate danger. A binding agreement is formed to enforce this constraint.
In the end, Chen Ran’s strategy operates on an entirely different axis from conventional approaches. He does not win by outthinking, outfighting, or outreacting his opponents. Instead, he breaks observation through Eternal Kingdom, externalizes cognition through others, distrusts his own perception when necessary, converts repetition into accumulated damage, and ultimately forces his enemies into defeating themselves. He never solves problems directly—he restructures the system so that the problems cease to exist. That is why Omniscient cannot fully predict him, why Hua Changsheng kills the wrong targets, and why Shu Renjun is defeated by his own logic.
