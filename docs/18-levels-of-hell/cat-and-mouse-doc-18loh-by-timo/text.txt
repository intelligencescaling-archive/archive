This makes every conversation in Hell a potential minefield. The Liar Killer also protects its owner from being physically touched by enemies: if someone tries to grab or touch you, they are repelled by a force field. Each dungeon has a theme, rules, and puzzles. There are multiple schools of Heart Cultivation:
Supreme Perfect Enlightenment (Buddhist-derived): The most common system. It suppresses emotions entirely, producing formulaic, perfectly rational thinking. Its weakness is that all players using it arrive at the same answers — making their behavior predictable to sufficiently advanced observers.
Highest Good is Like Water (Taoist-derived): A rarer, opposing system. Rather than suppressing emotions, it integrates them into thinking — producing genuinely unpredictable, intuitive leaps. Its weakness is extreme mental instability during use.
Unity of Heaven and Man (Confucian-derived): The rarest system. It involves actively challenging and transcending the rules and social order that constrain all players. Considered heretical by both Alliances because it threatens their power structure.
No-Distance: A high-level mental combat technique created by one of the ten True Self-level players. A player with No-Distance can pull opponents into a constructed "mental world" — a space inside their own consciousness. Inside this mental world, the No-Distance user has significant advantages: they can destabilize the opponent's thinking, extract lies, and at higher levels even simulate and predict the opponent's future behavior. There are three levels:

Ego State of Mind: Can project objects and environments into the mental world.
Superego State of Mind: Can project actual players into the mental world and simulate their behavior accurately enough to predict future actions.
True Self State of Mind: The ultimate level — the mental world becomes indistinguishable from reality.
Skills: Beyond Heart Cultivation and No-Distance, some players possess unique supernatural skills activated through their Liar Killer. To use a skill, a player must first receive a Lie from someone (which charges the weapon) and then speak a command. Skills range from spatial manipulation to time control to information recording. Two player types relevant to this arc:

Liar Killers: Standard players who must receive a Lie before using a skill.
Weird Speakers: A player type that can activate skills directly without needing a Lie first — making them more flexible but also more unpredictable.
Guǐ Yǔ (Ghost Language / Constructed Truth): A sophisticated technique where a player establishes a false premise as objective truth through a series of statements. Once the "Ghost Language" is in effect, anyone who contradicts the false premise is technically lying — even if the premise is objectively false. Breaking a Ghost Language requires someone to actively disprove the false premise in a way the system recognizes. This is one of the most powerful tools in high-level play.Future Lies: Binding oaths that players swear in advance. "I will do X" or "I will not do Y." THE CAT AND MOUSE GAME — CHRONOLOGICAL INTELLECTUAL FEATS
Context for newcomers: This is a death game taking place inside a supernatural dungeon. Players are divided into Cats (hunters) and Mice (prey) inside an 18-floor building with 6 rooms per floor. Mice must survive 2 hours without being caught; Cats must catch all Mice. A supernatural rule forbids lying — if you state something objectively false that can be proven as a lie, a gun materializes and shoots you. All surviving players possess "Heart Cultivation" — a mental discipline that suppresses emotion and allows near-perfect rational thinking. Players can transform into other people or objects, create parallel dimensions, and use special skills. The players involved are among the most elite survivors of this death game.

PHASE 1 — THE RULES ARE READ / FIRST FLOOR

The Mice read the Cat rules through Qiu Yinong's deduction
White: When the 12 Cats arrive at the building and read their version of the rules, the rules open with the phrase "You are the Cats." Qiu Yinong immediately recognizes the implication — if her rules say "You are the Cats," then the Mice's rules must have opened with "You are the Mice." This means each side received a different version of the ruleset, which means the two sides have access to information the other side doesn't.
🟣 Perception/Observation (Qiu Yinong): From a single phrase — the presence of "You are the Cats" at the top of her rules — she reverse-engineers what the enemy's rules must look like. Nobody else in the room processes this before she does.
🩷 Reasoning (Qiu Yinong): She uses asymmetric information logic: I know my rules say X at the top, therefore their rules must say Y at the top. She extracts an intelligence advantage from what appears to be a formatting detail.

Qiu Yinong checks the first floor for hidden Mice
White: Having deduced that the rules are asymmetric, Qiu Yinong immediately suspects that the Mice — who arrived at the building before the Cats — already read the Cat rules by hiding on the first floor during the transition. She scans the lobby: a bulletin board, a complaint box, a radiator, and a trash can. She walks directly to the trash can and reaches out to touch it — suspecting a Mouse is hiding as that object.
🟣 Perception/Cold Reading (Qiu Yinong): She doesn't scan the room randomly. She evaluates which objects are large enough to conceal a person and moves immediately to the most plausible one.
🔵 Foresight (Qiu Yinong): She anticipates that a Mouse would choose to hide as an inanimate object in plain sight rather than flee upstairs immediately — because the first floor is where the Cats assemble, and reading the Cat rules is valuable enough to risk staying close.

White: Before Qiu Yinong can touch the trash can, a Weird Speaker (a player-type that can use skills directly without lying) among the Mice triggers a skill — "Forbidden Bookstore" — plunging the entire floor into complete darkness. When the lights return, the trash can, radiator, complaint box, and bulletin board have all vanished — the Mice had been hiding as all four objects simultaneously. 🔴 Tactics (Mice collective): The darkness was not random — it was a pre-planned extraction window, timed to fire the moment a Cat got close enough to make a definitive check.

Ye Qian deduces that the Mice read the Cat rules

Therefore: the Mice deliberately stayed on the first floor to read the Cat rules, and only fled once they had the information they needed.
🩷 Reasoning (Ye Qian): She reconstructs the Mice's intent from their behavior — not just what they did, but why, which allows her to predict they will now adjust their strategy based on knowledge of the Cat rules.

After 5 rounds of memorization, the Cats will recognize any disguised Mouse instantly.
🔴 Strategy/Planning (Qiu Hua): The plan simultaneously accomplishes three things: builds up the Cats' room-knowledge database, eliminates Mice one by one from the first floor in a controlled sequence, and ensures that only the strongest Cats (those capable of No-Distance, a high-level mental combat technique) remain in the building at the end.
🟢 Intelligence/Knowledge (Qiu Hua): He calculates the precise time budget: 2 hours total minus ~10 minutes already spent = 110 minutes.
