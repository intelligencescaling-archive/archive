Chapter 5: Clearing the First Secret Room

 Types of Liars:

Lying due to emotions: When someone has their emotions controlled and lies with anger, they are considered a liar.

Lying based on common sense: Ignoring or misinterpreting basic common sense and making statements that contradict common sense is considered lying.

Lying about emotions: Of the three main human emotions (friendship, family affection, and love), anyone who lies about them, regardless of their psychological motivation, is considered a liar.

For example:

Lying by emotions: You are angry and curse the person who angered you: "I curse your ancestors!" But their ancestors are already dead. In theory, you cannot do it. In this situation, you are lying.

Lying with common sense: In common sense, we all know there is a sun in the sky, but you are the only one who doesn't know. When someone asks you if there is a sun in the sky, whether you answer "I don't know" or "No," you will be judged for lying.

Lying about emotions: Your girlfriend is generally ugly, visibly, objectively. When you comfort her by saying she is beautiful, you are lying.

Lying: I lied, but I don't believe I am lying; that is, the lie is influenced by subjective consciousness.

Judging if someone is lying: It is not I who judges whether I am lying or not, but others who do it from an objective perspective. It is the same principle we use to judge if someone is lying in life. Whether the other person lies or not is objective and is not affected by my subjectivity. I can only determine if they are lying from an objective perspective.

Judging by this closed room, it is clear that the principle of judging lies is followed, rather than the principle of lying.

In summary: whether you lie or not is not determined by your own assumptions, but by the judgment of the listener who evaluates it objectively, not subjectively.
