18LOH Chapter 63 is terrible

Right before one of my favorite arcs in 18LOH (65-75) there is a horrible mini scenario that runs for a chapter and a little more in which multiple characters act like complete fodders that don't even known how basic high school math works.

Fortunately this doesn't downscale Chen Ran since he didn't participate. He simply dominated them so hard prior to their game, he no longer had to participate and left them to their own devices.

There were 4 characters. Glasses, Unc, Auntie, and Liu Xing.

Nobody wanted to test a particular mirror first but if they wanted to continue the scenarios, someone had to test it. Thus Unc proposed a game to "randomly" determine who would become the test subject.

They must all draw lots, 2 of which contains X and 1 of which contains a tick (which I'll call Y). If you draw Y, you become the referee for the next round. If you draw X in the third round, you will have to become the test subject.

Unc proposed this game in order to utilize the Monty Hall problem in mathematics for the sake of increasing his probability of drawing the correct lot from 1/3 to 2/3. However, the author either misunderstood how the Monty Hall problem works or he intentionally made the characters stupid to the point of not understanding how it works (which isn't implied from what I've seen of followup arcs so most likely the author himself misunderstood).

The monty hall problem requires that the judge intentionally and definitively remove an X from the 2 remaining possible answers after you have already made your choice. Since the probability of the individual 2 choices you don't pick can be lumped together, this brings up the probability of success from 1/3 to 2/3. The only way to actually LOSE is to pick the correct answer for your FIRST choice which automatically leaves you no choice but to switch to the wrong answer when you do switch, because the remaining 2/3 are both losing options. Meanwhile if you pick the wrong answer first (2/3 probability) your win is guaranteed by switching after the judge removes the incorrect answer from what you switch to, leaving only the correct answer.

This only works IF the judge intentionally helps you by removing an incorrect answer from the 2 you don't pick and allows you to switch after. This does NOT work with the scenario that Unc was in because the person who goes first has a chance of hitting the correct answer on their first try which is a 1/3 probability. Moreover, switching is actually completely USELESS. The 1/3 probability for the first person is fixed, but assuming they pick X, the 2 remaining options are both equally likely to contain Y. If the choice you initially had was Y, then you'll switch to X and if the choice you initially had was X then you'll switch to Y. The initial choice you made has the same probability of being the correct answer as it was from the start.

Probability of player 1 getting Y multiplied by the probability of you getting Y after switching added onto the probability of player 1 getting X multiplied by the probability of you getting Y after switching is equal to 1/3 \* 0 + 2/3 \* 0.5 which is 1/3. In other words the Unc's trick literally doesn't even do anything. It's useless.

Absolute fodders. Literally a whole monologue where the Glasses guy tried to explain it... and coincidentally both of them got the tick when they switched which has a 1/9 probability in reality (or 0.5 if you assume the first guy definitively must get X but this is not even probable at all, much less definitive).

Not to mention the followup where Unc tried to explain what would happen if the number in his mind was picked and he tried to explain it off by saying that he thought of 2 numbers that he could switch away from which further proves how useless switching is in that scenario. Unlike in the Monty Hall problem, the revelation from the first picker in this chapter does not reveal information that differentiates the two remaining options from each other. It is completely different in the monty hall problem because the wrong answer being revealed also gives out information about the remaining door you didn't pick: it is less likely to be incorrect than the choice you're switching away from.

There is some allusion that the surface information is not what it seems. For example when Liu Xing was asked how he ended up among the people who passed as opposed to the Auntie who completely lost in the lot picking despite Liu Xing looking like a desperate gambler, ***Liu Xing smiled without saying a word.***

But if it's not a plot point... then it's a plot hole. This chapter and the followup reasoning was just bad. It made me lose some of my brain cells. It was absolutely horrible. Worse than Light Yagami's reasoning. Worse than Misa's reasoning.

Fortunately the self-killing arc (chapter 65-75) makes up for it. If you ever come across chapter 63, just keep in mind that's not how the math works. Maybe the Unc just got lucky. Maybe he was only trying to mess with their heads. Either way, this chapter and the followup reasoning is probably my least favorite.

TLDR: Author of Liar Game possibly neg diffs author of 18LOH in mathematics.
