Has anyone actually tried reading 18loh? None of the reasoning in this makes any sense.

I read around 50 chapters of this and it is a mess. The "deductions" make absolutely no sense whatsoever. This is not to say they have minor logic flaws or maybe they take some leaps in logic. No, they literally do not follow premises at all and seem completely contrived.

I'm going to go over the second "battle" here. The basic premise is that Chen Ran finds himself in a beginner's dungeon after clearing the first room. The room has a table with a photograph and a shovel on it. The ground is covered in salty beach sand. In the beginning the room has 10 people, 8 seeming veterans and 2 new players; including Chen Ran.

One of the players is instantly judged (killed) after he 'lies' that the photo symbolises a family with two parents a child. The lie is that the family actually has two children, with the reasoning being that the photo was taken in a studio and its common sense for an only child to pose in the lap of both parents and for two children to sit one on each parent's laps.

One of the veterans concludes "The photo being torn in half, by common sense, symbolizes that the couple has divorced." Really think about this for a second. Does that actually follow? Even if you consider the fact that the room requires a password and the items within it must have symbolic clues for people to figure out, would you really take such an insane risk and declare theyre divorced? especially when someone just died for something much less egregious? Being torn could mean one half of the family died or were separated.

they "reason further":
"The sand is very salty, indicating it was soaked in seawater. Based on the above, we can conclude: a divorced couple took their two children to the beach."
"The missing half of the photo shows the wife and the child who went with her. Therefore, the final conclusion is: the divorced couple brought two children to the beach, and for some reason, the wife and her child left early or disappeared, leaving only the husband and the daughter."

Think about it for a second: Does any of that follow? There's leaps in logic and then there's whatever the fuck this is. Bear in mind a person just DIED because he said there was one child and there were actually two, and this guy is speculating that sand on the ground and a torn photo means they were brought to the beach. Why couldn't it mean that the beach was the cause for divorce or separation. Or that the wife and child drowned? There are literally infinite meanings you could draw from just the clues given. Now if we're to assume that these are veteran players, why in the fuck would they take the risk to say all this out loud? Even if you're 99% sure about your reasoning there's no logical sense in saying it out in the off chance you missed something.

Luckily the great Chen Ran doesn't miss this. He clarifies that the wife and child's portion being torn can only show that they were missing but cannot be used to deduce they were taken away or left. He connects the fact that 10 men are present but 8 women are not (the game requires 18 people.)

"In a family of four, the only one certain to be male is the husband. Since all ten of us are male, don't you think we are playing the role of the husband? The eight missing players should all be female, serving as the 'wife.' The wife will definitely come, so what we need to do now is summon those eight missing players."

Is this really logically thought out? Why would there be an uneven number of husbands and wives? Would an actually rational person reason all this out loud? against veteran players who will kill him in an instant if they spot a lie? Why wouldn't you first assume there are 9 husbands and 9 'wives', or 8 husbands and wives and one each of a son and daughter?

>"How do we call the wife?" Lin Feng had approached the table at some point and asked curiously.
"The marital bond is broken, so it can't be romantic love. That leaves the parental bond with the daughter."
>Everyone suddenly turned their heads and stared in unison at the small child's shovel on the table. The beach, playing with sand, parental bonds. The answer was the daughter building a sandcastle in the shape of her mother, and then the husband using this to call the wife over. But the daughter was only about two years old and didn't understand anything. Therefore, the real answer was: the husband builds a sand sculpture in the shape of the wife, says the daughter built it, and calls the wife over.

Chen Ran just said that they couldn't say for sure the marital bond was broken. Even if that's not what he meant and I'm the victim of translation errors, does this follow? Does any of this truly logically follow as the only conclusion you could come up with? Why couldn't it be that the daughter actually just made an innocent family picture in the sand and despite an apathetic husband the wife was moved enough to go? Why couldn't the missing shovel (theres only one childrens' shovel for two children) mean that one of the kids was too sick to go to the beach and the wife had to stay behind. There are literally millions of interpretations of this scenario that are possible. In any case you would never risk 'deducing' all of this out loud in Hell where you might be killed for a single lie.

I'm going to stop here but this has been my experience for every single 'room' or game in this series. It's like people want to think its logical and smart just because the author pretends it is. Having gone over the google docs, I think those have this problem too. Huge logical leaps from premises that are way too broad to lead to conclusions as precise as characters come up with. I truly think it is impossible to scale characters like Chen Ran properly, not only because of the absurd length of these webnovels but because they do genuinely have some impressive smaller feats that are stuck in a mire of overconvoluted shitty "logical" thinking which is usually the writer working backwards to make the power fantasy mc seem as cool as possible. Absolutely no shade to anyone who enjoys this series, you should read for fun after all, but if you're going to scale Chen Ran against characters with genuine feats in well designed games like Akiyama or whatever then we need an honest discussion about the extremely low quality writing in this webnovel.
