Great War Strategy: Conclusion

Riku Dola’s Great War strategy was overall insane—especially in terms of foresight, preparations and manipulation.

It was an overall  ‘success’ and a ‘failure’ at the same time.
It was a ‘success’ in a literal sense, by respecting Schwi’s wishes to label her entire robotic race without physical ‘hearts’ as tools and not air breathing things like the rest of the races as a loophole to the rules of Riku’s game and strategy. While it’s true Riku said the heart wasn’t ‘physical’, it wasn’t because he believed it but more used as a scapegoat and tool to manipulate Schwi from the very first time they met in order to survive and can be labeled as ‘inconsistent rules’ which were mentioned as meaningless by Riku’s own ideology.
In that sense, ZERO casualties (except for Riku Dola himself) were inflicted in the Great War strategy while finally creating a “utopia” for everyone as Ex Machina were just tools used in order to fulfill it.

It was also a ‘failure’ in an emotional sense, as Riku believed Schwi human enough to even see human qualities in her as the story went on such as tears, love, affection and even marrying her. Furthermore, Riku believed that the wish of considering the Ex Machina race to be nothing but tools was the height of hypocrisy implying even himself didn’t consider it to be a win on his part.
In that regard, Riku failed and sacrificed the lives of thousands of Ex Machina in order to find peace in the Great War and in the end failed to physically take the Suniaster.

Either way, no matter how you interpret the Great War strategy, considering its initial very low success rate Riku carries and executes the entire strategy so well and efficiently for almost a whole year. Due to very subtle but major problems regarding Schwi, the whole strategy starts to fall apart drastically with nothing else for Riku to do when the climax has already been set up which led to the last ditch contingency formulated mostly by Einzig via Schwi’s will while executed by Riku to at least create a ‘stalemate’ while finally ending the endless Great War.
