Episode 1. "Beginner"
[ 6:07 ] - And here we already see the first blunder of the authors, according to the rules of the chessboard, the square a1, must be black and at the same time Sora says: “This is high-level chess,” no matter how long I stared at the unknown chess program displayed on the screen, to be fair, I still can’t figure out how Sora determined that it was “high-level chess.”
Sora asserts that "chess is a perfectly balanced game for two players, with no room for luck." I share his perspective, but I would add one caveat: the player with the white pieces always moves first, granting them a slight advantage of first to move. However, one might counter this by pointing out that in an ideal game, the outcome often results in a draw, which is also a valid point. In this particular moment, the authors did a good job.
________________

Then Shiro says this: “Chess is no different than tic-tac-toe.” A very controversial take as the narrator says that Shiro is able to calculate up to 10^120 moves - for me as unknown with the series of this novel, manga and anime as a whole, this statement is simply false. The Shannon Number (10^120) says about the possibility of 3 best moves with the game ending roughly to 40-60 moves and white are winning, for example mathematician G. H. Hardy estimated the possibility of chess games up to 10^(10^(50))  or “in any case a second order exponential” - G.H. Hardy. So what do I mean by that? Simple, when the narrator says that Shiro is able to calculate up to Shannon Number, she’s basically going depth 60 with 3 best moves variability. I don’t frankly understand when people accept that when you calculate up to 10^120 you basically solved chess, that’s ridiculous. Chess complexity (CC) goes far beyond the O(1) and O(n) the Big-O notation and chess hasn't even reached even 1% to be solved. So when it comes to complexity it’s safer to say it exceeds the O(n) notation. So for those who are unfamiliar with math: CC > O(n)
P.S. O(n) is infinitely larger than 10^120 - for those who didn’t get it.
