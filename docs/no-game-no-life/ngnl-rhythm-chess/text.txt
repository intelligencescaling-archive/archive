Rhythm Chess’s Prep/Fabrication

Rhythm Chess is a modified chess game personally designed by Sora himself. Because of the Ex Machina’s infinite adaptability and computational ability playing a regular chess and winning was pretty much impossible which is why this Rhythm Chess was built for the sole purpose of countering the Ex Machina’s adaptable abilities BUT Sora did this by making sure the Ex Machina would accept the challenge on the assumption Blank would face ‘triple layered limitations’ on top of chess being the Ex Machina’s advantage—convincing them to accept the game.

As the name describes, Rhythm Chess is a chess game associated with music as waves of light crash through the board in sync with Holou’s singing as she’s onstage while the actual chess players are offstage. Instead of regular chess, there are no turns as you had to slam the chess pieces in sync with the rhythm or the move would not only be invalid but the chess piece would be forced to be placed back on its starting position.

Holou had thirteen sets of songs given by Sora which is why the game had a total of thirteen rounds and whoever brings the game to a stalemate loses in order to sync up both Holou’s show and the game. The team who wins the most matches are the overall winners.

In addition, another special element of Rhythm Chess was the ‘Energy Gauge’ in which after the Ex Machina agreed to the game, Sora used the two Ex Machina Seher and Prufer among the group for the application use the Ex Machina’s natural ability to analyse and measure the crowd’s excitement, satisfaction and enthusiastic levels while also projecting the Energy Gauge visually to Blank, Steph and the others in the form of a green meter.

Furthermore, Sora added another special element to make his Rhythm Chess which was the ‘effect strike’ which utilised Jibril’s Materialisation Shiritori from which Blank obtained after defeating her.
This gimmick is responsible for maintaining the Energy Gauge’ to a high level throughout the show.
Instead of employing things within the limitation of whether something exists or not like in the shiritori game testing knowledge, there were no limits in Rhythm
Chess and as the name suggests it can launch ‘effects’ from how the striker imagines them in which both the main stage and back stage will experience.
In order to activate this event though, you’d need to hit squares that would flash on the board at completely random points in the game meaning there’s a high risk of potential bad moves.
