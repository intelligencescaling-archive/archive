Sora’s Volume 4 Reasoning

P1. Dhampir and Siren were in a strict relationship. Sirens are a female-only race and to reproduce they require a male of another race, but Sirens are known for killing their partners during reproduction. Dhampirs can only live by feeding off the life-essence of other races, with only the consumption of blood allowing them to grow. Dhampirs and Sirens ended up in an agreement that Dhampir males would assist Siren in reproduction, while being forbidden to suck blood of non-Sirens. For some time it worked out alright due to the 'once in a generation' siren who could reproduce without killing her partners; a siren with such capability is declared the queen.
The situation became much worse when the current Queen entered hibernation and male Dhampirs could no longer reproduce with her.
P2. Plum, one of the Dhampirs, claims that their race is at the brink of extinction and the last male Dhampir will soon die when reproducing. Plum asks for help with waking up the Queen which would solve that problem.
P3. To wake up the queen one needs to enter her dreams and make her fall in love with them.
P4. Plum says she has a spell that can consensually make the target fall in love with someone, supposedly allowing to wake up the queen with ease
P5. Despite having such an absurd trick that should easily allow to clear the condition, Plum still asked Blank for help
P6. Plum doesn't require Blank's help in clearing the condition of making the queen fall in love with them, that means Plum has something else to gain by involving Blank

C4. The only reason Plum wouldn't want to clarify this is if it would classify as a lie which would be detected by Werebeasts. This further points at the possibility of Plum and Amila intending to consume Immanity.
C5. Since the queen is sleeping, if Sora loses, everything he bet will go to Amila who is in charge of Queen's stuff. Amila wasn't going to give back what Sora and the rest had wagered, instead wanting to keep the profits to herself. If Blank bet "everything", that would include all of Immanity which would delay the impending doom of Dhampirs' race- for a while the Sirens would be free to reproduce without involving Dhampirs. This means Dhampirs would benefit through this scheme as well.
C6. Plum concealed what the Game-Prize is, but concealing it would be pointless if Blank were to obtain it anyway. The reason is that despite Plum actually intending to use the spell, it wouldn't be Blank that fulfills the condition of making the queen fall in love, but Plum herself. Consequently, the one who obtains the Game-Prize wouldn't be Blank, but Plum. Additionally, since it would be Plum who won, Blank wouldn't obtain the Participation-Prize either.

P14. Using the analogy of a horse race, it's two-sided. Amongst the ones betting on which horse will win and the actual horse-riders. Blank fulfills both of these roles, while Plum and Amila are betting on ‘which horses will win’.
P15. During their first interaction, Plum said "Please save our race" and wasn't lying, meaning she really wanted her race to be saved. However, Plum said "race" and not "races" despite both Sirens and Dhampirs being endangered, which means that Plum's goal is to save Dhampirs only.
->
C7. P15 shows that Plum actually intends to save Dhampirs, so the strategy executed by Plum should allow her to emancipate her race. This means that Plum believes the Game-Prize is something that allows her to do so and it's the preferable option over consuming all of Immanity that'd already delay the doom of Dhampirs' race. Most likely what the Queen had bet is actually her "Everything", which she'd wager before even becoming the Queen, now amassing to everything that Sirens were, after she actually became the Queen. That "Everything" would now include the Race-Piece, which Plum would be able to control the Sirens with.
In this game of "Horse-Race", the best outcome for Amila is if Sora and the rest lose, while Plum wants them to win using her magic. Regardless of whether Blank makes the queen fall in love through Plum's magic or they lose while trying, Plum will either completely free her race, or at least delay the approaching doom of Dhampirs' through consumption of Immanity.

P16. It's commonly believed by different races that the condition of waking up the Queen is making her fall in love. Even Jibril said so upon their first encounter with Plum.
P17. Amila should be aware of the Queen's actual wager which would be the main motive for her to maintain the status quo rather than awakening the Queen. That's because waking up the Queen should avert the races' crisis entirely and there would be no need to bait any other race into the game that Amila wants them to lose- when Sirens are just 1 step away from extinction. However, that wouldn't be preferred if awakening the Queen would grant the winner their Race-Piece, putting Sirens in a much worse position.
P18. Amila would have to be absolutely retarded to simply let Plum obtain Sirens' Race-Piece by clearing the condition and awakening the Queen.
P19. Amila still allowed Plum to try and make the Queen fall in love.
P20. The only reason Amila would sensibly allow Plum to do so, is if there's no chance for Plum to be able to clear the condition required to wake up the Queen.

The reason why Amila allowed Plum to execute her scheme is because Amila knew that Plum's Love Spell wouldn't allow her to clear the actual condition anyway, so there was absolutely no risk involved for the Sirens.

Volume 5
In V5 there’s an elaboration both on Sora’s reasoning and on Plum’s scheme.

(Plum is actually a guy, so I will refer to him as “he”. This was figured out by Shiro, not Sora, so I won’t explain it)
When meeting Blank, Plum only said that he formulated a plan that allows to make the queen fall in love. However, if one believes that “Making the Queen fall in love” is the condition to waking her up, couldn’t they simply say they have a plan to “Wake up the Queen”?
Sora was bugged by this, so after bringing Plum to Shrine Maiden, Sora emphasized saying:  “Okay, I get your sure win or whatever, but why don’t you do it yourselves?”
Sora kept on emphasizing that Plum’s plan is a sure-win over and over, but Plum himself had NEVER once said that it’s a sure-win. This, combined with other premises, hinted that not only was the supposed winning condition false (giving an even stronger basis for C8.), but Plum was already suspecting that it’s false from the start.

Sora likely conjured up 8 of the conclusions I listed earlier after Plum brought up the Participation-Prize: then laying out the trap to learn Plum’s true intent related to false-condition, simultaneously gaining more basis for C8.

*Based on the way Plum responded to Sora’s question, Shiro was able to infer that he’s actually a guy- the last male Dhampir.

Plum was seeking the emancipation of Dhampir despite suspecting that the winning condition was false, which means he believed Blank to be capable enough to uncover the true winning condition and fulfill it.
