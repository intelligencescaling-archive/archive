Sora’s Rule 10 Plan

This is Sora’s plan that he has which is aimed to achieve the goal he has to this world. This explains Sora’s political prowess, and his ability to weave together games to maximize his gains to his overarching goal. His design and goals for each game is more clear and buffs Sora’s ability to think far ahead.
Analysis
Sora after getting transferred to the world of disboard formulates his rule 10 plan. This plan was his overarching plan to take over the world of disboard to achieve this world's ultimate purpose. Sora in his previous life on earth was never satisfied with society. He knew in society winning constantly was frowned upon as no-one wanted to be the odd one out, and winning forever was simply not a viable way to live. Sora also saw that the society he lived in would never come to accept his sister Shiro’s talents as she had insane intellect but lacked social skills and composure to actually do something. Sora had despised life on earth as a shitty game with no real win condition or progression.

After being sent to the world of disboard by Tet, because they beat Tet in chess, Sora finds a new world that fits him best.
In this world all gains and losses are decided through games, so Sora can now win every time in games and progress off of it and not be frowned by others. This game also has a clear end goal which is to challenge the one true god tet.
In this world there are 16 races that are officially a part of this world. (Old Deus, Phantasma, Elemental, Dragonia, Gigant, Flugel, Elf, Dwarf, Fairy, Ex-machina, Demonia, Dhampir, Numania, Warbeast, Siren, Immanity). Sora and all other humans are classified as Immanity and they are the only race that does not have any abilities that make them stand out between the other races. They are also the only race devoid of all magic, meaning nothing in their power will allow them to see through magical powers.

Sora through all this adversity still decides to challenge Tet again. He knows Tet brought him to his world as Tet is a gamer like him and can’t end on a loss. This is why Sora knows Tet will be waiting for him to challenge him to a game again and Sora decides to bring the challenge to him as challenging a god through games is something Sora always wanted to do.

Sora learns that each race in this world has a race piece that the representative of the race owns and by betting it, you are wagering your entire race's rights.

Sora also learns about the 10 covenants of this world, which are :
1. All murder, war, and robbery are forbidden in this world.
2. All conflict in this world will be resolved through games.
3. In games, each player will bet something that they agree is of equal value.
4. As long as it doesn't violate pledge three, anything may be bet, and any game may be played.
5. The challenged party has the right to decide the rules of the game.
6. Any bets made in accordance with the pledges must be upheld.
7. Conflicts between groups will be conducted by designated representatives with absolute authority.
8. Being caught cheating during a game is grounds for an instant loss.
9. In the name of god, the previous rules may never be changed.
10. Let's all have fun and play together!

In this world there are giant chess pieces scattered all around the world, Sora later learns that the race pieces are also shaped like chess pieces and there are a total of 16 races, the same as the number of chess pieces on one side of the board. Sora assumes gathering chess pieces and challenging Tet to chess is the objective of this world. Sora assumes race pieces can not be taken, and cooperation between races are the way to gather race pieces through rule 10.

Aside from rules 1-9 Sora finds rule 10 to be peculiar, at first glance it does not seem like a rule meant to be kept in this world but Sora understands the deeper implication of this rule. Sora from the rule sets of this game understands that the 10 covenants were made so no more kills or conflicts happen, and everything can be achieved through rational confrontation. Rule 10 affirms this as it states to “have fun”. The overall objective of this world is to gather race pieces, so they have a full set of pieces on one side of the chess board. Tet has the other side of the chess board pieces and most people assume they are going to play chess with Tet after gathering those pieces. But it contradicted the purpose posed by these rules which were to resolve conflict with no casualties or violence and to have fun. Sora realized this rule 10 likely implied that the only way to challenge Tet was for all the races to cooperate, each using their respective race pieces to play the final game with Tet.

For this goal Sora needs all the races to cooperate with him without taking their race piece once. Now all the other races are of the assumption that gathering race pieces is the goal not cooperation so when Sora wants to play inter-racial games he is often forced to bet his race piece which he can not afford to lose.

Sora also sets one rule for himself, this world is one where it is possible to clear with no deaths caused, so Sora has a strict no killing policy, he does not want to burden the guilt or stain this peaceful world with chaos.

This no-kill law also coupled with benefits for himself as well to his plan, as if Sora just took over other nations by getting leverage over them, it would be an invasion not a cooperation. Sora through his inter-race games made sure to “cooperate with benefits of both nations protected” and “no one dying” to insure mutual trust.

Sora also can not let any other race go extinct as that will mean his entire plan is screwed.

Rules

The phrasing is very important as the subject pointed at is a key clue for uncovering the true nature of the game. Below is the translation.
1. Each of 7 participants start off with 10 dices, each equals to 1/10 of their ‘Mass of Existence Time’
2. Dice holder’s can roll the dices and are able to move forward base on what the dices come up with
3. Dice holder who rolls the dices, loss 1 dice after rolling
4. Players can choose to “accompany”, which a representative of is required and only he may roll the dice
5. When there are more than 2 players in an accompany group, you loss “k been numbers of people in an accompany group, [k(k-1)]” dices, instead
6. At start of the game, each player able to create 50 “Tasks”
7. Dice holder(s) who stopped at a square, must perform its designated Task
8. Dice holder(s) need to complete the Task within 72 hours, they cannot leave their square until complete or timed out
9. By result of Task completion, dice holders gain or loss 1 dice to each other
10. Each Task is to be described on a notice board, place on game board in different order
11. Task may cause change of environment on the square
12. A Task is considered invalid if: target particular participant, unachievable by at least 1 other participant, instruct participant to move forward/backward, or not written in Imanity’s language
13. Participant (Dice holder) who arrives at the finish point is winner, thus the game ends
14. The Old Deus, Holou will fulfill all wishes of winner to the best of her capabilities
15. If all participants loss all dices or died in the game, the game ends due to the players being in a state of “unable to continue”

Sora and Shiro plan to challenge a god, as their help will be of use. The game they play with a god involves Sugoroku, which is a popular board game.
You have a piece of your choice and you will have multiple players playing the same game on the same board. The board is a line of boxes that you slowly progress through. In the game you have a set of dice and you roll the dice and move the amount of squares the dice shows. All the players take turns rolling the dice and moving their pieces. There are challenges on each box and you often receive punishments, rewards or challenges when you step on each box. If you get to the end you win.

The game is a modified version, with the main difference being. The players are the pieces, moving on the board by themselves. You don’t take turns rolling the dice, everyone has their own and it's a free for all.

Understanding the Prisoner's dilemma
The prisoner's dilemma is a fictional situation present in game theory. Sora uses this analogy to analyze his situation so I will note down the key parts of this situation relevant to this story.
The following is the situation, two prisoners in different places both are trialed for a certain crime, and the police is midway through an interrogation when both prisoners bring up the same certain deal.
If two of the prisoners confess both get sentences for a full year
If both prisoners remain silent both get only a month
If one confesses and the other one does not, then the one who confessed gets out of jail free, the other one 3 years in prison.
A classic situation where it tests trust between people and what gives the best benefits for the individual.
To analyze this situation there is a concept of nash equilibrium. Which is a state where both parties execute their optimal choices, in the prisoner's dilemma case it is for both to confess.

Sora analyzes the situation further, saying that all players have their intentions, if the police had some part in making the deal it is likely that the one who is at the edge is the police as they do not have any other option but to wait for the choices of the prisoners, and the nash equilibrium is likely a misdirection to lure in the prisoners. The one who has the initiative is the prisoners. This is similar to the scenario with Sora and the others players in this game, Sora made everyone betray each other to put everyone on the same page, so they do not participate in the game of doubt the police or in this case Old deus set up, allowing him to maintain initiative.
