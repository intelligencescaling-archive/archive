Snowpiercer (2013): Analysing Curtis' plan to escape the tail section

***Context:***

The film Snowpiercer is set in a second Ice Age that has killed off almost all of humanity, with the survivors living on a supertrain, the titular Snowpiercer, that tracks around the planet. Curtis Everett, the unofficial leader of the lower class, forced to live in the grimey tail section in inhumane conditions, leads a revolution against the upper class, in an attempt to take control of the train.

The train was created by a mysterious man known only as Wilford, who resides in the engine room at the train's front. Curtis' mentor, a man named Gilliam, is a man the upper class hold in an askew sence of respect for.

Four years prior to the film, the lower class had attempted their first revolution, which was stomped out before they had even advanced out of the tail section.

***Curtis' Bullet Deduction***

When Minister Mason, a close subordant of Wilford's, comes to the tail end to put the lower class in their place after an upset, Curtis' mentor, Gilliam confronts her. Gilliam is an older man, with two makeshift prosthetic legs and an arm. As Gilliam approaches Mason, a guard instinctively raises their gun at him. To this, Mason tells the guard to "put down that useless gun". In the moment, this comes across as more elitism from Mason - that she is above brutish violence, and that killing an elderly and disabled man such as Gilliam is a waste of time. Curtis however suspects otherwise.

Curtis reasons that after the failed revolution four years ago, the guards used up all of their bullets, and their bearing of guns is just an elaborate bluff. In the four years, Curtis has not seen a single bullet fired, and has noted the miniscule manner of which the guards hold their guns. Although vague, the way Curtis eyes the guns suggests he is taking note of the guns' wear, or perhaps even the inconsistent weights.

Either way, Mason's comment seemingly confirms what Curtis has already theorised. From this, Curtis deduces the truth: bullets are extinct. And unbeknownst to the guards, they no longer hold the advantage of fear. Now, Curtis can put his plan into motion.

***Curtis' Escape Plan (part 1)***

The guards bring rations ("protein blocks", which eventually turn out to be processed cockroaches) in a cart to the tail end, daily. As they do, Curtis calculates the seconds between the automated doors. He uses this information to conclude there is a four second window to enacts his escape plan.

Throughout the tail end, Curtis works with the people to gather any and all barrells they have. These are fashioned together to make a giant battering ram. As they prepare the ram however, the guards make a surprise visit. The people do their best to stall, terrified, and not knowing what will happen to them if the ram is discovered. Thinking quickly, Curtis makes a move.

***The Bullet Gambit***

Rushing forward, Curtis confronts a guard who has their gun aimed at the crowd. Curtis grabs the gun and holds it up to his own head, to which the guard instinctively pulls the trigger. The click of an empty chamber floods the room, sparking hope and defiance within the tail section, as everyone else realises the guards have no bullets.

This gambit is a terrific showcase of Curtis' skills, and in my opinion, is his greatest feat. By surprising the guard with something they never expected, he forces them to act on instinct,  essentially manipulating them into pulling the trigger in a single moment before they can even think. Further, this is a brilliant act of leadership to display in front of the entire tail section. Not only does this act showcase his fearlessness, as someone brave and defiant, but it inspires those around him to be fearless - while simultaneously filling his enemies with fear.

***Curtis' Escape Plan (part 2)***

With the people of the tail section revolting, the guards are overwhelmed. Curtis grabs the food cart, dumping out the protein blocks, and rushes it in front of the ram. The ram fits into the cart, and rolls forward. This is where Curtis' four second calculation comes into play. The ram thunders forward, but rather than bursting through closed doors, the people drive it through the *open* doors, forcing them to remain open. This then allows the people of the tail section to climb through, and advance onwards, for the first time in the 17 year history of the Snowpiercer.

Throughout the beginning of the film, we see Curtis trading his protein blocks for portions of Kronole, an addictive drug aboard the train. Gilliam had earlier received information of a security sepcialist in the prison car named Namgoong Minsoo - Curtis had collected this Kronole in order to bribe Namgoong, knowing he is an addict. With every door Namgoong gets them through, the more Kronole he gets, weaponising his addiction. With the guards dispatched as a result of the tail section uprising, Curtis is able to easily free Namgoong.

***Night Vision Trap Counter***

The final aspect of Curtis' plan that I'll cover is the night vision trap counter.

In order to halt the revolution, hundreds of guards await the progressing group in an empty train car, armed with numerous melee weapons. When Curtis and his people arrive, they attempt to fight, yet are outmatched due to the guards' weaponary and armour. After tiring them out, the guards then cut all of the lighting, entrapping Curtis and the people of the tail section in utter darkness. Armed with night vision goggles, the guards start slaughtering the people.

Curtis calls back to his men, who pass the call back further and further. In response, a new weapon is passed forward to Curtis, all the way from the back - fire. As it turns out, Curtis had anticipated their opponent may take advantage of their control over the train's electricity, and makes a move that even Wilford admits to being blindsided by at the film's end. Curtis and his people use torches and flames to blind the night vision wearing guards, once again successfully turning the tables on their overwhelming enemy. From this, they manage to take Minister Mason captive, and they proceed to continue to the train's front, with a valuable hostage.
