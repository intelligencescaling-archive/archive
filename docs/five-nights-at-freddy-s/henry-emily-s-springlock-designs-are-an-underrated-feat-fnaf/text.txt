The designs of the Springlocks are a pretty underrated feat for Henry Emily

Yes, Henry created the springlocks

I’ll just address it before talking about the springlocks first: Henry was the one that made the springlock designs, which Murray was simply a contractor who was supposed to improve the springlocks due to them being deemed as too unreliable and unsafe (moisture). There was Safer versions made but they were never used due to MCM having a fire that burned it down (and there’s also the possibility that Fazbear Entertainment never used them out of spite or something). The prototypes in secret of the mimic are also just different from the one use for the rest of the franchise timespan. We’ll go over the dialogue about the springlocks from FNAF 3, which showcases the difference of Edwin’s design (lack of risk fron moisture) and what the FNAF 3 one said about their springlocks, which dates after Murray’s designs:

**“For ease of operation the animatronics, are set to turn and walk towards sound cues. This is an easy and hands-free approach, to making sure the animatronics stay where the children are, for maximum entertainment slash crowd-pleasing value. To change the animatronics to suit mode, insert and turn firmly the hand crank provided by the manufacturer. Turning the crank will recoil and compress the animatronic parts around the sides of the suit, providing room to climb inside. Please make sure the springlocks are fastened tight, to ensure the animatronic devices remain fixed.  When using an animatronic as a suit, please ensure that the animatronic parts are tightly compressed and fastened by the springlocks located around the inside of the suit. It may take a few moments, to position your head and torso between these parts in a manner where you can move and speak. Try not to nudge or press against any of the springlocks inside the suit. Do not touch the springlocks at any time. Do not breathe on the springlocks, as moisture may loosen them, and cause them to break loose.”**

As you can see from what is said, they’re different from the prototypes from Edwin’s. Anyways, moving on from this, I’ll discuss the springlocks themself:

The transformable architecture of the springlocks are incredibly innovative due to its endoskeleton being able to be spring outwards and put back together into its animatronic mode, meaning its endoskeleton came back into place, untouched on a programming level, and can still maintain functionality as a intended animatronic while it can also be worn like a costume. It’s not switching software settings, but it’s reconfiguring the physical architecture of the machine itself. Really think about it; the springlocks can go from suit to animatronic!

The weakness of the springlocks: now it may seem that the springlocks have a pretty dangerous weakness, which is its vulnerability towards moisture, but the way the FNAF 3 phone calls word it, along with other factors, such as Edwin saying to make teenagers wear them like they always do, or that the springlocks in general, before the bite of 83, was commonly worn enough for employees. Point is that the suits are likely not as dangerous as they may seem, although still risky, we can logically assume that there’s a fair distance between the actual springlocks for when they’re someone wearing it, and its wearer; meaning that the moisture which a person can produce from their breathe as example, isn’t as dangerous due to its considerable enough distance for normal breathing.

If it was so dangerous that breathing wrong can cause you to die, then I sure that only the most experience or trained individuals should be able to wear them, instead of them being such a common suit to wear for the employees which they were implied. To be clear, I’m not that the risk of moisture isn’t something to worry about, it’s just that it doesn’t seem so potent as it made out to be in the wider view of stuff.
