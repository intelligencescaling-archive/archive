Calculating a safe end

I do prefer the safe end to avoid wank. Yokoya’s scene is indeed unrealistic. However, I think establishing a safe low end is important so that the feat isn’t completely disregarded. Also there is a decent possibility that the upcoming anime might change a few things or add more context. If my calc isn’t consistent with the eventual anime adaptation, then I have no qualms about rejecting this calc entirely.

For a safe end I will use the most used unit of time to show a “moment” in fiction. And that is 1 second. Additionally, I will try 5 seconds and 10 seconds as arbitrary lower bound estimates. Anything greater than 10 seconds makes no sense simply because of the physics of it. I don’t see how 6 pages mid flip (essentially fanning the pages) can remain suspended in air for more than 10 seconds straight. So the conservative end is as follows:

Using 1 second timeframe: (250 x 2 x 6 x 60) = 180000 WPM (7.2x)
Using 5 seconds timeframe: (250 x 2 x 6 x 60)/5 = 36000 WPM (1.44x)
Using 10 seconds timeframe: (250 x 2 x 6 x 60)/10 = 18000 WPM (0.72x)

These estimates put Yokoya’s CPI at 7.2x, 1.44x, and 0.72x the CPI of peak humans respectively.

The Rebunk
This debunk isn’t directed to my calc so I don’t really need to rebunk anything. However to stop some arguments from being presented and people using this to debunk my feat, I’ll rebunk some of the arguments. I am not defending the original calc because I can’t even access the doc. Anyways, props to both the creator of the original calc and the debunk of it.
1. “It is most generally known that feats > statements”: This is the ad populum fallacy. Just because most people believe this, doesn’t mean feats actually hold more significance than direct statements from the author. If an author clearly asserts that a character can read a book in a minute, it would be illogical to disregard that statement. Also the author doesn’t need to prove to the audience that the character he created can do something. Secondly, this speed reading done by Yokoya isn’t even a statement. It is a solid on screen feat that I have calculated. So the statements and feats argument doesn’t even work here.
2. “So instead of interpreting the fact that he could speed read, he just rememorized the structure of the Liar Game Games” This directly ignores the LG employee complimenting Yokoya’s reading speed. Actual character statements > vague fanfic what-ifs. So unless you have proof that he knew about the structure from before, this is just a head canon.
3. “The Author Did Not Create a feat of Speed Reading, It was misinterpreted” No. The scene is clearly referring to speed reading techniques. The author's intention is crystal clear.
4. “ Furthermore, if we look at how the author draws and explains the panels, we know that the characters usually explain the feats before or after it is used.” I don’t even understand what the argument is here. But the debunk wants us to ignore the feat entirely because it can’t believe a show that explains most of its feats can have unexplained feats as well. Being unexplained doesn’t disqualify it from being a feat.

Yokoya’s VCI
The speed reading feat has a VCI component as well. At first glance it might not appear so since speed reading seems like a boost in CPI alone. However there is research done on speed readers. This states that at higher speeds, due to biological constraints, the number of words a reader can read per page reduces. Which means that the load on comprehension increases. Readers have less information to go off of but have to comprehend the text. This results in the obvious drop in comprehension. However in the story we know that Yokoya used the information gained from his speed reading to uncover the history of the Liar Game.

Here Yokoya explains that he is fluent in 4 foreign languages and routinely reads foreign novels which requires him to be a master in those 4 languages. He is obviously also fluent in his native language. So that makes him fluent in 5 total languages. This increases his vocabulary and verbal comprehension.

Here he explains how he actually understood the history of the Liar Game. Which means he actually comprehended the stuff he was speed reading. This combined with the earlier research tells us that he has the ability to comprehend text with minimal words.

Conclusion
Yokoya’s ability to comprehend foreign text with minimal words combined with his mastery of 5 languages puts his VCI at a very high level.
