Random elements from Liar Game's Final Arc that are Really Good

I have come to defend Record of the Four Kingdoms.

1- Nao doesn't beat Yokoya in the Game hands-down, but she Does cut into the core of his psyche. This shows what Nao's strengths are. She can never match Akiyama in strategic thinking, manipulation, or planning, but she Can win the psychology battle of ideals. This is similar to Ayanaokoji beating Ichinose in Y2 Vol. 12. He couldn't beat her in a direct confrontation in the Special-Exam, so he instead broke-down her ideals to make the battle easier. Of-course, this was different circumstances where Yokoya had already formed an alliance with the 2 other Players, and Yokoya is overall just better than Ichinose, which is why he still wins.

2- Akiyama fails for once. This is a Game where alliances with others are a necessity, and unfortunately for Akiyama, he's already freed all of his allies from the Liar Game, are is now left with the Players who are the worst of the worst. Everybody from the Pandemic Game bar 1 is gone, and even that one who's still here betrayed Akiyama in Musical Chairs. Akiyama Has backup plans up his sleeve, but even still, he does try to Win the game outright first, but his strategy just doesn't work. After all this time, the king of strategy finally forms a strategy of that simply fails him.

3- Nao VS Akiyama Is probably a disappointment on purpose. Throughout this whole Game, We've been teased with the idea of goodwilled people, who just turn out to be evil people pretending to be good, and they only ever truly become good people through proper exposure to Nao and Akiyama's ideal, and then winning. Throughout the whole Liar Game story, We are teased with ideas that maybe, just maybe, this opponent actually does have good intentions, only for the ruck to be swept up from under our feet as we see their true nature. But again, these people are fighting against our main characters, specifically because they aren't on the same team as our main characters. Well, Nao and Akiyama May seem like the unbreakable duo, and they're the only players who I participated in every single Game together, But they have also always been on the same team. Well, will they break if they're put on separate teams? No, obviously not. But that's just it. They're the only players who won't betray each other, even if they are objectively in a situation where they Should. They still work - together to find a more ideal - solution.

4- Yokoya finally accepts defeat. I honestly think this moment is underrated. Yeah, it's a little rushed, and maybe this manga could have done with a few more chapters to make things make sense, but I wouldn't be surprised if the author was dealing with some company that told him his mango was being axed at chapter 200, and he might have pushed Heaven and Earth just to get an extra chapter so that things didn't feel rushed into oblivion. As it stands, Yokoya simply Must accept defeat. There is no Future Game for him to get revenge, for him to act like he wasn't running on his full potential. No, this was it. This was his final chance to prove he was better than both Akiyama and Nao, and he simply failed. It's a common phrase that's maybe been said too often, but one of the most impressive things a person can do is admit that they failed- No more excuses, no more "You haven't seen the last of me mwahahaha!" cackle. Yokoya simply got beat by the better Players, and once he accepts this, he truly decides to follow suit. If you can't beat them, might as well join them.

5- The LGT Office Maybe chickens out a little bit too soon, and it doesn't even really make realistic sense why they're in trouble. Are they really forbidden from going to the grocery store and getting more food? I guess it must be a rule somewhere that all food must go to the Players as the price of being the Deals. I dunno. What I Do know though, and what all the Dealers here know as well, is that Akiyama and Yokoya are both formidable opponents, and if they're both working - together for once, they don't stand a chance. What's the point of dragging things out and waiting for an inevitable moment of the Players breaking into their headquarters as a united force against those who oppressed them- When they can just simply quit. Stop kidding ourselves. We can't beat Akiyama and Yokoya working together, so it's best to do what Yokoya did and just throw in the towel.

6- Finally, the ending isn't that bad. It's a message from the mange itself. We've shown you how the world and people work. The rest is up to you
