Second Revival Round | East/West Game:

Duration: Chapter 60–83 (23 chapters)

Total feats: (19)

Rules: This revival round is separated into three games played by two teams composed of three people. The three people from each team can only play one of the games. Here is each game explained:

[1] The first game is 24-shot Russian Roulette. 6 rounds can be loaded into the gun, a loud bang represents "death", and when a player dies he has to pay 50 million yen to the enemy team. Chips can be used to skip turns of shooting the gun. If a player dies, all the chips in the table goes to the enemy. If a player doesn't die after firing the gun, he gains all of the chips in the table. When a player skips a turn after another player has skipped their turn, they will have to pay a double amount of chips in return, each time one of them does it. If the players pass their turn 5 times it becomes a draw and the dealer takes all the chips from the table.

[2] 17 Card Poker is a game of poker played with only 17 cards. 4 aces, 4 kings, 4 queens, 4 jacks, and one joker. The joker can replace any card. The strongest to weakest card formations are as follows: Five-of-a-Kind, Royal Flush, Four‐of-a-Kind, Full House, Straight, Three-of-a-Kind, Two Pairs and One Pair. A dealer shuffles the cards in random order, and in each round the cards get replaced with a new cards from a different package to avoid cheating. Players can cut the cards from the top of the deck, placing it below the deck itself. The ante for the game is at least 5 chips. The maximum bet for the game is three times the ante. You can raise, call or fold a hand, with folding leading to the dealer getting the chips. Players can discard cards after placing their chips in order to make stronger hands. The first one to get their bet called on can cut their cards first. The winner of a round has to cut the entire deck of cards first right after the shuffle.

[3] Stationary Roulette is simple. There is a roulette where a ball can be placed to fall in one of the 4 sides inside the object chosen by the player. There is a betting table, with 4 sides represented as 4 squares. Each player alternates between the role of the dealer, putting the ball inside the roulette, and betting on any squares that he wants. The participant opposed to the dealer can only bet on 2 squares in comparison, and has to bet the same or a higher amount than what the dealer had proposed. The roulette and the table go from the number 1 to 4, and the other chips that didn't fall into a winning number are added together and divided between players with the ratio of how much they bet on said winning number (Player 1 bets 10 chips on winning number, player 2 bets 15 chips, meaning that there is a 2:3 ratio, and the rest of the 35 chips are divided into 14 chips for player 1 and 21 chips for player two). When no player bets on the winning number all chips go to the real dealer, the LG member overseeing this game.

1) Akiyama analyzes the russian roulette game, coming up with important insights about it. He realizes that you can place 3 rounds with an interval of 7 shots between each other, knowing that you will certainly get killed in 8 shots after dying by the first round. Another trick that he comes up with is loading all your 3 rounds in succession in order to anticipate the other two rounds after getting killed by the first round. If you get killed on the first shot, you will choose to pass the second shot, as the second shot not having a round reveals that the first shot's round was one of the enemy's rounds, and if the enemy gets hit by another round in the second shot you will know that those are your rounds, meaning that you can anticipate another one coming up next. (Tactics/Strategy Depth|Complexity/Logical Thinking/Logical Reasoning/Analysis Depth|Complexity)

That is why Akiyama asked Nao to pick numbers 18, 20, and 23, to see the deviation cause by the bullets positioning on the order that the shot would be fired after the spin. This means that when filled with all the bullets in the lower side one can at least expect that the starting 6 chambers have almost 0% chance of having a bullet in it. —Estimation of Akiyama's IQ—

We all know how intelligent Akiyama is, but how much IQ does he actually have. For starters, his official IQ has never been shown in the manga itself, not even in other media such as the live action show or the Roots of A chapters that have him as the protagonist. The truth is that, for as much as we can speculate on it, we will never get the answer on that without asking the author himself (Shinobu Kaitani) for an answer, and even then many people might not agree with it. So what methods should we try to use, and how can we calculate IQ anyways? Well, the answer is pretty simple. IQ can be measure by taking an individual's mental age, dividing it by their actual age and then multiplying it by 100. So the formula basically is written down as this:

Mental age/Actual Age × 100 = IQ

We already have his age, 27 years old during the present events of the manga itself. What we need to find is his mental age. For that we have some reference points, like comparing him to Yokoya or other characters in relation to intelligence, and how these other characters would rank in IQ based on their feats, and how these feats translate into quantifiable intelligence in real life areas of work. For example, leaders and speakers of multiple languages tend to have higher IQ's, and this would place Yokoya and Harimoto quite high when measuring their intelligence. We know that Akiyama should be smarter than those two, and at least as smart as Yokoya is. But how do we quantify that? Let's give it some examples:

Yokoya states that he can solve university entrance exams while graduating elementary school, which means that at 12 years old he would be completing tasks that a 19 year old would need to solve, placing his IQ at the 158 range, at the very least, since he could do that easily. Taking that into account, his IQ could be much higher, since he could be able to solve questions that are made for 22 year old students that are ready to graduate from university, placing his IQ at the 183 range, which does seem fair, and since he did solve the entrance exams easily, as stated by himself, it should be more accurate to suggest an IQ of this range for him.

Anyways, professionals of important areas have IQ's towards the 120 range, with the smartest of leader's and presidents reaching IQ's of the 170 range, as seen with the IQ of Harvard Graduate John Quincy Adam's, the president of the USA with the highest IQ among all others, having an estimated IQ of 175. Of course, Harimoto should be in between these ranges, since he is smarter than your avarage profesional, but he isn't the best leader possible, since his cult was made better by Kimura, so it could be expected that he has an IQ of 130-140, specially with his vast knowledge on tactics and the heart of people. But even then he isn't the intellectual type, but more of a manipulator.

So how high is Akiyama's IQ? Well, for all we know, his IQ should realistically be in the 180 IQ range, along with Yokoya, but a more accurate data might be comparing him to his university teacher, Okabe Yukiya, who is 62 years old, and seems to represent his age both chronologicaly and mentally. Akiyama is in many ways his successor, with Okabe being someone respected by everyone that loves psychology, being the greatest psychology teacher in Japan. Akiyama has learned from him, and they do think alike, even if they are completely different people. The manga makes parallel between them, as teacher and student, with it highlighting Akiyama's intelligence over Okabe. So it would be fair to assume that Akiyama's mental age would be towards that of a 62 year old, maybe a bit less.

With that we only get one conclusion:

62 years/27 years × 100 = 229 IQ

So Akiyama's IQ should be 229, or if you want to get more technical, it could be said to be in between the 183–229 IQ range. As Akiyama is shown to be smarter than gifted and genius people in intelligence, it seems fair for him to have such a high IQ, even if it is an estimation.
