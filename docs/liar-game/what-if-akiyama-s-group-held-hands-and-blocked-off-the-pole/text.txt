What if Akiyama’s group held hands and blocked off the Pole in Musical chairs?

I’m currently at Musical chairs arc in Liar Game. I love the “nations” theme built into the story. Extremely fascinating. But since the Practice Game, I wondered if this could work.

# Rough idea

At the very start after the 1 hour of strategy time, he’s got Nao, Bandana, 4-eyes and Fukunaga. He recruits Baldy soon after.

When the alarm rings, you need to touch the pole first, then sit in your chair, with your bum touching the seat, and both your hands on the chair handles. You need to touch the pole first before sitting.

What if Akiyama just got all his spectator and player allies to hold hands and surround the pole in the first or second round, whenever he got enough allies?

Remember in the practise game: running into someone who’s blocking the chair is considered violence, but simply standing in the way of the chair is not. Thus it stands to reason that blocking people off from the pole also isn’t considered violent.

Also, everyone needs to touch the pole before sitting down. So, everyone would need to go to the pole. If Akiyama’s group blocks everyone, then it’s only his group left. It doesn’t even matter if everyone else but akiyama’s group votes for a leader who eliminates one of Akiyama’s chairs. After this tactic, it’ll just be Akiyama’s group left as players, and they’ll decide internally who will be the last player sitting.

Before the sitting time starts, other groups and lone players will be going around searching for chairs and strategising. In the meantime, Akiyama’s group has already dragged their chairs over to the pole. And everyone’s ready.

The moment the alarm rings, prompting everyone to sit, Akiyama and other players touch the pole and immediately sit, holding hands with the spectators in their group. The people not from Akiyama’s group rush back to the pole and see a ring of people tightly packed, holding hands around the pole.

The people are standing or sitting such that the gaps between them are not big enough to squeeze through nor can you climb between their legs. In theory, they could bring a chair over and jump over the group without harming them, but only a limited few would be able to do this, if any at all. And maybe they could stand in such a way as to obstruct even that.

To recruit spectators for this plan, he could tell them of the plan, convince them of it, then bribe them with his group’s medals. With the exception that Akiyama’s group will win, they’ll take the medals and help out.

Moreover, he can get the bigger guys, like the ones circled in blue, to help him. This eliminates the taller guys with longer arms who can reach the pole, and flips them to his side.

# Calculations

Japanese male shoulder width measurements for clothing generally range between 42.5 cm (16.7 inches) for an XS to 47 cm (18.5 inches) for a Large. These measurements refer to the garment's shoulder width, typically measured straight across the back from shoulder seam to shoulder seam. I’ll take the average Japanese male shoulder width to be about 45.5 cm.

Observing from the photo, it seems like the pole is roughly 70 cm in diameter.

The average arm length for an adult male is approximately 76.7 cm (roughly 30 inches). To be safe, Akiyama’s group’s circle formed must be 80-90 cm away from the pole to prevent people from touching it even if they’re reaching.

That makes the group’s circle’s diameter approximately 35 cm + 85 cm, 120 cm. This makes the circumference of the circle approximately π x 120 cm  = 376.991 cmwhich we’ll approximate to 377 cm.

If they were packed shoulder to shoulder, assuming the aforementioned 45.5 cm average Japanese male shoulder width, they would require 377/45.5  = 8.286people.

However, if they held hands while remaining tightly packed, something like in the photo attached, they could do this even with 6 people.

Not to mention, they will sit on chairs while blocking the pole, and the chairs are slightly broader than their shoulders (see screenshot attached). While sitting on the chairs, players can have their arms more outstretched, requiring even fewer players to pull this off.

If Akiyama gets just one or two of the big guys on board, they’ll have no problem pulling this off physically.

To make it harder, they would max need 2 big spectators or players in addition to their 5 existing members.

# What will likely happen

Let’s say Akiyama convinces the bigger Spectators and Players to join in on this scheme. He pays them handsomely with medals from all his teammates.

They have enough people to build a circle without using Nao. Nao stays within the circle, holding leftover medals, anticipating what will happen next.

The other groups come back to the pole after discussing things amongst themselves. They see what’s going on. The other groups will be given a choice at this point:

Commit violence to attempt to touch the pole

Or lose immediately in the first round

Here’s where there can be some strategy.

As the timer is ticking down, each group, Samue’s and Yokoya’s, become more desperate. They really seem like they’re going to barrel into the players.

As Fatso gets closer to the circle, Nao hands him some of their medals. One from each of the players. Fatso backs off.

Because the circle is fortified with bulky players, even if he barrels at them, there’s no guarantee that he’ll get through. So it’s confirmed risk ($100M yen) for no guarantee of reward.

In fact, now that he’s got their medals, and knows they will internally pick a winner amongst themselves, it is in his interest to have them win.

The cost of violence is incurred PERSONALLY. And there is no guarantee your medals would be worth anything. In fact, with this circle, it seems like no other group would win, meaning no one from outside the circle could bribe any one individual to barrel through and destroy the circle, because it seems like their medals are soon going to be worthless.

At the same time, Nao is handing out medals from their own group, incentivising both spectators and players to help them.

Both Yokoya’s group and Samue’s group wants the other to act first, as it would benefit them. If Yokoya’s group charges and breaks the circle, Samue’s could sneak in and touch the pole for free. Likewise if Samue’s group charges in first. So neither will act unless absolutely necessary.

Samue, the cult leader, could possibly order his girls to suicidally charge at the circle, since they are a cultish. If all 3 girls charge at the weakest link, there is a good chance they will break through. However, Nao could bribe Samue to not do that. After a single round, Akiyama’s group will win, and Samue’s 6 Akiyama-group medals will be 100% confirmed money in hand. It is likely that he would prefer this deal rather than charging in and incurring a massive fine.

As time ticks down, Yokoya panics. Just like in Pandemic game, Yokoya might try to bribe Spectators with real-money checks. He finds the biggest muscular guy not yet in Akiyama’s group and pays him $300M in checks to barrel at Akiyama himself, enough to cover the cost of violence and then some. He takes the checks then asks for $500M.

Akiyama had purposely left out one of the bigger guys for this, anticipating that under pressure, Yokoya would fall back on the same tactic. After trolling Yokoya for all he would give, the big guy shows that he has 10 Akiyama-group medals, and goes back to Akiyama’s group to further fortify it.

10 minutes go to 0 and Akiyama’s group eliminates all other groups. Even if Samue and Yokoya vote a leader who eliminates Akiyama’s chair, they’ll all just purposely lose and let one of the group’s members win.
