2° Downsizing Game
Rules

* The first round of voting will take place 3 hours after the start of the game.
* Voting will take place every hour after that for 10 hours. After the tenth round of voting, the game will end.

3° First round of Contreband Game
Rules
* The cards can be used to place money in an ATM as well as withdraw it.
* One team must choose a "smuggler" while the other team chooses a "customs inspector". The teams will then reverse roles. When both teams have had a shot at both that will mark the end of one "game".
* The smuggler will carry any money in a briefcase, preventing the customs inspector from seeing how much they are carrying.
* A smuggler is only allowed to carry a maximum of 100 million yen to customs per turn but can withdraw as much from the ATM as they like.
* The customs room is soundproof, meaning other players can't hear what the smuggler and customs officer say to each other. The maximum time allowed in the room together is 10 minutes.
* If the customs inspector thinks the case is empty the may press a button and say "pass" into the speaker. If the smuggler is carrying money it will be allowed through to the "Third Country".
* If a customs inspector thinks there is money in the case then they will call "doubt", followed by the amount they think is in the case.
* If an inspector calls doubt but the case is empty then half of the amount they called doubt on is given to the opposing country. This is called an "indemnity".
* If an inspector calls "doubt" and it exceeds or equals the amount in the case then the inspector will take the money for their country.
* If the inspector calls doubt and it is less than the money in the case then the smuggler will get all the money in the case, plus an indemnity amount, added to their amount in the "Third Country".
* The entire length of the round is 50 "games". Any money left in a country's ATM once the round is finished will be given to the opposing country.
* The amount each player earns individually is made up of how much they manage to smuggle, plus any indemnities they received, plus any money they managed to collect from smugglers, plus the money left in the opposing team's ATM (which is divided equally), plus the 100 million yen they had in the "Third Country" to begin with.

We know that Kikuzawa monopolized the Inspector rounds due to his “clairvoyance” so it’s natural to know that the Southern Team would be attempting to exclude him from this post once his connection to Yokoya was discovered.  So before Kikuzawa was found, the player’s rounds were like this:

You might be asking yourself, why did the Southern Team choose this order ?
Well, let’s think this through: what happens once Kikuzawa is exposed?
Naturally, he should be punished. And in this game, the obvious penalty would be to make him skip a few turns according to Fukunaga’s strategy, right?
That would both punish him and help offset the damage done to the victims.
But here’s the problem.
If Kikuzawa’s turns are skipped, the total number of active turns drops from nine to eight since each team has 9 members.
In other words, the lineup now has an even number of moves.
Do you see where this is going?
With an even count, if Kikuzawa is forced to miss—say, two turns—the sequence of roles for everyone else doesn’t shift.
That means each player keeps the same job round after round.
For example, if Glasses is currently a Smuggler, he’ll stay a Smuggler every time.
Imagine the order:
Smuggler – Inspector – Smuggler – Inspector – Smuggler – Inspector – Smuggler – Inspector – Smuggler
If Kikuzawa keeps missing, and the rest continue in this same pattern—one at a time, no double turns—the roles remain locked.
And that’s a disaster in a smuggling game.
Why? Because everyone needs to be a Smuggler at some point to earn money. That’s the most effective way to win.
So if Yuga’s group simply skipped a few of Kikuzawa’s turns without changing the system, the four players stuck as Inspectors would be in a hopeless position.
The southern team would have no choice but to come up with a workaround.
Their options?
                                                                     * Don’t punish Kikuzawa at all.

                                                                     * Give one teammate a huge advantage (unlikely, given Nao’s nature and everyone else’s greed).

                                                                     * Or allow players to take two turns in a row to break the cycle.

What Yokoya did was to create an extreme version of the "prisoner dilemma".
To be more simple, Imagine two suspects (let’s call them Alice and Bob) are arrested for a crime they committed. They’re interrogated separately and can’t communicate with each other. If you both stay silent, you both get a light sentence. The best collective outcome: Both stay silent (only 1 year each). The best personal outcome is if you confess while the other stays silent (you walk free).vIf the other stays silent, you get 0 years by confessing. If the other confesses, you get 5 years by confessing (better than 10 years if you stay silent). 5) Endgame
    On the final day of the contraband game, Yokoya announced that he would personally act as both inspector and smuggler for every remaining round. His reasoning was simple: if he monopolized those roles, Akiyama would be powerless to execute any last-minute strategy, just as he had once turned Akagi and Shibayama to his side. He asks the Northern Team to gather any suspicious information whether through the window or by checking their own teammates as it’s unrealistic to believe that Akiyama will stay still during the last third of the game like this when there has already been a lot of chaotic movements in the first 2 days.
Still, Yokoya claimed he had a plan to earn another 500–600 million in the outside account.   One of the Northern members pointed out, however, that the South believed they controlled all the ATM money. Because of that, the South was deliberately trying to lose in the outside  account by a narrow margin.
At this point, the numbers stood as follows:
                                                                                                                        * North: 1,769,490K in the outside account

                                                                                                                        * South: 1,681,670K in the outside account

With no funds left in the Northern ATM, every Southern briefcase would be empty. That meant the North couldn’t call “doubt” without hurting their own winnings, and the South would have to “pass” as well. If things continued this way, the game would simply end in the current state.
Yokoya, however, devised a workaround. His plan was for the North to smuggle money after all. They still had the funds they had hidden from Akiyama. Before smuggling, Yokoya deposited 100 million into the South ATM, immediately withdrew the same amount, and carried it into the inspection room.
The purpose of this maneuver was not to transfer funds, but to confuse the Southern team. On paper, it should have been impossible for the North to smuggle anything—yet they would find money in the trunk. This contradiction would make the South doubt their calculations and suspect a flaw in their logic. As Yokoya expected, the South adjusted their records, unintentionally crediting money to the North’s account.
But misdirection was only part of Yokoya’s design. His true goal was to secure the funds for himself. By depositing the money into the South ATM before withdrawing it again, he effectively changed its ownership. Once the custom official confirmed the smuggling attempt, the funds were officially credited to Yokoya’s personal outside account.
This maneuver allowed him to pocket all of the smuggled money. While the eight other Northern members believed they were safe—convinced that the 1.8 billion they carried would eventually be divided equally among them—Yokoya steadily redirected everything into his own account under the pretense of smuggling. By the end, he alone held the full sum, raising his personal winnings from 500 million to over 1.03 billion. He managed to redirect that leadership status that he built against his own players at the right moment, which is the end making any possible counter impossible.
Yokoya achieved his main goal by a huge margin (gaining 1.03 Billion surpassing his 500 million yen goal) but failed his other goals (crush the Southern Team and maintain his promises to the Northern Team). The Northern members were left crushed. Each of them had expected a guaranteed extra 200 million, but instead they fell deeper into debt while Yokoya walked away with everything.
