"why is Akiyama so underrated?" answered

1. Liar Game can be boring. Even some of the most pro-methodology scalers will rate Liar Game low in enjoyment. How can a character become popular if people don't enjoy the medium that contains the character's feats? How can people focus on the feats if they can't even focus on reading the story in the first place? I personally didn't find it boring but I can see why other people would.

2. A higher working memory is required to understand Akiyama's feats. There's a reason why so many people commented that they found Contraband Arc complicated. It's because it **was** complicated, at least compared to most SCD feats. You cannot easily upscale a character's feats if the feats are beyond your working memory capacity; you would have to reread it to comprehend it properly, and as I mentioned in point 1, most people don't find it enjoyable enough to reread.

3. Your working memory undergoes a greater load if the things you think about are vague, similar, and not "distinct." For example numbers. Numbers are harder to distinguish than objects. If I tell you 20 random numbers to remember in order, it's going to be harder for you to keep them in mind compared to if I tell you to remember 20 objects or places (this is how memory palaces work). It's even more difficult when you have to manipulate the numbers arithmetically. Humans are not built for this, we are visual creatures. Not many authors can take on such an ambitious endeavor as Shinobu Kaitani. Akiyama's situations have heavy numerical manipulation requirements, so casual readers mostly choose to ignore the numbers and try to understand what's going on upon the surface, which results in them not understanding the deeper intellectual layers and contextual cognitive load requirements of Akiyama's feats.

4. It's harder to have Akiyama's feats judged as a win in a debate. The previous points all tie in here. Akiyama's feats are more complicated; the judge is less likely to understand what is going on because he lacks the cognitive capacity to holistically and integratively evaluate Akiyama's intellectual capability through the feats presented; judges will find it far easier to evaluate a feat that the author can come up with in a few days compared to one that is constantly refined quantitatively and qualitatively over several weeks in order to achieve perfection. This is assuming Akiyama's feats are even bought into the debate in the first place. They aren't lol. Akiyama's feats are too complicated for most Liar Game scalers themselves to even document or use, so most of the feats that you'll see used in debates are the simple ones that appear in the first few chapters, or microstrategies within the overarching schemes rather than the complete schemes themselves. This is just how memory works, people aren't gonna be able to remember the abstract quantitative details of the contraband arc compared to something like an explicit qualitative action that Akiyama took. How can you clip and present a feat requiring heavy layers of integrated reasoning and filled with working-memory heavy information in a comprehensible manner? Even with text, this is hard enough, much less something like a voice chat which makes it nigh impossible.

5. It's easier to overrate and upscale a character to the skies when the feats are dependent on narrative greatness. This makes Akiyama relatively underrated because he has virtually 0 feats that can be exaggerated through narrative implication. Even the ones that can be upscaled using narrative implication, such as Akiyama's contingency plan of knocking out Yokoya if he escaped the cordlock during musical chairs, are down-to-earth and logically follow rather than being supernatural or narrative overreach. People love ambiguity; they twist ambiguity to fit their interpretation when they should instead be fitting their interpretation to what the ambiguity is implying. Since Akiyama doesn't have the advantage of narrative ambiguity, it is harder to upscale him using drunken headcanon.

6. Lacking documentation. This is actually a problem that plagues all characters with complex and intricate feats. Value-Time ratio basically. It's easier to document feats when the whole thing can be summarized in a few paragraphs once you leave out your personal interpretations.

These are the reasons why Akiyama is underrated. I know people are thinking that I overrate Akiyama too much, but the reality is this: if Akiyama scalers overrated him and exaggerated his ability to the sky by relying on a few ambiguous narrative statements, I would switch positions and start destroying Akiyama both narratively and methodologically, and then I'd destroy the whole Liar Game verse cosmologically for good measure. But it's simply the opposite; I have almost never seen an Akiyama scaler give a drunken headcanon interpretation. They are probably the most realistic scalers in SCD. Compare this to the kind of delusion you see spreading among other fanbases when it comes to upscaling their faves.

Anyways, until someone creates an actually comprehensible and systematic document which lays out all of Akiyama's reasoning and the intricacies of his strategies in a way where people can read it methodically and use the feats for debates with the judge being able to properly comprehend it, Akiyama will continue to stagnate lower and lower. This isn't a stretch. Akiyama scalers will need to create a more qualitatively perfect document if they want him to scale higher. Maybe Cridemord's 5 hour Akiyama video will cause Akiyama's comeback. Who knows?

**Regardless of what actually ends up happening, the important thing you need to keep in mind is this:** try not to delude yourself into thinking that a lack of documentation and debate support automatically translates to a lack of intellectual capability for a character.

Some people would believe 2 + 2 = 5 if their favorite debater pushes that opinion and gets it judged as a win.
