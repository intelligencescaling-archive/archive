As Aki later explained, the nature of Musical Chair Game is a Battle of Conquest between Nations. An individual most likely won’t be able to survive for long in this game due to the mechanism that ‘’A person cannot sit on the same Chair in 2 consecutive Rounds’’, so forming an Alliance is pretty much obligatory in order to switch Chairs between the members. Round 2)
1. Strategy Time
In the Observation Room, we saw Leronira’s elaboration of the concept. Below are the main points:

- Musical Chairs uses "territory," "population," and "influence" to represent key components of a nation. These are symbolized by chairs (territory), players and Gayas (population), and medals (influence).
* Territory (Chairs): Chairs represent the land or domain of a nation. As players acquire chairs, they expand their territory, akin to a nation growing in power. However, a large surplus of chairs also makes a nation vulnerable to theft by rivals, showing the balance needed in territorial expansion.

* Population (Players): Population is crucial for national strength and self-defense. A larger population increases security, as it brings allies who diminish the number of potential rivals. This highlights the value of alliances and unity within the "nation."

* Influence (medals): Medals symbolize influence and loyalty, essential for maintaining control over both players and Gayas (since each of the Winner’s medal can be converted to 100 million Yen after the game). Properly managing these medals is crucial to prevent internal discord and maintain the stability of the nation.

Leronira also noted that expanding too rapidly or acquiring too many resources without appropriate management can lead to counter-productive results such as betrayal or even internal collapse, evident by historical instances where empires grew unsustainably. All of that is the pretext, and is precisely why this game will be decided by the way the Leader of each Alliance uses their resources (both Human/Players/Gayas and Materials/medals).

* If D sees that the number is fine to be eliminated (meaning it does not belong to D’s team), he says <OK>.
* If D sees that the number is one of his Chairs, he says <NG>. Whenever <NG> is said, the role of A and D immediately switches and this phase continues until both have reached a mutual satisfaction.
* Then the A gets to call a representative (the one who will receive the combined vote in the Selection phase), and the game follows the same principle as above.
* Only after both <The number to eliminate> and <The representative> were agreed/OK on will the game end.

This way, they can build a relationship that was just enough for the 2 to struggle jointly, eliminating the Chairs that would definitely be Aki’s. Even though the number of one’s Chair will be learned when he says <NG>, the probability for their number to be called out was basically 50-50, so there would be little unfairness or calculating/exploiting the other.
=> Tactics, Negotiation, Problem-solving, Creativity
