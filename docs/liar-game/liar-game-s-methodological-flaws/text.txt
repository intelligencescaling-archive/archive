Liar Game's methodological flaws

I personally scale Liar Game very very high in strict methodology, but this time I'll evaluate it from a more critical viewpoint, by pointing out some of its main methodological flaws, or aspects where other series trump it in.

Biased scaling might be fun to do, but if your biases reach a point where you become completely blind to every single criticism and start subconsciously forcing yourself to see perfection where it doesn't exist, you deceive the person you should be least deceptive against (yourself).

Jokes are one thing. Fun agenda you internally acknowledge as biased is one thing. But mindless subconscious self deception on such a deep level that you cannot even think from fear of chancing upon the harsh truth is a completely different thing altogether.

If you glaze every aspect of a work in every comparison, then your comparison of no aspect at all can be considered valid.

*A friend to all is a friend to none.*

Anyways, here are some of the main methodological flaws in Liar Game.

1: Other than Akiyama's early psychological feat, where he ontologically disintegrated Fujisawa, for the remaining games, the opponents and allies could be very simply modelled, with basic rational actions that are not necessarily *forcefully* stupid, but is definitely lacking in psychological depth or even realistic unpredictability. For example, in musical chairs, physically dominant players did not stalk weaker players and steal the chairs from them. Reasoning? The terrain was too complicated so people could escape getting tracked. That's all. Does this hold up under actual physics? Obviously not. It isn't that difficult to stalk someone who has hidden a chair, then steal the chair the moment they both chance upon it, or more likely, to take it from them when they untangle it from its hidden spot, or even wait until the player has to inevitably leave behind the chair in order to participate in the voting session or even upcoming rounds. Why would the eliminated player instead participate in the voting, just to get 1 or 2 medals each time, or become so fully dependent on the informal leader of the Gaia (Young Jump)? This is an unrealistic level of lacking independent thought. They had to be game-theoretically modelled as simple agents by the author, in order for the overarching strategy to succeed.

2: Liar Game's non-strategic methodology feats are lacking. For example, philosophical or psychological depth. The manga was indeed made to focus on strategic methodology, so it's inevitable that the non-strategic feats are usually very surface-level, but this is a flaw nonetheless. Being grounded can increase the rigour of the feats, but at the same time, it means the series holds back from exploring creative peaks of human rationality. This is probably the most major flaw.

3: Almost all the scenarios are isolated. Every new feat is given new rules by the author that is mostly methodologically independent from the previous game. There sometimes is a connection to be found such as Yokoya's bribed members continuing on to the next game with him, or the overarching non-performing loan plan dictating the way Akiyama deals with the games, but the general detachment of every new scenario from previous or even later ones gives a lot of leverage to the author to be able to shape the rules in a way where the intended result is achieved. The internal rigidity is still high due to the fixed nature of the games, which prevents the author doing asspulls by introducing random non-fixed variables that the reader could never have predicted simply because the author can conjure anything from his imagination for the plot, but the early freedom of not being bound by the constant introductions of narrative elements still makes it easier for the author to formulate the methodologies.

Some of these flaws might seem pedantic, and they do not truly detract from the methodological superiority that the creative Liar Game scenarios possess over many many many other series, by maintaining such an extreme level of rigour alongside the complexity of the feats' methods.

However, my point is that if a truly top-tier work in methodology scaling were to ever be produced, it would be far greater than this. It would require a truly insane mind to produce. It would need to blend in extremities from both fixed and non-fixed scenarios.
