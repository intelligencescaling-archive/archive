About Akiyama's Scaling

Normally, I'm not a massive Akiyama fan, nor am I someone who loves Liar Game all that much—for me, it's just a good series, nothing extraordinary or legendary. Today, I want to share my thoughts on Akiyama's scaling in SCD. Honestly, while Akiyama is still a character who gets heavily overrated in SCD at times, he has also become a character who gets underrated. In my opinion, the biggest issue regarding Akiyama happened this year; in 2026, Akiyama was being compared to Baku on one hand, and to Sakayanagi on the other. Personally, I disagree with both.

Those who overrate Akiyama usually claim that he is great in methodology and that their own scaling leans toward—or prioritizes—methodology, so they scale Akiyama really high. First of all, let me say this: even though Akiyama is good in methodology, sorry, but that doesn't put him anywhere near Baku. As for the crowd that underrates Akiyama, they downplay him by claiming he has insufficient narrative feats and lacks cognitive narrative feats compared to characters from series like COTE or Death Note—which, sorry, still doesn't drop Akiyama that low. Let me explain my thoughts on both sides.

1. For those who overrate him by saying "Akiyama is great in methodology, definitely High Tier in SCD"

I'm not here to say Akiyama's methodology is bad—on the contrary, Akiyama is genuinely very good in methodology. However, there is a trap people fall into, which is the series' storytelling technique. This is something Ayanokoji and Johan fans used to fall for a lot (and still do). The thing is, to increase the impact of the story—let's take Ayanokoji as an example—Ayanokoji doesn't tell the reader his own thoughts in order to impress them, and even if he does, he lies and hides his true goal. So when certain feats are revealed, it leaves a huge impression on the reader, making them think Ayanokoji is insanely smart. While this storytelling helps certain feats get the recognition they deserve, it usually leads to them being overrated. Back in the day, this was way worse—I'll never forget seeing videos in 2022–2023 claiming Johan could take on both L and Light at the same time (they're probably still up; if I find the link, I'll drop it).

This exact narrative technique is also present in Liar Game. Unlike other series (again, using COTE and Monster as examples), Liar Game has Yokoya, who is close to the level of the protagonist—or semi-protagonist—Akiyama. That's actually a good thing; for instance, in Death Note, L and Light, or Light and Near, don't have a massive gap between them either. Unlike Death Note, however, Liar Game uses the storytelling technique I just mentioned very frequently, and on top of that, introduces a character on Akiyama's level like Yokoya. Because of this, readers blow the moments where Akiyama's strategies or plans succeed way out of proportion. In my opinion, this causes Akiyama to be somewhat overrated.

2. For those who say "Akiyama doesn't have narrative feats, so he's not that good"

In this situation—where I think L's influence plays a huge role—people have started overrating characters with good narrative feats unnecessarily. First, you've probably noticed that L has been one of the fastest-rising characters in SCD over the past year, maybe even the single most upscaled character. L, who usually wasn't scaled much above characters like Lelouch or Johan in the past, is now almost surpassing Patrick Jane, and according to some, has even reached a level where he competes with Baku and Souichi (which is obviously an exaggeration). Still, there are logical reasons behind L's rise. People recognized the terrible position L was in against Light much better and analyzed his genuinely great narrative feats in greater detail. Thanks to that, L rose significantly.

The problem, however, is that some people started misusing these narrative feats. First of all, almost no one in SCD has a narrative feat like L's stock market feat. That feat is so absurd that it impacts a massive number of categories: FSIQ, Intelligence, Thinking, Reasoning, Foresight (and probably Planning). There is practically no other single feat that affects this many categories (I'm talking about realistic and semi-realistic characters). For example, all of Ayanokoji's feats in the White Room probably don't affect many categories outside of FSIQ, Intelligence, AC, and Adaptability. Or Light being the top student in Japan probably doesn't affect many categories beyond FSIQ and Intelligence. I think people are seriously mistaken on this point. Narrative feats usually don't even impact that many categories, yet some people treat them as if they do, which in turn leads to Akiyama being underrated to a certain extent.

3. Everyone having their own arbitrary scaling (The biggest reason)

If SCD is dead, it probably died because of this. Back in the day (around 2023–2024), 80% of channels used the exact same categories when making videos, or there were only tiny differences, and the criteria for scaling were similar. Nowadays, SCD has reached a point where people hold ridiculous opinions, which has caused many people—including myself, especially older SCD members—to drift away from the community. Some say Takuya > Akiyama because in a place where the human limit is Level 5, Takuya placed first in Level 4 and supposedly had much higher potential. Others started claiming Ayanokoji can low-diff L. Everyone started scaling their favorite characters based on their own custom scaling systems; just to upscale their favorites, they ignored other characters' feats or didn't give them the credit they deserved. This created massive inconsistency in SCD. Now, according to some, even characters like Mello can beat characters like Akiyama. This is what made Akiyama probably the most inconsistent character in SCD this year.

Personally, I think people really do overrate Akiyama sometimes. He isn't way better than Light, Ayanokoji, or Lelouch, nor is he on Baku's level. But he is not a Takuya victim or a Mello victim either.
