Aizen Kyoka Suigetsu visualisation feats

*“People are all imitations of monkeys…*
*Gods are all imitations of people”*
\- Bleach vol 48, God Is Dead

Man, Aizen has so many good poems

Aizen’s use of Kyoka Suigetsu is one of his most underrated intelligence feats. Some of what I’m going to say involves interpretations, but with how Aizen’s intelligence is portrayed throughout Bleach and his other feats, there’s a logical foundation for them.

To explain how KS works, Aizen himself states that its complete hypnosis can make a fly appear as a dragon or a swamp as a flower garden. It allows him to manipulate all five senses, meaning everything his targets see, hear, smell, taste and feel can be altered. There’s also spiritual perception, which shinigami rely on heavily during combat. Once someone witnesses the release of KS, they become susceptible to its hypnosis.

CFYOW also gives us an idea of how much accuracy matters when using KS. When Tokinada tried to imitate Hisagi, he realized that he didn’t know him well enough to perfectly reproduce his voice and fighting style. He needed to observe Hisagi more closely to replicate his voice, appearance and even the trajectories of his attacks. The novel explicitly states that even the slightest inconsistency would be noticed by Kyoraku, who was familiar with Hisagi.
Now consider what Aizen has to visualize to make these illusions convincing. Everything that’s fake has to be recreated in his mind with extreme precision, down to the smallest details. If he’s making someone appear as another person, he has to perfectly replicate their entire appearance, anatomy, bodily movements, facial expressions, voice, behavior, fighting style etc. The same applies to the appearance and sensory effects of their abilities. And since KS manipulates perception, he has to account for the other senses too. What someone sees has to match what they hear, feel and otherwise perceive.

All of this has to happen simultaneously. He’s keeping track of different movements, voices, surroundings, sensory information and the actions of everyone involved. The illusions also need to remain consistent with reality. If someone moves, attacks, touches an object or interacts with another person, their perception has to align with what’s physically happening. Even a small discrepancy could expose the deception, as CFYOW establishes.

Aizen would have to continuously maintain and adjust these details in his head. If he stopped mentally maintaining an illusion, it would probably disappear or become inconsistent. And he’s doing all of this while thinking, fighting and carrying out his plans.

Then there’s the fake corpse that fooled Unohana, someone who’s over a thousand years old and has extensive medical knowledge. She personally examined Aizen’s supposed corpse and initially believed he was dead. She even investigated the possibility of it being a fake body. The anatomy, injuries, textures and other sensory details had to be convincing enough to withstand an examination by one of the best healers of the verse. Unohana eventually noticed something suspicious, yet she still couldn't figure out how Aizen was fooling her until he revealed it. Reproducing everything she was examining with that level of accuracy would require extensive anatomical knowledge.

Aizen also used KS to have a substitute appear as himself around Shinji for an entire month. He had the substitute learn his mannerisms, speech and behavior, while KS concealed the substitute's actual identity. What Shinji perceived still had to align with what the substitute was physically doing. Every interaction and conversation needed to remain believable. Aizen even exploited Shinji’s distrust, knowing that Shinji kept him close enough to observe while remaining too distant to notice small differences in his behavior

This went on for an entire month while Aizen was elsewhere conducting his Hollowfication experiments and carrying out his other plans.

Constantly maintaining such a detailed illusion while working on completely separate activities would require a huge amount of cognitive effort.

During the Soul Society arc, the people we know had witnessed KS’s release were mainly captains and lieutenants, with some fodders potentially being affected too.

Aizen couldn't simply rely on hypnosis to deceive every random fodder in Soul Society, which is a common misconception among people who consider him a KS merchant. He had to account for those who weren’t affected, since they could potentially notice something different from what the hypnotized captains and lieutenants were experiencing. Their interactions could expose inconsistencies, so he had to consider who was under hypnosis, who wasn’t, what they could observe and how information could spread. And that’s only one part of it. Aizen had to account for a huge number of variables throughout the entire arc, and he still managed to succeed with his plan. I won’t go into all of them here since there’s way too much to cover.

His Central 46 plan is another example. Aizen murdered the council members and concealed their deaths while using their authority to manipulate events throughout Soul Society. He exploited the restricted access to their chamber and had to ensure that the orders being issued remained believable. All of this had to fit into his larger plan surrounding Rukia's execution.

During the FKT battle, Aizen manipulated multiple captains and Visored simultaneously, eventually making Tosh stab Momo while believing he was attacking Aizen. This was happening in a battle with extremely fast movements, different abilities and constantly changing positions. Aizen was also fighting and reacting to his opponents himself while manipulating what they were experiencing.

And finally, against Yhwach, Aizen used KS to make him believe he was fighting Aizen, Renji and Ichigo, while Aizen was all three of them all along. Yhwach couldn't distinguish between them even with The Almighty (probably also an infinite processing speed feat for Aizen, though that’s debatable), while the real Ichigo remained hidden from his perception long enough to attack him from behind.
