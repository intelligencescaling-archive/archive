The beginning

Suzuki is a man rejected by society, forced to be a homeless beggar who lives life unseen by the eyes of society, by the time the movie takes place he had almost lost all his hope in humanity as a whole and was tired of their hypocrisy, until he met Ishikawa Asuka. Asuka treated Suzuki with respect and saw him as a true companion, giving him a Dragon's hat (japanese baseball team) as proof of their friendship. Ishikawa had met Suzuki after becoming homeless due to the suicide of her husband, Yukou Hasebe, a member of the police force who had been involved in a scandal that ruined his career.

After she made a recovery from the homeless lifestyle she went on to live with her son, Tatsuma, alongside his two friends Yamawaki and Kaji. Tatsuma had hated society for what they had done to his father, so alongside his friends they planned to bomb many locations in Japan to send a message, taking advantage of their jobs to hide the bombs in specific places. The two friends are later poisoned by Tatsuma after some disagreements and Asuka comes home to find the aftermath, discovering her son's terrorist plan.

She goes on to kill her own son because of what he has done, later she brings Suzuki to her home. From this point on there are two different interpretations of her request for help directed to Suzuki, one from Suzuki's perspective and one from detective Ruike's perspective.

Suzuki sees her request as if she's asking him to take the blame for the terrorism, in order to hide the fact that she killed her own son and that her family image would be tainted by Tatsuma being revealed as the bomber. Tagosaku sees this as a betrayal, but accepts either way to go against society and expose its flaws, transforming their plan into his and erasing all evidence that could tie the three bombers to the actual plan.

In Ruike's vision, Asuka was asking Suzuki for moral support, to make her confess to the police what she had done and what the three terrorists had planned. Ruike points out how the blame from the bombs could be placed on the two poisoned terrorists instead of Tatsuma, which makes Suzuki think deeply about the meaning of Asuka's cry for help. Either one of these versions is valid, but what remains true is that Suzuki's version compels him into enacting his revenge against society itself.

Riddle 1
“You climb the slope and look down at the town, seeing people watch a baseball game. A Tigers game is about to start. Speaking of pro baseball teams, don't you think there is a mix of strong and weak creatures? But Dragons seem strong. Well, compared to Swallows or Carp. But wouldn't imaginary ones be better? Like Phoenix or Pegasus, even a Minotaur. Wouldn't that be cooler? Speaking of which, Japan also has a monster with the body of a cow and the face of a human. It is said that humans have eaten everything there is to eat, but I wonder what monsters and yokai taste like. Which parts do you think would be delicious? Skirt steak is good too, but personally I would love to try the tongue at a Yakiniku restaurant. In the town you are looking down upon there is a Yakiniku restaurant. Thick smoke is rising from it, but upon closer look, it isn't a Yakiniku restaurant. Strange, it should be arriving soon. Then, inspiration strikes you. Oh I see, why are God's words only for mother and child? So that is why smoke is rising from there. Now then, where is the smoke rising from? Heaven is always fickle, you see.”

9) He raises his first finger. When asking about the hours in the investigation room Suzuki mentioned “The third quarter of the hour of the ox, from 2:00 to 2:30 AM”, which ties into the concept of the twelve earthly branches. “A Tigers game is about to start” indicates the start of the explosion. The hour of the tiger is 3 to 5 AM, but with the “Third quarter of the hour of the ox” the middle between 3 and 5 AM is picked, so that the detonation time is revealed as 4 AM.
(Creativity/QRI/VCI/TRI/Thinking/Analysis/Deception)

10) He raises the second finger and third finger. The second one about “the body of a cow and face of a human” is a reference to a yokai named Kudan, which Tagosaku tries to represent with his sixth sense. The third one is either about “Yakiniku restaurant” or “eating a tongue”. The tongue in Japanese is “Shita”. If you connect the yokais name with “shita” it becomes “Kudanshita”, the location where the bomb is going to blow up.
(Creativity/TRI/Thinking/Analysis/Deception)

11) The clue about “God's words” actually means “paper”, as paper and God is said as “Kami” in japanese. The “Heaven is always fickle, you see” is a palindrome in Japanese (when the word “Ten” is taken as diacritical marks), being able to maintain its meaning when the letters are reversed. Connect the two clues, with paper and something that is a palindrome, leading to a newspaper company in Tokyo called “Shinbunshi”.
(Creativity/VCI/TRI/Thinking/Analysis/Deception)

Riddle 2
“”Potential” is a lovely word. But it is also a cruel word isn't it? Because it is something that only decreases. So when times like that come you just drink alcohol and drift off into sleep. But lately there has been something bothering me. During the day, from outside the house, I started hearing energetic singing. A kindergarten nursery school must have been built. Even though I just wanted to sleep, I kept hearing the singing. So sometimes I think: “Hey kids, could you please keep quiet?”. It is true that I don't have a shred of potential left but I still have a healthy sexual appetite. Though considering my age lately my drive has been noticeably decreasing. Even so, occasionally… a gentle night and a savage night… two different nights come around. The night repeats, always on Thursdays. Reason and instinct seem different but they form a single entity don't they? However you can't choose both at the same time. “He who chases two hares catches neither”. There's also the proverb “the mountain has labored and brought forth a mouse”. But seriously, why do mice swarm like that? Squeak, squeak, squeak. Horses are so quiet. They just stand quietly at the feeding trough, lined up neatly at the gate. The important thing, Mr. Kiyomiya is whether you can make that choice.”

Suzuki's writing

Ever since Suzuki was born society has hated him, to the point that he became homeless. He had lost hope on humanity, seeing human beings as hypocritical and selfish. He sees in every human being two different types of personality that go against each other, fighting for their own desire. These themes can be seen in Yukou Hasebe and Ruike. Ruike feels detached from most people and shares similar feelings with Suzuki, however he sees solving a crime harder than committing one, which motivates him into doing good. He also likes human beings and sees their value. Yukou was a respectable officer with a normal attitude, but he would go on to disrespect the crime scenes with his actions. In the novels, it is shown that his emotions are mixed, as he feels bad for doing certain deeds at the crime scenes, recognizing the deplorable acts of his alternate nature. Just like a bomb, these alternate sides of a human being can be set off, leading to suicide or something worse in the case of Suzuki.

Ruike can control his wild side however, resisting Tagosaku's psychological control, which gets him his admiration. The proposal about Asuka's alternate intention shakes Suzuki, who declares a draw against Ruike, who was able to shake his belief about the evil inside human beings. When he met Asuka, the last bit of hope he had reignited, but that all changed when she asked him to cover up the terrorist plot. Suzuki saw it as a betrayal done by a close friend, whose selfish actions would go as far as putting a homeless man behind bars to save her own skin. Suzuki accepts and goes against the entirety of Japanese society.

He exposes hypocrisy, a false sense of morality, the violence and dormant evil in everyday people, how people act in desperation and in their true instincts. The two sides of a person can be seen as instinctual and logical, as his riddle says. The second riddle itself describes a lot about his character. One side of Suzuki is an inoffensive homeless man, the other tries to be a human representation of the yokai Kudan, waging chaos and predicting misfortune to all. Tagosaku hates everyone, old people, feminists, lawyers, politicians, people with and without families, etc. His bombs hit without distinction.

Both in the original work and the movie it is shown that he gets sexual satisfaction for some of his actions, such as the scene with the female officer Koda when she tries to kill him after her partner got hit by an explosion. He's also shrouded in mystery, nobody knows who he is. All of his actions as explained in these works are explanations of the detectives, which should be true, but still, not actual facts. This makes the work more interesting, as the only facts are the initial accidents and the bombs, as noted by a random Japanese user on YouTube.

Overall, Suzuki's message to Society is that the real bomb is inside of us all, just waiting to go off. And if it is about to go off anyway, you might as well press the button and get an advantage with it, even if that bomb falls on your head. That…
