2. The Suitcase That Destroyed the Ransom Money
“We dismantled the suitcase and discovered a false middle layer inside the lid.
This layer contained mesh compartments, each holding sodium metal (Na) — an extremely reactive substance.
To prevent early reaction, the sodium was coated with pure lime (calcium carbonate) to block air and moisture.
Why lime?
      * Lime doesn’t react with sodium.

      * Lime absorbs water very efficiently, shielding sodium from reacting prematurely.

      * It also prevented air from entering and triggering an early explosion.

Once the suitcase was thrown into the lake:
         1. Water seeped in.

         2. The lime absorbed water → exposed sodium.

         3. Sodium reacted with water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen gas.

         4. Hydrogen pushed the lime aside, fully exposing sodium, accelerating the reaction.

         5. Massive heat was released, and the suitcase started bubbling violently, which the police noticed.

         6. The generated strong alkali (NaOH) began dissolving the aluminum foil wrapping the money.

         7. Exposed money was instantly corroded and partially charred from the heat.

This is why, when they retrieved the suitcase, all the money had disintegrated into mush.”
