Feat: Tasuku (from Kamisama no Iutoori)

Introduction and overview:
Tasuku and the protagonist Akashi (who don’t know each other) appear on a large floating disc in the sky, along with six other people.
They are currently forced to participate in a game with no clear rules, except that they must move forward to survive.
A doll acts as the guide and starts Round 1. The players must step across various plates to proceed.
At the moment, aside from the letters K and P appearing on the plates, there’s no other information.

Tasuku is extremely strong — just a heads-up so no one gets confused when he lifts someone bare-handed.

Alright, now onto the feats:

To test the tiles, Tasuku takes advantage of a moment when a nearby streamer is distracted and grabs his sleeve to drop him and see what happens. (But Akashi recklessly jumps forward first.)
Akashi risks his life to save the other guy → after stepping on a K tile, he realizes he can’t place his other foot down.
Then, two other players jump at the same time — one falls, while the other manages to balance on one foot and is fine.

From this, Tasuku deduces the basic rule of Round 1:

K tiles allow landing on one foot.

Two P tiles placed next to each other allow standing with both feet.

After that, without wasting time, Tasuku rushes forward and clears Round 1, then stands back to observe and gather more rule information:

You cannot switch your supporting foot.

Plates disappear after being stepped on if you jump to another tile.

(Observation, Deduction, AQ)

Round 2:

Situation:
The plate everyone stood on after finishing Round 1 now displays numbers. The doll says:
"Please go within 24 hours."

At first, since the only visible reference point is a scarecrow, Tasuku estimates the distance to be around 250 meters and starts moving early to avoid the plates disappearing.

New challenge:
This time, in front of them is a series of letters, no longer just K or P like before.
Then, a flock of birds gathers around the scarecrow at the checkpoint, and the scarecrow shouts: "Don’t touch the harvested potatoes."

Everyone hesitates for a moment but eventually rushes forward.
Only Tasuku and the main character stay behind to analyze the situation.
The people who went ahead were immediately attacked and killed by the birds.

Through the main character’s observations, we see that Tasuku quickly deduces a homophone-based trick in English and further infers that the core mechanic of the game is based on a clock face.

He then calculates the timing and watches the clock, confirming that one full rotation of the hand takes 3 minutes, and that you can only step on a number when the clock shows the corresponding hour. (Deduction, Fast Thinking)

At the start of the plan, Tasuku said, “For the next three minutes, no one move if you want to live,” and he began by spelling the word "ONE", but in reality, he was looking for the numbers ELEVEN and TWELVE (Deception).
By starting with "ONE," he triggered a sense of urgency in others, prompting many to immediately search for "TWO," which allowed him to confirm that players could only step on plates at the exact corresponding time (Deduction, Cold Reading).
At the same time, this tactic reduced the number of players early on, as people rushed to claim the early numbers (Manipulation).

Based on the starting position, there were six one-way paths, and even if a finishing plate appeared, it would be located far away.
Because of that, Tasuku chose not to make the finish plate appear from the beginning.
He explained that there are two reasons why no one stepped on ELEVEN and TWELVE:

No one dared to risk walking down a one-way path without knowing whether they could come back. If they failed to find ELEVEN or TWELVE, it would mean certain death.

The plates appear closer to the center (something Tasuku inferred by testing with ONE and TWO).
Since players had to both spell out numbers and reach the goal, no one bothered to explore the outer edges.
In other words, an obscure area located far away would have a very low chance of being stepped on. (EQ, Mind Reading, Deduction, Logical Reasoning).

Then, taking advantage of Akashi’s plan, which went like this:

Form a group of six people, divide up the task of spelling out numbers, and decide who steps on the plate through rock-paper-scissors. The plan wasn’t foolproof, since the sixth person would be left behind, but it was enough for Tasuku to exploit.

Tasuku went along with the plan and led the group by using reasons like the need to be ruthless. He used his observation skills to track and control positions and asserted himself with confidence to control the situation. Then, he devised a fan-shaped movement pattern for the team, allowing him to eliminate plates.
To make everyone comply, he said it was to better observe both sides, to avoid missing any numbers, and to prevent betrayal.

(Leadership, Deception, Manipulation, Influence Building, VSI, IQ EQ, AQ, Logical Reasoning)

However, the plan wasn’t guaranteed to succeed, so if Tasuku won at rock-paper-scissors, he would simply secure victory immediately.

Tasuku’s chance of survival was ensured when Akashi saved his friend, causing an interruption in the process and forcing the team to search for the number 10. Tasuku seized this opportunity to run into the center and erase the plates there.
Then, using Akashi’s plan once more, he circled around and followed Akashi, erasing all the plates behind them to block off others from entering the area with ELEVEN and TWELVE.

He lied, saying the other two should wait there while Akashi scouted ahead, but in truth, he followed from behind and sealed off the path to ELEVEN and TWELVE, ensuring only he and Akashi would have access to the plates. (Deception, Manipulation, VSI, IQ, EQ, and AQ)
