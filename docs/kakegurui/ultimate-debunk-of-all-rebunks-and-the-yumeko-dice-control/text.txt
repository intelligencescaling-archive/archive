I, of course, have it explained in math too why error corrections with not enough input cause more errors:
- E {old} = Number/size of errors before correction
- E {new} = Number/size of errors after correction
- C = Effectiveness of correction (proportion of errors actually corrected; 0 < C < 1)
- F = Number/size of new errors caused by the correction

Then:
E {new} = (1 - C) * E {old} + F

- (1 - C) * E {old} are the errors remaining after correction.
- F are the errors additionally introduced by the correction.

Suppose you have E {old} = 10 errors. The correction corrects C = 70% of the errors (C = 0.7), but causes F = 3 new errors:

E {new} = (1 - 0.7) * 10 + 3 = 0.3 * 10 + 3 = 3 + 3 = 6

So, after the correction, there are 6 errors. If F is large or C is small, the error correction may cause more errors overall. If F is small and C is high, the errors are actually reduced.
