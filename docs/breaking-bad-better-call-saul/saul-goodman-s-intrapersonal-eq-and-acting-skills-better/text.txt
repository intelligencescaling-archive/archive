Saul Goodman’s intrapersonal EQ/Acting Skills (ANALYSIS)

Hey!!! Anyhow, I wanted to release another small analysis on an aspect of Saul/Jimmy that I actually find to be ludicrously impressive - his intrapersonal EQ and acting skills, which both fall under the general umbrella of metacognitive understanding. This is honestly one of Saul’s best areas and I hope this analysis can show why.

**[ ANALYSIS ]**

In Better Call Saul S6 EP10, we see Jimmy enact a complex scheme (which warrants its own analysis, to be honest). This scheme required months of preparation and was focused around one objective - stealing valuable goods from a mall Jimmy now works at, as a manager at Cinnabon.

The scheme itself was highly calculated, however an issue did arise. This issue wasn’t an actual flaw in Jimmy’s plan, but was rather the result of a misplay by another individual involved (so he got Mikami’d, if you will). The individual in question (Jeff) slipped and got knocked out, leaving him vulnerable to view on the security cameras. Jimmy (who purposefully integrated himself as a friend to the man) needed to distract the mall security guard just long enough to re-stabilize the scheme. To do this, Jimmy feigned an emotional breakdown.

This breakdown consisted of Jimmy (“Gene” to the security guard) venting his frustrations of feeling alone and his overall insignificance. These two aspects are common in observed cases of clinical depression, henceforth Jimmy’s “breakdown” feels authentic. However, one thing I don’t see people pointing out enough is the fact Jimmy’s acting skills here were generally impossible to see through (of course outside of the feat’s initial start and end, where the emotional climax has been lost - which makes sense regardless). This is because the emotions he was displaying here were genuine at a deeper level and aren’t actually entirely falsified. Jimmy integrates several elements into his breakdown which play on his subconscious psychology —>

1. “My brother is dead…” — Jimmy mentions the fact that his brother is dead. Initially, this seems like a detail he included in this “Gene” character’s life to add some plausibility to his mental state, however we know this quote actually ties into Jimmy’s personal history incredibly deeply. Jimmy’s actions, years ago, indirectly resulted in the death of his brother - Chuck. Jimmy and Chuck’s relationship was extraordinarily complex, so much so that Chuck’s mental disorder is hypothesized to directly tether to Jimmy. Jimmy spent decades suppressing his guilt over this loss, to the point he read Chuck’s final letter to him and didn’t feel a thing (still capable of eating as if nothing was wrong during the reading, which contradicts the fact most individuals lose their appetite when feeling intense depressive emotions). This suppressed feeling is one Jimmy had subconsciously removed from his psychological equation, seemingly “turning off” his guilt altogether. Here, Jimmy purposefully reactivated this subconscious strain of internal conflict - anticipating the level of psychological impact it’ll have on his externally-perceived state, abusing very real subconscious trauma to achieve this.

2. “I have no wife” — Again, this seems like a fabricated detail for Gene’s characterization, however it once again goes much deeper. Jimmy actually did have a wife at one point, with that being Kim Wexler. For how complex of a relationship Jimmy had with Chuck, this one may be even more impactful dependent on the interpretation. Kim had divorced Jimmy as a result of his endeavors as “Saul Goodman” around the time Breaking Bad begun chronologically, with her exiting his life being tied to the very habit Jimmy is displaying known (scamming/conning). Jimmy’s loss of Kim is another squandered relationship he subconsciously suppressed through his new persona (Saul Goodman), which is another component in how effective this piece of the breakdown is. Jimmy converged these two psychological ties (Kim’s severed connection being a result of what he is doing in the scene itself and the fact it’s a subconscious regret) within one line to further push the legitimacy of the pain he’s trying to convey in his feigning.

3. Overall nihilism/pessimism — Jimmy also states “no kids, no friends… if I die tonight, no one would care” which he continues with by saying life would carry on as if nothing happened if he disappeared. This is a very common belief in many individuals diagnosed with clinical depression, however for Jimmy there’s a different purpose behind it. Jimmy had suppressed the psychological trauma he endured by constantly overloading himself with either work or putting on a falsified exaggerated persona (Saul Goodman). Perhaps even more crucial is the fact that Jimmy’s entire career as a lawyer initially started because he wanted to impress his brother Chuck, seeking approval. With Jimmy now forced to fly under the radar (and the death of his brother), this recognition and extravagant profile will never be realized - making these words embody the very experiences that shaped Jimmy in his earlier years. This sentiment aids in surfacing Jimmy’s subconscious pain, which Jimmy purposefully utilizes and predicts the trajectory of.

Jimmy’s internal tactics result in tears forming in his eyes, which is incredibly difficult to achieve on command. Jimmy abused aspects of his deep subconscious and psychological profile to draw these tears out - showcasing understanding over how his past expenses influence who he is now as well as the latent trauma he holds. This requires an intricately tight and deep-rooted comprehension over one’s self, anticipating the physical trajectory (tear duct activation) in response to certain pulses on the subconscious mind (Chuck, Kim, identity, etc). This is made even more impressive by the fact Jimmy had spent years suppressing these details without losing track of the effects they could hold on him if utilized, given the fact he’s able to integrate them within his acting skills.

Jimmy managed his self-facilitation with how vague he needed to be with his words - he couldn’t be too specific and needed to fit these psychological jabs within the context of general symptoms related to clinical depression, increasing the innate impressiveness of what Jimmy enacted in this feat.

To finalize the quality of this feat, Jimmy is able to swiftly detach himself from this display almost instantly upon the scheme being re-stabilized, meaning his level of control over these intrapersonal aspects is near perfect.
