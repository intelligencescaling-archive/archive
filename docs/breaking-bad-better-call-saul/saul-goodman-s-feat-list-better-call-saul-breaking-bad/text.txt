Saul Goodman’s feat list

This post presents a list of feats attributed to criminal defense attorney James Morgan McGill, nicknamed Jimmy, also known by his business moniker, Saul Goodman.

Better Call Saul:

Chicanery Plan V V V

P1: Chuck secretly recorded him confessing to tampering with the Mesa Verde documents to help Kim Wexler. Chuck did this by intentionally allowing a prior confession tape to leak to the authorities, knowing it would provoke him into breaking into his house to destroy it while confessing again.

P2: His goal in countering this was to prove that Chuck suffered from a mental illness rather than electromagnetic hypersensitivity, thereby making Chuck’s testimony—and the reliability of the tape—appear to be the product of a mental breakdown.

P3: He flies in Chuck’s ex-wife, Rebecca, to sit in the courtroom. To distract Chuck and make him feel vulnerable and humiliated, as he had previously gone to great lengths to hide his condition from her.

P4: He then hires Huell Babineaux to secretly plant a fully charged phone battery in Chuck’s breast pocket after intentionally bumping into him, making it appear to be a simple accident before the hearing begins. This works because he anticipated that electronics were confiscated before the hearing, so he had the battery planted on Chuck—someone who supposedly cannot tolerate electronics—so it would not be expected or searched for, allowing it to remain concealed.

P5: He has Francesca, his receptionist, remain on standby during the hearing in case any issues arise with the plan so he can be notified through her.

P6: During the hearing, he keeps an empty cellphone in his own pocket. He later reveals the empty phone to Chuck, knowing Chuck will point out that it has no battery. This misdirects Chuck into thinking the fake phone is his real strategy.

P7: He then reveals the battery hidden in Chuck’s breast pocket, causing Chuck to spiral into a public mental breakdown after realizing he has been carrying a charged battery the entire time—right in front of his ex-wife.

P8: He then frames his earlier confession as a fabrication, claiming he only admitted to the allegations in order to soothe his mentally ill brother. Making the tape unreliable.

P9: He had to overcome several more obstacles during the hearing: Chuck had already made a copy of the tape, anticipating that the original would be destroyed; there were two witnesses present; and Chuck had a far better reputation.

Nippy Plan V V V

P1: While hiding as Gene Takovic, a mall Cinnabon employee, he encounters Jeff, a taxi driver who recognizes him as the infamous Albuquerque lawyer Saul Goodman. Jeff now has the ability to expose him to the police. To prevent this, he plans to implicate Jeff in a heist so that Jeff cannot turn him in without incriminating himself.

P2: Through off-screen surveillance of Jeff’s house, he figured out that Marion is Jeff’s mother.

P3: After off-screen studying Marion’s routine through stalking, he makes sure that her mobility scooter will get stuck in snow by off-screen pouring powered snow where she would ride across beforehand. On that day, he positions himself nearby stapling lost dog flyers he had printed earlier, pretending his dog “Nippy” jumped out of the car window while he was driving. He helps Marion’s scooter gets unstuck, with a push while secretly cutting the scooter’s power cables so it cannot run properly. This ensures he can manually assist her back home, allowing him to build trust with her and secure an invitation to dinner so he can get closer to Jeff.

P4: He then goes outside to confront Jeff and manipulates him to participate in a mall heist, framing it as an opportunity to make serious money. He knows Jeff is financially vulnerable since he works as a cab driver and thus likely wants a larger source of income.

P5: Prior to this, he had befriended the mall’s security guard, Frank, by bringing him a Cinnabon every night. Over time, he measured exactly how long it took Frank to eat the pastry—three minutes—during which Frank’s back was turned to the security monitors. During these three minutes, he secretly scouted an upscale department store, counting his paces between clothing racks to build a precise mental map of what could be stolen within that window. He later recreated the store’s layout in a snowy field using sticks and tape so Jeff could rehearse the exact route and learn which items to take.

P6: For the heist, he smuggles Jeff into the store inside a large delivery crate. Once Frank begins eating his nightly Cinnabon, he signals Jeff to begin. Jeff follows the choreographed path, stealing specific high‑value items while making sure not to take too many from any single location.

P7: When Jeff slips and knocks himself unconscious during the heist, he has to improvise. He pretends to have an emotional breakdown while talking to Frank, keeping the guard distracted and away from the security monitors long enough for Jeff to recover and escape.

P8: In the end, he now has leverage over Jeff, ensuring Jeff cannot expose his true identity without implicating himself, because the store now has a recording of Jeff committing the robbery.

CC Mobile Strategy V V V

P1: He works as a manager for CC Mobile during his one-year law license suspension.

P2: He uses this position, under the name Saul Goodman, to sell burner phones to criminals. By doing this, he builds a massive database of criminal contacts and begins establishing a reputation in the underworld.

P3: He also paints “IS THE MAN LISTENING?” in large red letters on the store windows to attract conspiracy theorist believers who are paranoid about surveillance and looking for privacy.

P4: By doing this, he builds both a large network of criminals and a customer base of conspiracy-minded buyers, allowing him to accumulate enough money to fund the eventual opening of his law office and the hiring of Huell Babineaux for security.

P5: He also buys phones at wholesale prices and resells them at CC Mobile for a significant markup, allowing him to make far more than his official store salary and generate untaxed profit.

Tuco Persuasion V V V

P1: He needs to talks Tuco Salamanca a psychotic drug kingpin down from a death sentence to six months probation for two skateboarders to prevent them from being murdered this is not an easy task since one of the skateboarders had made the mistake of insulting Tuco’s grandmother.

P2: He first appeals to Tuco’s empathy highlighting that the twins have a hardworking, widowed mother with arthritis who relies on them. And that while the twins might not deserve her, she doesn't deserve to lose her only sons. When this doesn’t work.

P3: He then appeals to Tuco’s pride Instead of challenging authority, he strokes Tuco’s ego by framing him as a tough but fair judge who believes in true justice rather than senseless murder.

P4: He then introduces the concept of the punishment fitting the crime. Through an eye for an eye to move the conversation away from death and toward physical injury.

P5: Finally he suggests that since the twins are skateboarders, breaking one leg each is the most effective way to hit them where they live, as it prevents them from scamming for months while instilling a permanent fear of Tuco. This works.

Breaking Bad:

Fake Heisenberg Strategy V V V

P1: After Badger is arrested for selling meth, the DEA pressures him to reveal the identity of Heisenberg. To save Badger and keep Walt's identity secret, He arranges a strategy involving a professional fall guy.

P2: He hires James Kilkelly, a career criminal who gets paid to go to prison for others.

P3: He then has Badger set up a meth deal where he will expose Heisenberg to the DEA on the condition that he will be released. In reality, the meeting is arranged with James Kilkelly, who disguises himself as Heisenberg.

P4: When James Kilkelly is arrested, Badger is released and the Heisenberg case is closed—saving Badger, keeping Walt’s identity safe, and making the entire Albuquerque Police Department and Drug Enforcement Administration believe the case was over, except for Hank.

RV Escape Strategy V V V

P1: Hank has the RV with Walt and Jesse inside surrounded and is attempting to break in with a lug wrench. Since it is a “private domicile,” Hank cannot enter without a warrant, but refuses to leave while waiting for one to arrive.

P3: He needs to get Hank away from the RV so Walt and Jesse can escape. He does this by having his secretary, Francesca, call Hank’s phone using a burner phone while pretending to be an officer named Lynn Tanner from the Albuquerque Police Department. Having her claim that Hank’s wife, Marie, has been in a mobile accident and is being rushed to Lovelace Medical Center. This causes Hank to rush to the hospital, allowing Walt and Jesse to escape.

P4: Afterwards, he breaks the burner phone so it cannot be traced back to him when Hank eventually realises he was tricked. He also made sure Francesca sounded as professional as possible, during the call describing it as a “mobile accident” and referencing a real police department and a real hospital so that Hank would assume the report was legitimate.

Breaking Bad & Better Call Saul:

Trauma V V V

His parents were dead. His brother, who openly hated him, was dead. His marriage had ended in divorce. He endured his brother intentionally sabotaging him by preventing Howard from hiring him at HHM. He watched Howard be shot dead in front of him. He lived with the knowledge that he had indirectly caused his own brother’s suicide and had blamed it on Howard. He also knew he had helped destroy Howard’s life over pettiness. He endured being beaten up by thugs. He endured being beaten up by Jesse Pinkman. He endured being beaten up by Howard in a boxing match. He endured being stranded in the Mexican desert for two days, carrying 150 pounds of cash for 36 hours and drinking his own urine to stay alive. He endured kneeling in front of an open grave with a gun pointed at his head. Despite all of this, instead of giving up, he continued living under the identity of Gene Takovic.
