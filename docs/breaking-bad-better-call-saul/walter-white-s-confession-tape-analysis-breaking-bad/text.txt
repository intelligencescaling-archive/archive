Walter’s Confession Tape Analysis

This is a feat from Breaking Bad S5 EP11 “Confessions” he did this with only one resource a video camera.

Part One: V V V

He records himself lying that DEA Agent Hank Schrader was Heisenberg, specifically that after his 50th birthday, Hank came to him, manipulating him to use his chemistry knowledge to cook methamphetamine to sell through Hank’s DEA connections, with Hank knowing he wouldn’t refuse because he was reeling from a cancer diagnosis that was going to bankrupt his family. And Hank took him on a ride-along, showing him how much a small meth operation could make, to convince him to use his chemistry for money for his family after he dies from cancer.

W.W specifically used a real event here that other DEA agents saw: the ride-along, to make it logically seem like Hank was most likely Heisenberg. Why else would Hank take his brother-in-law, whom he knew was a chemistry teacher, to a dangerous drug bust? It makes no sense that he would want to put his brother-in-law in danger just to see something as simple as a drug bust, unless Hank was using it to convince his brother-in-law by seeing how much the small meth operation was making, just like W.W said.

W.W manipulates the viewer into thinking Hank doesn’t care for family beforehand by admitting that, if they were watching the tape, he was most likely murdered by his brother-in-law, Hank Schrader. This is to make the viewer feel like following the narrative that he is creating for Hank. See, once someone imagines Hank murdering his brother-in-law, the brain starts scanning backward for signs that he could be capable of it. Hank, up to this point, was publicly seen as brash, loud, insensitive, and hyper-focused on work. So it’s not impossible to the viewer.

W.W. also manipulates the viewer into thinking he was shocked by Hank’s proposal, as it is a logical reaction to the proposal and to how he initially thought Hank was a moral man, which is why he was shocked and confused by it. He acts the way other people thought of Hank back then, and how they would react to a hypothetical proposal like that, making him seem more trustworthy so the viewer thinks the confession is more true.

W.W also paints himself as a victim to the viewer by reflecting on the drug proposal with supposed regret for it every day after realising that he was way over his head. Since the viewer already believes he is trustworthy, they think he truly does regret it and shouldn’t be held accountable, as Hank is to blame.

Part Two: V V V

He then lies that Hank partnered with Gustavo Fring, selling him as a slave to Gus as a chemist and forcing him to work for Gustavo Fring.

W.W. used a real event here again, it’s true that he started working for Gustavo Fring as a chemist beforehand, making it seem like his testimony was trustworthy again. It was known during this tape that Gustavo was a drug kingpin after his death, so Hank working with him also makes sense if we assume he was Heisenberg.

W.W. manipulates the viewer again by mentioning that he tried to leave the meth business, as he was regretting accepting the initial drug deal proposal mentioned before, but Gus threatened his family, coercing him into staying as a chemist, making his testimony seem more trustworthy and painting himself again as a believable victim. He also uses the real event of Gus threatening his family here to reframe the situation.

Part Three: V V V

He lies that Hank and Gus had a falling out because of Hank’s pride, pushing for a greater share of the business, which is in character for Hank given his pride. He then frames the narrative so that Gus realised Hank was too prideful for a proper partnership flatly rejected the offer and arranged a hit on him using the Salamanca twins to end their partnership. Hank survived the hit and worked with Hector Salamanca to arrange a bomb to kill Gus in revenge on both sides—a bomb he was forced to build.

W.W. used real events here—the hit on Hank, the bombing of Gus, and the construction of the bomb—making it seem like his testimony was trustworthy once again. He also relied on the believable personalities of both men, matching their established characters, to manipulate the viewer into trusting his narrative. He further uses the real event of paying for Hank’s hospital bills to reinforce the story that Hank was a drug kingpin, forcing Walter to cover the costs with his own drug money.

Part Four:

He then reinforces his victim persona by lying about contemplating suicide and wanting to go to the police, but being terrified to do so because of Hank and Gus. That Hank had risen in the DEA ranks to become Head of the DEA, which gave Hank the authority to keep him in line by taking his children away from him for three months. And after the three months, when he tried to quit, Hank had given him a black eye, indirectly coercing him to keep working. After this, he was too scared, living in fear every day of what Hank would do to him or his family, so he made the confession video.

W.W. used real events here—Hank’s rise in rank and the three-month separation—making it seem like his testimony was trustworthy for the final time.

W.W. exploited the black eye Hank gave him previously, after discovering he was Heisenberg, to use it against him.

W.W. deliberately altered his body language and vocal tone, making his voice crack and pitch high to give the illusion that he was crying, so the viewers would emotionally believe his testimony.

Conclusion: V V V

The confession tape is believable. It has solid logical reasoning, abductive reasoning, counteraction, logical manipulation, acting, and circumstantial evidence.

He reflected on those past events in a 24-hour period and used them to create a very well-crafted misdirection and fabrication.
