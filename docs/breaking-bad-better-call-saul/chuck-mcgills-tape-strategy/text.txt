This doc will explain and analyze what is probably Chuck’s best feat in the show, as well as a few other miscellaneous feats.

Tape Strategy

CONTEXT:

HHM has been in a small tug of war over a client, the bank Mesa Verde, with Chuck’s brother Jimmy and Jimmy’s girlfriend Kim, specifically the latter. Chuck is able to convince Mesa Verde to stay with HHM (more details in the miscellaneous section - see: “Persuading Mesa Verde”), but during the meeting, he’s exposed to high amounts of electricity, causing him to later pass out because of his electromagnetic hypersensibility (a psychosomatic condition). Because of this, Jimmy is called to take care of the unconscious Chuck.

Seeing the Mesa Verde files, Jimmy has an idea. He takes the files to a late-night copy shop and changes the written address of Mesa Verde’s proposed new branch from 1261 Rosella Drive to 1216 Rosella Drive. This causes Chuck to screw up the address during a meeting with Mesa Verde, as he uses the wrong address and insists on having read 1216 on the files, and his insistence on the matter causes him to lose his reputation with Mesa Verde, on top of having pushed back the matter by six weeks. Chuck checks the files as soon as he gets home and sees that the written address is 1261. He figures out almost immediately exactly what happened (more details in the miscellaneous section - see: “Figuring out Jimmy’s swap”, I recommend reading that part before reading the strategy for better context).

While probing for evidence on Jimmy’s actions, Chuck finds the copy shop Jimmy used, but he finds that the employer there has been bribed and thus refuses to confirm Jimmy’s presence the night of the copying. While arguing, Chuck has an intense attack of his electromagnetic hypersensibility and passes out, hitting his head.

The only way this is possible is if Jimmy was already close by. Chuck figures that this was the case because Jimmy had been to the copy shop to bribe the employer, explaining the change of statements between the time Ernesto talked to him and the time Chuck talked to him. All of this makes it very likely that Chuck didn’t believe Ernesto’s version here, and the fact that he didn’t point this out could indicate that he had already thought of his strategy and did so deliberately to begin constructing the narrative he needed, which would be extremely impressive given that he has just woken up from a state of unconsciousness, but it’s a bit of a stretch, so I’ll leave it as a possibility and nothing more. At the very least, Chuck recognized that Ernesto’s loyalty made him an asset for the future and chose not to jeopardize it by doubting him and possibly creating friction. Let’s get into the strategy.

The entire tape strategy can be broken down into two main phases and a third one serving as the conclusion, the second phase being an extension of the first one, and both relying on an accurate prediction of Jimmy’s behavior, as I will detail later.

This first phase consists of exploiting a situation to construct a narrative that allows Chuck to manipulate Jimmy into confessing to what he did and to thus get a recording of it (the infamous “tape” I named the strategy after). What this recording is meant to achieve will be explained in the second phase. [Strategy Vision]

In order to manipulate Jimmy, Chuck needs to fabricate a precise state of mind that Jimmy can believe to be a result of the situation he himself put Chuck into. The crux of this is that every negative emotion Chuck is simulating has to stem from Jimmy’s address-swap. At the same time, these negative emotions have to be potent enough for Jimmy to want to take the burden off of Chuck by confessing. Keep this in mind as I explain the trap itself. VERDICT - DID JIMMY OUTSMART CHUCK IN THE END?

There is no doubt that Jimmy (or Saul) scales higher than Chuck overall. He just has way better feats on almost every front, even though there are aspects in which Chuck is superior. That being said, the Tape Strategy as a whole is clearly an example of Chuck outsmarting Jimmy, though you could argue that this is not “prime” Jimmy, since he’s only three seasons in.

Jimmy later came up with a strategy to goad Chuck into a breakdown in the courtroom, which many might point to as proof of Jimmy eventually getting the upper hand. While this is true, I feel like Jimmy had an unfair advantage in that Chuck actually believed his condition to be real, meaning that he could’ve never anticipated Jimmy’s specific move that caused the breakdown. So Jimmy’s move is not something Chuck could’ve ever considered as a possibility, which makes it dubious if this should count as a proper victory or rather a situational win. Chuck managed to remain a step ahead the entire time until Jimmy pulled the rug by using Chuck’s greatest weakness, something that Chuck’s mental health didn’t allow him to anticipate. That aside, Jimmy was also helped by Kim, who is very smart in her own right and also provides motivational support. In this doc alone, there are two separate instances mentioned that would’ve led to Chuck’s victory if Kim hadn’t been there for Jimmy, and on top of that, she contributed to Jimmy’s counterstrategy, even though he did most of the work, effectively making this a 2v1.

As for Chuck’s placement as a whole amongst smart characters, I believe that his feats are really solid and worthy of consideration, but I don’t think he could ever scale too high due to a lack of versatility and variety in feats, and most of all, his condition rendering him useless in a lot of situations. The fact that he can do something completely ordinary and it counts as an AC feat should be telling. Regardless, you can still take his moves in a vacuum and scale those just based on how smart they are, and his performance within the feats in this doc is impressive considering his limitations.

* Figuring out Jimmy’s swap
Right after the Mesa Verde fiasco, Chuck went to his home to check the files and found that the written address was, in fact, 1261. Almost immediately, he figured out what happened. Later, he gives Jimmy and Kim an extremely accurate account of his suspicions, but he might not have figured out everything immediately. Keep in mind that there’s nothing suggesting that his original inference couldn’t have already been complete and detailed, but I’ll low-ball the feat to show that it’s still impressive. Let’s break this down:
* Chuck was sure of having read 1216. He checked the number multiple times, he’s implied (and shown) to have a very good memory, and most importantly, he had made a mental note of how the number was “one after Magna Charta” (which was signed in the year 1215).
* This means that the files Chuck read and the files with the real address number, which were now on his desk, are different.
* Chuck thus infers that someone has tampered with the files in his house before he could work with them, then replaced the tampered files with the original copies before he came back from the meeting.
* What’s above is what Chuck 100% had already inferred originally (he said “It wasn’t a mistake”), but I think it’s unreasonable to claim he didn’t at least clock in Jimmy at this point.
* For whom could have done this, Chuck realizes it was Jimmy because:
   * Jimmy has reason to do this. Specifically, Chuck understands that Jimmy did it for Kim’s sake, as he loves her and believes she deserves Mesa Verde as a client.
   * Jimmy was at his house while he was delirious from the effects of the hypersensitivity, so he would’ve been in a situation where he could’ve seen the files and thought of the scheme.
   * Jimmy has the keys to Chuck’s house, thus he could’ve waited for Chuck to leave, snuck back in, and replaced the tampered files with the original copies.
   * Jimmy has a history of conning and something like this is very much in his style. Chuck makes the example that when Jimmy was younger he was good at falsifying ID cards to buy beer for himself and his friends.
* So Chuck infers the entire process of Jimmy’s scheme: he found the files unattended, Chuck unconscious, he took the files to a late-night copy shop, he meticulously created copies that were completely identical except for the address number, he replaced the original files with the fakes, he waited for Chuck to leave for the meeting, entered his house, and put the original files back in their, leaving no trace of his interference.
