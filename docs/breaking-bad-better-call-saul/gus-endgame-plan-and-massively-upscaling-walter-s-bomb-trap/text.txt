Gus' Endgame plan + massively upscaling Walter's bomb trap

The most common interpretation of Walter's bomb trap is just this:

\\> Understand the intense feud between Hector and Gus.

\\> convinced Hector to die alongside Gus using the bomb.

\\> Have Hector call the DEA, specifically requesting for Hank.

\\> Gus will personally come and kill Hector soon after because it seems Hector rat out Gus.

\\> this is believable because of the torture that Gus put him into, so Hector has nothing to lose.

\\> Gus dies alongside Hector.

However, this goes MUCH DEEPER after reanalyzing this.

The first thing to point out is Gus' motive:

Why would Gus even kill Hector especially just immediately after the interview? Even if he wanted to kill him and successfully covered his track, you obviously shouldn't do that when that would bring more suspicion towards you. Gus learned this with Lalo and that's also why he couldn't kill Hank when he began investigating him. And you definitely shouldn't do that this early.

How do we know that Gus doesn't already know that Hector didn't rat him out? Gus has vast network/connections, from law enforcements to media. The entire media and DEA made a big deal about this meeting that they invited numerous TV networks expecting Hector's confession so Gus has much easier access to info.

But Gus still tried to kill Hector, why?

Simply, Gus' target wasn't just Hector, it's Hank and the Cartel.

To understand this is to first know Gus' Endgame plan:

After taking out the Capos of the Cartel, Gus still has to deal with Hank who is still investigating him and Walter who needs to be replaced by the newly transformed Jesse.

As he returned to ABQ, he initiate his move. Gus dragged Walter in the desert, declaring that he's fired and announced that he will now make his move against Hank, Gus tells him if he interfere, he will kill his family.

As predicted, Walt surely believes this and panics. This tactic of dragging Walt here is doubled layered to make Walter believe that Gus is doing this for show as he cannot kill Walter but this only makes Walter more scared when Gus threaten to kill his family as Walt realize that this protection doesn't extend to them, and he knew Gus would go this far.

Gus utilized Saul to accomplish what he predicted Walt would do to simultaneously protect his family and his brother-in-law.

1) the vacuum escape plan will be given to Walt by Saul and Walt will surely have enough money to pay for it.

2) a call to the DEA to have maximum security to Hank.

While Walt originally wanted to mention Gus, Gus knew that Walt needed Saul to be one to do it, knowing that:

1) Walt wouldn't call personally knowing his voice would be identified.

2) Walt only has the quickest and reliably Saul available for him especially on a panicked state and convenience of already intending to use him to leave.

Gus also predicted that Saul would not mention Gus for fear of him and logically conclude that the cartel is the best option for the authorities to actually take it seriously.

Unbeknownst to them, they both played the move that Gus was hoping for them to do. This strategy being similar to One minute strategy that Gus played prior.

When the DEA hear of the Cartel coming back to Hank, what would be Hank's and the DEA's reaction if Hank's sister-in-law and her family just suddenly went missing after this threat? The suspicion will instantly be put on the cartel.

This time, the response by the government would actually be more severe as an entire civilian family just presumably got kidnapped. And the Cartel is in its worse state as it has been as the many of the Capos just got killed, leaving a massive power vacuum. This power vacuum which Gus will take control over just like what he did when he used the government in the one minute strategy. This could be played in many ways like offering protection to gangs that will side with him or redirecting it to other enemy cartels like what he did with the Peru cartel in BCS.

Besides the Cartel, this kidnapping would absolutely psychologically destroy Hank likely leading him to quit the DEA permanently due to guilt, which Hank is already struggling against that Gus knows about. Hank will either drop Gus' case or hand it over to someone else, making it easier for Gus to get rid of this entirely. Even killing Hank will only put on the suspicion on the Cartel again if Gus chooses to.

To ensure that Walt and his family would leave and not testify, Gus' men could just picked them up pretending to be the Vacuum guy as Walt and Saul doesn't even know who his face and name, to then kill them and hide the bodies. This fulfills Gus' promise to Walter that he will kill his family if Walt interfere with Hank in which he did, just not the way Walt expected. Characteristic for Gus who constantly like to play with the intent of his words while keeping them still being true. A monkey's paw.

However due to bad luck, this plan fails Skyler had suddenly given Walt's money to Ted, and Gus couldn't have readjust as:

1) Skyler giving away the money happened during Gus' and Jesse's stay in Mexico, meaning Gus was too occupied in his plan with the Cartel.

2) Mike's is at rest due to his injury, so Mike couldn't have checked on what's happening with Saul.

This then leads to Walter's bomb trap.

After reconsideration of recent events, Walter was able to reasoned Gus' endgame plan that had failed. This will be self evident with the moves that Walt would do to perfectly bait Gus.

With the failure of the hospital bomb, Walt learn that Gus' keen reasoning would see through even the most miniscule of details as long as Gus feel not in control. So Walt would now bait him by providing him a situation where he's in the most control, this is by continuing Gus' endgame plan but also adjusting it to Walt's contingency in S6.

First, by returning back to the cartel.

Walt knows the plan of baiting Gus by making it seem like Hector rat out is insufficient due to Gus' network and Gus would be too busy fighting the accusation. However, this would perfectly fit if this is the scheme that Hector came up on his own just to scare Gus.

Why would Gus care about what Hector did if Gus knows that Hector didn't say anything? Because Walter knows that even calling to the DEA that look like you would confessed, even just as a stunt would be an absolute insult to their pride as a cartel member. Walter knows this when he heard from Jesse that Hector was willing prove to the DEA that he's completely competent just to shit himself afterwards than to say anything, to Walter this means that even this would be honorable and respectful than making it look like you're going to betray the cartel publically.

What make this more effective, that instead of Hector's previous interrogation where it's a small room and audience, this now has a large audience of DEA and news reporters. Walt gave Gus opportunity to control the narrative of what really happens in this room, even though nothing was received, rumors could easily be spread that a deal was made that happens privately due to such a large attention that was given to this.

Who will this news be for? The Cartel. Walt reasoned that Gus' fight with the cartel has likely concluded when he and Jesse got home from Mexico. Yet, Gus still needed absolute control over the South. The current state of the Cartel is likely very weak, by spreading news of Hector Salamanca possibly betraying the Cartel, their plan to regroup and fight back will heavily diminish. At the same time, this would heavily demoralize them even more seeing a Capo leaving a legacy of being a rat.

This is a layer where it fits perfectly to Hector's character of destroying his external pride that will make him look bad because he has nothing to do left but to scare Gus while still being realistic that Hector choosing to keep his internal pride of still not choosing to be an actual rat in the end. This is why when Gus faced Hector, he only talks about how the others would see Hector and the legacy he would leave behind, and not explicitly what he personally think of it.

Second, by returning back to Hank.

During the One minute strategy, Walt discovered that Gus also planned to kill the twins during this. With the sudden death of one of the twins in the hospital, Walt now reasoned that it's likely not natural for him to die this way with Gus likely being responsible. Likely medically inserted to him like a syringe, this then lead to Walt considering the possibility that Gus will use a syringe to kill Hector.

Why the syringe? Because Walt manipulate Gus to kill Hector the same way as one of the twins. Because Walt predicts that Gus will have Hank be framed for Hector's death. This is why Walt have Hector call for Hank specifically and have him continually insults him, to manipulate Gus into attempting to also target Hank by killing Hector as he could be put as a suspect.

When Hector dies, the suspicion would be likely be on the cartel but Walt gave Gus the opportunity to direct it to Hank, which is also why Gus planned to kill Hector as soon as possible.

Walt knows that Gus would not preferably physically harm him but further destroy his career alongside his credibility in Gus' case.

How would Hank do that? He's physically handicapped? This could be done by Hank instructing one of his DEA men close to him, to do it to Hector. Hank has strong connections and heavily valued by the rest of the DEA agents to the point that they enthusiastically donated their blood to him but most importantly, Walt was there when Gomez and the rest celebrated one of the twins death in the hospital, saying "burn in hell". Walt knows that Gus has strong inside connections inside the DEA and could frame one of the DEA agents in killing both Marco and Hector using the syringe. Motive? Revenge for what they did to Hank. Gus would be encouraged to kill Hector as soon as possible since the entire DEA recently filled this building, making it easier to believe that one of the agents stayed to kill Hector. Walt knows that Gus' influence has reach hospitals.

Why would Hank do that? Walt knows that Hank has PTSD due to his numerous encounters with the Salamancas, and Hank even personally open up to Walt and we know Heisenberg Walt gladly use this vulnerabilities often to effectively manipulate people like Jesse and Skyler. Combined with the sudden threat call that happened prior, Hank would have the motive to kill Hector due to fear and anger which he has history of when he beats Jesse and got suspended and records of PTSD.

Not only is this a masterful trap to kill Gus, but Walter was also able to align Hank and Gus together for Walt's emergency contingency to frame Hank as Heisenberg. which I discussed \[here\](https://www.reddit.com/r/IntelligenceScaling/s/qQs4q3WQrf)

To perfectly fit this together, does this change the narrative? It doesn't, it made it even better. Gus could have successfully killed Hector and live if he didn't personally choose to do it himself. He still died wanting to be the one to kill Hector and destroy his legacy. The parallel of both of this final schemes will always come back to the one minute strategy, specifically their meeting. Walt was able to think like Gus because as he said during the meeting when he revealed Gus' strategy, "I would have done the same". Then s6 is Walter trying to be like Gus fring and Mike calling him out for it.
