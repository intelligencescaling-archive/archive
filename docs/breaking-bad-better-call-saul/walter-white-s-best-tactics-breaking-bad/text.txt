What is Walter White’s best tactic?

Walter White is, in my opinion, the best in his verse when it comes to tactics, and it's probably his most underrated offensive aspect of all, which is why l've made this poll.

Counting moves within schemes, these are what I believe to be his best tactics.

Brock Poisoning: Walter processes Lily of the Valley and, through an unexplained process (most probably breaking into his home) makes Brock ingest poison.

This not only demoralizes Jesse heavily, but also manipulates him into forcing a confrontation with Walter before any of Gus' henchmen. This tactic also simultaneously works as setup for an expert, double layered emotional manipulation and sophistry which was achieved through another tactic.

With one move, Walter manages to set up the only way in which he could get leverage on Gus, all while he runs on borrowed time and using a literal plan to turn the situation on its head.

If we take the interpretation that Walter formulated the blueprint of Lily of the Valley in the shot where he's looking at the aforementioned plant, this tactic must've been thought on within 6 or 7 seconds.

\*Fabricated confession\*: On the next episode, Walter convinces Hector to work with him. After negotiating with him, Walter makes Hector ask the lady taking care of him in him to contact the retired cartel elite with the DEA.

He specifically makes Hector say he wants to speak to Hank Schrader. This is due to two (maybe three) things;

\* Hank is being tracked by Gus, therefore setting up a meeting with the former ensures the latter knows who he's talking with.

\* Hank killed Hector's nephews, Tuco, Marco and Leonel. Arranging a meeting with Hank would give Hector an opportunity to also go out insulting one of the people who hurt his family the most, thus making the deal even sweeter.

\* (If Gus were to consider the option of Hector not snitching) Hank has been strategizing against Gus for months now. The fact that a cartel member that hasn't been active for years decided to try and spit on Hank the exact moment he was investigating Gus and being safeguarded by the DEA would shift an incredible amount of suspicion to the chicken man.

Do note that the DEA would interpret Hector seemingly trying to snitch as him just trying to lure Hank out so the cartel can kill him, but Gus doesn't know this- and given he believes Walter tipped off the DEA, it'd only make sense for White to pin it on Gus or keep it vague.

Finally, when Gus see Hector pull in, he'd be at a crossroads. No matter how he approaches the situation, taking personal revenge on Hector is the only course of action he could take. Walter, of course, foresaw all of this through intricate profiling and induction.

\* Possibility one: Hector ratted on Gus. Fring, a man who takes betrayals personally, would have basically nothing to do but kill Hector, given his empire would essentially crumble after a solid testimony against him.

\* Possibility two: Hector didn't snitch. Even if Gus were to think this, the tactic still makes it seem that Hector is willing to at the very least interact with the DEA, and the moment he chose to do so shifts a LOT of suspicion into Gus anyways.

This tactic has an insane mount of intricate layers and invincibility and manipulates three people into playing Walt's game expertly.

\*DEA Deviation\*: During Felina, Walter Skinny Pete and Badger to spend their time giving anonymous tips to the DEA (most probably via pay-phone).

The calls are strategically designed to spend as much of the DEA's resources as possible. From what we're told, they contain:

\* Fake info regarding possible locations on where

Walter is.

\* More concrete pieces of info that would force the

DEA to spend a lot of man hours into essentially nothing (manifestos, bomb threats, etc.)

These not only severely waste a lot of the DEA's resources, they simultaneously also paint the police as incompetent, which would make some people not report any actual vital information due to the fact that the police aren't actually doing anything and that they might see the call as another prank.

Not only that, but we see that the DEA is safeguarding Walter's family. Marie, Skyler and Junior are all being surveilled by multiple agents. This adds a contingency into Walter's strategy, essentially disrupting the only line of leverage Lydia and Jack have over Walter temporarily.

Another important use of this tactic is how Lydia is an extremely paranoid person. How do you think she’d react after Walter purposefully challenges the DEA and manipulates them into obstructing her own territory? She’d be even more inclined to kill him, which just adds another layer to Walter’s logical manipulation in the M60 plan.
