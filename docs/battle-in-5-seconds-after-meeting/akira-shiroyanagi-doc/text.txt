* Two players are assigned a random topic and a position on that topic.

Chapter 5-6
Akira has started the first programme. He sees his opponent, Kirisaki, and instantly sees the stick in his hand, and he thinks he must have an ability that uses tools. Kirisaki instantly starts charging at him, which makes Akira realise his ability must be a close-ranged one that relies on him getting close-up and using his stick. The stick Kirisaki holds turns into a sword. Akira uses the tables as meat shields so he doesn’t get sliced. Kirisaki says Akira must not have an ability that’s good for combat since he’s only running and blocking, so he asks Akira to surrender. Before the fight, Akira had thought of the factors that could mess up his ability. He could simply tell the opponent, “I can make anyone die”, but they could possibly mishear him due to the tension/noise of the fight and hear something like, “I can make anyone cry.” Potentially, they could imagine your ability being different from what you want them to believe. For example, let’s say Akira told his opponent he could create hammers. His opponent might imagine he could create a hammer, but while the hammer Akira may have in mind could be a massive war hammer a warrior would wield, his opponent could visualise a harmless hammer you’d see in a whack-a-mole game.
Furthermore, there is no guarantee the opponent will believe him in the first place since it’d be strange to announce your ability to a stranger. This inherent dependency on the opponent's imagination means that Akira has to be careful about what he suggests. The ability has to be plausible and recognizable to the opponent for it to manifest effectively. Therefore, Akira stuck to avoiding attacks and acting weak to get Kirisaki to feel convinced that he is superior. By forcing Kirisaki to feel superior, Akira encourages him to make demands, such as surrendering or to fight back. This is tactical because it creates a situation where the opponent listens to what he says. Using the tables previously wasn’t just for defense but to feed into the impression that he is weaker than he is, making Kirisaki overconfident. Akira tells him that he’s hesitating to use his ability because it’s too dangerous. This leads Kirisaki comes to the conclusion that Akira has an ability that takes time to activate or has to be activated at the right time, he goes on to attack him anyway.
Akira predicted what Kirisaki entire thought process would be when he told him this.
Kirisaki thoughts were “He hesitated because his ability is too dangerous? Even while he's being slashed at by a sword? That's not possible. He has to be bluffing.. However... if he really has an ability that can't be used in combat, he should've surrendered by now... his ability... is there a reason behind his movements? Regardless of whether it is or isn't...if he won't surrender, and I don't know what his ability is..then all i can do is get him before he gets me!”
Akira announces that his ability is to turn his hand into a cannon. This works because all of the contestants have seen Mion use this ability before, so it is simple to visualise plus materialising the cannon takes a while so that fits his reasoning of it being an ability that takes time to activate. Plus Akira had said he was hesitant to use his ability because it was too dangerous which logically makes sense as the area they were fighting in was fairly small and enclosed so it was possible one could accidentally blow themself up with cannon if they were not careful and didn’t find the right opening to fire. Furthermore Akira had declared his “ability” was the cannon right between the moments of Kirisaki swinging his sword and himself getting striked down. The reasoning for this was that Kirisaki wouldn’t be able to question what Akira claimed in such short time and at point and Kirisaki's adrenaline would limit his ability to critically analyse the claim meaning he’d believe him and Akira’s real ability would 100% activate. Akira blasts Kirisaki away but did not kill him , he then points his cannon at him and then tells him to surrender or he’ll shoot him, and with that, he wins. Another note to be made, Akira not killing Kirisaki here is not him being merciful, it is more convenient for Akira if someone who thinks his ability is the cannon is out there so he could use them later which he does.

Akira’s opponent (Koji Kitajima) has his hands on the ground and doesn’t seem to be approaching him. Akira’s Thought Process: When shooting his cannon at the opponent, the cannon ball disappears. At the same time, Akira notices that his handcannon dematerialised, but obviously he isn’t certain if the timing is the same, so he shoots again, and he watches as the handcannon vanishes. The fact that the cannon ball and handcannon disappear simultaneously is what leads Akira to his deduction. There was a chance the Koji's ability was to simply make any projectile that touched him disappear when he had his hands on the ground, but if that were the case, then why would the handcannon itself disappear too? This would mean he has the ability to negate the power as a whole, so to counter this, Akira throws his shoes at the opponent’s head since the shoes wouldn’t disappear since they aren’t part of any ability.
Some may argue why didn’t his opponent move out of the way of the shoe or hit the shoe away, this isn’t possible as both of these things would require him to take his hands off the ground which he could not risk as he could be shot by Akira’s cannon. Calculation
Firstly I’ll estimate the swing duration.
For a centrifugal slam using a chain approximately 1.5-2 meters long (based on the cuff attachment shown in the panels).
Angular velocity of a forceful overhead swing: ~4-5 radians/second
Arc traveled from peak to ground impact: ~180° (π radians)
Time for complete swing: π ÷ 4.5 ≈ 0.7 seconds
Akira's Decision Point
From the panels, Akira doesn't react immediately when grabbed. He processes the threat as he's being lifted/swung. The visual storytelling suggests he acts roughly halfway through the swing trajectory when the danger becomes clear.
Available processing window: 0.35-0.4 seconds
