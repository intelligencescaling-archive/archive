Kenjaku vs gweyn  | death battle style | is soul identity or body?

# Core Theme

Both Gwyn and Kenjaku are scientists who experiment with the concept of the soul and use it for their own feats and schemes.

Kenjaku literally desires to see something new and beyond what currently exists. Based on what Gwyn did with soul replication, I feel he has something similar going on.

However, I think the difference between them is that **Kenjaku studies broadly, while Gwyn studies deeply.**

# Kenjaku's Feats

Kenjaku created the Death Paintings through experiments involving cursed spirits and human bodies. This shows how far he's willing to go when experimenting with the relationship between different forms of existence.

His CT is also worth considering, as it's basically optimized for inheriting memories and cursed techniques from different bodies. This allows him to acquire different abilities and knowledge, which he can then use for his own research and schemes.

What's interesting is how he can further utilize and improve upon the possibilities available to him through different bodies. For example, he was able to use an Open Domain Expansion in Geto's body, which shows his exceptional understanding of jujutsu, particularly barrier techniques and how to utilize them without a conventional closed barrier.

However, I wouldn't say his CT alone proves that the soul is the body. Rather, it shows how well he can exploit the relationship between them. This is evident during Gojo's sealing, where Kenjaku discusses the relationship between the body and soul with Mahito, and Geto's body responds to Gojo despite Kenjaku's control over it.

It's also interesting when we consider how a person's CT can influence their perception of the world and their interaction with it. Kenjaku's understanding of these mechanics makes his research into the body and soul even more impressive.

Then there's Yuji's birth. Kenjaku's role in Yuji's creation is another example of his experimentation with the body and its properties. Yuji is genuinely a special case in the JJK world, considering his physical capabilities and his ability to contain Sukuna. This shows Kenjaku's impressiveness in experimentation and AC, as he was able to create a highly unusual individual with significant implications for the jujutsu world.

Another major achievement is his reincarnation plan.

He managed to prepare ancient sorcerers for reincarnation through cursed objects, exploiting the relationship between the soul, body, and cursed energy. This wasn't just theoretical knowledge; he actually used his understanding to establish a system for his Culling Game plan.

Kenjaku also recognized the usefulness of Mahito's Idle Transfiguration and incorporated it into his plans. This shows his ability to identify useful phenomena and exploit them for something much bigger.

Overall, Kenjaku's research covers the body, soul, cursed techniques, cursed objects, reincarnation, and human experimentation.

His main strength is the sheer breadth of his research, how he optimizes the resources and abilities available to him, and how he applies his findings to his schemes

# Gweyen feats: -

[read this doc to know about the feat](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1EXxDeO88CkzI8q0hBQ8vi5EDOygpFHTZ6SvTcSq02gY/edit?tab=t.0)

Now, Gwyn is a different case, with kinda the same overall idea as Kenjaku, but his application is different.

His soul replication of Delta, Dante, and Amane is particularly interesting because he wasn't simply copying their appearance or basic behavior.

 what Gwyn would ply need to understand to pull this off.

He would need to identify how each individual thinks, processes information, makes decisions, reacts emotionally, and behaves under different circumstances. He would also need to account for their motivations, values, emotional triggers, cognitive processes, and behavioral tendencies.

However, the important part is that these things don't operate independently.

Someone might usually make logical decisions but become emotionally driven when their pride is threatened. Another individual might be aggressive under ordinary circumstances but become cautious when something they genuinely value is at stake.

Gwyn would need to understand how these different components interact and reproduce those interactions within his replicas. Otherwise, he'd just be creating superficial imitations rather than accurate replications of the individuals themselves.

What's even more interesting is that the replicas can retain the characteristics associated with their originals while possessing their own distinct traits without fundamentally losing their identities.

This means the feat isn't necessarily about creating three identical copies that behave exactly the same way in every situation. It's about reproducing their defining characteristics while allowing their personalities to function and respond to different circumstances.

And this is where Gwyn's intelligence comes into play.

He's stated to possess the ability to instantly quantify and identify any phenomenon. Considering his soul replication feat, this ability becomes particularly relevant because he would need to process and understand the characteristics that define each individual before reproducing them.

Take Dante, for example, whose central theme is friendship. characters embody the concepts represented by their themes, and those concepts are part of their souls according creator of the series of beyblade

So, if Gwyn replicated Dante's soul, he would need to reproduce the characteristics associated with his conceptual identity, not simply copy someone who values friendship.

And the interesting part is that Dante himself doesn't necessarily have to consciously understand the full extent of what his soul embodies for Gwyn to replicate it.

In other words, Gwyn could reproduce something fundamental to Dante's identity that Dante himself might not fully understand or be able to explain.

This is kinda like creating a good Mahito out of Yuji in JJK terms. He would be reproducing an individual's underlying characteristics rather than simply copying their outward personality.

And this is where I think the feat becomes particularly impressive when compared to Kenjaku.

also Gwyn managed to replicate these souls without needing to experience the same kind of direct soul awareness or perception of the soul's contours that characters like Sukuna demonstrate through their experiences with Yuji's body.

# VERDICT:

Of course, Beyblade and JJK operate under different metaphysical systems, so we can't automatically equate their soul mechanics.

But that's precisely why Gwyn's feat is interesting: **he was able to perform soul replication within Beyblade's own framework without relying on the specific methods of soul perception demonstrated in JJK.**

* **Breadth of soul-related research:** both
* **Diversity of demonstrated experiments:** Kenjaku.
* **Application of soul-related knowledge to long-term schemes:** Kenjaku.
* **Replication of distinct psychological identities:** Gwyn, under the interpretation outlined above.
* **Replication of soul-associated conceptual identity:** Gwyn, provided the narrative premises supporting this interpretation hold.
* **Overall specialization in soul replication:** Gwyn.

Gwyn is the better **soul replication specialist**, even if Kenjaku is the more broadly experienced researcher.
