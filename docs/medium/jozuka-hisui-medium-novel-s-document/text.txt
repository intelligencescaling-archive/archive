THE SERIES OF WOMEN'S BODY DUMPING MURDERS

   The culprit in this case was Kogetsu, who had accompanied Hisui in solving the previous three cases.

   His motive was rather twisted. When he was young, his older sister was stabbed by a robber, and when the robber pulled the knife out, it caused her death. However, he could not accept this, so he began abducting beautiful young women to experiment on whether being stabbed and having the knife pulled out was painful and fatal. He could not believe that he had killed his sister.

   He changed his name, became a mystery novelist, and later joined the police investigations into his own murders. This allowed him to stay informed about the police's progress and find ways to counter it. He killed his victims and dumped their bodies in different locations, erasing all evidence, making it nearly impossible for the police to solve the case.

   From their first meeting, as a psychic, Hisui felt something was off about Kogetsu. She decided to observe him and staged an elaborate act to make him believe in her powers—if she was wrong, so be it.

   The first suspicion came from the case of the weeping woman. When she saw the victim's body, Hisui observed Kogetsu's facial expression and saw shock, admiration, and indignation rather than grief for a close friend. It was as if someone had stolen his prey. And indeed, he had once intended to make Yuika his target, but due to the complexity of his social relationship with her, he abandoned the idea.

   The second suspicion also arose from the same case. Previously, the culprit wrapped the bodies in vinyl sheets before disposing of them, but starting from the sixth case, there was a change. He began using shower hoses and bleach to wash them clean. The culprit was already cautious, so why become even more so? From this, Hisui deduced that he was afraid of his DNA being discovered. Since Kogetsu had also had his DNA taken in the Yuika case, her suspicions grew. Hisui understood that the culprit was inherently cautious, and no one knew what the police might actually do with the DNA sample. It was this excessive caution that made Hisui suspect him.

   At the Glass Villa, Kogetsu sketched out a misleading profile of the culprit and even defended him, making remarks that seemed to deny any deviant desires on his part. This further deepened Hisui's suspicions. However, lacking concrete evidence, she set a trap to catch him.
