The World's Most Elaborate Gaslighting Campaign (Cinder's Manipulation of Ironwood)

Cinder's plan has the least visible presence throughout Volume 1.

Ultimately, most of the major elements of Cinder's scheme are already being put into motion during this period.

Ironwood, his military forces, the transfer students, and eventually the mechanized army all play significant roles in the events leading up to the Fall of Beacon.

However, despite all of these moving pieces, Cinder herself has very little to do throughout Volume 1 after saving Roman and maintaining her mysterious presence in the background.

And that's precisely the point.

Cinder isn't supposed to be the center of attention yet.

In fact, her entire approach during this stage of the plan relies on the exact opposite: keeping herself out of sight while allowing other people to attract the attention that she deliberately wants them to attract.

![img](12wo1g7mlazh1 "\"Do you have any Idea how hard it is to find a dust shop open this late?\"")

Roman's role in this operation is to be as conspicuous and attention-seeking as possible.

He's essentially the public face of the operation, tasked with stealing as much Dust as he can get his hands on while making absolutely sure that people notice him doing it.

This accomplishes several things simultaneously.

For starters, Roman's thefts steadily deplete the Dust reserves that would otherwise be distributed between the White Fang and Cinder's own operations.

Every shipment stolen is another resource that can be redirected toward their larger objectives.

However, the thefts themselves are only one part of the equation.

More importantly, Roman's activities create an atmosphere of instability throughout Vale.

The repeated Dust robberies, combined with the increasingly aggressive activities of the White Fang, gradually undermine the public's sense of security.

People begin questioning the effectiveness of Vale's authorities, and distrust starts developing between the general population, the government, and the various organizations operating throughout the kingdom.

This is beneficial to the White Fang.

![img](jfj5gbyqlazh1)

As tensions continue to rise, the organization has an increasingly effective environment in which to recruit and radicalize more individuals.

The growing hostility toward the authorities and the general instability surrounding Vale provide further opportunities for the White Fang to bolster its numbers and strengthen its influence.

![img](wzyhr9vslazh1 "\"We're gonna need more men...\"")

In other words, he's creating the circumstances that allow the rest of Cinder's operation to flourish.

![img](egktydqvlazh1)

And while everyone is busy looking for the criminal responsible for the increasingly brazen robberies, Cinder remains comfortably in the background, allowing the pieces to fall into place without drawing unnecessary attention to herself.

Leading into Volume 2, which marks the point at which Cinder begins taking more deliberate steps toward executing her plan.

By this point, the necessary circumstances have finally presented themselves.

![img](daf44moylazh1)

Ironwood has arrived in Vale alongside his military forces, bringing with him an entire fleet of airships, advanced weaponry, and an extensive collection of Atlesian robots and androids.

Meanwhile, the transfer students are beginning to enter Beacon Academy in preparation for the upcoming Vytal Festival.

From Cinder's perspective, this is the perfect opportunity.

The combination of an international event, an influx of students from different kingdoms, an increased military presence, and the enormous amount of security required to oversee everything creates an absolute nightmare for Beacon's security infrastructure.

There are simply too many people, too many moving pieces, and too many potential security vulnerabilities for anyone to effectively monitor everything happening within the academy.

And Cinder takes full advantage of that.

![img](u2bmmmn7mazh1 "Grown ahh woman btw ")

With the Vytal Festival providing the perfect cover, she manages to infiltrate Beacon alongside Mercury and Emerald, slipping into the academy while keeping her actual intentions hidden from everyone around her.

And she wastes very little time putting her subordinates to work.

Mercury is immediately assigned the task of analyzing the most powerful fighters attending Beacon.

* His objective is to observe their fighting styles, assess their capabilities, and, most importantly, determine the nature of their Semblances ahead of the tournament.

This is an incredibly important part of the operation.

After all, Cinder isn't simply preparing for a conventional battle.

She's preparing for an attack that will eventually involve some of the strongest Huntsmen and Huntresses in Remnant.

Having information about their abilities beforehand provides an enormous tactical advantage.

Knowing what an opponent can do before actually having to fight them allows Cinder's forces to prepare appropriate countermeasures, identify potential weaknesses, and avoid being caught off guard by abilities they weren't expecting.

Mercury essentially serves as their reconnaissance specialist, gathering information on the academy's strongest combatants while remaining inconspicuous enough to avoid attracting suspicion.

Emerald, meanwhile, takes a considerably different approach.

* Rather than focusing primarily on combat analysis, she plays the role of a social butterfly, making connections with the other students and getting on good terms with various teams.

By establishing friendships and becoming involved in the social environment surrounding the tournament, Emerald can discreetly gather information about the participating teams.

In particular, she learns which fighters are being selected to represent their respective teams in the upcoming matches.

This gives Cinder's group another avenue of information gathering.

While Mercury is observing their opponents from a combat perspective, Emerald is gathering information through social interaction.

![img](uy33r5xamazh1)

Together, the two provide Cinder with a considerably better understanding of the people who will eventually be involved in the events surrounding the Fall of Beacon.

And then there's Neo.

Who was busy helping Roman and only really appeared during the first episode of Volume 3, so people wouldn't start wondering who the fourth person on Cinder's team was.

Jokes aside, Neo's involvement is largely separate from the infiltration taking place within Beacon itself.

While Cinder, Mercury, and Emerald are handling their respective responsibilities inside the academy, Roman and Adam are preparing for the larger events that will eventually unfold.

At this point, the operation inside Beacon is predominantly a three-person mission.

Cinder is coordinating the operation, Mercury is gathering combat intelligence, and Emerald is establishing social connections and collecting information from the other students.

Each member has a distinct responsibility, and all three are contributing toward the same overarching objective.

And this brings us to another particularly interesting aspect of Cinder's infiltration.

The school dance.

At first glance, the school dance episodes might seem like relatively harmless downtime between the more important action sequences of Volume 2.

They're primarily concerned with the students' personal relationships, social interactions, and romantic interests.

Compared to the larger developments surrounding the White Fang and the impending Breach, they might initially appear to have very little relevance to the actual plot.

However, that's not really the case.

In fact, these episodes are made considerably more important by what Cinder is accomplishing behind the scenes.

![img](48248dfdmazh1)

While everyone else is occupied with the dance, Cinder manages to infiltrate the CCT and plant Watts' Queen Virus.

This is arguably one of the most important steps in her entire operation.

The virus establishes the means through which Cinder will eventually compromise Atlas' military technology and turn its own weapons against the people they're supposed to protect.

And the timing is particularly important.

Cinder is essentially using the chaos and distractions surrounding the Vytal Festival to prepare the technological component of her plan while simultaneously allowing Mercury and Emerald to continue gathering information.

The dance episodes also give us a clearer understanding of how those two are contributing to the operation.

Mercury and Emerald aren't simply hanging around Beacon pretending to be ordinary students.

Their interactions are part of a coordinated intelligence-gathering effort, with both of them using their positions to acquire information that would otherwise be difficult to obtain.

Meanwhile, Cinder is working on something considerably more consequential.

![img](wdttht8gmazh1)

She's establishing the infrastructure necessary to compromise Atlas' military forces before the actual attack even begins.

When you consider everything happening simultaneously, it's rather impressive how much groundwork Cinder manages to establish without anyone realizing what she's doing.

By this point, between the White Fang's involvement, the intelligence gathered by Mercury and Emerald, and the Queen Virus being planted within the CCT, Cinder has already completed a substantial portion of the preparations required for the Fall of Beacon.

![img](e4g9qshjmazh1)

Now, something else worth discussing is Roman's actual role in the operation, particularly when considering how Cinder treats him throughout these events.

I think it's relatively safe to say that Cinder didn't particularly care whether Roman survived after the Breach.

His usefulness to the operation was never necessarily dependent on his continued survival.

If Roman happened to survive, then that was certainly beneficial. Cinder could take advantage of the situation, have Neo break him out, and put him back to work during the subsequent stages of the plan.

However, if he died, it wouldn't have fundamentally compromised the operation either.

Roman was deliberately disposable.

His primary responsibility was to attract attention, destabilize Vale, and provide Cinder with a convenient public enemy for the authorities to focus on while she worked behind the scenes.

And the Breach provided him with an opportunity to fulfill another particularly useful function.

![img](fh6g7mcmmazh1)

* By allowing himself to be captured during the incident, Roman effectively positioned himself inside Ironwood's flagship.

This is an incredibly convenient position for Cinder's operation.

After all, the Atlas military is eventually going to become one of the most important components of the Fall of Beacon.

Having someone capable of infiltrating the flagship from within provides another avenue through which Cinder can compromise the military's security.

And that's precisely where Neo comes in.

![img](bq85e9aomazh1 "\"It's so good to be back!!!\"")

During Volume 3, Neo infiltrates the airship, releases Roman, and helps seize control of the Atlas military fleet from the inside out.

In other words, Roman's capture wasn't a failure.

It was an opportunity that Cinder could exploit.

Whether he survived the Breach or not, his role had already served its purpose.

His capture simply gave them another potential opening to use against Ironwood and his forces.

This is what makes Roman's position within the overall scheme interesting.

He isn't the mastermind, nor is he an indispensable component of the operation.

He's a useful asset whose continued involvement is conditional on whether he can still provide something of value.

Cinder is perfectly willing to let him take the fall if it means protecting the larger operation.

Regardless, this brings us to the Breach itself.

![img](sucplx7smazh1)

The entire incident was ultimately a massive distraction, a calculated gambit that Cinder was perfectly willing to lose.

![img](hzer68lumazh1)

Even though team RWBY ended up jump-starting the events earlier than originally anticipated, the overall results were essentially the same.

The Breach was never supposed to be enough to destroy Beacon.

There was no realistic expectation that the attack alone would be sufficient to take down the academy, even if Cinder, Emerald, and Mercury had personally joined the fighting.

Their objective was never simply to overwhelm Beacon through sheer numbers.

Instead, the Breach was intended to accomplish something considerably more important: undermine the public's confidence in Ozpin's leadership.

Think about the circumstances surrounding the incident.

The Vytal Festival is approaching, bringing together students, officials, and spectators from across Remnant.

Beacon is supposed to be one of the safest and most prestigious academies in the world, with Ozpin serving as its headmaster.

Yet, under his watch, a massive terrorist attack manages to reach the very heart of Vale.

The Breach exposes serious vulnerabilities in the kingdom's security, and Ozpin is left looking completely incapable of preventing or adequately responding to the situation.

It doesn't matter that Beacon ultimately manages to repel the attack.

The damage to public confidence has already been done.

People begin questioning Ozpin's ability to protect them.

The authorities are forced to reconsider their security arrangements, and the growing distrust surrounding Vale creates an opening for other political and military figures to step in.

![img](p4bpisiymazh1)

Most importantly, the council begins granting James Ironwood additional privileges and placing greater emphasis on the Atlas military as a means of maintaining security.

And this is exactly what Cinder wants.

Because the more influence Ironwood gains, the more control Cinder's operation can eventually exert over the circumstances surrounding the Fall of Beacon.

The increased military presence, the additional androids, and the growing dependence on Atlas' technological superiority all contribute to creating the conditions necessary for her next move.

Remember, the Queen Virus has already been planted.

Cinder doesn't need to manufacture an entirely new military threat.

She simply needs to ensure that the military forces already present are positioned in the right place at the right time.

And when the Fall (Heh..) finally begins, those same forces can be turned against the very people they're supposed to protect.

The more androids stationed throughout Vale on the night of the Fall, the more opportunities there are for the virus to wreak havoc.

The more Atlesian mechs deployed throughout the kingdom, the more weapons can be turned against civilians.

And the more footage there is of Atlas military technology firing upon innocent people, the more devastating the resulting public reaction will be.

The public doesn't know about the Queen Virus.

They don't know that someone has deliberately compromised the military's systems.

All they see is Atlas' machines attacking their people.

And that's precisely the image Cinder wants to create.

The Breach was never Cinder's endgame.

It was simply Act 2.

![img](dsyewy91nazh1)

Now, this leads into the next part of Cinder's overall scheme: the isolation of Atlas from the rest of the world.

Everything that happened previously was essentially groundwork for this particular development.

![img](2le9th63nazh1)

Roman's activities helped destabilize Vale and create distrust in its authorities.

The Breach further undermined Ozpin's leadership and encouraged the council to place greater reliance on Ironwood's military forces. Meanwhile, Cinder had already infiltrated Beacon and planted the Queen Virus, giving her the means to compromise Atlas' technological superiority when the time came.

All of these developments eventually converge during the Fall of Beacon.

When the Queen Virus is activated, Atlas' military technology is weaponized against Vale.

The very machines that were supposed to protect the kingdom become instruments of destruction, with Atlesian androids and military equipment turning against civilians.

And the consequences extend far beyond the immediate destruction.

The incident effectively destroys the international trust that the rest of Remnant has in Atlas.

Consider how this looks from the perspective of the other kingdoms.

1. Atlas is one of the most technologically advanced military powers in the world.
2. Its military forces are supposed to represent security, stability, and protection.
3. Yet, during an international festival intended to promote cooperation between the kingdoms, Atlas' own military technology is seen attacking innocent people.

Regardless of the actual circumstances, the resulting public perception is disastrous.

* The other kingdoms have little reason to immediately trust Atlas' explanations, especially when the destruction is occurring in front of them.
* The incident creates an enormous political and diplomatic problem for Ironwood, who is now left trying to justify the actions of a military that has seemingly turned against the very people it was supposed to protect.

And this is where Cinder's plan begins producing another consequence.

Ironwood starts spiraling.

![img](u0f5sg47nazh1 "\"Something is happening, and I don't trust LEO to handle it...\"")

With his military's reputation in ruins, the destruction of Beacon, and the increasing uncertainty surrounding the state of Remnant, Ironwood becomes increasingly paranoid about the possibility of another attack.

* He no longer has the same confidence in the international system that he once did.
* His trust in the other kingdoms deteriorates, and his willingness to cooperate with them becomes increasingly limited.

Eventually, this paranoia contributes to his decision to close Atlas' borders, recall military forces, and impose an embargo on Dust exports.

Each of these decisions further isolates Atlas from the rest of the world.

Closing the borders limits outside access and interaction. Recalling military forces removes Atlas' presence from other kingdoms, while the Dust embargo disrupts an important part of Remnant's international economy.

In effect, Atlas begins withdrawing from the very international system that Cinder has spent so much time destabilizing.

And the most important part?

Ironwood is operating almost entirely in the dark.

* He doesn't have the information necessary to understand what is actually happening behind the scenes.
* He doesn't know the full extent of Cinder's operation.
* He's left trying to make sense of a situation in which the world seems increasingly hostile, his military has lost the public's trust, and the possibility of another attack appears to be constantly looming over him.

Without the necessary information, his paranoia has nowhere to go but further inward.

He becomes increasingly isolated, increasingly distrustful, and increasingly convinced that he needs to take matters into his own hands.

Which, of course, is exactly where Cinder needs him to be after the Fall of Beacon.

Because at this point, Ironwood is a man whose decisions are becoming progressively easier to anticipate and exploit.

Cinder doesn't necessarily need to control every action he takes.

She has already helped create the circumstances that will push him toward making those decisions himself.

And that's what makes this stage of her plan so effective.

* She's turning Atlas against the rest of the world, while simultaneously allowing Ironwood's own fear and paranoia to do much of the work for her.
* By the time the consequences of the Fall have fully settled in, the international cooperation that once existed between the kingdoms has been severely damaged, Atlas has withdrawn into itself, and Ironwood is left increasingly disconnected from the people and institutions that could otherwise provide him with perspective.
* Ironwood, unknowingly, has been placed in precisely the position Cinder wanted him to occupy.
