The Ethical Execution of Hope (Cinder's Manipulation of Ozpin)

The Invincible Girl, the champion, the hero, and the face of the Vytal Festival has seemingly murdered another competitor in front of an international audience.

![img](svlfe5zablzh1)

And Cinder couldn't have asked for a more effective opening.

She has taken one of the most beloved figures associated with Beacon and turned her into the apparent perpetrator of a horrific act of violence.

The image of Pyrrha's heroism is now inseparable from the image of Penny's destruction.

And, more importantly, the entire incident happens during an international event that was supposed to represent unity between the kingdoms.

Cinder has effectively turned the Vytal Festival into the stage for the beginning of Remnant's collapse.

Once the Fall kicks off, everything goes straight to hell in a handbasket.

The Atlesian mechs go rogue, Adam's White Fang brings Grimm into Vale, and an enormous army of Grimm begins charging toward the kingdom.

![img](t0bhv6sdblzh1 "Royally...")

![img](a25e9asdblzh1 "FUCKED!!!")

Cinder cuts into the broadcast, showing footage of Atlesian mechs firing on civilians while delivering a monologue that drives home just how completely screwed everyone is.

![img](5tgc0gzhblzh1 "\"Who can you trust?\"")

The Huntsmen are scattered, Beacon is overwhelmed, and the Atlas fleet begins firing on itself thanks to Roman and Neo's infiltration.

Everything that Cinder has been preparing finally comes together.

The military forces are compromised, the White Fang is attacking, the Grimm are invading, and the international audience is witnessing the destruction of one of the most prestigious academies in Remnant.

And all of this is happening while the world is watching.

Which brings us to the final stage of Cinder's plan against Ozpin.

![img](uoootgymblzh1)

With the other layers of deception in place and the Fall of Beacon reaching its climax, Cinder can finally move toward her actual objective: obtaining the Fall Maiden's powers and securing the Relic of Choice. (Honestly, that second one was—and—is debatable in my opinion—seeing as Cinder has mostly only cared about the Maiden Powers.)

However, there's one particularly important obstacle standing between her and that objective.

Ozpin.

And Cinder needs to get him out of the way.

More specifically, she needs him away from the CCT and the location of the Maiden's powers.

Cinder needed Ozpin to come out onto the battlefield.

Why?

Because doing so would leave the CCT effectively unguarded, giving her an opportunity to reach Amber and complete the transfer of the Fall Maiden's powers without having to deal with Ozpin interfering.

This is where the entire situation becomes fun.

Ozpin is essentially forced into a dilemma.

1. On one side, he has the people he has sworn to protect: the citizens of Vale, the Huntsmen fighting throughout the city, and the students at Beacon.

These are people he genuinely cares about, people he sees not merely as soldiers or pieces on a chessboard, but as individuals whose lives and futures matter.

* On the other side, there's Amber.

![img](w2pm8kj4flzh1)

* 2. A sickly, dying woman whose remaining life is tied to the Maiden powers, and whose safety is absolutely essential to preventing Cinder from obtaining them.

From a purely strategic perspective, the logical option would be to double down on protecting Amber and the Relic.

After all, a few additional casualties, however tragic, cannot reasonably compare to the potential destruction Salem could unleash if Cinder successfully obtains the Fall Maiden's powers and the Relic.

The consequences of allowing that to happen are potentially catastrophic.

Ozpin knows this.

![img](o2x1l4d1clzh1)

He understands exactly what's at stake, and he knows that the enemies attacking Beacon aren't simply interested in causing destruction for its own sake.

They're coming for something specific.

Yet Cinder knows something about Ozpin that makes this situation considerably easier to manipulate.

She knows which option he's going to choose.

Because no matter how many times it puts him at a disadvantage, no matter how many times his compassion interferes with his larger objectives, Ozpin will always prioritize the people he has sworn to protect.

And Cinder knows that.

She knows that Ozpin cannot simply sit back and allow the people of Vale to die while he remains safely inside the CCT protecting Amber.

He has spent his life trying to protect humanity, and that responsibility isn't something he can easily abandon, even when doing so would be the strategically superior decision.

In fact, that's precisely what makes him so predictable in this particular situation.

Cinder doesn't need to convince Ozpin to make a strategically sound decision.

She needs to put him in a position where making the morally correct decision, according to his own principles, becomes the very thing that compromises his mission.

And that's exactly what happens.

![img](uxwhqqp5clzh1)

As the situation deteriorates, Glynda and Qrow approach Ozpin, looking for direction amid the chaos.

They're understandably concerned.

The academy is under attack, Grimm are pouring into Vale, the military is compromised (Not that they know yet anyway at that point), and they have no idea how many enemies are actually involved or how powerful the opposition might be.

They know their responsibilities.

They know that the enemy is coming for the vault, and they understand the importance of protecting what lies beneath Beacon.

Yet Ozpin makes his decision.

![img](g3xkizycclzh1)

He sends them into the city.

Rather than keeping Glynda and Qrow alongside him to reinforce the protection of Amber and the Relic, he chooses to have them assist the people fighting throughout Vale.

And he goes alone.

This is arguably one of the most important decisions Ozpin makes during the entire operation.

Because, from a purely tactical perspective, it's an incredibly questionable move.

He's leaving himself without the support of two of his most capable allies while the enemy is actively advancing toward his position.

![img](v73zgbgiclzh1)

He doesn't know the full extent of the opposition, he doesn't know how many combatants are involved, and he has every reason to suspect that someone particularly dangerous is coming for the vault.

Yet he still chooses to separate his forces.

Why?

Because Ozpin cannot bring himself to prioritize the security of the Relic and the Maiden over the immediate safety of the people outside.

He knows that people are dying.

He knows that Huntsmen and Huntresses are fighting desperately to protect the kingdom, and he knows that the situation will only become worse if someone doesn't intervene.

And so he makes the decision that he has repeatedly made throughout his life.

He puts his people first.

Even if it means sacrificing his own safety.

Even if it means leaving the most important defensive position vulnerable.

Even if it means walking directly into a confrontation that he might not survive.

And the particularly interesting part is that all three of them understand what this decision means.

Glynda and Qrow know the risks.

Ozpin knows the risks.

And Cinder is counting on those risks being taken.

As an aside, look at their faces during this exchange.

![img](gfbhbrxnclzh1)

There's an understanding between them that goes beyond simply deciding where everyone should go.

They're aware of the severity of the situation, and they understand the consequences of separating their forces.

Yet Ozpin still makes the choice.

The logical option would have been to double down on protecting Amber and the Relic.

The potential consequences of allowing Salem to obtain the Maiden powers and the Relic are simply too enormous to justify weakening their defenses.

But Ozpin isn't approaching this situation as someone who can comfortably sacrifice a handful of people for the sake of a greater objective.

He's approaching it as someone who genuinely feels responsible for every life being lost outside.

Cinder understands that Ozpin's greatest strength is also one of his most exploitable weaknesses.

His compassion makes him a leader people can trust, someone willing to put himself in danger for the sake of others.

But it also makes him predictable.

Cinder knows that, when forced to choose between protecting his people and protecting the larger objective, Ozpin will struggle to abandon the former.

And she builds her final move around that knowledge.

![img](1ug4wujtclzh1)

With Ozpin now separated from his allies, Cinder has the opening she needs.

The CCT is no longer being protected by the same concentration of forces that would otherwise make reaching Amber considerably more difficult.

And more importantly, Ozpin is no longer in a position to immediately intervene.

Cinder has effectively maneuvered him into removing himself from the very location she needs to access.

This is where all the previous stages of her operation finally pay off.

The infiltration of Beacon, the gathering of intelligence, the planting of the Queen Virus, the manipulation of the Vytal Festival, and the coordinated attack on Vale have all contributed to creating this particular opportunity.

And now she's finally in a position to claim the Fall Maiden's powers.

![img](72agc35zclzh1)

Even better, she's managed to separate Ozpin from the Maiden without needing to defeat him beforehand.

She has effectively used his own priorities to create the opening.

![img](3bdrmt21dlzh1)

And with that arrow (heh), Cinder wins.

She's obtained the full Fall Maiden powerset, Ozpin is alone, and his decision to prioritize the people of Vale has left him without Qrow and Glynda to support him or protect Amber.

The only thing standing between Cinder and the next part of her operation is Ozpin himself.

![img](rovsd0sddlzh1)

And she makes short work of him.

Even with all of his experience, Ozpin is ultimately overwhelmed. Cinder brings the full force of her newly acquired Full Maiden powers to bear, immolating him in their confrontation.

It's a rather brutal conclusion to the encounter.

And it also reinforces just how thoroughly Cinder has managed to manipulate the circumstances leading up to this moment. (Also exactly how royally FUCKED everyone was.)

She didn't simply overpower Ozpin through superior strength.

* She created a situation in which he was separated from his allies, forced to divide his attention between multiple crises, and ultimately left to confront her without the support he would otherwise have had.

Of course, Cinder later interprets Ozpin's decision as arrogance.

But Salem knows better.

Because Ozpin's decision wasn't necessarily born from an inflated sense of his own abilities or a belief that he could handle everything himself.

It was born from something considerably more fundamental to his character.

He couldn't bring himself to abandon the people who needed him.

And that is precisely what Cinder exploited.

* She understood what he would choose, created a situation in which he would make that choice, and turned his greatest virtue into the opening that allowed her to win.
