The Vytal Festival Curtain Call (Cinder's Manipulation of The Vytal Festival)

The combination of an international event, an influx of students from different kingdoms, an increased military presence, and the enormous amount of security required to oversee everything creates an absolute nightmare for Beacon's security infrastructure.

There are simply too many people, too many moving pieces, and too many potential security vulnerabilities for anyone to effectively monitor everything happening within the academy.

And Cinder takes full advantage of that.

With the Vytal Festival providing the perfect cover, she manages to infiltrate Beacon alongside Mercury and Emerald, slipping into the academy while keeping her actual intentions hidden from everyone around her.

And she wastes very little time putting her subordinates to work.

![img](46xhhto48dzh1 "\"I pickkkkk...her.\"")

Mercury is immediately assigned the task of analyzing the most powerful fighters attending Beacon.

His objective is to observe their fighting styles, assess their capabilities, and, most importantly, determine the nature of their Semblances ahead of the tournament.

![img](9afpmxof8dzh1)

This is an incredibly important part of the operation.

After all, Cinder isn't simply preparing for a conventional battle.

She's preparing for an attack that will eventually involve some of the strongest Huntsmen and Huntresses in Remnant.

Having information about their abilities beforehand provides an enormous tactical advantage.

Knowing what an opponent can do before actually having to fight them allows Cinder's forces to prepare appropriate countermeasures, identify potential weaknesses, and avoid being caught off guard by abilities they weren't expecting.

Mercury essentially serves as their reconnaissance specialist, gathering information on the academy's strongest combatants while remaining inconspicuous enough to avoid attracting suspicion.

Emerald, meanwhile, takes a considerably different approach.

![img](5n9i9n2l8dzh1)

Rather than focusing primarily on combat analysis, she plays the role of a social butterfly, making connections with the other students and getting on good terms with various teams.

By establishing friendships and becoming involved in the social environment surrounding the tournament, Emerald can discreetly gather information about the participating teams.

![img](8dhpvaho8dzh1)

In particular, she learns which fighters are being selected to represent their respective teams in the upcoming matches.

This gives Cinder's group another avenue of information gathering.

While Mercury is observing their opponents from a combat perspective, Emerald is gathering information through social interaction.

Together, the two provide Cinder with a considerably better understanding of the people who will eventually be involved in the events surrounding the Fall of Beacon.

Meanwhile, Cinder herself is working on the more important aspects of the operation, while Roman and Adam are preparing for the larger events that will eventually unfold.

And this brings us to another particularly interesting aspect of Cinder's infiltration.

![img](e8ixp3jt8dzh1)

The school dance.

At first glance, the school dance episodes might seem like relatively harmless downtime between the more important action sequences of Volume 2.

They're primarily concerned with the students' personal relationships, social interactions, and romantic interests.

Compared to the larger developments surrounding the White Fang and the impending Breach, they might initially appear to have very little relevance to the actual plot.

However, that's not really the case.

In fact, these episodes are made considerably more important by what Cinder is accomplishing behind the scenes.

![img](if4zyh3w8dzh1)

While everyone else is occupied with the dance, Cinder manages to infiltrate the CCT and plant Watts' Queen Virus.

And with that accomplished, Cinder has essentially put one of the most important pieces of her operation into place.

She has her virus, she has her intelligence, and she has her infiltrators positioned exactly where she needs them.

At this point, she basically just needs to orchestrate one more major incident before she can sit back and enjoy the fireworks.

And that brings us to the actual centerpiece of her plan: the Vytal Festival finals.

![img](vmnx46h29dzh1)

With her infiltration of Beacon complete, her intelligence gathered, and the Queen Virus successfully planted, Cinder has the necessary groundwork in place to begin manipulating the tournament itself.

And what makes this better is that she doesn't necessarily need to control every individual involved.

She simply needs to identify the right people, put them in the right positions, and create circumstances in which their actions will serve her purposes.

Cinder essentially has three roles prepared for the opening act of the Fall of Beacon:

The Assaulter, the Murderer, and the Patsy.

Each serves a different purpose, and together they create the perfect scenario for turning the Vytal Festival into a worldwide catastrophe.

1. The Assaulter — Mercury Black

Cinder has her actor in Mercury.

![img](2by8gmhb9dzh1)

His responsibility is relatively straightforward: provide the audience with a convincing display of brutality and establish the idea that the tournament has become something considerably more dangerous than a friendly competition.

After watching the Vytal matches, it wouldn't have been particularly difficult for Cinder to identify someone with a short temper whose behavior could be manipulated into appearing genuinely malicious.

Yang was the perfect candidate.

She's an aggressive fighter, she's emotionally reactive when provoked, and her fighting style already revolves around absorbing punishment before returning it with considerably more force.

More importantly, Mercury could exploit her tendency to respond to provocation.

By deliberately antagonizing her and manipulating the circumstances surrounding their match, Emerald could create a situation in which Yang's reaction would appear completely disproportionate to the audience.

And that's precisely what happens.

![img](36osihmg9dzh1)

Mercury deliberately presents himself as the victim, making Yang's response appear like an act of excessive violence rather than the result of his own deception.

The tournament audience is left with an incident that damages the perception of Beacon's students and raises questions about the academy's ability to maintain control over its own competitors.

This is important because Cinder isn't merely trying to make the tournament entertaining.

She's establishing a narrative.

She's demonstrating that the supposedly prestigious Vytal Festival, an event intended to symbolize cooperation and unity between the kingdoms, can just as easily become a stage for brutality.

And Mercury is the person she uses to establish that narrative.

2. The Murderer — Pyrrha Nikos

Now, this is where things become considerably more interesting.

Cinder's second piece is Pyrrha Nikos.

And unlike Mercury's role, which could potentially have been assigned to several different individuals, Pyrrha was arguably the most important person to Cinder's intended performance.

Cinder identified Pyrrha as a potential candidate for the next Fall Maiden relatively early in the operation.

After all, Pyrrha isn't simply another talented student attending Beacon.

She's the Invincible Girl.

She's an internationally recognized champion, an exceptionally accomplished Huntress-in-training, and someone whose reputation precedes her wherever she goes.

She's practically the perfect symbol of everything the Vytal Festival is supposed to represent.

And that's precisely why Cinder wants her.

If she can turn someone like Pyrrha into the apparent murderer of another competitor in front of an international audience, the resulting damage to the public's perception of Beacon would be enormous.

Think about it.

Pyrrha is the person everyone expects to behave honorably.

She's the champion, the hero, and the girl who has built her entire public image around being an exemplary fighter.

She's the kind of person people wouldn't expect to commit an act of genuine brutality.

She's even featured on the covers of Pumpkin Pete's Marshmallow Flakes boxes.

And Cinder wants to take that image and tear it down for the entire world to see.

There is also another important detail regarding Pyrrha's potential selection as the next Fall Maiden.

If Cinder hadn't already considered Pyrrha a candidate, seeing her enter the CCT alongside Ozpin would have been a rather significant indication that something was happening behind the scenes.

It would have been an enormous red flag.

Cinder was already interested in the Maiden powers, and Pyrrha's proximity to Ozpin, combined with her extraordinary combat abilities, would have made her someone worth investigating.

And once Cinder identified her as a potential candidate, she had another reason to make her the centerpiece of the tournament's final act.

She was looking for someone whose involvement would make the resulting incident as devastating as possible.

Pyrrha was practically tailor-made for that role.

3. The Patsy — The Victim

The final piece is the person who gets killed.

And this is where Penny Polendina comes into play.

Cinder needed someone whose death could be used to create the appearance of a genuine murder during the tournament finals.

Penny was an excellent candidate.

Her robotic nature made her particularly vulnerable to Pyrrha's Polarity Semblance, which allowed Cinder to construct a scenario in which Pyrrha could seemingly tear her apart in front of an entire audience.

However, Penny being the ideal target doesn't necessarily mean she was the only possible target.

Cinder wasn't dependent on a specific victim.

She needed someone whose death could be presented as the result of a deliberate act of violence committed by Pyrrha.

Penny simply happened to be an exceptionally convenient choice.

Her mechanical construction gave Pyrrha's Semblance an obvious means of causing catastrophic damage, and the resulting spectacle would be considerably more disturbing than an ordinary tournament injury.

Cinder had Mercury and Emerald gathering information about the participating fighters and their respective teams.

Once Cinder knew which competitors were advancing, she could begin adjusting her plans around the available candidates.

Hell, in an alternate timeline, who's to say Ruby wasn't the one being gutted on Pyrrha's spear during the Vytal Festival finals?

The point is that Cinder had the information necessary to work with the competitors who were actually going to be present.

4. Pyrrha's Semblance — Why Penny Was the Perfect Target

Now, there are a few additional details worth discussing regarding why Penny was such an effective choice.

For starters, Pyrrha's Polarity Semblance made her particularly dangerous against someone with a mechanical construction.

Her ability to manipulate metal gave Cinder an extremely convenient way to manufacture the circumstances surrounding Penny's death.

However, the tournament itself also provides some interesting opportunities for Cinder's manipulation.

Consider the JNPR versus BRNZ match.

JNPR is placed against a team that includes at least one fighter with an electrical weapon, in an environment that literally generates thunderstorms.

That is a convenient combination when considering Nora's Semblance.

The electrical energy available in the environment, combined with the potential for her to absorb electricity, creates an opportunity for Nora to become significantly more dangerous during the match.

And this is worth considering alongside the SN versus NP match that was cut for time.

Neptune's weapon could potentially have provided another means of supercharging Nora, meaning that the matchup might have produced a similar result, particularly considering the water-based environment involved.

These details illustrate just how much the tournament's individual matchups could contribute to the circumstances surrounding Cinder's plan.

The more information she gathers, the more opportunities she has to identify useful interactions between the competitors, their weapons, their Semblances, and the environments in which they're fighting.

And Penny is arguably the most obvious example of this.

Her mechanical nature makes her an exceptionally convenient target for Pyrrha.

![img](g4jsepbt9dzh1)

In fact, Cinder's reaction upon discovering Penny's blueprints provides further evidence for the idea that Penny's identity as an android influenced her decision-making.

It tells us that she wasn't committed to using Penny from the beginning.

![img](nm41hca1adzh1)

Rather, once she discovered what Penny actually was, she recognized an opportunity to use her as the victim in the tournament's final act.

Had she not discovered Penny's mechanical construction, Cinder could have simply selected someone else.

The overall objective wouldn't have changed.

She still needed Pyrrha to kill someone in front of the world.

![img](rxocsixx9dzh1)

Penny simply made that objective considerably easier to accomplish.

And this brings us to the actual Fall of Beacon.

Once the tournament finals arrive, Cinder finally puts all three pieces into motion.

The audience has already witnessed Mercury's supposed victimization at the hands of Yang, establishing an atmosphere in which the tournament's competitors are beginning to appear increasingly violent.

Then Pyrrha enters the picture.

After everything that's happened over the preceding days, she's already in an incredibly fragile mental state.

She's been pressured by Ozpin, confronted with the possibility of becoming the next Fall Maiden, and forced to grapple with the enormous responsibility that comes with such a position.

She's also been placed in a situation where the future she had imagined for herself is beginning to collapse.

And Cinder takes advantage of that.

Emerald's Semblance provides the final ingredient.

![img](l8r5ok38adzh1)

Through her illusions, she manipulates Pyrrha's perception of the situation, making the events unfolding around her appear completely different from reality.

The result is a disaster.

Pyrrha believes she's about to be attacked by more weapons than there actually are.

![img](f8tlwhqaadzh1)

Her Polarity Semblance tears Penny apart, and the entire incident is broadcast to the world.

The Invincible Girl, the champion, the hero, and the face of the Vytal Festival has seemingly murdered another competitor in front of an international audience.

![img](dzivzj6madzh1)

And Cinder couldn't have asked for a more effective opening.

She has taken one of the most beloved figures associated with Beacon and turned her into the apparent perpetrator of a horrific act of violence.

The image of Pyrrha's heroism is now inseparable from the image of Penny's destruction.

And, more importantly, the entire incident happens during an international event that was supposed to represent unity between the kingdoms.

Cinder has effectively turned the Vytal Festival into the stage for the beginning of Remnant's collapse.

Once the Fall kicks off, everything goes straight to hell in a handbasket.

The Atlesian mechs go rogue, Adam's White Fang brings Grimm into Vale, and an enormous army of Grimm begins charging toward the kingdom.

Cinder cuts into the broadcast, showing footage of Atlesian mechs firing on civilians while delivering a monologue that drives home just how completely screwed everyone is.

![img](apdapmcqadzh1)

The Huntsmen are scattered, Beacon is overwhelmed, and the Atlas fleet begins firing on itself thanks to Roman and Neo's infiltration.

Everything that Cinder has been preparing finally comes together.

The military forces are compromised, the White Fang is attacking, the Grimm are invading, and the international audience is witnessing the destruction of one of the most prestigious academies in Remnant.

And all of this is happening while the world is watching.

She has essentially prepared everything for her masterstroke against Ozpin (Which I'll be making an analysis on later):

![img](vwl67q55bdzh1)

* The Assaulter: Someone who can manufacture a public spectacle of brutality and establish an atmosphere of violence surrounding the tournament.

![img](lld27egbbdzh1)

* The Murderer: Someone whose actions can destroy the public's perception of Beacon and turn an internationally recognized hero into an apparent criminal.

![img](d55uu8mrbdzh1)

* The Patsy: Someone whose death can be used to manufacture the incident that helps trigger the larger operation.
*
The Assaulter and Patsy are primarily functional roles.
*
Their identities can be adjusted according to the available competitors and circumstances.
*
The Murderer, however, is different. Cinder wanted Pyrrha. Specifically, Pyrrha.

* She was the Invincible Girl, the greatest Huntress of her generation, an internationally recognized champion, and a hero in the making.

There is a certain dramatic irony in having someone whose entire identity revolves around being an exemplary hero become the apparent perpetrator of the very incident that helps bring about the destruction of Beacon.

And Cinder absolutely loves that kind of dramatic irony.

* She wanted the world to watch a hero seemingly murder another competitor, destroying the image of the Vytal Festival and contributing to the collapse of the trust that held the kingdoms together.

* Penny was the perfect victim because of her mechanical construction and Pyrrha's Semblance, but the underlying objective was always bigger than her.

>Cinder wanted to turn Beacon's greatest symbol of heroism into the face of its greatest tragedy.

And by the time the world realized what had happened, Cinder had already moved on to the next stage of her plan.
