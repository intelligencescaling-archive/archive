The final confrontation.

So, the end of the season has arrived (perfect time to post my favorite intro). Now I will describe the final plan of Igor Samarin—aka "You Won't Catch Me"—to destroy Rodion Meglin and Yesenia Steklova. Let's go.

Let’s start with descriptions of previous events:

1. Samarin using Evgeny (Yesenia's husband) framed Meglin for the murder of Colonel Andrey Steklov, causing the police to hunt for him.

2. He used Evgeny, who kidnapped Yesenia’s daughter, killed Meglin’s friends, and repeatedly tried to kill Meglin himself, making Yeseniya and Meglin think that Yevgeny is YWCM.

3. After everything that had transpired, Samarin knew that Yesenia would have no choice but to kill Yevgeny during their final meeting. This led to her arrest and interrogation at the Pyatigorsk prison—an interrogation conducted by Samarin himself.

4. After the interrogation, Samarin deliberately allowed Yesenia to take him hostage and escape from prison to meet with Meglin and her daughter. This enabled Samarin to convince Bykov (Yesenia’s superior) of the danger posed by Yesenia and Meglin, thereby gaining a possible access to his original objective: the case regarding the murder of Meglin’s family.

Now let's move to the events of the final episodes:

Yesenia and Meglin framing + the abduction of Yesenia's daughter (yea again):

After Yesenia escaped from prison, Samarin needed to further convince Bykov of the danger posed by Meglin and Yesenia. To this end, he enlisted a pair of serial killers—theater actor Maxim Sokolov and his girlfriend, Zorya. Acting on Samarin's orders and using wigs, makeup, and costumes to impersonate Yesenia and Meglin, they committed a series of murders designed to frame the protagonists. They specifically mimicked Meglin’s past cases (for example, killing a pedophile on a bus using the exact same method Meglin had used to kill a serial killer in the second episode of the first season.)

Of course, these murders weren't committed solely to frame Meglin and Yesenia. Samarin needed a way to kidnap Yesenia’s daughter. To achieve this, he needed Meglin to meet up with his old friend Vitaly. (A bit of background: Vitaly was a friend of Meglin’s with hyperthymesia, a condition that allowed him to remember absolutely everything. He had already appeared in the first season; Meglin used Vitaly’s memory as an archive for all his cases. However, there were downsides: Vitaly’s memory worked in such a way that he constantly heard voices describing everything stored in his mind—voices that were often far from pleasant. As revealed in the show's final episode, Samarin had recruited Vitaly by helping him silence those voices while Meglin was still presumed dead.) I’ll now explain what the plan looks like, point by point:

1. He uses a previously recruited killer to frame Meglin. The killer has to be one of the past criminals Meglin arrested (which is precisely why Maxim Sokolov was chosen).

2. With the help of Sokolov and his girlfriend, he frames Meglin and Yesenia, thereby gaining access to the classified case file regarding the murder of Meglin's family.

3. Realizing that the murders committed by Sokolov and his girlfriend would attract Meglin’s attention and prompt an investigation, Samarin ordered Sokolov to leave a mobile phone at one of the crime scenes (which Meglin took with him, understanding that it had been left there specifically for them).

4. After contacting them by phone, Samarin declares that in order to put an end to everything, Meglin must "remember everything." This makes Yesenia realize she needs to go see Vitaly (who was recruited by Samarin a year ago—a fact she is, of course, unaware of).

5. From Vitaly, Meglin learns—first—about the cases and murders he handled while he was a detective, and—second—that the perpetrator is Maxim Sokolov, whom he had arrested previously. Thanks to this, he also recalls Sokolov’s address; he and Yesenia set off for the location, leaving the daughter with Vitaly and his mother.

6. Vitaly informs Samarin about this, and Samarin sends Sokolov to kidnap Yesenia’s daughter, killing Vitaly and his mother in the process.

When Yeseniya and Meglin arrived at Sokolov’s apartment, they found only his beaten girlfriend (who had refused to go any further with him because he intended to harm a child) and realized they had been deceived. However, upon returning to Vitaly’s house, they discovered only his dead body. Samarin then called them again, stating that they needed to go to the place where it all began and that Meglin would remember what that place was.

The destruction of Meglin’s psyche

Now, I want to explain what Samarin meant, as it relates to the case of the murder of Meglin’s family. While reviewing the case files and video footage from Meglin’s recent interrogations, he realized two important things. First, the murder of Meglin’s family involves more than meets the eye, and the theory that an intruder broke into their home is incorrect. And second, Meglin suffers from a split personality (mentioned it in one of the posts), and his alter ego harbors all of Meglin’s own traumatic memories. By the phrase "the place where it all began," Samarin was referring to Meglin's childhood home—where his family had been murdered—knowing that Meglin, in his current state, would remember it and come there.

Just as Samarin had anticipated, Yesenia and Meglin arrived at the house where he finally revealed his true colors. Despite her initial attempts to beat him, Samarin simply reminded her that her daughter was in Sokolov’s hands and could be killed at any moment. Yesenia let go of Samarin; he first ordered Meglin to handcuff himself to the wall, and then Sokolov—having crept up behind Yesenia—knocked her out, leaving Meglin and Samarin alone together.

Samarin initiates a conversation, but he addresses not Meglin himself, but rather his alter ego—a version of Meglin as he was at age twelve (whom he simply calls "Rodya"). Feigning an understanding of Rodya’s pain over losing his parents—and claiming to have lost his own as well—Samarin gains the boy's trust. He then presses him to recall what actually happened on the day of the murder. Through this process, it is revealed that it was Meglin who killed his parents; he had intended to save his mother, who was being beaten by his father at the time, but while stabbing his father, he failed to notice his mother trying to intervene and inadvertently stabbed her as well. The entire story about an intruder breaking into the house turns out to be a fabrication of his mind, an attempt to shield himself from the horrific memories.

Samarin was overcome with triumph and a wild joy at finally forcing Meglin to confront those terrible memories. Convinced that Meglin was broken, Samarin attempted to recruit him, promising to help him cope with the pain of realizing he had killed his parents; he insisted that they were the ones who had made him who he was and that it was not his fault. At the same time, he began manipulating Yesenia—who had just regained consciousness—convincing her that Meglin was the root of all her troubles and that his very existence had led to the deaths of her mother, father, and other loved ones.

Samarin removed Meglin’s handcuffs and left him with a single knife. He announced that they were going to play a game: within five minutes, only one of them could remain alive—either Meglin would kill Yesenia, or she would kill him. However, if they tried to defy him, Yesenia’s daughter would die. Leaving them alone, he went to a spot nearby where Sokolov was waiting, and where they could use a tablet to watch Yesenia and Meglin fight for their lives.

Left with no other choice, they began to fight; during the struggle, Meglin gained the upper hand and stabbed Yesenia in the abdomen. Afterward, Meglin left the house and headed toward Samarin, who was already fully confident of his victory.

However, Samarin was overconfident, failing to realize that it wasn't over yet. Meglin hadn't actually killed Yesenia. During the scuffle, he had snapped his knife, shortening the blade so that when he struck Yesenia in the abdomen, it didn't damage any vital organs. To make the ruse convincing, he cut his own hand to create the appearance that Yesenia was bleeding. Furthermore, he deliberately positioned himself in front of Samarin to block his view of the tablet, allowing Yesenia to slip out of the house unnoticed; she then crept up on Sokolov and slit his throat. Samarin tried to get up, only for Meglin to stab him in the abdomen with a shard of glass he had picked up inside the house. That was the end for Samarin—and for the "You Won't Catch Me" case.

Phew, yea, never expected that such menacing character like Samarin would be so... dumb? Seriously outsmarting everyone in first season and achieving his goals, always being one step ahead, always stay in the shadows and leave not even the slightest hint that it was him—"You Won't Catch Me"—until he revealed it himself. Just to be crushed, because of his cockiness and being killed by piece of glass.... but perhaps that was the most fitting end for a villain like him. Anyway, bye for now.
