Lore/Background:

As a young girl, Dahlia had an extremely difficult upbringing, her father married her mother believing she’d become a powerful political figure in the Fey Clan, however when she lost that position to her sister, Dahlia’s father divorced her and took Dahlia and Iris.

Dahlia’s father remarried and thus she met her step sister- Valerie Hawthorne. Besides this, Dahlia found her twin Iris to be a nuisance and convinced her father to abandon her to the Hazakura Temple.

Over time, Dahlia would grow to loathe her father and begin concocting her plan to rob him.

Plot against her father/kidnapping plan/new identity:

At the age of 14, Dahlia had already begun her plan to rob her father. She got into a relationship with her tutor- Terry Fawles- and clearly having made him fall into an intensely deep love, made a pact with him- if the pair would ever reach a point where they could no longer trust each other, they would drink poison and die, this poison is kept in a bottle-necklace that appears as so:

(Yes, this is the very necklace Dahlia would later give to Phoenix Wright)

Following this the plan was incredibly simple, but also very ingenious. The sisters (taking advantage of Valerie’s role as a police officer) and Fawles were to stage a kidnapping, with Fawles being the kidnapper so that they could scapegoat him for the entire thing and demand a diamond worth $2 million. They selected “Dusky Bridge” as the location for the exchange, with its close proximity to the temple Iris had been left at as a child so that Dahlia could recruit her to assist in the plot.

When they met at the bridge, Valerie shot Terry in the shoulder and Dahlia intentionally leapt from the bridge taking the diamond with her- Valerie then arrested Terry and testified to him murdering Dahlia. As a result of this, Fawles was convicted and sentenced to death row- Iris never showed up to assist in the plan, however this has little to do with anything, and only serves for why Dahlia despises her in the final case of Trials & Tribulations.

Their plan had worked and Valerie- through her position as a police officer- forged Dahlia the necessary documents to swap her identity to “Melissa Foster”. Her new identity had allegedly lived in another country until only two years prior and after the deaths of her parents, had fled, but without her original ID papers.

Diego Armando’s “death” & Extras

This segment is for Dahlia’s attempted kill on Diego and also just some minor narrative statements that I want to mention.

As mentioned in Turnabout Beginnings, Dahlia is in fact a literature student at Ivy University.

Following the events of Turnabout Beginnings, Diego Armando attempts to investigate more into Dahlia. Six months later, Diego meets Dahlia at the cafeteria of the courthouse, where she slipped some poison (that she had stolen from Doug Swallow - her then boyfriend) into his coffee using her bottle necklace. Diego would end up going into a coma as a result of this, only eventually waking up and donning the mask of Godot, but that’s irrelevant for now.

Dahlia flees the cafeteria and meets Phoenix Wright at the reading room, giving him the bottle necklace and claiming that she had fallen in love with him to dispose of the evidence. As a result of the stealing of poison, Doug breaks up with her. At the time, Dahlia planned to kill Wright to take back the necklace, but Iris begged her not to and opted to take the necklace back for her, adopting her sister’s identity and attempting to get the necklace back herself.

Dahlia’s testimony here is deliberately weak and deliberately contradicts Phoenix's own testimony from earlier- she does this so that she can appear to be protecting Phoenix whilst throwing him under the bus all the while. Mia manages to catch onto this, but pretty much has to press forth anyway-

Of course, she’s correct and Phoenix is found innocent, later Dahlia is sentenced to death and hung.

Bridge to Turnabout:

In the previous Dahlia doc, I glossed over this segment and in my opinion, for good reason: Morgan Fey- Dahlia’s mother- is the true mastermind behind the revenge plot to kill Maya. Dahlia only decides to go along with it since she views it as a last effort to get back at Mia Fey after her death by killing her little sister- unfortunately said plan goes horrifically and Maya winds up being perfectly alright, ALTHOUGH it costs Misty Fey’s (Maya/Mia’s mother & Morgan’s sister) life at the hands of Godot (who’s attempting to prevent Morgan’s revenge plot from coming into fruition). As you can guess- Morgan’s revenge plot would be a feat for Morgan & naturally the counter strategy is a feat for Godot.

So what does Dahlia do here? Not much. When she is initially channeled by Misty Fey- according to Godot’s plan- which is in complete contrast to the original plan she believed she was following (to be channeled by Pearl- her younger maternal half-sister, Morgan’s daughter). Dahlia hunts down Maya and attempts to kill her, however loses consciousness after being stabbed- she assumes that Maya did this (it was in fact Godot from behind). Wanting to ensure she could destroy Maya, she writes “Maya” in blood before passing out.

She wakes up again later in Sacred Cavern and during an earthquake is able to swap places with her identical twin Iris- this allows her to pose as her in trial, in court she testified that she had tried to cover up the murder for Maya, placing her under heavy suspicion and almost pinning the murder of Misty on her.

Through typical Ace Attorney perseverance however, Wright eventually proves that “Iris” is in fact Dahlia Hawthorne, and later reveals that her final last ditch attempts had failed and that she had not in fact been channelled by Pearl, but rather Maya herself. As a result, Dahlia breaks down one final time, her spirit leaving Maya’s body having been exorcised.

(Note that this isn’t the end of the trial, Wright has to go on to establish who the real killer was- Godot, but it is the end of Dahlia’s involvement, so we’re finished here.)

(adaptability, perseverance, determination, improvisation, fabrication)
