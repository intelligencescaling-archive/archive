Lore:
Dahlia Hawthorne is a major antagonist in the third game in the Ace Attorney franchise - Trials & Tribulations. Dahlia initially presents herself as sweet and innocent, with her design reflecting her seemingly naïve personality. However, that couldn’t be farther from the truth. In reality, Hawthorne is a cutthroat monster, cruel and full of avarice, in truth only ever caring for herself and never anyone else.

Hawthorne is a member of the Fey clan, however she possesses little to no spiritual power (if she does have any it’s never used), she is Morgan Fey’s daughter - the branch that would fall to have practically no influence due to Morgan’s sister being a more skilled Spirit Medium. As a result of Morgan not being elected as the next head of the Fey clan, Hawthorne’s father divorces Morgan and leaves taking his daughters to remarry (Iris & Dahlia). Dahlia convinces her father to leave Iris at the Hazakura Temple because of her dislike towards her - it should be noted that Dahlia never liked her father either, plotting to steal his riches.

Kidnapping Plot:

At the age of 14, Dahlia was in a relationship with her tutor Terry Fawles, having made a pact that if the two couldn’t trust each other anymore they’d drink poison from a bottle-necklace (Dahlia never planned to follow through on this mind you) and alongside her step-sister Valerie, the three plotted to steal a diamond worth 2 million from her father. (Dahlia also attempts to recruit Iris to aid in the scam).

During the meeting on the bridge, Valerie and Dahlia began the next part of the plan by betraying Fawles; Valerie shot him in the shoulder, while Dahlia intentionally jumped off the bridge into Eagle River below, taking with her the ransom diamond. Valerie arrested Fawles and falsely testified that he had murdered Dahlia by pushing her off the bridge, leading to the betrayed man's conviction and a sentence on death row. (Iris never showed up to assist Dahlia in her plot, as she was too frightened to help).

Valerie had in truth found Dahlia and secretly looked after her. As a policewoman, Valerie was able to forge the documents necessary to create a new identity for her stepsister. The identity was that of "Melissa Foster", a girl who up until two years before lived in another country at the brink of civil war and was able to escape after her parents' death, but was unable to get her original ID papers. (Since Iris had refused to assist her in the plot, Dahlia viewed her twin as a traitor and backstabber).

Turnabout Beginnings (First half):

Case Summary (first half): In her first major trial, Mia Fey defended Terry Fawles, a death row inmate who escaped custody and was accused of murdering Detective Valerie Hawthorne, the same woman whose testimony had led to his original conviction.
Miles Edgeworth, also in his first trial as prosecutor, presented strong evidence: Fawles had arranged to meet Hawthorne at Dusky Bridge—the site of his first alleged crime—and her body was later found in the trunk of a stolen car, stabbed with a knife. A photo taken by an alleged eyewitness, Melissa Foster (Dahlia Hawthorne), appearing to show Fawles and Hawthorne together at the bridge.
Melissa Foster (Dahlia Hawthorne), claimed she had seen the entire altercation and that her camera had run out of film before she could capture the moment of violence. Her detailed testimony closely matched the evidence, and she even anticipated questions about her photos by noting the use of a camera timer. Her testimony initially aligned perfectly with Edgeworth’s case.
However, Mia Fey dismantled Foster’s account by exposing contradictions: Foster (Dahlia Hawthorne) couldn’t have seen the trunk due to a rock outcropping, yet described scratch marks on it. Fey argued this was only possible if Foster (Dahlia Hawthorne) herself had placed the body in the trunk. She revealed that Fawles wouldn’t have needed to force the trunk open since he had the car keys. Furthermore, she suggested the woman in the meeting photo was not Hawthorne but Foster in disguise—exploiting Fawles’s memory loss and his request for a white scarf for identification.
Fey’s repeated arguments and strong approach eventually leads to Foster “fainting” and requiring a break in court for her to recover.

Turnabout Beginnings (second half):
Mia Fey was confident she could prove Foster was the real murderer. However, with no clear motive, investigated further and uncovered a key truth: five years earlier, the alleged “kidnapping” was a staged plan between Terry Fawles and his then-girlfriend, Dahlia Hawthorne—Melissa Foster’s true identity. Dahlia, her sister Valerie (the murder victim), and Fawles had planned to extort a $2 million diamond from their jeweler father. But at Dusky Bridge, Valerie betrayed Fawles by shooting him, and Dahlia jumped into the river, faking her death. Fawles had been convicted of Dahlia’s “murder” and Valerie’s testimony had sealed his fate.
In court, Edgeworth claimed Foster had no motive and was merely a witness. Foster testified again, cleverly asserting she'd never been to the area before college and didn’t know anyone involved—yet she suspiciously referenced the white scarf, which wasn’t public knowledge. Fey used this to expose her knowledge of a confidential note between Valerie and Fawles, revealing her as Dahlia Hawthorne.
Dahlia took the stand and claimed Fawles had pushed her off the bridge, but Fey dismantled her testimony. She proved that due to the bridge’s structure and the circumstances—Fawles having been shot and under threat—Dahlia could not have been pushed. She must have jumped to fake her death and keep the diamond for herself. Dahlia’s carefully constructed lie was further undermined when Fey demonstrated that both sisters had planned the betrayal, and Dahlia had committed murder to protect her secret.
Fey called Fawles to testify. Still in love with Dahlia, he was torn and tried to protect her. But under pressure, he revealed he had retrieved a pendant containing poison during a 30-minute window—enough time for Dahlia to have committed the murder. As the truth came to light, Fawles admitted he had lost faith in Dahlia and due to his promise of if trust was broken, the betrayer would drink poison. He collapsed on the stand and died. This allowed Hawthorne to walk free due to Fawles untimely death.

Unfortunately for Diego, the very incident that'd ruin him occurs here, during a meeting with Dahlia six months after the trial, she poisons him which subsequently leaves the esteemed defence attorney in a coma. To avoid suspicion she fled and handed the necklace (containing the poison) to Phoenix Wright claiming it to be a “love gift”. (Her then boyfriend - Swallow - would later learn that she stole poison -not that she murdered anyone - and break up with her - eventually leading to Turnabout Memories).

Sadly for Phoenix who'd been completely swooned by the devil, she plotted to murder him and regain the necklace to remove all evidence. Iris (her sister) begs her not to and opts to pretend to be Dahlia so she can date Phoenix and regain the necklace without hurting him.

Bride to the Turnabout:

“You're as foolishly optimistic as ever, aren't you... my darling Feenie? Do you want to know the truth? Ever since we met... I've despised you. Your sniveling naïveté and your pathetic faith in other people” - Dahlia mocking Wright.

This is one of the longest trials in the series, the final case of Trials & Tribulations and the final downfall of Hawthorne. Due to this being a Hawthorne document, certain areas will be glossed over, full transcripts of the trial are available online.

The trial is an absolute mess on paper (don’t get me wrong it’s an absolute joy to play), with 2 judges presiding over the case (Judge’s younger brother on day 1, and Judge himself on day 2), involves two prosecutors (Franziska von Karma on day 1, and Godot on day 2) and two defence attorneys (Miles Edgeworth on day 1, and Phoenix Wright on day 2). Furthermore it's one of the only trials to involve Spirit channeling as a major aspect in the trial (DL-6 is another).

“‘Shichishito’. The treasured Kurain Village heirloom whose name means ‘7 Branched Sword’. It is said that this sacred sword represents life itself. Though the branches may appear to be infinite, the choices limitless... ...like our destinies, the sword comes to but one end. One merciless point. And when the silver cord, the fragile thread that binds us to this world, is severed... ...the illusion is revealed and the implacability of fate is finally laid bare…” - the explanation of the sword used to murder Misty Fey.

Essentially to summarise the plot, Godot (Diego Armando) had awoken from his coma one month after Dahlia’s death, learning of Morgan's plot to kill Maya he constructed a murder plot to protect her. To be frank, a lot of this trial is the work of Godot's strategy with Hawthorne mostly being dragged along for the ride, quite literally failing miserably at her plan for revenge. The plot involved having Misty Fey channel Dahlia and restrain her so she never gets the chance for Pearl to channel her and kill Maya. Unfortunately the plan went miserably and Godot ends up killing Misty to stop Dahlia attacking Maya, Dahlia retaliates wounding Godot. (This leads to the trial with Iris as suspect number one). Dahlia is knocked unconscious and soon rechanneled by Maya (not Pearl) and she acts out under the impression that Maya is dead (falling off a bridge) and that she's in Pearl's body.

Hawthorne's feats in this case: retaliates against Godot, causes the death of Misty Fey indirectly, concealed herself and feigned being her sister (Iris). That's essentially all she really does, most of this Trial is Wright working to prove Godot killed Misty and not Iris.

Wright over the events of the trial proves that Dahlia was acting as Iris, proves that Maya is still alive and that Dahlia failed. (This causes her to cry out in anguish that Mia (who had told Maya to channel Dahlia so she couldn't harm her) always foiled her plans and then left Maya's body, never to be channeled again).
