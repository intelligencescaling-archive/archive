Phoenix Wright Intelligence Feats Document
Created by: Silhouette9999 on YouTube

Below is a document containing a collection of Phoenix Wright’s best intelligence feats. For some background on the character of Phoenix Wright:

Phoenix Wright is a defense attorney who works primarily in murder cases. He defends innocent defendants in cases that seem impossible to win. He is extremely skilled in a court of law and manages to turn around cases from impossibly low odds with seemingly completely unrelated clues, hence why most/all cases in the games begin with or have “Turnabout” in their names. Phoenix’s best skills include deduction, thinking, some manipulation, and emotional intelligence.

It is important to note that in the first 3 games in the Ace Attorney series, Phoenix receives hints and major clues from other people. However, within these cases Phoenix still demonstrates plenty of exploits that would put him at a genius level. Hopefully by curating these feats, we can bring his character into the spotlight some more. Do keep in mind though, this doc is not meant to be a replacement for a full viewing and analysis of the games themselves, only a hopefully well explained detail of them.

The Ace Attorney series contains six mainline games, with Phoenix Wright being the figurehead for all but one of them. For the sake of proper organization and explanation, this doc will be organized by game, then by case, and then by specific feat. Each case section will have an explanation containing the actual details of the murder and how it happened, then the feats will explain individual showcasings of specific intellectual skills. I won’t be including case 1-1, since next to no notable feats were displayed by Phoenix alone.

ALSO, feats from cases in the series get more and more complex as it continues, and requires more context to fully understand, which is why I’ll be providing short rundowns of each piece of evidence and information gathered throughout the cases as well as how each line of reasoning plays out in order to show the full process of coming to the conclusions made in each case.

Turnabout Sisters (Case 1-2)

Actual Case Events

Redd White murders a woman named Mia Fey for having documents that could harm his reputation. He gets a woman in a nearby hotel to testify against her unfortunate sister, and plants a bloody scribing of Maya's name on a receipt in the scene of the crime. He then blackmails a lawyer named Grossberg into not defending Maya. It should be noted that Redd White also has the police in his pocket, and the opposing prosecution can forge and conceal evidence if it does not support their case, making a legal acquittal of Maya Fey nearly impossible.

We’ll now cover Phoenix’s involvement.

Within a few seconds, Phoenix confabulated a story about how a strap connected to Maya's phone was a collectors item and that she was concerned it may get lost because of police interference. Doing this allowed him to take a look at Maya's phone when it was in Gumshoes possession.

After pointing out the contradiction between May saying Mia dodged to the right, and Redd saying Mia dodged to the left, Phoenix used this to propose the idea that Redd saw her dodge in the opposite direction of April May because he was in the point of view of the killer, who was facing in the other direction.

When Grossberg noted that the trial would be held the day after Phoenix spoke to him, Phoenix immediately noticed this, knowing that Grossberg shouldn’t have known.

Phoenix pressed a hotel bellboy about his process of checking April May into the hotel. After the bellboy said that May’s presence was a “disappointment,” Phoenix pressed him again to find that April May had checked in with another man.

Grossberg told Phoenix about a painting in his office that he loved. He told Phoenix that he would not be willing to part with it to anyone, and Phoenix remembered this when he saw the same painting hanging in Redd White's office.

During the opening of the first trial day, Phoenix noticed that Edgeworth (the prosecutor), The Judge, and Gumshoe were all referring to the Thinker as a statue rather than as a clock, meaning that they had not listened to the phone call between Mia and Maya that discussed the removal of the clockwork from the statue.

Phoenix called out the contradiction in April May's testimony by pointing out that she couldn’t have heard the Thinker statue act as a clock, because the clockwork had been removed long before the murder (as proven by the phone call recording). Phoenix then proved that she could not have seen this statue in a store because it is homemade, and then followed this line to conclude that April May heard that it was a clock because she was wiretapping Mia Feys law office.

During a cross-examination, Phoenix realized that the statement that Mia wrote Maya's name in her own blood contradicts the autopsy, due to the fact that the autopsy states an immediate death.

Called out that April May noted Maya Feys slender, girlish physique first rather than her abnormal clothing, signifying that she was lying about who she saw.

Bluffed that Phoenix would take a sworn affidavit from the bellboy saying that Redd White checked into the hotel with April May.

After Redd White refused to talk unless Phoenix “put him on the stand” (which would not be possible) Phoenix realized that Redd should have been called as a witness since he would’ve seen the same thing as April May, yet he was not called.

After discovering the missing painting in Redd White's office, Phoenix concluded that Grossberg was being blackmailed by Redd, because Phoenix knew that Bluecorp was an intelligence gathering agency.

After being arrested, Phoenix anticipated that a trap would be set for him with the collaboration of Redd and Edgeworth, as well as the rest of the court, starting with Phoenix being assigned a public defender. He instead chose to defend himself.

Case 1-3

Turnabout Samurai

Actual Case Events

This murder took place on the film set for a fictional TV show named Steel Samurai. Jack Hammers, the actor for the antagonist of the show, had accidentally killed a man on a set about 5 years prior to the case. Because of this, Dee Vasquez (the killer in this case) had been giving Jack insignificant roles and very low pay for them. Eventually, Jack Hammers got fed up and attempted to kill Vasquez. Jack wore the suit of the Steel Samurai in an attempt to frame the actor for the protagonist of the show, Will Powers. (Please bear the ridiculous names.) Jack not only wore the Steel Samurai suit, but was photographed walking to the scene of the crime by the security camera and spotted by a kid trying to sneak into the set. After Jack attempted to murder Vazquez, she accidentally pushed him onto a spiked fence, which he fell back first onto. This all happened in studio two of Global Studios. After the killing, Vasquez took the body to studio one, and propped up the body on a spear prop which went directly through the chest, making it seem like he was murdered via a spear.

Map of Global Studios for reference

Phoenix’s Involvement

Phoenix was aware of 3 important factors during the moments before the murder: 1. A photo had been taken of someone wearing the steel samurai costume. 2. The person wearing this costume had an obvious limp. 3. Wendy Oldbag (security guard for global studios) was aware of the injury that Will Powers had sustained on set earlier that day. Phoenix used these facts to conclude that Wendy Oldbag, not Will Powers, had been wearing that costume and was therefore the killer (this was wrong, but it was also before Phoenix even knew of Dee Vasquez’s existence. He also admits later he was just using this theory to buy time.) Phoenix also pointed out that Oldbag knew where the camera was placed, meaning she could have easily set up that photo to frame Powers.

Coming into the second day of the Trial, a kid named Cody Hackins, who had snuck into the studios, gave a testimony along this line: “I wandered into the studios and saw the steel samurai and his nemesis fighting. I saw the steel samurai kill him” Phoenix managed to discover the truth, which was:”Cody snuck into the studios and saw the steel samurai and his nemesis fighting. Cody took a picture with his digital camera, but erased them because the steel samurai lost, which went against everything he believed in.” Here’s the line of reasoning:
1.Cody stating that he hadn’t brought his camera with him to the studios was contradictory, because he told the court that he brought it with him everywhere.
2.When asked how exactly the steel samurai killed his nemesis, Cody described a bunch of random terms such as “Samurai punches, kicks and slaps” as a means of killing his nemesis. Phoenix picked up on this and pointed out that there was no possible way the steel samurai could have killed anyone in the way that he described.
3. When pressed on the matter of his camera, Cody said that he was too lost in the action of the moment to take a picture. Phoenix noted that this was contradictory due to the fact that Cody had a scrapbook filled with photos of all of the steel samurai’s best moments, so it was impossible for him not to photograph such a moment.
4. Cody explained that he was unable to take a picture because he couldn’t figure out his new digital camera in time for the shot, making the lens unable to open in time. Phoenix didn’t buy this at all since he knew he would’ve taken a picture no matter what the quality or timing, even if it was after the critical moment. Due to his knowledge about the scrapbook, Phoenix knew he wouldn’t miss an opportunity to take a photo of the steel samurai standing victoriously over his opponent.
5. To conclude, Phoenix theorized that Cody Hackins took a picture, but the picture contained an image of the Steel Samurai losing, so he erased the photos. And because of this, it was actually the victim Jack Hammer in that costume, not Will Powers. Since one of the main concrete pieces of evidence against powers was the photo of the steel samurai which was assumed to be him, this changes the entire course of the case. Cody reveals that he did in fact take a picture, and it shows someone, in a costume, standing in studio 2. This means that the crime took place in studio 2, not one, which pretty much clears Will Powers. At about 2:15 on the day of the murder, the head of a giant monkey statue fell onto the pathway between studio 1 and 2, blocking anyone from moving between either studio for a significant amount of time. Phoenix had exposed via some contradictions in the timeline earlier in the trial that the staff and cast had taken a 15 minute break between 2:30 and 2:45. The producer and director of the steel samurai were both in studio 2 at that time, meaning they were in the same studio at the same time as the murder. Not only that, but because of the statues head, it was actually impossible for Will to have committed the crime.

Coming into the final day of trial, Phoenix knew to a fact that it was Dee Vasquez who had committed the murder. He only needed to prove it by catching Dee in her own testimony. Dee’s first testimony states that she was in a meeting with the executives of the studio from 12 to 4, taking a 15-minute break to eat T-bone steaks at 2:30. After the meeting, she had the director Sal Manella take her from studio 2 to studio 1 in a van, since she was fatigued. When she arrived at studio 1, she discovered the body of Jack Hammer. Phoenix quickly noticed that there were no bones on the plates of Dee Vasquez’s steak, despite there being bones on the plate of Will Powers’ steak. Phoenix says, therefore, that she did not eat the steak but rather disposed of it in some way such as the incinerator nearby the trailer. This is when Phoenix first proposed the idea of Vasquez being the true killer to the court, saying she killed Jack during the 15 minute break. Dee counters this by saying that the proposed murder weapon, the samurai spear, would be far too heavy for her to wield, as she is a petite woman. This is when Phoenix claims that the murder weapon was not the samurai spear, because the spear had been broken during a rehearsal earlier in the day, and only repaired with duct tape. Therefore, it’d be far too frail to stab and kill someone with. Phoenix then proposes that the cause of death was a spiked gate that surrounded the trailer in studio two, which Jack was pushed onto. This was further backed up by a photo of an incident from 5 years before the case where the exact same thing happened. Vasquez points out that the body would have to have been moved to studio 1, the body put into the evil magistrate costume rather than the steel samurai costume, within the span of 15 minutes, which doesn’t even include the time it would take to return to studio 2. Phoenix proposes that Sal and Dee put the body into the van mentioned earlier and drove the body to studio 1. At this point, though the evidence and testimony logically points to Phoenix being correct, he still doesn’t have any solid proof. After some stalling and some character development, Edgeworth asks Dee to testify one more time about the events after the discovery of the body, giving Phoenix one final opening. Vasquez states in her testimony that she, Oldbag, Sal, and an assistant at the studios discovered Hammers body in studio 1, while Powers was absent. When they called the police and they showed up, Powers then appeared. Dee then says she asked to be left out of the typical investigation proceedings, she went back to studio 2, got her notes, and then went home. Phoenix asks why she didn’t bring her notes with her to the rehearsal, and Dee responds that she thought there would be no rehearsal because of the murder. Shockingly, Edgeworth is the one to point out that she would’ve had to known about the murder before going to studio one in order to have the reasons to not bring her notes. Dee then covers herself by saying she knew Hammer was injured, therefore they couldn’t do rehearsal, therefore, she didn’t bring her notes. But Phoenix instantly points out that it was not Hammer that was injured, but Will Powers. Only Powers had been injured, and Hammers was simply wearing the samurai costume and pretending to have a limp to convince people he was Will under the costume. Dee attempts to cover herself by saying that Sal told her that Hammer was injured, but Phoenix knows Sal was at the rehearsal where Powers was injured and would have no reason to tell her the wrong person. Therefore, the only way she could’ve known about this limp was if she saw Jack Hammer, in the steel samurai costume, in studio 2. There was only one hole: Vasquez had no motive to do something like this. To Vasquez, Hammer was a useful actor that she had to pay very little. She had no reason to get rid of him, much less kill him. Phoenix took no time in presenting a clear motive: the incident mentioned 5 years before the case was an accident where Hammer accidentally pushed a co-star onto a sharp fence which they fell onto and died on via piercing. Vasquez held that incident over his head for 5 years, making him work for chump change and only in villainous roles. Hammer got sick of this and decided to act out his revenge, using Powers’ limp and costume to frame him for the crime. He attempted to kill Vasquez, but she managed to push him off the stairs that led into the trailer and onto the same spiked fence, killing Hammer in self defense. Case 1-4

Turnabout Goodbyes

To fully understand this case, we have to understand a case from long before the games timeline: case DL-6. This is the name the police gave to the case. I’ll only be giving the known facts of the case up to this point so that I can reveal it wholly later. 15 years before this case, In the DL-6 incident, Gregory Edgeworth (the father of Miles Edgeworth) had just finished a trial and was headed home with his son. On an elevator, they were with a court bailiff by the name of Yanni Yogi. The elevator crashed and cut off all oxygen in the elevator. The three quickly fell into a panic, with Yogi attacking Gregory. In an attempt to stop the fighting, Miles picked up Yogis dropped gun and threw it between them. They soon after lost consciousness. When they all awoke, Gregory was dead. The police were desperate for leads, so they hired a spirit medium to solve the case privately. Misty Fey, head of the Fey clan, summoned the spirit of Gregory, who accused Yogi of his murder. They prosecuted Yogi, but with his lawyer, Robert Hammond, he was found not guilty due to a lying plea of brain damage due to oxygen deprivation. This ruined Yogi's life, as he had to pretend to be brain damaged from then on. Redd White would later expose the police for using a spirit medium, humiliating the police and the Fey clan, causing Misty Fey to go into hiding. The case would go unsolved for another 15 years. Miles was no older than 10 during this incident.

Actual Case Events

Edgeworth, not knowing Hammonds face, assumes that Yanni Yogi is not Yanni Yogi but instead is Robert Hammond. Yanni then fires two gunshots, and then dives into the lake and swims back to his shop. He puts Hammonds coat back on his body, and then dumps his body into Gourd Lake. This entire plan was carefully orchestrated by Manfred von Karma.

Case Analysis
Damon Gant’s planning in this case is one of the most complex in the series. This case was actually designed as a sort of DLC for the American release of Phoenix Wright, so it was designed after Trials and Tribulations. Gant had it set up so that if Phoenix were to pursue the truth of the case, he’d either fail and get Lana declared guilty under the weight of how convoluted the case was, or fail to save either Ema or Lana due to the forging of evidence in the SL-9 case. Yet, Phoenix was able to counter this by a subtle strategy that isn’t directly talked about in the text of the game. See, Phoenix had already established during the final day of trial that Ema had killed Neil Marshall by accident by pushing him into the statue. He did this even without presenting the cloth. He had to strategically withhold the cloth until the exact right moment, that being when he could prove that the cloth was proof of Ema’s innocence, not her guilt. He also tricked Gant himself into revealing that he himself had cut the cloth out, making it both relevant to the case at hand and proof that Gant had arrived first and was the murderer.

On top of this, Phoenix’s reasoning and critical thinking skills are very well shown in this case. From taking a case where it was almost 100% sure that Lana had killed Goodman, to solving the double murder mystery, to solving a case from over 2 years ago, to solving the truth of the entire story, pretty much alone.

Analysis
Where to begin. Firstly, the true purpose of this plan was to bring Kristoph to justice in spite of Phoenix’s lack of an attorney’s badge. Phoenix knew Drew would end up killed eventually, that is why he created the jurist system. Personally, I think Phoenix playing poker in that club room all those years was him biding his time and waiting for Zak to reveal himself. Once he finally did, his plan could be set into motion. This is supported by the way Phoenix made that fake fifth ace, and used it to make a bunch of contradictions in Kristoph's testimony. He also knew the mystery of how and why Zak was killed would be vitally important to ultimately solving the whole mystery. I think it’s fair to say that Phoenix had also anticipated the proceedings of this trial as well. He knew Vera was going to fall into the trap of poisoning; this is when Phoenix took the opportunity to show the MASON system to the court. Much like the first trial, Phoenix’s drip feeding of information to the court at the right moment and only at the right moment suggests to me that Phoenix was subtly guiding the players in court to their conclusion; even when not even inside the courtroom. Ultimately, I hope you can see Phoenix’s full plan, and not only that, but his ability to reason through incredibly complex issues and come up with a plan in foresight to catch criminals even in the absence of an attorney's badge.

So, here’s a basic explanation of the plan:

After being disbarred, Phoenix talked to Drew and Vera, finding out (via breaking the psych locks) that the true forger was Vera and learned about the stamp as well as the nail polish.

Phoenix befriended Kristoph and pretended to be his friend for 7 years, biding his time.

All the while, he was working with the courts to create the jurist system. He intentionally hid this from everyone he worked with; he kept it from Kristoph because he believed he’d never be caught due to a lack of evidence, and hiding it from him allowed Kristoph to act carelessly and therefore allowing the deaths of Zak and Drew and the poisoning of Vera to happen, which allowed him to catch Kristoph. He also kept the jurist system hidden from Apollo through this entire game, up until the start of case 4. Why? Well, it connects back to Phoenix’s consistent information withholding. He needed to execute this trial perfectly; and that meant that Apollo would only be on a need to know basis.

Zak Gramarye reappears. He collects the information about Apollo Justice and prepares to use him for his trial. He knew that evidence gathering would not be enough to defeat Kristoph; he needed something that could dig up the truth based on witness behavior.

Zak Gramarye dies, and then Phoenix pulls off everything he did in the first case of the game.

Phoenix gathers all the necessary information needed to solve the case, and then organizes it in the MASON system in such a way that would make it clear to the jurors that Kristoph is the killer, allowing them to chase down the leads through the different locations and time periods.

Drew dies, and then Phoenix allows Apollo to make his deductions up to the point that Vera also gets poisoned, and then he shows the MASON system to the jurors.

He then lets Apollo and Kristoph finish up the case and then uses the jurist system to finish Klavier off.

It may occur to you that Phoenix seemed to be doing some very over the line things in this game; letting Drew and Zak die and almost letting Vera die. Why? Well, this case, and the first case, is mostly a direct confrontation of the legal system in the ace attorney world. In the court system of the game, cases have to be finished in 3 days or less, evidence is required to prove pretty much anything, and in order to get their client acquitted, the defense has to pretty much solve the entire thing and find out who the true killer is. Von Karma, one of the most prolific prosecutors in the country, Damon Gant, the police chief, had been proven to not only be evidence forgers but also murderers. When Phoenix was given that forged diary page and disbarred, he knew he had to redefine the legal system at any cost. This also explains why Phoenix carelessly took that forged Diary page from Trucy; it’s only a standard practice under this sort of legal system.
