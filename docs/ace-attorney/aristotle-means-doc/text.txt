“Professor Means”

“Professor Means always says what he means and means what he says by all means!”

Lore:

Aristotle Means was a teacher at Themis Legal Academy and also a defence attorney. He had a firm belief in his philosophy that the “ends justify the means” and taught that a lawyer's job was to win no matter the cost. This clashed with fellow teacher- Constance Courte- who taught that a lawyer’s job was to find the truth.

According to the narrative, as a result the student body was split 50/50, with one half following Means’ philosophy and the other half following Courte’s philosophy. Furthermore due to his philosophy (and later on we have a concrete example), we know that Means’ has no qualms with forging evidence for whatever result benefitted him the most.

Means was in fact an alumnus at Themis Legal Academy (prior to being a defence attorney and later teaching there), he graduated as a valedictorian, displaying his brilliant academic skills and knowledge of the law.

“>Athena: But that scrap of paper with Hugh's name had the same mark as Prof. Courte's planner--
>Blackquill: Yes, about that... Those planners are bestowed upon all students graduating at the top of their class.
>Athena: Really?! So, then…
>Blackquill: Indeed. Once upon a time, Prof. Means was also awarded the same notebook.”

Means (initially) comes off as very well-mannered and friendly (albeit with a creepy smile), however this is vastly different to his true self that he reveals in court once cornered which is far more aggressive and strict, acting like an angry teacher, going so far as to assault the court with chalk.

Turnabout Academy - Plan:

Means had been taking bribes for students to pass and get good grades, however, having been discovered through Juniper Woods overhearing one of his students discussing said bribes with his parents, he is confronted by his colleague Constance Courte about the matter. As a result of this situation, Means murders Courte, impaling her with his staff on her side.

Aristotle uses his knowledge of the “mock trial” to stage Courte’s death in such a way that he can frame Woods. Means would use two banners (a school banner and a “Gavinners” banner) at the stage to get rid of the blood, using the Gavinners banner to then create a false bloodstain in the art room three stories above the stage. Having fabricated the evidence, Means then disposed of the banner in the school incinerator.

Means would then take advantage of the fact that Robin had been unable to finish her Phoenix Wright statue for the stage and position the corpse in such a way that it could resemble the unfinished statue, using a cloth to cover up the body. As a result of this, Means was able to safely leave the body alone until the next day without fear of it being noticed.

The following day, during the events of the school mock trial, Means used a prerecorded speech to create an alibi for himself and proceeded with his plan to frame Woods. He went to the art room and launched the remolded statue of Lady Justice down a makeshift pouch intentionally crashing it into the “statue” of Phoenix Wright (Courte’s corpse) and the statue of Klavier Gavin.

This would create a loud noise, alerting people to Courte’s body.

Furthermore, to create more evidence that implicates Woods, Means would use the recorder tape to fabricate evidence of a woman - identified as Woods - screaming “You’re a goner!” which he recorded during the mock trial as it was one of the lines. Unfortunately, whilst doing this, Means overlooked his speech and even though he deleted it on the recorder, he accidentally left white noise on the tape that lasted 10:35s (the exact length of his speech, more on this later). He hands the tape over to Hugh O’Connor (one of his students and an extremely close friend of Juniper Woods), telling the boy to give it to the police (naturally, without telling him that it’s phony).

Finally, Means planned to take Woods' case himself, opting to be her defence attorney, all the while planning to use this to ensure that he couldn’t be traced to the crime in its slightest. However, due to Athena Cykes firm commitment to defending her friend, Means was forced to give up on this, which would later result in his downfall.

Turnabout Academy - Trial:

AT THIS POINT IN THE TRIAL, ATHENA HAS YET TO DISCOVER THE TRUE LOCATION OF THE MURDER, AND STILL BELIEVES (THANKS TO MEANS’ FORGED EVIDENCE) THAT IT OCCURRED IN THE ART ROOM AND THE BODY HAD THEN BEEN MOVED FROM THERE TO THE STAGE

When called to testify, Means is immediately under heavy suspicion from Cykes. She asserts that upon hearing Means’ speech, the line:

>“Once again, our pure white Lady Justice will be watching over all of you today."

is a glaring contradiction as said “white” statue was in fact gold and proves that he had used a pre recorded speech to go and finalise the details of the murder, to pin the blame on Juniper Woods- the only student who was allowed to move between areas as she was in charge of audio and the mock trial.

EXPLANATION FOR MEANS’ “MISTAKE” INCOMING:

EXPLANATION OVER!

Means skillfully plays her claim off, however, claiming that it was merely an oversight on his behalf, and that since he was in a balcony seat and the statue was originally planned to be white- he had made a simple mistake. The Judge accepts his claim and asserts that it is not sufficient evidence to declare that Means’ speech was pre recorded.

UNFORTUNATELY, (recall Means’ oversight regarding the white noise on the tape?) Cykes points out the timestamps on where Means’ speech begins and when it finishes as being the exact amount of time of white noise on the tape recorder. Means attempts to brush this off as well, explaining that due to his own philosophy- even if Juniper was guilty, he’d defend her rigorously- the fact that he offered to be her lawyer shows that he didn’t want her to be convicted.

Being cornered further, Means then goes on to say that he had been keeping quiet on purpose, to avoid implicating Woods further. He claims that since he’s being blamed, he has no choice, but to reveal “the truth”.

Aristotle’s testimony:

>Means: I will now reveal the truth behind why I had to pre-record my speech before the mock trial. I, Prof. Means, always say what I mean and mean what I say by all means!

>Means: The defendant came to me with what she said was very important, and by necessity, secret. She asked me to pre-record my speech and come to the audio room during the mock trial. Juniper confessed to the murder and asked that I get her declared innocent in court. She also said I'd become an accomplice after losing my alibi due to the pre-recording. But when I said I would protect Juniper, I meant it because it's the humane thing to do.

He further solidifies his claims, by going on to say that Juniper had asked him to defend her, but to also ensure her two friends- Robin Newman and Hugh O’Conner- were also kept safe.

HOWEVER, Athena uses his forged tape recording that he tried to use to implicate Woods as proof that he was not in any way shape or form, trying to protect her. Rather that he has only been lying and saying whatever will benefit/protect him the most.

Here, Means drops his friendly/polite façade. Adopting his more ruthless, strict teacher persona.

Following further assertions from Athena that he is the only possible culprit, Means claims as follows:

At this point, Athena has literally NO means to object and disprove his claim- this would result in Means no longer being a suspect and thus, successfully getting away with his crime.

Unfortunately for Means, Robin Newman then steps forward with new testimony, revealing that she had not completed both statues for the stage (where the body was discovered).

Explaining that she had only been successful in completing one and requested that Means finish the other.

Displaying that Means’ had taken advantage and understood the depth of Robin’s desire to be an artist. Thus proving he would still be in school at the time of the murder.

This leads to Means having to testify further to defend himself, it goes as follows:

>Means: Now, pay attention! Robin took over half a day to complete one statue. I, however, finished most of the other statue between 7:00 and 8:30 PM. The defendant Juniper saw the two statues with her own eyes at 8:30 PM. Completing a statue so quickly meant I could not leave the stage for a single second! How could I have possibly had the time to go to the art room and commit murder?!

Means clever testimony here is not possible for Athena to break at any point. She is forced to concede that Means never left the stage. However, she then goes on to assert that Means had killed Constance Courte on the stage.

Means quickly claims that if the murder took place on the stage- then there should be far more blood. However, Athena explains that Means used the banners to clean them up.
