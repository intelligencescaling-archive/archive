Keyser Söze's Usual Suspects plan explanation.

Keyser Söze is a notorious crime lord across America and parts of Central Europe. Söze has a problem, and that is that there is one criminal, a rat who knows Söze's face who will be sent to America to identify Keyser Söze for a Hungarian gang. Söze finds three miscellaneous criminals who have in some way interfered unintentionally with operations of his in the past year. There was Todd Hockney who a few months ago stole a truck carrying gun parts belonging to Keyser Söze. There was Michael McManus and Fred Fenster, who hijacked a cargo flight earlier that year carrying gold and platinum wiring belonging to Keyser Söze. Lastly there was also Dean Keaton, who a decade ago participated in the hijacking of a truck, carrying a cargo of raw steel belonging to Keyser Söze, and was a former corrupt cop. Keaton had good influence before everything collapsed and he was caught, enough so that some years ago he was believed to be dead, reported to have died by two witnesses who then died under mysterious circumstances 2 months later. This clearly made him a suitable candidate for a patsy to be set up as Keyser Söze, and based on the fact that his crime was a decade ago we could even say that setting him up was a backup plan that Söze had held onto for as long.

–First, Söze creates a false identity as a lowlie con artist with Parkinson's named Roger "Verbal" Kint, so nicknamed for his extensive yapping. Under this identity, he gets himself arrested in a country prison for 90 days, a prison Keaton was in. With this, "Verbal" establishes some rapport with Keaton as an ambitious, but harmless younger crook. (Fabrication, Acting Skills, Disguise, Relationship Building, Preparation.)

–Söze has a truck loaded with gun parts hijacked, and ensures the driver hears a voice but gets no look at the culprit. Through pulling some strings in the police, Söze ensures that he and the others are the ones pulled in on suspicion. Fenster and McManus worked together frequently, and putting them in a room with Hockney made them happy to work with him on an upcoming job, a job suggested by a fence called Redfoot put up to it by Söze. Söze then reaches out to Keaton. Söze knew that Keaton's past as a corrupt cop meant that the other police hated him specially, however Keaton was trying to move on, but police pestering, such as bringing him in for this line-up had been severely affecting his attempts to move on in life. Söze leverages this desperation to improve his life and irritation with the police, as well as the image he'd placed in Keaton's mind of an amateur trying to play with the big boys, as Söze claimed that the other three wouldn't let him join unless he brought Keaton, who was more well known. (Tactics, Indirect Manipulation, Psychological Manipulation, Emotional Understanding, Emotional Manipulation.)

–The job was intercepting smuggled stolen gems, being transported by the "New York's Finest" taxi service, a organization of corrupt cops who transport things for criminals. After taking the gems, they smashed the windscreen and set the roof on fire. This prompted serious investigation, leading to the uncovering of the organization, while also making it apparent that this was organized by someone seriously hating the police, yet with good knowledge of them and their corruption. (Tactics, Info Control, Preparation.)

–Keaton distanced from girlfriend.

–Söze indirectly has Redfoot give the suspects another job in which Söze kills a man. After which, Redfoot informs the suspects of a lawyer called Kobayashi who wants to meet them. Kobayashi informs them he works for Keyser Söze, known to them all, and tells them of the crimes against Söze all of them committed, adding a fake one that Verbal Kint supposedly had done, and lets them know that it was Söze who had drawn them together thus far with the line up in New York and the jobs from Redfoot. What's more, now that "Verbal" killed a man on a job with them all, which Redfoot would happily testify to under Söze's orders, and they further go on to hand over folders for each suspect containing detailed information on every person they had ever worked with, every job they'd done, everyone they'd ever done time with, all of their personal info, everything since they turned 18. The suspects now have no choice but subservience to Keyser Söze. (Fabrication, Info Gathering, Coercion.)

–The job Söze wants the suspects to do is to intercept a shipment of cocaine from some other competitors and throw the drugs into the sea. However, there was in truth no cocaine on the boat. Instead, it was the boat that the rat who could identify Söze was traveling on. Leading up to the job Fenster tried to escape and was killed. During the job, Söze kills the other three and the rat. He then blows up the ship to dispose of Keaton's body, and allows himself to be picked up by the police. With his influence, Söze basically can get off scot-free. Under intense interrogation, Söze tells the cops the story from the perspective of Verbal Kint, who was forced into this by Keyser Söze along with the other suspects, who was the lucky only survivor, and who watched Keaton being killed himself, as well as changing key names (Redfoot and Kobayashi were made up.) However, this whole story of him witnessing Keaton's death rings hollow to the detectives, instead reminding them of the last time they thought Keaton was dead. After all, Keaton was a big dirty cop with a wealth of connections and a great understanding of the police system. Organizing a line up to assemble a group of thieves who'd stolen from him, making their first big job a hit against other corrupt cops who got away with it while he didn't and grooming a younger, crippled amateur to be able to report his death to the cops, then using influence to give him immunity as a reward are all things Keaton 100% would do, or at least they're things that the spiteful detectives who still hate him think he would do. Söze himself says that cops look for reasons to arrest for the people they want to be guilty, and predicted based on his understanding of how cops see corrupt cops that they would pin it all on Keaton. (Indirect Manipulation, Logical Manipulation, Emotional Understanding, Emotional Manipulation, Fabrication, Misdirection, Strategic Deception, Long-Term Planning.)
