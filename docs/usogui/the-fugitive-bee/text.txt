Background info
Gakuhito and Hal are solving a weapons smuggling case and finding the wirepuller who is
leaking info and creating these incidents. The leaker is hypothesized to be a high-ranking
administrator of the MoD. The wirepuller intends to sell missiles (presumably to the Middle
East). To disguise it as a burglary incident, they let a foreign smuggling group join the deal. The
foreign group will smuggle the weapons out by boat, and they are scheduled to leave today,
putting Gakuhito and Hal under time pressure. In exchange for the weapons, the wirepuller will
receive money that needs to be laundered. Gakuhito and Hal will wiretap the office of the
launderers to find a trail back to the leaker.
Hal also has to hide from Kakerou after the events of last arc. He realizes that there must be a
recording of the phone call he had with Gakuhito at the MPD that could lead Kakerou right to
him through Gakuhito (see figure 1). Hal must fake Gakuhito's death so that Kakerou's
investigation on him will come to a dead end. He steals Gakuhito's ID card to do so as he plans
to plant it on the ship and let it sink at sea.
Hal and Gakuhito locate the client of the money laundering ring. He is still sitting at the diner
where they held their meeting.

Diner Encounter
Despite Gakuhito's suggestion of taking a passive approach, Hal decides to confront the client in
the diner primarily because he cannot waste more time and let Kakerou catch his trail through
Gakuhito. Hal identifies Kidou, the "Dark Sleeper", as a man from an organization deeply
connected to the JSDF and U.S forces in Japan and was abolished due scandals. Hal figures that

Kidou would be the client of the money launderers based on his background. Kidou himself is
not the Wire Puller but an agent working to orchestrate the smuggling operation and money
laundering.
Before meeting Kidou at his table, Hal asks the diner’s reception to hold onto his bag with the
100M. He must find out the location of the boat from Kidou, which is why establishing a reason
to be there is important as Kidou would refuse to speak if he finds Hal too suspicious. But he
observes a few details about Kidou
- Kidou’s habit of tapping the table twice when asked if he wishes for a refill on his drink
- Kidou's habit of touching the bottom of coffee creamers to check the numbers printed
below them
- The stacks upon stacks of empty plates that are already on the table
The first is a gesture in gambling card games when a player wants to receive another card such as
in Poker or Blackjack. The second is a common practice in Mahjong, another gambling game.
Mahjong players perform this maneuver when they want to check the pattern of a tile before
discarding it, allowing for easy memorization of several tiles at once. These together with his
gluttonous behavior of having stacks of finished plates at his table, Hal profiles Kidou to be a
gambling addict with huge debt issues. He accordingly pretends to be a hounding debt collector
looking for money Kidou owes from gambling. He tells Kidou "The amount of money has
become very large", letting him fill in the blanks and come to the intended conclusion himself.
This clears Kidou suspicion of Hal for now.
Kidou does not have enough money to cover his debt, so he offers Hal to play a simple game
with a wager. If Hal wins, he’ll pay the debt straight away, but if he wins, Hal has to leave him
alone for now.

The rules of Coin Pusher are as follows:
1. The beginning
2. Decide who will go first.
3. Take turns pushing one of their coins
4. Each turn, players can push their coin any number of squares forwards or backwards as
long as they remain in the squares of the row and do not cross the opponent’s coins.
5. If a player cannot move either of their coins on their turn, they lose.

Upon hearing the rules, Hal immediately deduces the mechanism of the sure-win strategy of the
game, because there is a way for a player to secure the win with perfect play. The strategy boils
down to, “you want to maintain the separation between the coins on both rows when it is your
turn” ✞✞✞. Since the first player can use this strategy by moving the 10-row coin 5 squares,

making the separation in both rows 3 squares, then the first player will always win. Of course,
this also makes the moves of the first player very predictable, as they have to mirror what the
second player plays ✞✞✞. Hal will use this fact later on to engineer the precise game state he
needs for his trap.
When Kidou was about to decide who to go first using the creamers, Hal excuses himself to
retrieve the bag which, unbeknownst to Kidou, contains the 100M. When away from the table, he
gathers 18 coffee creamers with specific numbers on them and instructs the waitress to bring
them to his table. Since Kidou was going to use the creamers to decide who to go first, it’s likely
that he would use them as dice for the coin pusher game as well if needed. Kidou would
memorize the numbers on the 18 creamers and distribute them in the most advantageous way for
him. Memorizing 18 different objects in this way would be a tall task, but Hal knows Kidou, the
avid Mahjong player, would be capable of doing so. If the movement of the game were to be
randomly decided in this way, the side with more different numbers producing more options
would have the advantage. From this principle, Hal precisely predicts that Kidou would
distribute the 18 creamers he picked into:
- Hal: 66, 12 11, 8, 3, 33, 38, 20, 13
- Kidou: 72, 5, 2, 1, 15, 39, 21, 43, 63
Hal also pockets a creamer with the number 6. As stated before, since the 1st player has to mirror
the 2nd player’s moves, Hal can control Kidou’s moves to engineer a game state where Kidou
would instantly lose if he chooses a number 6. This shows that Hal has already constructed a way
to trick Kidou into using the 6 in the game, and even the number 6 itself has strategic meaning as
we will see later. He has also memorized the patterns on each of the creamer covers, so he can
easily identify any even when their numbers are faced down.
Hal returns to the table with the bag and plays one game with Kidou. The waitress returns and
places the 18 creamers on the table before leaving. Hal forces Kidou to yield 1st turn to him, but
he loses on purpose to make Kidou think that he does not know about the strategy. However, Hal
doesn’t technically lose, he only pretends to be put into a forcibly losing position and refuses to
play the game until completion. Accusing Kidou of trying to trick him with a rigged game, Hal
demands Kidou to play another game or he won’t leave him be. Kidou doesn’t think that Hal
knows about the strategy based on his 1st game performance, so he agrees with Hal’s proposal.
However, to convince Hal that there is no trick, Kidou suggests using the coffee creamers as dice
to add randomness into the game. This way, Hal cannot claim that Kidou is cheating since his
moves should be decided randomly anyways. Of course, this is a façade and Kidou plans to
memorize the creamers and distribute them unfairly. Hal completely predicted this down to the
exact numbers both would receive.
Since Kidou is granting Hal’s request for a second game, he forces Hal to bet money on this
second game as well. Specifically, he adds a few rules to the game

1. You can’t stop the game with excuses like last time, the game has to be played until its
end.
2. Both players will be betting money on this next game and the total pot is determined by
adding the numbers used during the game multiplied by 10,000
When it comes to picking who would go first in the second round, Kidou and Hal use the
creamers. Hal picks 66 and forces Kidou to pick 72 to get the first move. Hal then retrieves both
the 66 and 72, opens the 66 and pretends to accidently drop it on the floor. Underneath the table,
away from Kidou's sights, Hal opens the 72 and empties its contents. He then slots a number 6
which he had hidden into the empty 72 container, effectively disguising the number 6 as 72. He
then dropped the opened 66 and the 6 disguised as 72 into separate glasses.

By this point, Kidou has had to deal with mental exhaustion from multiple sources (the purchase

decides that he must run away and start a new life to avoid retribution from his client. So, Kidou
chooses to tell Hal the name, location, and schedule of the boat to raise the wager and try to win
the rest of the money ✞✞✞. Gakuhito receives the information from the wire that Hal is wearing.

Boarding the Jalloud
The boat's name is Jalloud and the port is at the 5th Mooring, the central wharf of the port of
Yokohama. On their way, Gakuhito calls Yokoi who's supposed to already be at the port looking
for Jalloud, but Yokoi informs them that the Jalloud has already left. Instead, they should meet
him on another boat called the Gallop as he has found witnesses on board. Hal sends a message
to Yokoi's phone with Gakuhito's other phone during their conversation. Yokoi's voice on the
other end very briefly became quiet. Hal abduces the reason for this is because Yokoi is being
held captive by the crew of Jalloud ✞✞✞.
When Hal and Gakuhito arrive at port, due to what happened to Yokoi and various other reasons,
Gakuhito decides to reconsider his covert approach and calls for reinforcements from the MoD.
Gakuhito volunteers to board the ship to buy time for backup to come. In the meantime, Hal
notices that there are still some containers carrying the weapons that have yet to be loaded on
board. He manages to steal those containers and hide them, going as far as to throw away any
documented information related to the containers to conceal them even further. Now, Gakuhito
can use those hidden containers as leverage against the captain of the ship as they cannot leave
without them. This is also where Hal finds out that there are 20 weapons containers in total.
If Gakuhito’s reinforcements from the MoD arrives, Hal can no longer enact his plan of faking
Gakuhito’s death at sea to conceal them both from Kakerou. Not to mention that Kakerou’s eyes
and ears may be within the MoD, which would lead them to Hal sooner. Hal must prevent this,
and he does so by using Kidou. After losing everything in Coin Pusher, there were only two
things Kidou could’ve done. The first is to follow Gakuhito and Hal in hopes of retrieving the
money for his escape, since Hal spent so much of the money that making it back is impossible
for Kidou. The second is to call his handler to ask him for assistance, which Kidou will find
possible since he can shift all the blame onto Hal. The handler will relay this information through
different people within the wirepuller's network until it reaches the wirepuller who is a high
ranking official in the MoD, which means that Gakuhito’s call for reinforcements from the MoD
will be intercepted.
Of course, Hal indirectly manipulated Kidou to make that call and set off that entire chain of
events as it benefits him on multiple layers.
1. the reinforcements Hal predicted Gakuhito would call will be intercepted by the
wirepuller, which allows him to proceed with his plan of faking Gakuhito’s death by
sinking the boat at sea.
2. Hal predicts that Kidou would follow them onto the ship and he will use Kidou for
potential misdirection tactics against the ship’s crew, but also to fortify one of his final
goals of compelling the wirepuller to use Gakuhito’s presence on the sunken ship to
frame Gakuhito as a dead traitor (Hal plans to do this by getting Kidou killed and using

his corpse to give the wirepuller the perfect opportunity to declare Gakuhito dead, but
more on that later).
3. Hal will retrieve Kidou’s phone to memorize all of its stored contacts to investigate
phone call recordings of people connected to Kidou and the wirepuller. Hal knows that
this is possible since he has been worried about Kakerou finding the recordings of the call
between him and Gakuhito at the MPD.
4. In the wirepuller's frantic reaction to intercept Gakuhito's request, he will undoubtedly
leave traces of his actions and identity that can later be investigated by Hal through
Kakerou ✞✞✞.
Note that Hal indirectly manipulated Kidou into taking these actions through his performance
against him in Coin Pusher, which means Hal would’ve had to foresee everything from
Gakuhito’s call for back up to Kidou’s actions after the game and all of the ways that would
benefit his strategy as early as when the rule for Coin Pusher was explained to Hal ✞✞✞. Since
Gakuhito’s reinforcement will never come, Hal can proceed with his plan.
Gakuhito enters the bridge and meets Captain Lacy for the first time. He is also wearing a
transmitter so that Hal can hear everything he hears. Yokoi and Kaji is also in the same room as
Lacy and Gakuhito, as well as most of the crew of the Jalloud. Usually, Gakuhito would be in
trouble, but his postering and the weird e-mail sent by Hal during their call with Gakuhito gives
Lacy the suspicion that Gakuhito has more up his sleeve. He correctly infers that Gakuhito may
have called for reinforcement, but Gakuhito purposefully keeps that ambiguous to buy time for
the reinforcement he thinks is coming. This is also the time when Hal hears Lacy give someone
called Voja several orders through the transmitter on Gakuhito. Hal deduces that Voja must be the
second in command of this ship and is most likely someone highly competent and poses a lot of
danger. He may have to come up with a way to eliminate them if needed.
News that weapons containers are missing sends the crew into a panic. Because of the perceived
time constraint from Gakuhito’s bluff of reinforcements, Lacy has no choice but to send most of
the crew to go looking for them. Hal uses this opportunity he purposefully created to disguise as
one of the crewmates he subdued and infiltrates the ship where he can move around more freely
with the lowered security. The crewmate Hal subdued was planning to steal a box of diamonds
belonging to the captain. With him were several items necessary for the heist, such as
communication with accomplices, a key to the captain's cabin and the safe box, and information
on the safe code. Hal takes all of this with him.
Gakuhito and Lacy maintain the deadlock with both sides having to tread carefully. Lacy cannot
harm Gakuhito as (to his knowledge) only Gakuhito knows the whereabouts of the hidden
containers, not mentioning that Lacy is still unsure about probability of Gakuhito’s
reinforcements showing up. Similarly, Gakuhito cannot push too hard as Lacy could decide to
abandon those hidden containers the moment he deems it too risky to wait any longer. In the
meantime, Hal moves on to the ballast pump room where he rigs the ballast pump to discharge

the ship's ballast water the moment it leaves port and damages the pump so that it cannot be used
to refill the ship's ballast mid journey. The ballast serves to stabilize a ship by using the water it
stores to submerge the ship at an appropriate level. Discharging the ballast water will cause the
ship to float too high above the water and easily capsize from the high waves in open ocean. Hal
sabotages the ballast pump to sink the boat well into its journey.
At this point, the rest of Hal's plan roughly speaking was to sabotage the ballast pump, plant
Gakuhito's id card, use the crew’s preoccupation with finding the hidden containers to extract
Gakuhito and Yokoi, and sink the boat in the middle of the ocean. However, Kaji proposes to call
a Kakerou referee to settle the stalemate between Lacy and Gakuhito. This is disastrous for Hal
as the introduction of Kakerou completely shatters his plan on multiple fronts.
1. During his proposal, Kaji articulates the possibility that Gakuhito’s reinforcements may
not come as there is a possible high-ranking accomplice within the MoD that has
intercepted his request. This inference was dead on and invokes a visible reaction from
Gakuhito. Even though Lacy is still skeptical, it is no doubt that the advantage has shifted
to his side in the negotiations, which makes extracting Gakuhito more difficult for Hal.
2. Through Kakerou, Lacy will eventually negotiate loading the hidden containers onto the
ship as a prerequisite for the game. Gakuhito will have no power to argue against it for
long. Loading the containers would bring the entire crew back on board, which nullifies
Hal’s efforts in lowering the number of enemies on board to begin with and makes
extracting the hostages and Gakuhito even more difficult. Hal will have to incapacitate or
distract as many of the crewmates as possible and eliminate Voja, who he has deemed to
be the most dangerous.
3. Kakerou will see Gakuhito alive when they arrive at Jalloud. It isn't enough to just extract
Gakuhito without Kakerou noticing as it would not fool Kakerou into believing that
Gakuhito's is truly dead when the ship eventually sinks and his ID is found. Faking
Gakuhito's death to fool conceal the last trail towards him is one of Hal's primary
objectives from the start. Hal must find a way to make the referee believe that Gakuhito
will be sent off with the boat and die when it eventually sinks
4. Kakerou would just add another layer of obstacles for Hal on the ship. For starters, if
Hal’s apprehension about Kakerou finding the phone call between him and Gakuhito has
already happened, then that is the end for Hal right then and there as Kakerou will stay on
high alert and may even call for reinforcements to retrieve their leader whom they now
know is on board. Even if this isn’t the case, Hal now has to dodge Jalloud crewmates
and Kakerou staff simultaneously.
Hal swiftly restructures his approach.

Battleships
Hal tracks down Kidou on the ship and kills the crewmate who was hounding him. He steals
Kidou's phone and memorizes its entire contact list as he intended to do ever since the beginning
of Coin Pusher. A call was made 40 minutes ago, around the time Kidou was following Gakuhito
and Hal by car. This call was indeed to Kidou’s handler for this case, and Hal confirms his
manipulation of Kidou was successful. Hal plants all of the items and information from the
crewmate trying to steal the captain's diamonds onto the dead crewmate's corpse and orders
Kidou to hide in the restroom with it. He also switches the corpse’s phone with the phone of the
crewmate planning the heist. Finally, he tells Kidou that he will be busy with something on the
deck and leaves Kidou with some inspiring words, telling him to he should find his "last chance".
This is Hal manipulating Kidou to steal the diamonds. After doing so, Kidou would eventually
side with the crewmates by telling them that Hal is the true intruder that they've been looking for.
In the first place, Kidou was the agent who introduced the foreign smuggling crew into the
weapons deal, so he can easily divulge information about the case to convince crewmates that he
is on their side. However, if Kidou surrenders to the crew, they will inevitably search and find the
diamonds on him. Kidou, with his established gluttonous character, would then have to eat the
diamonds to conceal them. Since they're dealing with an intruder who has at the very least
murdered two of their members, the crewmates who find Kidou will report back to the highest
authority available, which is Voja now that Lacy is preoccupied with the game. Kidou will lead
Voja and a portion of the crew towards on to the deck as that was where Hal told Kidou he would
be. Foreseeing all of this, Hal already has the intention of using Kidou's phone to construct a trap
using one of the containers on the deck for Voja and her allies.
Referee Mogami enters the scene. Since Kakerou is now involved, Hal tells Gakuhito to accept
the match while he sets up a situation where Lacy won't be able to overwhelm Gakuhito in the
bridge with his crew after he wins, so that Gakuhito can be extracted. As one of his conditions,
Lacy demands that Gakuhito raise his arms as high as he can so that he can search Gakuhito for
concealed weapons. If Lacy was truly searching, he should've easily discovered the transmitter
Hal is using to listen to their conversation. Since this doesn't happen, Hal knows that Lacy must
have another motive for that demand. Gakuhito calls Hal for instructions under the guise of
listening to a recording of the rules. Hal wants to establish way to cheat in this game, so he tells
Gakuhito to do what he says:
- First, don't accept the condition of loading the hidden weapons' containers onto the ship
and deny the game system for being too suspicious as it belongs to Lacy to begin with.
- Bring the example of ship positions changing mid-game through special software in the
system. If Lacy's system has to be used, then he needs 100% guarantee that the positions
of Lacy's ships will not change during the game
- Even with referee Mogami's word to be guarantor, deny her too by saying he doesn't trust
her or Kakerou, who appeared out of nowhere.

- Finally, urge her to look outside towards the deck of the ship, he's powerless and deep
into enemy territory so he wants irrefutable evidence of Lacy's placements.
Lacy informs Mogami that there are 20 containers with weapons upon her inquiry. The total
number of squares taken by ships in the game is also 20. Since the number of weapons'
containers matches the number of ship squares, Mogami decides to create the "irrefutable
evidence" for Gakuhito by arranging 20 containers in a way that exactly corresponds to Lacy's
arrangement. Containers are arranged in a 10x10 grid on the deck and the squares with Lacy's
ships in the game will have a weapon container placed on its corresponding spot on the deck, as
illustrated in figure 5.

Hal had Gakuhito put on that act to manipulate Referee Mogami into creating that irrefutable
evidence using the containers. The first point communicates that Gakuhito has valid concerns
about the game and that the match will not start unless his suspicions are eased. The second
plants the idea of establishing irrefutable evidence to prevent the possibility of Lacy changing his
ship positions mid-game. The third point forces Mogami to find a way to establish this evidence
so that Kakerou does not control or cannot interfere with it in any way. This makes the evidence
accessible to Hal during the game. The final point is meant to inspire the idea of arranging the 20
weapons containers into a 10x10 grid corresponding to Lacy’s ship placements. Hal knew that
the weapons containers tally up to 20 from when he hid some of them. Hal aimed for this
development and plans to check each container during the game so that he can discover Lacy's
entire placement and send it to Gakuhito throughout the game. He chooses this method knowing
that it would reveal himself to Lacy during the game, since Lacy would be suspicious when
Gakuhito start hitting his ships as if he knew where they were all along. However, revealing

himself to Lacy in this way helps his goal as a hidden intruder on his ship threatening to win his
game and forcing his capture would push Lacy into sending all of his crewmates out of the
bridge to find Hal, leaving Lacy alone with Gakuhito in the bridge and making Gakuhito’s
extraction easier.
During these negotiations Hal is on deck looking into the bridge with binoculars while the rules
are being explained. He sees Lacy lowering the screens which they will be playing on with a
remote control and sees the odd shape of the ships being used in the game ✞✞✞. This is important
later on when he deduces Lacy’s final trick in the game.
Fast forward to the beginning of the game, after the containers are loaded, Hal checks the first
row of the container grid. He informs Gakuhito that there is a ship on A7, A6, and A5. Gakuhito
sinks a cruiser (3x1) ship on those squares. Hal continues and finds A3, A2, and A1 as ships.
Gakuhito sinks a destroy (2x1) on A3 and A2, but he fails to hit anything on A1. Hal uses this
and the clues he has gathered so far to deduce the hidden mechanism of this game and Lacy’s
final trick of adjusting the screen height to make Gakuhito miss ✞✞✞. With this in mind, he
continues to check all the containers, finding useful tools and waiting for Kidou to make his
move.
Kidou notices the phone on the corpse is ringing and checks the messages. It is worth noting that
Hal may have messaged the phone in the corpse himself to bring Kidou’s attention to it. While
reading the crew's communications with each other, he spots messages about a box of diamonds.
There are instructions on how to open the safe and keys to the safe box and the captain's cabin
can be found on the corpse. Kidou makes it to the cabin, steals the diamonds, and eats them to
conceal them from the crew. Then he cooperates with some crewmates by telling them he knows
the whereabouts of the true intruder. They meet up with Voja to deal with the intruder. Hal
notices that there are more people on the deck now looking for him and reasons that it must be
because Kidou has finally sided with Voja. He places Kidou's phone into a container that only
opens on the outside, lights a concealed fire in the container, and hides in another container.
Kidou recalls that Hal stole his phone. He calls his phone, thinking that the ringtone would reveal
Hal’s location. The ringtone lures 5 crewmates into the container and Hal jumps out to lock the 5
in from the outside. He also ties knots around the container's latching mechanism and tells Voja

Coin Pusher Certain-Win Strategy Explained
In this game, you win if the other player cannot move either of their coins. So, the enemy's coins
must be between the wall of the final squares and the enemy's coins on their turn, illustrated in
figure xxx. Both of your own coins must be touching the enemy's coins by the end of your turn.
Notice that the separation between the coins in both rows is the same by the end of your turn in
this state. True enough, if we partition the set of all game states into subsets, "game states where
the separation of the two coins in both rows is the same" and "all other game states", we see that
the game state corresponding to our win falls under the first subset. So, if we can guarantee that
all our turn ends with a game state in the first subset and all the opponent's turns end with game
states in the second set, we prevent the opponents from ever winning and will eventually win the
game as well (certain win strategy). Notice that if a player manages to force the game state into
that of the first subset, any move made by the other player will force the game into the second
subset, which allows the initial player to once again to force the game state into that of the first
subset by mimicking the other player's move. This is precisely the certain-win strategy. Since
nothing is stopping the first player of the game to force this strategy. Thus, with perfect play, the
player that goes first is guaranteed to win. ✞✞✞

Forcing 1st player’s moves
Let Kidou start as player 1. To seize the certain-win strategy, he will move 5 squares in the 10-
row to equalize the coin separation in both rows. See figure xxx. Suppose that Hal now moves 2
squares in the 10-row and reduces the separation in that row from 3 squares to 1. Now, since
Kidou would want to follow the certain-win strategy, Kidou will have to move 2 squares in the 5-
row to maintain equal separation. This pattern is universal in this game after Kidou’s first move.
However many squares Hal moves in either of the rows on his turn, Kidou must move the same
number of squares in the opposite row. This is what we mean by the 1st player must mirror the
movements of the 2nd player. Hal can essentially control Kidou’s movements throughout the
game with this trick and he can engineer the precise game state that he wants to trap Kidou using
the 6-72 creamer trap. ✞✞✞
