Baku's Tracking of Hal Throughout STL and Leader's Counter to The Friendship Trap – An Analysis

(I've posted these same images like 1 billion times I swear 🥀😭 What would I do without STL to waffle esoteric nonsense about 🥹)

Yes, Friendship Trap is kinda a cheesy name for a strategy but it is of vital significance for the lore, trust 🙏

I believe that throughout STL, Baku is shown to be purposefully keeping track of Hal's presence throughout the gamble, eventually concluding that he was erased during Round 2. Simultaneously, Leader is attempting to conceal the disappearance of Hal from the superorganism and manipulate Baku in turn.

As a brief reminder of what the Friendship Trap is: it is something mentioned in Chapter 2 of The Prince Bee Book, the idea that Baku will set a trap to win the 'second duel' (second STL). This involves purposefully targeting Hal within Leader's Perfection, using their closeness and Hal's inclination towards gambling to his advantage.

Baku intentionally hints at Leap Second early on in STL, hoping that Hal will figure it out first, and the Alien (the manifestation of Leader's perfectionist desire) will erase the 'wedge' Hal, fearing this trap. Hal is a double-edged sword to Baku: he is the easiest to manipulate and read, but Hal also knows Baku the best also, and is the most capable of countering Leap Second, so Baku devises this trap to get rid of Hal during the gamble, and have him return after his monthly memory loss.

As planned, Hal is erased during his first near-death during Round 2. However, Baku has no way of confirming it yet, hence why when he realises that Leader's death has opened the LS route to him, he fears that Hal has seen through his plan (image 1). From Leader's perspective in image 2, he tries to access Hal's thoughts, only for Hal's memories to suddenly disperse when he tries to focus on them. It is at approximately Round 4 that Leader begins to realise that Hal has been erased, and so begins to conceal this to try and manipulate Baku using this idea.

Leader claims that he is trying to surpass the bond between them (image 3), claiming that it is his 'destiny' to win the gamble. Now this might seem counterintuitive, as Hal specifically out of all of Leader's personalities grew disillusioned with the idea of destiny during his time with Baku as a teenager, eventually resolving to throw away his responsibilities to become the 'True Hal' (image 4); Baku notices this and is immediately perplexed, suspecting that Hal might have been erased (image 5).

However, as shown during second Bookstore, the 'Hal' now is very different from past Hal (image 6), and so Leader is essentially trying to portray Hal as under the control of the superorganism, aligning with the rest of Leader's beliefs. Leader is trying to say that Hal is now beyond the reach of Baku's manipulations, which will play into Leader's outward dominance over Baku during the later rounds.

Baku is shown to be skeptical (images 7,8), testing Leader with remarks that the gamble has 'nothing to do with destiny' to see if Hal is still present. Unphased, Leader stops Baku from walking past him, ridiculing him by waving his finger and claiming that Baku's 'destiny' is 'horrifyingly clear'. Given Hal's personality and need to surpass him, Baku concludes that Hal must be bluffing to try and weaken Baku's flow, which is currently dominating the match.

Baku goes on to achieve an instant check during Round 5 (image 9) and is overwhelmed with happiness at first. Even though Baku was still trying to die to enter the Leap Second route, considering that Baku doesn't believe that Hal has seen through LS yet, he views this as basically a free perfect check. However, this joy immediately disappears when Leader reveals that he knew Baku was trying to die on purpose, believing that Hal has figured out LS and perhaps even evaded the Friendship Trap.

Despite this initial panic, it becomes clear to Baku that Leader has misinterpreted Baku's objective as that of the 3 Near Deaths strategy once he begins to explain his reasoning, leading to Baku regaining his confidence, seeing this all as a desperate attempt to one-up him (image 10), once again assuring Baku that Hal is still present. This idea is further solidified by Leader's bold claim that he will use 3ND himself, a risky strategy very unlike a Leader that claims he will win perfectly without dying, and this very much like Hal, using his rival's own strategy against him.

In reality, Leader's claim of dying first is just part of his bluff during Round 5 Turn 2, using echolocation to check at 15 seconds. Leader once again reinforces his fabrication of Hal's presence by claiming he has 'always wanted to one-up Baku since the day they met' (image 11). Leader continues with the rest of his Endurance Battle strategy, letting Baku die during Round 6.

It is in images 12/13 that Baku begins to see through Leader's deception; after all, Baku has been led to believe that Hal craves the feeling of dominating Baku in a battle of wits, and yet when he looks over at Leader during his second near death, he looks almost disinterested, a clear difference with during Baku's first ND, where Hal bluffed that he had tampered with the drug to win the match, trying to worsen Baku's mental state as much as possible. The cracks in Leader's acting are beginning to show, so he eventually abandons Hal fabrication during the later rounds.

Baku finally sees through Hal fabrication, addressing Leader as 'Kiruma Souichi' (image 14) instead of Hal (Baku often refers to Leader as Hal regardless of his state, so calling Leader by his real name likely has some significance), stating that Souichi cannot win if he 'cannot read his opponent'. Well, hasn't Leader been reading Baku's moves perfectly so far?

Well in this case, Baku is referring to Hal no longer being present; as discussed earlier, Hal knows Baku better than all other personalities, so by getting rid of him, Leader cannot 'read' Baku. And by 'read', Baku specifically means seeing through Leap Second. By getting rid of Hal, who knew about Leap Second as early as round 2 as Baku planned with the Friendship Trap, Baku will instead be the one to 'read', tricking Leader at the 'last moment'.

Skipping ahead to Round 7 Turn 2, Baku's visualisation of Hal, Hachina and Souichi while explaining his echolocation reasoning gives us the best insight into Baku's awareness over Leader's different personalities (images 15-19): initially, Souichi, Hal and Hachina were working together to facilitate echolocation usage, but then Hal was erased during round 2 (Hal fades into the distance after Baku finishes discussing Douji's initial manipulation to lower the volume of the speaking clock during Round 2 Turn 1), then Hachina took over until Round 7 Turn 2, and then finally Leader reached a 'God-like' state, using both of his cubes simultaneously to search through Hachina and Hal's memories, leaving only Souichi left.

And then STL continues as normal, Souichi fails his instant check during Round 8, Hal returns afterwards just as Baku planned, falls for Leap Second, Hal survives and the Prince Bee duo lives happily ever after yadda yadda.

Ok, great, brilliant. But what's even the point of concealing Hal's disappearance in the first place? Firstly, it adds yet another layer to Leader's Round 5 bluff, making it so strong both logically and psychologically, even fooling Baku by manipulating his perception of Hal's presence. Being able to control his exact accumulation is a crucial part of Leader's Endurance Battle strategy, so by ensuring that Baku drops instantly, he can safely check at 15 seconds and guarantee that same time in accumulation.

Additionally, it tricks Baku into wasting his time with his manipulations,  such as during in Round 4 Turn 2, when Baku tried to repeat his trick with walking in front of him like in Round 1, only for Leader to stop him and use echolocation, achieving a successful check. It is much easier for Leader to mask echolocation under the guise of Hal simply 'mocking' Baku, allowing him to more precisely control when he figures it out, and hence giving him time to consider how Baku might counter it.

Overall, this is just a nice bit of psychological nuance to add to an already goated arc, so I thought I would make a brief analysis covering it. I'm willing to debate/discuss/provide my reasoning for any points here and regarding my overall interpretation of STL.
