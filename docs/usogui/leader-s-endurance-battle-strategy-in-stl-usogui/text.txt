Leader's Endurance Battle Strategy

Since I'm now thoroughly convinced that 2SD is debunked, I've written a small analysis/speculation piece to try and explain what Leader's overall strategy was through STL. I posted it on the Usogui sub earlier with fairly good reception, so I'd like to know what you guys think of it.

Hal's Strategy/Context:

Hal plans to use knowledge of leap second to manipulate Baku by making him predictable by opening leap second route to him (Image 1). His attempts to implement this strategy were stopped after his first near-death, with the Alien repressing Hal and his memories (Image 2) after fearing that Hal is being manipulated by Baku.

Leader's Endurance Battle Strategy:

Leader experiments with echolocation before round 2 (Image 3). After removing Hal from Leader's personalities and thus erasing leap second from his memories, Leader is forced to conclude that Baku is using the 3ND strategy later on since the premises to realize leap second are forgotten.

Leader first starts to become 'God-like' in round 4, dropping after 7 seconds, first hoping to bait Baku into self-destruction, and then dropping to observe if Baku will check early or not, thus confirming whether or not Baku has an underlying plan or trick (Image 4). Leader also tricks the referees into lowering the volume of the speaking clock so that he can use echolocation (Image 5).

In round 5, Baku tries to die to enter leap second route but is prevented by Leader: from Leader's perspective, the 3ND strategy has been confirmed (Image 6), and using it, Leader can predict Baku's future moves by making him desperate to enter leap second route, despite the premises of 3ND being technically false. Leader then sets up his bluff to get Baku to instant drop nest turn (Image 7). During Turn 2, Baku believes Leader's bluff and drops instantly (Image 8). Leader uses this opportunity to further his echolocation trap and checks at 15 seconds.

Leader lets Baku die to enter Leap second route during round 6 Turn 1. Since Leader has been doing risky checks recently, Leader concludes that Baku will believe that he will take a breather and go for the safe strategy (Image 9), so Leader knows that Baku will drop early to accumulate his drug more.

Leader uses echolocation at 8 seconds and checks successfully. Since Baku can't risk failing his check again for numerous reasons he takes the safe strategy during Round 7. Leader knows that Baku can't afford to die again, so is free to accumulate 60 seconds into his container. Leader then uses his echolocation trap to limit his accumulation to 1 second.

During Round 8 turn 1, Leader baits Baku into self-destruction for 5 seconds before dropping, accumulating 55 seconds. This is when Baku takes advantage of Leader's perfect state (Image 10,11), predicting another instant check and tricking Leader into making a mistake right as he is about to figure out the truth: that Baku has been to the Arizuka Building and had  orchestrated all of the events up to that point (Image 12,13,14).

From this mistake, Leader's strategy comes to an end, and upon his revival, Hal's subconscious allows him to accumulate 60 more seconds into Baku's container. Leader then falls for leap second and while unable to be revived within the time limit, still manages to survive.

What I find interesting is in images 15 and 16, due to the difference between what facts 'only the two of them know" and only "he (Leader) knows". In images 17 and 18 we can clearly see that Baku understands the entirety of Leader's echolocation trap spanning multiple rounds, with this being a fact that "only the two of them know". Despite this, Leader is stated to be the only one aware of "how insanely difficult the path to arrive at the decision of making that instant check was". So what is this "insanely difficult path"?

On the surface it might just seem that Leader had to be extremely careful, as if he made a mistake, he would lose his perfection and memories, hence the "narrow thread". But why wouldn't Baku know about this? Baku is well aware by now of Leader's memory loss and Perfectionism; he intentionally forces Leader to make a mistake in round 8 so that he loses his memories.

So it seems quite unlikely that Baku would be unaware of how risky this "path" is for Leader. Therefore, I conclude that while Baku expected Leader to try and basically 'brute force' STL (Image 19), Leader's survival is a manifestation of Baku's fears, so his words are actually just how Baku expects Leader to act), Leader actually formed his own strategy spanning rounds 4-8 to place him in an advantageous position to initiate an endurance battle against Baku.

This is the main narrative piece of evidence that supports the idea of Leader predicting everything between rounds 4 and 8. His thoughts process from round 4 would go like this: If Baku drops early, he is trying to die intentionally -> 3ND theory is confirmed -> Leader knows that Baku will try to die in the next round ->   Leader can now use echolocation to begin establishing a time wedge to create a one second check during a later round -> Leader has already noticed Yakou's worsening condition and can use this as a bluff for round 5 to limit his accumulation -> He can let Baku die during round 6, forcing him to use the safe strategy during subsequent rounds as dying too close together could be fatal -> Leader is free to accumulate a full 60 seconds during round 7 -> Leader knows that Baku expects him to take a breather during round 6 Turn 2, prompting him to drop early, so Leader can limit his accumulation via echolocation -> Leader can do an instant check during round 7 using his echolocation trap -> Leader can accumulate another large amount during round 8 since he knows Baku will take the safe strategy (note that by around this point Hal's memories begin to re-emerge, so he could also be subconsciously aware of leap second, and is close to figuring it out consciously) -> Leader can predict that Baku will do another instant drop during round 8 (obviously he makes a mistake here and fails) and therefore get another 1 second check -> Leader is basically guaranteed to win the endurance battle.

All of these predictions were made to ensure that Leader would win the endurance battle of cylinders once Leader confirmed his suspicions over 3ND, as well as Baku's hidden time trick. If Leader's instant check during round 8 was successful, the difference between the cylinders would have been so great that Leader wouldn't even have to worry about Baku's time trick, and would figure out leap second in time, and then win the gamble with a resounding victory.

Leader's plan was so close to perfection, but one mistake ruined it all. Of course, I'm not suggesting that Leader processed all of these steps during round 4, but I believe he built it up rapidly once he confirmed 3ND.

To summarise, these are all the tactics/reasoning Leader prepared in advance for each round to ensure he wins the eventual endurance battle: R4T2) Leader can begin his echolocation trap setup, while also limiting his accumulation by using echolocation at 30 seconds. R5T1) Leader will drop instantly to prevent Baku from dying as per 3ND, while also setting up his bluff. R5T2) Leader bluffs to make Baku drop instantly, and uses echolocation at 10 seconds to both further his timing wedge and limit his accumulation further. R6T1) Leader will let Baku die to make him predictable and make him take the safe strategy later on. R6T2) Leader will use echolocation and limit his accumulation to 8 seconds (Leader begins to remember leap second due to Hal's re-emergence, further justifying his belief that Baku will continue to drop instantly). R7T1) Leader knows Baku will sue the safe strategy, so is free to accumulate 60 seconds. R7T2) 1 second check using echolocation trap. R8T1) Baku will use safe strategy, Leader is free to accumulate further or perhaps try and make Baku fail his check and finish him off that way. R8T2) Baku will continue to drop instantly given his pattern, giving Leader another 1 second check (failure). After this, Leader just needs to initiate the resilience battle.

Additional Theory: Leader Was Aware of the actions he would take after he lost his memories.

While checking immediately during R8T2, Leader did expect a successful check, thus he certainly did make a mistake. However, his words suggest that he also had a backup if he made a mistake as well (Image 20), hence why the check was made 'after deliberation' (notice that this is also the title of the chapter, suggesting its significance).

As he approached round 8, Leader's Hal persona was beginning to re-emerge ("Today is the first time I managed to be one step ahead of you"), so he may have subconsciously regained some memory of leap second. The reason why Leader is so confident that it will be his win after he wakes up regardless of losing his memories is that he knows that Hal will still have the subconscious influence of leap second in his mind, thus guaranteeing that he will accumulate a full 60 seconds in Baku's cylinder.

After that, he will fall for leap second (not on purpose), and survive with two seconds of deviation. I'm suggesting that Leader predicted the events of round 9 well in advance of him making the mistake, and fortunately due to him limiting his accumulation in rounds 6 and 7, those 2 seconds deviation could be seized by him to survive. He did not intentionally create those two seconds, but was merely aware that they existed, and so could take advantage of them in the event that he should make a mistake.

So to add onto Leader's thought process, near to round 8 he would have thought: Baku is desperate to get the flow back, so will try and force Leader to make a mistake -> Leader is crushing Baku's accumulation attempts, so he might try and make him fail his check instead -> he suspects that Baku will drop instantly during round 8 -> even if he doesn't drop instantly, and Leader loses his memory, since Hal is beginning to re-emerge, he is aware that Hal will remain after Leader loses his perfection -> Hal has memories of leap second -> Hal's subconscious will influence him to accumulate 60 seconds in Baku's cylinder during round 9 -> due to rounds 6 and 7, if Hal dies during round 9 (which Leader suspects giving the timing of the round at 8:59, Baku's trick is related to time, he is nervous about 9:00 on the stopwatch, the stopwatch is shown on Leader's memory cube as he loses his perfection, proving that he is aware of it) as well, he will be left with 2 seconds of deviation (he did not create these intentionally, they merely exist for him to "seize" should he make a mistake) -> Hal will at minimum survive the gamble due to not going overboard -> at best, Hal revives, initiates another endurance battle, therefore winning the gamble.

I believe that this is the "deliberation" Leader had over his instant check during round 8. But the evidence for this is literally one sentence, and that words from that sentence are one of the chapter titles, so are probably important, but still take all of this with tremendous skepticism.
