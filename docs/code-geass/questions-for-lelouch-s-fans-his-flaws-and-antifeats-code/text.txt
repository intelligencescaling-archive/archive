Questions for Lelouch's fans

**Lelouch's character flaws and mistakes:**

Hello everyone! I know that there are a lot of people in this community who like Lelouch. I often see people putting him above Ayanokoji and even Akiyama, which seems strange to me after watching the anime. I'm not a Lelouch hater, and I don't intend this post to be a serious debunking of his feats. However, during my time watching the anime, I've accumulated a significant number of questions, as there are numerous unexplained and illogical aspects, as well as foolish actions by characters, in the Code Geass storyline. I want to share my observations and invite you to correct me if I'm wrong.

Throughout the story, we are shown certain traits and personalities of Lelouch: he is motivated by revenge for his mother and the desire to build a perfect world for his sister, he is a brilliant strategist who thinks 10 steps ahead and destroys Britain's best generals in battle, he has perfect emotional control and is capable of killing his brother Clovis. It seems that we have been presented with a certain image, but the subsequent story actively contradicts it. In episode 8 of season 1, during a conversation with Euphemia, he says, "You're still the same..." which is almost a direct acknowledgment of their familiarity. This wasn't a clever tactic or deliberate misinformation; it was simply a moment of emotional vulnerability. He made a foolish mistake, which he later acknowledged.

Further, if he wants to create a new world for his sister and uncover the secret of his mother's death, why does he agree to Nunnally's proposal to create an autonomous zone for Japan and question whether people need Zero? Firstly, this action negatively affects the search for Lelouch's mother's killer. Secondly, Nunnally's decision to abandon Zero is also negative, as she is essentially trapped in a cage. He's certainly safe right now and can do whatever he wants, but anything can happen at any time, and she could be removed from her position. In other words, her current situation is an illusion that Lelouch could dispel if he had any foresight.

Next, in episode 19 of season 1, Lelouch arrives on the Island of the Gods with Euphemia. Due to Lelouch's mistake in episode 8, she recognizes him, and he removes his mask, leading to a dialogue that doesn't hold much significance for us. However, after this, Lelouch spends an entire day wandering around the island without a mask! Although he has no idea: has someone else moved to this island (by the way, yes, it is), are there British military on the island looking for the princess, will a search helicopter appear now and so on. He doesn't know any of this for sure, but he absolutely doesn't care about hiding his identity.

At the same time, Lelouch often seems to forget about his Geass. For example, why didn't he make Cornelia his slave when they first met in episode 8 of season 1? They were literally standing in front of each other, and Lelouch did the same thing with Jeremiah. It's important to note that at that time, no one knew about Lelouch's abilities, and the order could have been phrased as a simple conversation. Similarly, why didn't Lelouch order Schneizel to become his servant? Most importantly, he did it much later, although he could have done it at the first meeting, when all he had to do was stand on the right side and no one would notice. However, there are no restrictions in Gease, and Lelouch often uses it even off-screen. How did Cornelia let them leave?

At the end of episode 8 of season 1, Lelouch and his team went out on a boat in the middle of the lake to give an interview, and in the next episode they were fine. The question is, how did they get away? The military is waiting for them on the shore. Explain to me what this is if it's not a plot armor.

**The myth of Lelouch's strategic and tactical genius:**

Here is the criticism primarily from the point of view of methodology. Lelouch's military strategies are very abstract, he explains them very superficially. He does not explain in detail his plans, he does not explain why it is necessary to strike from a certain flank and so on. Plus, a lot of Lelouch's tactical instructions look like this: "Surround the enemy and finish him off!" - it does not look like something ingenious. Therefore, Lelouch's military genius is very much based on pure narration.

This is not to mention that there are a lot of pianos in the bushes in the anime, for example, in episode 2 of season 2, Lelouch finds a train with robots (he never explained how he found this train, how he captured it and what he was doing there in general). At the same time, all robots in the anime need an access code, but not for these! At the same time, these British robots are not tracked yet! Why doesn't the British army see them on the radar? Why is the tracking system disabled on robots? which are delivered to the battlefield? The British army will certainly try to take some measures, but to no avail...

**Stupid characters all around:**

I also want to point out that Lelouch's genius is heavily based on the fact that everyone around him is an idiot. For example, in the 8th episode of the first season, a hotel was taken hostage, and one of the hostages was Euphemia (the Princess of Britain). The best British generals were ordered to rescue the hostages (I'll remind you that according to the story, Britain has conquered 1/3 of the world, so the generals of the army should be strategic geniuses), and guess what they decided to do? Blow up the building's support so that it sinks into the water within 8 minutes, and during those 8 minutes, the military must rescue the princess? At this point, I can't contain my emotions. What a ridiculous idea!? The generals didn't decide on the exact details of the operation, but their plan was to flood the building and somehow rescue all the hostages within 8 minutes. This "plan" puts the hostages at risk and will result in significant financial losses. Euphemia, who should be their top priority, could be killed in the ensuing chaos, by terrorists who would be enraged and shoot hostages, or by a military mistake. I'll repeat, according to the plot, this is the best that the famous British generals could come up with!

Here's another example. Schneizel has the opportunity to shoot Lelouch's ship and win. What do you think he does? That's right, he doesn't shoot! Justifying it by saying it's... greed?

Also, the British army knew about a whole network of underground tunnels that the revolutionaries used, but they didn't do anything about it. Why? Because they didn't have the money, and the Japanese might start a rebellion, which would lead to China's involvement. Are you serious? Britain, which has conquered a third of the world, can't manage to control its territories after seven years? And yes... The same China that has always favored Britain and was repelled by a couple of Nightmares.

If you think these are rare examples, you're wrong! This is literally the entire anime. Explain to me why so many people rate Lelouch so highly when he has more antifeats in the anime than Joe Goldberg in Season 5? He either makes a super-genius move or makes a completely stupid mistake.
