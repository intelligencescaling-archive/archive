"Code Geass" and its remarkable protagonist, Lelouch. Code Geass, an anime that premiered in 2006, is set in an alternate reality where the Britannian Empire has conquered Japan, renaming it Area 11. The story follows Lelouch Lamperouge, a former prince of Britannia who acquires the power of "Geass," enabling him to command others with absolute obedience. Motivated by a desire for revenge against his father and a longing to create a better world for his sister, Nunnally, Lelouch embarks on a mission to dismantle the imperial regime and liberate Japan from its oppressive rule. Through his transformation into the masked vigilante Zero, he leads a rebellion known as The Black Knights, navigating complex moral dilemmas and personal sacrifices along the way. Without further delay, let's dive right in. I will focus ONLY on on-screen games and chess related feats.

COMMENT: In general, this game should have portrayed Lelouch as an exceptionally strong chess player in the eyes of the audience. Did it succeed? If we disregard the chess semantics, then yes, undoubtedly. However, upon closer examination, while the speed with which he assessed the position and identified the best move is indeed impressive, it becomes clear that his old teacher did most of the work for him. Lelouch merely executed a checkmating maneuver, which diminishes the overall impact. Since this was their first episode, the creators opted for a safer approach than in other works by featuring a game of two real chess masters, a decision for which they deserve commendation; they executed this task remarkably well.

NOTE: If you think about it properly, then the whole game was essentially played against himself by Lelouch. Of course, it is possible that I do not thoroughly understand the plot; in the future, you can correct me if necessary, and I will make adjustments. Mao actually did not lie when he claimed to be a beginner in chess; yes, Lelouch has every right to say that he is not a beginner, but this was true only until he learned about Mao’s ability to read minds with his unique Geass. It is also fair to say that Lelouch was emotional throughout the entire encounter and was not only focused on chess but also on who was sitting in front of him and what plan Mao had devised against him. It's reasonable to assert that Mao could have anticipated moves that Lelouch might think of, which could lead to mistakes or blunders. Mao couldn’t have won if Lelouch had remained calm, because, as mentioned earlier, it turns out that Lelouch was effectively playing against himself. Therefore, technically, the game should have ended in a draw; however, due to his lack of emotional control, he lost…

Nunnally Held Hostage
[ Season 1, Episode 16 ] At Ashford, Lelouch has Suzaku stay with Nunnally. Afterwards, Lelouch goes back up, finding Mao (who is complaining to himself about all the students' minds) inside a chapel. Mao questions why Lelouch hasn't done anything to stop Mao physically, Lelouch ignores this question and goes straight to a point and it is revealed that Mao called him up there personally for a "game", which Mao reveals to be chess, a rematch. Mao then explains the rules of the chess game: it matters not whether checkmate is called but rather how many pieces are taken. Once the pieces are taken they are placed on respective sides of a scale. If Mao gets enough pieces, a switch will trigger the bomb and detonate it; however, if Lelouch gets enough pieces, another switch will disarm the bomb.
