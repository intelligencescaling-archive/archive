Now, a supercomputer is a type of computer that has a high level of performance compared to a general purpose computer. These machines are designed to perform extremely complex tasks that require large amounts of calculations, such as scientific simulations, large-scale data analysis, climate modeling, nuclear research, and cryptography, among others.
Supercomputers are characterized by having thousands of processors working in parallel, a large amount of memory, and high-speed storage systems. They are essential tools in scientific research and engineering, allowing scientists and engineers to solve problems that would be impossible or impracticable on more conventional computers. The speed of a supercomputer is measured in terms of its processing capacity, which is usually expressed in petaFLOPS (peta Floating Point Operations Per Second), that is, how many quadrillion (10^15) floating point operations it can perform per second. . The world’s fastest supercomputers can reach speeds exceeding one hundred petaFLOPS, and some are approaching exaFLOPS (10^18 floating point operations per second).
For example, by 2021-2022, supercomputers such as Fugaku in Japan and Summit in the United States were at the top of the list of fastest supercomputers. Fugaku achieved a speed of over 442 petaFLOPS in floating point performance tests, making it an extremely powerful tool for compute-intensive tasks such as molecular simulations, climate studies, and large-scale scientific data analysis. In comparison, a typical personal computer with a good processor can perform around a few teraFLOPS (10^12 floating point operations per second), which shows the magnitude of the difference in processing power between a supercomputer and a conventional PC. This makes supercomputers indispensable tools in fields that require enormous amounts of mathematical calculations, providing the power needed to carry out research that would otherwise be impossible.
