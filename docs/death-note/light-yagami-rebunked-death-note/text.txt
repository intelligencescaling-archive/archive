Light Yagami rebunked

I know I've been hating on Light a lot, but that's only because it's the truth and Light is overrated. This post will be a break from my usual Death Note hate, because I don't blindly hate on characters that don't deserve the hate.

Here is the post I want to debunk: [https://www.reddit.com/r/deathnote/comments/1pcr26q/why\_is\_he\_so\_naive/](https://www.reddit.com/r/deathnote/comments/1pcr26q/why_is_he_so_naive/)

The main argument from the post can be summarized as such: **"Light is naive surrounding topics like morality and justice. If he did his research (studies, books, etc.), he would know that killing criminals doesn't change the world for the better!"**

1: reading a bunch of research papers is going to get Light nowhere because there were no research papers available for him to study the effects of what he wanted to implement with the Death Note. It was the first time in public human history that a death note was even available as a tool. Research on the correlation between law and crime rates is subject to heavy external interference from other factors that change over time such as education and health. This makes it unreliable for Light who has a tool that can kill with faster rates, over a larger scope (global), and without external factors like corruption and nepotism getting in the way. MORE IMPORTANTLY, Light's long-term plan involved accidental deaths rather than heart attacks for lesser crimes which would exert a supernatural image in the minds of people, which is a far stronger deterrence because **people would fear getting caught more when they assume they're dealing with something supernatural that isn't physically grounded and evadable**. For these reasons, bringing up research papers to call Light's methodology naive is itself naive because there is a vast chasm between the available research on changes in corporal punishment laws and the method Light wanted to use.

2: the idea that capital punishment cannot deter crime according to research is not even true. It might be the result you get if you cherry pick a bunch of websites for your high school assignment, but if you research a little deeper into sociology and psychology, you will also find research that indicates the complete opposite, such as "In Defense of Flogging" by Peter Moskos which is a book that defends corporal punishment as being a more effective deterrent of crime compared to something like imprisonment.

3: for you to say in your post that Light "chooses instead to put all that blame on those less fortunate" goes to show that your post is politically biased. Yes, maybe some of the criminals that Light killed were falsely accused, or maybe they had a terrible upbringing which led them to crime. But the aim of Light (or at least his external goal) is not just to judge the bad guys, but also to prevent other people from committing crime. In this case, the upbringing of the criminal or the factors that led them to crime are irrelevant to Light's ideals. What he wants is to repel other people away from committing crime, as well as to get rid of the existent criminals so that the good people remaining are the ones influencing each other. This is why Light didn't kill people who accidentally murdered without intent. It is human psychology that people imitate those around them, so if there are less criminals for people to learn from, people themselves will be less likely to commit crime. If you want a more concrete example of the effects of removing criminals from public society, there is El Salvador's massive crackdown on criminals which led to one of the most significant historical drops in crime rates. Many "experts" brought up morality and complained about the method taken to reduce the crime rates, but if you look at the actual results, the difference in crime rates prior (103 per 100k in 2015) vs after (1.9 per 100k in 2024) is significant. Therefore, the argument that some of the criminals Light killed were just people who happened to be less fortunate is irrelevant even if it was true.

I don't think the way Light went about achieving his goal was the best, but it was far more rational than doing something like cherry picking research papers to enlighten yourself to the conclusion that criminals are actually just unfortunate victims and Light would have produced greater results by burning the Death Note and becoming a political activist for peace. I don't think Light was perfect, but the result he achieved was far more than what most of you people would have achieved, especially people who think killing criminals is just putting blame on the less fortunate. That being said, I do think many criminals are falsely accused, and far more criminals do what they do as a result of their upbringing, so don't assume this analysis is based on a simplistic understanding of criminals as being definite bad guys.

**Additional argument people could make against Light: why did Light write down so many names into the notebook at the start? He should've waited a month after killing Kurou so that nobody can link him to Japan.**

One of the justifications for why Light spammed so many names into the notebook during the first 5 days was because he expected the owner of the death note to come back to claim his soul. This is why he told Ryuk "I've been waiting for you" and also "I used the notebook knowing it belonged to a shinigami... and now the shinigami's here. What happens to me now..? You take my soul or something?" This is why he was shocked when the shinigami said the Death Note now belongs to him.

Given that he expected to soon be disposed of by the shinigami, Light was only choosing to maximize the remaining time by writing as many criminal names as he could. It was inevitable that he ignored the information he would give away during these 5 days, since he thought the shinigami would be the obstacle that would soon take care of him rather than other humans. Of course, this does not completely justify his lack of caution, and the imperfection of his methods, so you can still make other arguments to show superior alternatives Light could have taken, but it is still valid reasoning for Light to do what he did given his inexperience and the information he had access to from his own perspective.
