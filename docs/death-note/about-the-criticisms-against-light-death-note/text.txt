About the criticisms against Light

Today I wanted to make a post about the criticisms people have regarding Light. Honestly, I can't say this post will be completely objective because I genuinely love Light as a character, so I might step out of objectivity in some places. And additionally, if you don't agree with my opinions, you can express it in the comments section.

The topic I will talk about today is that the majority of criticisms about Light are of low quality. Generally, the criticisms about Light consist of 2 subjects: Light's ego or anti-feats, and Light's luck. I am not going to make a ridiculous claim here like "Light wasn't lucky" or "he has no anti-feats"; I will talk about how people unnecessarily exaggerate these situations here.

First, let's start with the anti-feats or the ego problem.

There are people who say Light couldn't control his emotions well, made a lot of mistakes throughout the series, the characters he is compared to don't make such mistakes, so Light would lose to some characters and because of these anti-feats, Light actually doesn't deserve his position in SCD and is overrated. I don't really agree with this situation, but why?

Reason 1:

Light of course makes mistakes in the series, but people don't consider this: Light goes through character development throughout the series. For example, the broadcast trap he fell for at the beginning of the series is one of the topics people criticize the most, but after this broadcast trap, Light almost never makes an abnormal mistake. Of course, sometimes he yields to his emotions, but these don't create problems. For example, when L tells him he is L, he goes home and gets really angry, but does this have an effect? No. Or situations like Misa going to prison are entirely because of Misa's mistakes. I mean, Misa makes so many ridiculous mistakes throughout the series that Light naturally falls into bad situations. Well, does Light make zero mistakes after the broadcast trap? No, he does. For example, the raid he did on the SPK. This is a mistake, but even this mistake is not that big of a mistake in my opinion. For those who don't remember this raid incident: Mello lures Mogi to where Near is. When Light realizes he fell into a trap, he can't control his emotions and makes the people who support Kira raid the SPK. But think about it like this, Light's logic is actually not that bad either.

Let's think about it, there are so many people who are Kira supporters. If I make these people raid the SPK, how long would it take them to enter the SPK? In my opinion, maybe 20 minutes max or it could be shorter. Near needs to do something such that he can distract that many people there, and Near uses money for this. Of course, Light should have thought that Near had money, but I think it's not that surprising for him to think Near wouldn't have enough money to give to that many people. I'm not saying this isn't an anti-feat, just that it's not a very, very big anti-feat. Other than that, Light generally controls his emotions quite well throughout the series, except for the last part, and doesn't make many mistakes. Some people say Light talked very stupidly in the last part; people think he could pull Matsuda or the others to his side, and they claim Light talked stupidly here because he couldn't control his emotions. But this is also absurd. For example, if someone tried to kill you, someone who worked right next to you for a very long time, would you believe them? I think nobody would. So no matter how much Light talked there, I think he couldn't have pulled anyone to his side.

Let's come to the 2nd reason: Luck.

I personally am not saying Light was unlucky, but people really exaggerate this situation. I also think Light was between 10-15% luckier than L in his struggle against L thanks to the Notebook. While L made all his moves within a certain limit, Light could act more freely. Or Light encountering Naomi Misora was luck too, I admit, but at least Light had feats in these parts, or he still had unlucky situations throughout his clash with L. For example, Light encountering Naomi Misora was luck, agreed, but Light at least manipulates Naomi Misora in a truly impressive way.

Some people think this manipulation is luck, but let's think about it from Light's point of view:

"The woman in front of me is a woman who recently lost her fiancé. It's obvious she wants revenge for this since she went this far. If this woman sees a ray of hope, she might believe it. When I came down just now to give my dad's clothes to the police, she must have definitely heard that I have supported cases before. Not just that, she probably wouldn't think I would like Kira, who forces my dad to work this much. If I act really well and, with my previous thoughts about Kira (Light told Naomi he thought Kira could control people's actions), assuming I can at least prove my ability too, I can actually learn this woman's name."

And it's really not a bad idea. Light acts quite well here anyway. The police previously saying he had supported cases before, and Light bringing up an impressive idea about the Kira case, makes it logical for Naomi to believe Light. Assuming Naomi has a hard time controlling her emotions because she lost her fiancé, Light displays a pretty good feat here. In the parts where he is lucky, he displays feats in categories like Thinking, Reasoning, Manipulation. And additionally, Light falls into such bad situations because of the ridiculous mistakes Misa makes against L that seriously, I sometimes tell people they need to see these bad lucks of Light. Also, L has as much experience as Light's lifespan. I mean, I think Light displays a pretty good performance against a guy who is much, much more experienced than him. Of course, I am not claiming this: I don't have a claim like Light wasn't luckier. But I think people exaggerate even Light's luck situation too much. We can't even say Light was lucky in the Near part anyway. Of course, they didn't lack luck in the Near part, for example, Takada being chosen as the spokesperson or Mikami actually turning out to be smart. But Light had such ridiculous bad luck, for example, Shinigami Sidoh suddenly appearing and saving Mello. This is such an unlucky situation for Light, think about it like this: If Mello didn't live, Mello couldn't have trapped Mogi. If Mogi didn't fall into the trap, Aizawa wouldn't have gone to Near. And if Aizawa didn't go to Near, the name Light Yagami wouldn't have come up. Not only that, there is also the situation of very critical information passing to Near, like the fake rules info Mello told Near. So in short, Light wasn't even as lucky throughout the series as people think.

In conclusion, what I mean is this: Does Light have mistakes? Yes. Does he have lucky moments? Yes, but people really exaggerate these situations too much. Light is seen as very good even by the author of Death Note; the author even says his only problem is his confidence in himself, which he is right about, but people just throw things around without analyzing this in detail.

For example, in the databook where L's stats are (it could also be Light's section, in those parts there are not just stats, the characters' information, personalities, etc., are also written), in that part, he says something like this: he praises L's intelligence, then he sees L as a rival worthy of Light. I mean, he really doesn't see Light and L that far apart from each other. Lately, the idea that L beats Light mid diff or with less difficulty is so present that I felt the need to mention this. Of course, L is probably superior to Light, but I think people exaggerate this too.

Anyway, in short, most of the criticisms made against Light are not good. I'm thinking of doing a post like this for Akiyama soon (I hope I do). Akiyama has started getting so downplayed in SCD, especially on YouTube, that I'm thinking of doing a post about him too.

(By the way, you might not agree with my opinions, I respect that)
