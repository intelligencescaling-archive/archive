Why is L considered that good in strategy?

I was thinking about this and I'd like someone to explain. Don't get me wrong, he's not bad, but not much more than just okay, either. Frankly, to justify a high tier placement (which I've seen is fairly common for L), good strategy and planning are a must. L is decent, but none of his schemes are that complex or structured.

His best feat is probably the Lind L. Taylor trap. The effectiveness is good mainly because of the accuracy of the profiling, which is treated here as an absolute truth instead of the approximation that it should reasonably be. It probably works as a way to confirm said profile, but it could ironically make it less viable to exploit if it pushes Kira to be more careful. It's a simple mechanism too (say Kira is evil > Kira will kill Taylor). You could argue there's a second layer being that it would make sense for Kira to kill his main opposer, but there's nothing really pushing him to do it immediately other than to make an example out of him, which is also reliant on the profiling as an assumption for L to make. Aside from that, the main thing that this trap achieved was narrowing Kira down to Kanto, but there are still some issues. Kira could've been temporarily away, lived elsewhere and had a net of informants, or simply have moved out after the transmission. Not to mention, I don't see how the "transmitting to other regions after Kanto" could've worked out assuming Kira checks his forums immediately or even assuming (to make a random example) he has a friend in Kanto who banally just calls him to ask him what he thought of the transmission. I'm mostly willing to look past these things, because even then, it doesn't really deal with too many variables. It only manipulates a single agent and relies on a single simple mechanism as I mentioned (if X, then Y), and in terms of effectiveness, it's not that great either. L sacrifices the advantage of investigating secretely for this, and he did also have an extreme amount of resources as a starting point. As I said earlier, it could've also pushed Kira to become more cautious. It didn't, and you might argue this was intentional on L's part, to lure him into a game of egos (debatable, since it was never confirmed, when most moves and tactics generally are in Death Note), and I do think this scheme's accuracy in how it works is really good, but it still relies on assumptions made with a lack of information, so it could've easily gone wrong.

The second one would be the self-reveal strategy, which is basically a hail mary to temporarily tie Light's hands. It's clever, but not that elaborate. The tactic with the TV guy's name is smart, but that's about it for this strategy. Again, not bad, but are we calling this high tier?

Third, getting Soichiro to fake execute Light. Extremely simple in concept to be honest, not really anything to brag about. Decently effective I guess, though it relies on the assumption that Light could've killed Soichiro in that position, which in hindsight, thinking about how the Death Note works, I'd argue isn't the case.

Fourth and last, the plan to save Matsuda. Clever idea, execution relied on a lot of resources which L thankfully had, not much to say otherwise.

There are some other minor moves here and there which I didn't mention, as well as tactics, L's preferred method, which are admittedly fairly good, though his tactics rarely achieve much in terms of goals from what I remember. All this is to say, L hasn't shown particular capacity for strategy and planning.

I will note something, L's position naturally put him in a more reactive role, but that doesn't really mean much if you scale based on feats.

This post is all based on what I recall, so I could absolutely be wrong with anything I've said, and please correct me if I am. This is all just because I don't think L is as good in this aspect as most people seem to think he is, and I want to understand where all the hype is coming from.
