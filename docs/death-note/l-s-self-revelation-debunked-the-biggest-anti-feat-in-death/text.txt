Self Revelation DEBUNKED! L DEBUNKED! (the biggest anti feat in Death Note)

Most people believe self-revelation is a feat by L rather than an antifeat. They falsely assume the move was logical just because L gave some reasoning to justify it, and the reasoning seems complicated on the surface so people find it hard to refute.

In reality, it's an antifeat! A severe decision making mistake.

I made this post to clarify WHY it's an antifeat!

People seriously need to look beyond how far the characters themselves reason. If L gives reasoning for 1 + 1 being equal to 5, rather than immediately considering it to be true just because the author portrayed him as the smartest character in the story, you need to consider that his reasoning may be wrong and he simply committed an antifeat because the author is human and can't keep the intelligence of the characters consistent all the time, especially since unlike the characters, the author needs to focus on things like pacing, enjoyability, etc., which sometimes does not leave it in his best interests to make the characters do what is optimal.

Partial credit to my friend because I'm getting the majority of these ideas from his document (although tbh many of these ideas were pretty obvious while reading the manga). He didn't want me to name him in the post though.

I've read many documents that aim to defend the feat, but none of them have provided a successful justification so far.

**There are 5 components of L's revelation antifeats (inability to withhold/control information well).**

Component 1: L revealing he is still alive after Lind L. Tailor (semi-major antifeat).

Component 2: L revealing himself to the taskforce (extremely major antifeat).

Component 3: L revealing himself to Light (major antifeat).

Component 4: L revealing the task force to Light (major antifeat).

Component 5: L revealing the truth about the fake L behind the computer to Light (antifeat).

There are actually more scenes where L makes poor information control decisions, but those ones were not as major as these from what I remember, since the sacrifices were less drastic. In this post, I'll only explain component 2-5, but you should be able to logically reason out how problematic component 1 was as well after reading the post.

A lot of people downplay Light for falling for the Lind L. Tailor trap, but honestly Light's mistake there can even be considered mild compared to some of these severe blunders.

You can skip to any section you want, but this is a very useful learning opportunity, so I recommend you read it in its entirety to learn how to scale better, and improve your own reasoning skills, especially since there is a strong interconnectedness between these 4 decisions that L made.

**L revealing himself to the taskforce (extremely major antifeat)**

After Light exposed that he had access to information from the National Police Agency (NPA), it was revealed by L that there were 141 people who had direct access to NPA information (such as Light's father).

The immediate next in line suspects were the family members of these 141 people. They were suspected because they could have accessed the information through the NPA members as a medium (such as Light Yagami himself who got NPA information by hacking his father's computer).

For this reason, L reduced the suspect list to NPA members and their families. This was definitively confirmed once Kira killed the FBI stalking the families which implied his identity was under threat.

Statistically, the average household size in Japan around 2004 (which was when Light revealed that he is connected to the NPA) was approximately 2.55 to 2.6 members per private household.

This means that if we concentrate the suspects to the people who have access to NPA information (which is exactly what L did), then the probability that Kira is part of the 141 members of the NPA is equal to 1/2.6 = 38.5%.

However, this is only true if we assume the probability of each NPA member having NPA information is equal to the probability of their family members having access to that same top secret information. This is obviously not true, since the NPA members having access to NPA information is confirmed, meanwhile the probability their family members having access is logically at a low percentage. This is because the 2.6 members per household average includes people that may not be able to access the information from that main member. For example, if the NPA member is a man who has a baby and a wife, it is almost impossible for the baby to have gained access to the NPA information, and it is also somewhat unlikely for the wife to have access to it (possibly at around 10-20% or likely even lower).

In other words, filtering out the family members who are unlikely to be able to obtain access to NPA information from the 141 families (such as babies, etc.) and setting a reduced probability for the other family members (since police are unlikely to give top secret information to their random family members), the probability that Kira is part of the 141 members of the NPA shoots up from 38.5% to an extremely high percentage (80-95% if I'm being lenient towards L).

Conclusion: Probabilistically, with L's limited information, it is almost certain that Kira is part of the 141 members of the NPA.

This is why L revealing himself to the members remaining behind from the 141 members of the NPA was a major decision making mistake.

The reasoning L gave for making this move was that he's testing the resolve of the NPA by letting any of the officers leave if they fear Kira, so that only the genuinely righteous ones remain behind, and then he can reveal himself to these righteous officers remaining behind.

But you need to realize that the person who has the biggest incentive to remain behind is literally Kira! If he remains behind, he can finally see the faceless detective and make a breakthrough in killing L.

Since L gave an opportunity for every single NPA member to remain behind, and the probability of Kira being part of the NPA was determined to be extremely high, and Kira had every incentive to remain behind, then L was simply letting a trojan horse enter the task force (Kira himself).

L got lucky here that Kira ended up being an external member that only got the information via hacking as opposed to being a member himself that could stay behind and join the task force. If L did not get so lucky, all Kira would need to pass L's tests would be some good acting skills and adaptability. Just as L determined the remaining members of the NPA to be not guilty after going through an interview, if Kira had been part of them, he would be deemed not guilty and be able to bide his time to eventually kill L, since L revealed his resources (like Watari) to the task force.

Counterargument: it could not have been the 141 members since Kira was confirmed to be a student.

This isn't true. Light exposed the fact that he could set the timings of the deaths, which overturns this perception. In addition, the shifts of policemen can overlap well with the school timings, and since L does not know how the killing mechanism works, he cannot assume arbitrarily that the timings automatically eliminate the NPA members as suspects, especially after it was directly revealed by Light that Kira has access to NPA information.

Additional point: L trying to filter for righteous policemen does not even make the move very useful, since he's filtering out the rational policemen who choose not to place their lives at risk, and leaving behind only the overly zealous emotional people, which is why almost every member of the task force remaining behind was shown to be stupid throughout the entire story such as Ukita unplugging all the phones because he can't be bothered dealing with all the calls even though L entrusted him with them, Aizawa running off to get himself killed after seeing Ukita die then act enraged when L tried to stop him, Matsuda who goes around without orders to Yotsuba and almost ends up getting killed, etc.

Counterargument: what if L simply deduced that Kira was afraid and thus didn't stay behind?

Based on Kira's profile, it's clear that he is proactive, prideful, childish, and hates to lose, especially after Lind L. Tailor, 48 hour prisoner kill, eliminating the FBI etc., which happened prior. If Kira had been part of the NPA, the probability he remains behind is almost 100%. Kira cowering away and letting the opportunity pass would contradict his profile.

This was overall a very stupid move, unless you use hindsight to justify the move by saying that Kira luckily turned out to be an external family member who hacked his father's computer to gain access rather than being a direct member of the NPA so Kira didn't infiltrate the task force. That justification relies on hindsight from a reader's perspective though.

**L revealing himself to Light (major antifeat).**

The reasoning people use for justifying the self-revelation "feat" where L revealed his identity to Light on campus is pretty simple:

1. If Light really is Kira, the pressure L places on him would make Light break, revealing Light to be Kira.

2. However, if he isn’t Kira, Light would still be a valuable resource to track down who Kira may actually be, since Light is intelligent.

3. Additionally, if L dies after revealing himself, then Light obviously becomes the primary Kira suspect. L lied about there being other Ls (himself as a proxy) to make Light think twice about killing him.

4. L also used the fake name "Hideki Ryuga" who is a celebrity, so if Hideki Ryuga dies, L saves his own life and Light is determined to be Kira.

5. Also, since L has now revealed himself to Light, he can use his close up tricks to try and force Light to reveal himself as Kira.

These points do not justify the tradeoffs of the move.

Firstly, point 1 does not even make sense for L to hinge the plan on: L mentioned prior to the scene that Kira had reached a "divine" psychological state because he killed criminals without batting an eye. Why would someone like this psychologically break just because L reveals himself to them? The arguments about L subtly exerting psychological pressure (such as by staring at Light during the examination) doesn't make sense either since getting Light used to his presence that stands out would have been likelier to reduce his shock than increase it.

The distinction of how Light reacts is also not useful because a normal human would feel psychologically disturbed too, not just Kira. If Light had reacted with shock, that would not reveal anything about whether or not he is Kira, since it would be a perfectly normal reaction for a high school student to have in the same circumstance.

Pathway 1 (Light acts shocked): Normal reaction for an innocent student being approached by a world-famous detective and having their private information uttered.

Pathway 2 (Light acts calm): Could be Kira hiding his guilt OR could be him simply being an exceptionally poised honor student. This is the pathway Light took.

Either way, no information gained on whether he's Kira. This point is especially useless because L was trying to indict Light in court, not just figure out whether or not he is Kira.

Even if Light shouted "WHAATTT??? YOU ARE L!!!??? IT CAN'T BEEEE!!!" which is an extremely exaggerated reaction that even a Kira with horrible acting skills wouldn't do, L still wouldn't be able to use this information in court.

The second point (L needing Light's intelligence for the Kira case) also does not make a lot of sense: L called Light 'very intelligent' after Light said that the 4000 FBI agents being sent to Japan were probably a ruse to put pressure on Kira because it doesn't make sense for them to announce it on the news and let Kira know.

This is average human level reasoning in our world. At best, slightly above average. For some reason, L thought this was enough to consider Light 'very intelligent' and drag him as a student into the Kira case and the headquarters despite him being the most highly suspect person. From L's perspective, the intellectual gain should have been almost nonexistent compared to the risk.

This even clarifies how unintelligent the average character in Death Note is. Basic human level reasoning gets praised as very intelligent by the smartest guy in the verse.

This only worsens the usefulness of L's shielding (that Light would become the suspect if L or the celebrity dies), since people as unintelligent as the task force members are susceptible to deception and Kira can easily deceive them once he dies. L knew the task force was emotional and deeply deferent towards Light's dad, and the suspect's father (Light's dad) being the leader reduces the likelihood of the suspect being treated with suspicion even more, so this component of the strategy was merely a superficial shield.

In addition, deaths can always be attributed to coincidence or factors other than Light himself as the window of time after self-revelation increases, just as the task force never suspected anything wrong after L died. This is why the strategy shielding is only short-term. The deterrance of the shielding decays over time. As weeks or months pass, the causal link between L's death and Light's guilt becomes diluted, especially if L dies from a means other than a heart attack, which should have been a strong possibility to consider given how little L knows about the Death Note.

Additionally, L already knew Kira could manipulate the time of death and dictate actions prior to dying (from the prisoner experiments) so from L's perspective, Light could simply have set his time of death to 6 months later and mimick L suiciding before his heart attack or somehow giving up on the Kira case by saying that it's an actual god that has been doing the killings (or something along those lines) which isn't that hard to think of.

You could argue that Light can't actually set the timings of the deaths to 6 months later but you're using hindsight again, because L does not know this, all he knows is that Kira can set death timings. From L's perspective, Light could even have L attack the task force members, or do something to make them think he is a traitor and the true Kira, and that he was trying to scapegoat Light, before having him die.

Counterargument: Light can't kill L without his name so L's probability of dying is 0 from his perspective.

First of all, L knows almost nothing about the Death Note. What if Kira only needs a face to kill someone when there is no digital barrier? What if it depends on proximity to reduce the conditions? L doesn't know about the shinigami eyes, but these other possibilities seem like logical rules. L's assumptions were already overturned twice (when Light showed him he can set the timings of the deaths and when Light showed he can control the victims actions). Why can they not be overturned again? Why do his strategies depend so heavily on these assumptions that could easily get him killed if the plot of the story went differently?

Second of all, L is destroying his information asymmetry even if you assume he's impossible to kill for Light (which is not true from L's own perspective, because he even added the shielding to deter it).

It was a major mistake for L to bet his life on an inductive premise, when he's dealing with something supernatural which should have made him cautious about rules that he cannot deduce rather than assuming he has all the correct information and Kira has no way of killing him without his name.

L getting closer to Light and being able to implement direct tactics (such as cafe interrogation) is the only useful thing about the self-revelation strategy. The problem is that Light obtains the same advantage and crosses an even bigger distance closer to L, when previously he had 0 information on him while he was being surveilled. L may have gotten a little closer to Light, but Light got much much much closer to L than he had ever been before.

Additionally, the moves L could implement such as the cafe interrogation were only useful for his subjective indictment of Light as Kira. Subjective tells like these cannot be used in court, especially with Light's background as an honour student.

Basically L gained approximately nothing from this move, while sacrificing almost everything.

Counterargument: With this move, L reduced the suspects list from billions to only 1 person.

Debunk: This is nonsense. L only had 2 primary suspects which was the daughter of the NPA director and Light. L already used a proxy (without revealing himself) to rule out the daughter as Kira, so Light would have been the only suspect anyways even without self-revelation. In other words, L reduced the suspect list fom 1 person (Light Yagami) down to only 1 person (Light Yagami).

The self-revelation move was not effective at all, it gained almost nothing for L while sacrificing almost every major advantage he had over Kira.

Another thing L sacrificed from his own perspective was permanently destroying any chance of passive authentic surveillance, since Kira now knows L is watching him. Using hindsight, Light already knew he was being surveilled since he had the door trap and Ryuk told him of the cameras, but L didn't know that Light knows, so from his own perspective this was a sacrifice.

The tactic also wouldn't have even worked if Kira ended up having an accomplice or there were multiple people with the ability, or Kira had the ability to pass on his powers, which L could not have predicted due to the supernatural nature of the case. Sacrificing himself (one of the only people who can even deal with the case) just to make Kira do something makes no sense, he should have been prepared for these terrifying scenarios given that he's one of the only people in the world who can deal with Kira and has an absolute advantage of anonymity that severely limits Kira/s. L was too presumptive.

Counterargument: Light went home and started crashing out, calling the death note useless etc. which shows how effective the strategy was.

Debunk: That was foolish on Light's part. This is like an artisan always complaining about his tools. Light should have been celebrating that L was getting desperate and also that he's now a step closer to killing L. Not only that, he can literally infiltrate the task force and bide his time until the day when he writes down L's name with details such as "XXX continues the investigation for 22 days and gradually becomes more and more depressed as Kira continues to evade his detection, eventually committing suicide with no major clues to Kira's identity" which I think Light even thought of later on in the story.

Rather than take the severe and very probable risk of sacrificing his life, L doing nothing would have been a better strategy. While Light continues to give away more information, L could gradually come closer to catching him.

Maintaining his absolute anonymity ensures Kira cannot eliminate his sole existential threat.

But instead, L turned his entire elusive image and background into a localized physical target for Light.

**L revealing the task force to Light (major antifeat)**

Light ended up becoming the only Kira suspect. During the cafe interrogation, L even said that Light's probability of being Kira is a specific percentage, and according to the author of Death Note, L is a liar and whenever he give his percentages, he implies more than 90%. In other words, the probability that Light is Kira is more than 90%!

What does L do from here after discovering Light is more than 90% likely to be Kira? He invites Light to the task force. Literally the only remaining people who can take care of Kira in the event L dies, and L introduces Light to most of them by bringing him into the headquarters. This is like the Chinese saying "Inviting the fox into the henhouse," L just pulled in a trojan horse with the reasoning that he's keeping Light close.

Now Light has insight into the details of the investigation which completely bridges L's prior information asymmetry, allows Light proximity to manipulate his father's emotions, allows him to understand the structure of the task force, this is a basic strategic mistake. (It's like placing the primary spy suspect within the inner command post with no hard evidence to detain them which grants them real-time reconnaissance without being able to prevent their movements)

Counterargument: If L waits and does nothing, Light is going to kill more criminals in the meantime.

Debunk: Let him kill criminals in the meantime. If you throw your life away, Light is going to kill criminals his entire life.

Another sacrifice of the self-revelation strategy is that Light was previously forced to think of his battle with L as abstract which was why he kept making decisions that ended up bringing L closer and closer to him, but now that L had revealed almost everything going on in the background, the entire situation became crystal clear in Light's mind which put him on the same strategic plane as L, giving him an easier vantage point for scheming his death.

Rather than maintaining his information asymmetry and keeping Light in the dark, L threw it all away just to push the case forward somewhere.

**L revealing the truth about the fake L behind the computer to Light (antifeat)**

After Light had lost his memories, L finally started trusting him, to the point where he revealed that the L behind the computer is actually just Watari and he is the only true L.

The main barrier blocking Light from wanting to immediatley kill L was the possibility that L is just a proxy, which would make his kill not very useful since L would simply get replaced over and over by more proxies, no matter how many Light kills.

L decided to just give the biggest Kira suspect the information he needs to completely lose all his restraint and kill him. What if Light was still pretending during the memory loss arc? What if Light becomes Kira again (L even considered this possibility and admitted it was highly probable)? L gains almost no benefit in giving away this information. The only thing he even gains slightly is Light's trust since Watari's true identity could leak through the task force members, but this information itself does not make catching the new Kira (Higuchi) much easier, and the payoffs are lacking in comparison to the sacrifice of destroying the primary shielding he had against Light killing him.

You could argue that L sacrificing the information protects Near and Mello from being found out by Light, but this conclusion hinges on L dying in the first place which would only be impelled by the revelation he did here. In addition, even if Light had the "other L" behind the computer be killed without this information, he would have obtained the same information about Watari from the task force, so there's no benefit here to L revealing the information in the first place.

It's not as major as the other antifeats though, since you could argue that it was already pretty obvious to Light that the L he had been interacting with was the true L.

**Counterargument: If you're so smart, then come up with a better idea than L!**

Rather than having FBI stalk the entirety of the NPA based on luck, L could have fed subtly different pieces of false information to subsets of the 141 members. It's not that difficult to make the suspect pool shrink from 141 families down to a handful without revealing a single detail about himself by making Kira act on false information.

Also, the very fact that Kira would almost definitively be among the people remaining behind can be a logical starting point for who among the NPA to investigate, even if L did not implement the previous tactic of shrinking the suspect pool with false info. These are just a few ideas I'm coming up with on the spot and I'm not the greatest detective in the world.

You also need to understand that L had access to all his resources and the situation on hand. I don't have access to that. It's easier to come up with a strategy when you know what you can use.

Either way, it's better to do nothing than to do something foolish.

**Conclusion:**

L went from being anonymous and almost impossible to kill, with an extreme surveillance advantage over Kira, already having him as the primary suspect, to closing the distance to the point where Light knew his appearance, behaviour, the structure of the task force, a rough idea of L's background and resources, etc., in exchange for L merely getting close to Light and having subjective gains that he cannot legally use.

This is why the move was an antifeat. It gained L almost nothing, risked almost everything, sacrificed the majority of his advantages, gave Light massive advantages, and eventually culminated to result in his own death.

The majority of his supposed "gains" were superficial, and the majority of the shielding which people use to justify the move was just as superficial and fragile.

The strategy L implemented favours Kira much more than it favours himself. It is an antifeat no matter what perspective you use to analyze it.
