Analyzing FN Light Part 1: Memory Loss Plan.

The original plan for Light's memory loss in the canon is very good but has flaws: High risk > Moderate reward. Uncontrolled variables. Purely reactive plan leaving some loose ends (13-day rule not justified). In the canon, L planned to test the notebook because he suspected the 13-day rule was false. Why? First, because Light and Misa were still alive. According to the rule, if they, as the owners, went more than 13 days without touching the notebook, they would die. But they didn't. After 50 days of confinement, they were still alive. This provides some premises that aroused L's suspicion:

 P1: According to the 13-day rule, if the owner stops writing for the next 13 days, they will die. P2: Light and Misa didn't die during the 50 days of confinement, nor after being released. Based on these two premises, L concludes two hypotheses explaining why nothing happened:

First and main hypothesis: Kira's power is transmitted through people.

 therefore, there is a strong possibility that Light and Misa are "puppets" of a larger being. This is why L is depressed, as he sees the pointlessness of chasing a phantom.

Second hypothesis: The rule is false.

Light and Misa lost their memories, they didn't die, and Light surrendered voluntarily. Knowing Light's nature, L knew he was setting a trap. The obsession with believing that Light is Kira persisted until the end, leading him to reconsider: "What if the rule is false, and this is what Light was after?" If Light is Kira, he was cornered, with Misa being captured. It's logical to think that if Kira were in a similar situation, he would do anything to prevent L from discovering his identity. Light is proud and hates to lose, so he fits this hypothesis perfectly. L almost discovers Light's identity but dies at Rem's hands. This is the canon version.

Memory Loss Plan (FN):

In the novel version, the plan is similar to the canon, but with variations. Light, upon hearing Rem talk about memory loss after relinquishing the notebook, realizes that the notebook can extract memories from the times the user uses the Death Note. With this, he creates the Memory Loss Plan:

 This Light profiles L and reasons about his enemy's way of thinking. He knows that L has two ways of evaluating and justifying his conclusions:

 External Justification: Data and evidence. Internal Justification: Intuition.

Light knows that L sees him as the only possible suspect of being Kira, and being obsessed with this internal justification, the plan works like a bucket of cold water. What happens if the External Justification contradicts the Internal Justification? This is what Light seeks to exploit in L. L is also proud. Winning the war is also about lowering your enemy's morale, even about making them self-destruct. L has the profile of a Thinker; his critical weakness is mental and emotional exhaustion.

Light asks Ryuk to hide the 23-day rule and replace it with the false 13-day rule. This makes the rule that the original owner will die if they don't write for a certain period plausible, and it justifies why Light doesn't die later on. After exchanging notebooks with Ryuk, he asks Rem to give the notebook to a businessman or politician with an ambitious profile and an addiction to power and money, but someone who isn't stubborn, rather someone willing to negotiate. He promises to grant them this power "permanently" if they fulfill the objective of killing criminals whenever you ask; this will be the "Third Chronological Kira."

 And now the big difference between the canon and the novel: The original, fake Kira.

This version of Light includes something new in the plan: Someone who came before him. Light writes the name of a random criminal, knowing the type of notebook paper. He instructs this criminal to buy identical sheets and write the following: First and last names of random people (6 people in total) Then he will also write: Light Yagami. He will find a notebook at his school and write at home or in any private place according to the rules written in the notebook. His goal will be to start eliminating as many criminals as he deems necessary to reduce crime and violence. Using all his intelligence and all available resources to avoid being caught or having evidence against him obtained, if he feels threatened, he will relinquish the notebook (losing his memories). He will die of a heart attack in 2011.

Everything written here is false, as the criminal was induced to write on ordinary sheets of paper he bought. Upon dying and leaving them somewhere Light could find them, he inserted these pages into the beginning of the Death Note. Since they are made of the same material, they fit together as if they were part of the notebook itself.

 All of this was done by Light to:

 Create a false and plausible narrative that Light is not Kira but someone being controlled. Leave traces and handwriting different from his, Misa's, and the third Kira's, supposedly making it appear that there are four users of the notebook. The criminal wrote that the supposed six people would die in a traffic accident, and the people were varied, not all of them criminals, giving this user a completely different profile from the rest of the "Kiras." And finally, justifying the 13-day rule: If this unknown user wrote Light Yagami's name, that's the reason he didn't die after the confinement, changing L's reasoning:

P1: The 13-day rule states that the original owner dies if they don't use the notebook for 13 days.

 P2: Light doesn't die after the confinement.

P3: At the beginning of the notebook, there are strange deaths that don't match Kira's modus operandi (cardiac arrest) or those of criminals (there are innocent people).

P4: There's a user who wrote names before Light, and this user wrote in detail that it should be Light once he has the notebook.

P5: Light supposedly died in 2011. Strong conclusion: Light isn't Kira; he was being controlled.

But why was there a whole plot for himself and not for Misa? So that L could capture Misa and put Rem in a situation where she would sacrifice herself to save Misa, killing three birds with one shot.
