FN Light Analysis Part 2: Kira's Fake Geoprofile Strategy

Okay, in this second part I'll talk about a feat that isn't an improved version like the memory loss plan, but rather a new strategy that doesn't exist in the canon. The Russian Fake Profile Strategy It consists of:

 Light writes down the names of criminals following a logical regional pattern. The canon Light started killing criminals from Japan on the first use, his first victim dying of a heart attack (which is why L deduced that Kira might be in the Kanto region). In this version, Light kills the same guy while testing the notebook, but the death in this alternate version is by suicide (shooting himself in the head during the kidnapping). After that death and the death of the motorcyclist who killed him, also by accident as in the canon, he concluded that: He could choose the time of death, the type of death in detail, and according to the rules, if he doesn't write the cause of death, or if it's poorly written or unrealistic, that person will die of a heart attack. With this, Light knew that if he wanted to eliminate criminals, he had to think carefully and not kill randomly. It was better to create a false impression, a pattern that would steer the entire investigation in the direction he wanted. His criminology studies show that detectives look for clues and evidence to draw conclusions, develop a profile, and deduce information from the crimes, like pieces of a puzzle, to narrow down the possibilities and find the likely suspect.

So, he simulates how he himself could find someone who kills criminals from a distance. It must be something divine. If there's no toxicology evidence and people support the idea of bad, condemned people dying out of nowhere, they'll believe it's an act of divine justice. As a detective, it would be very difficult, if not impossible, to draw a sound conclusion with such weak premises.

 But how to make it more believable? By following a supposed pattern. Light thinks that if he only writes the names of the criminals without specifying the cause of death, making them die of a heart attack, there he has it: A distinctive and clear signature. It's faster and more dynamic. Seeing that only criminals die, without regard to age or health, they'll easily conclude that it's "Someone" and not "something." He can choose where to start at will, but that beginning must have a story, a meaning. Light chooses Russia because of its variety of time zones, adjusting it to his own routine. (The names are written to highlight the contrast between local and foreign time zones: the local time, where Light is sleeping or in class, and the foreign time zone are programmed so that criminals die. The times are mostly in the afternoon in the Russian time zone, but at the same time it's morning or early morning in Japan. Light writes the names with a purpose: to create a false profile.) If the "first death" from cardiac arrest happens in a Russian city (ground zero), and this same pattern continues within the city and in other cities of the country, then in the capital, and then to countries geopolitically or culturally related to Russia (Turkey, Azerbaijan, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, etc.), a solid pattern of deaths develops, leaving false premises that consequently create a major false conclusion: The killer or the weapon killing the criminals is Russian.

  This is Light's strategy involves developing a system of programmed deaths by cardiac arrest, with slight deviations (between minutes, to make it more plausible and less robotic and suspicious, unlike making all the deaths happen at a fixed time). Light's third victim is the first victim of this "killer" in chronological order, anticipating and manipulating the perception and orientation of the Kira case investigation, making it more complicated than in the canon.
