II– VISUAL SPATIAL INDEX
Ryuk’s Facial Reconstruction

________________

In the making of a Shinigami Facial Recognition program, Adult Near constructs a near perfect and detailed 3D bust replica of Ryuk. Near's only reference of Ryuk's face would have been a video clip from the Japanese News, where Ryuk had recently appeared to announce Minoru’s auction of the death note.
Using this clip, Near would have had to reconstruct Ryuk's face himself using, without the help of any 3D copying tools. This is because it is established in the series that shinigami cannot be captured on camera or video and can only ever be perceived by those who have touched the notebook belonging to them. And because 3D rendering software and any copying tools would need existing image data to reference, it is thus impossible for them to obtain it. This would necessitate that Near himself take on the challenge of sculpting Ryuk's bust.

The most recent showing of the camera rule is from the C-Kira One Shot.

Sculpting such an accurate and detailed replica would suggest peak human visualisation skills. Even with an immediate video reference, it would be very difficult to reliably produce a near‑perfect likeness of a specific person's face (and especially difficult if we are to believe this sculpture is supposed to be accurate enough to be used in a facial recognition software).

Since the 19th century this process has become more efficient with the advent of photo and later video technology, which offer a consistent and vast reference boost. In the great civilizations of antiquity, the sculptor would typically observe the subject directly, often over several sessions, to get every detail as close to realistic as possible, similar to how portrait painters and sculptures worked in later centuries. Photo references ostensibly allow for the same thing, but without the need for the subject itself to be present.

For quantification, since Near doesn't have a huge library of images to choose from, his VSI would be comparable to that of a pre–modern master sculpture.

III– PROCESSING SPEED INDEX
Monitor Hax

________________

What is Monitor Hax? To put it succinctly it is the ability to observe and perceive multiple monitors at once. Though L and arguably Mello can do something similar, no one in the series has ever come close to performing this feat at the scale Near has. It is stated in HTR, that this ability is a particular skill of Near’s, and that it is incumbent on a person's cognitive capacities.

There are approximately somewhere between 100~150 monitors in the room as estimated in the many panels from Nears Monitor room throughout the series.

With each monitor having both,
>visual and
>auditory output.

Assuming Near is situated exactly in the middle of the room, and assuming a total monitor count of 100 (for a conservative estimate), Near would be able to visually perceive roughly 60 monitors. Why 60 instead of 50, which would have been half of the previously assumed 100?
This is because human vision typically caps at approximately 200°~220° horizontally and about 130°~135° vertically. This would allow Near to perceive an extra 12 or so monitors, so 60~62 is the estimate.

Near would also be perceiving all auditory information from all 100 monitors because unlike his range of vision, there would be no limiting factor like the biology of his eye for his auditory perception. (Besides the distance from the subject of course, but that's not the case in this instance.)

Near is able to take in both stimuli from all monitors in his periphery, then process, memorise, then mentally manipulate these several dozens of distinct and dynamic sets of visual and auditory information. With some monitors even broadcasting footage in different languages on top of this.

Quantification:
While in real life the average human can technically view more than one monitor (think a security guard sitting behind multiple monitors with different camera feeds), they cannot actually focus on multiple screens simultaneously in the truest sense. The human brain is designed to be a monotasker, (meaning it processes high-focus information for only one task at a time.)
What seems like simultaneous focus is actually rapid task switching, the brain quickly jumping its attention between screens.

For Near though, who is  stated to have actually focused on 60 monitors at once in the literal sense, (and this is a lowball, ignoring the total sum of his auditory and visual focus on the remaining 40 something or so other monitors) his cognitive performance would be so far beyond human limits that they would essentially exist outside the standard bell curve.

The natural conclusion would be that Near has 60X the average human CPI.
While 60X the capability sounds like it should be 6000 IQ CPI (100 x 60), the Cognitive Proficiency Index (CPI) is a normative scale, not a linear measurement of speed. Claiming Near has 6000IQ/CPI would be Insane Wank. Here is how Near ought to be scaled theoretically against human statistics.

I – Position on the Bell Curve:
Standard cognitive scores are mapped to a Normal Distribution where 100 is the mean and the standard deviation (SD) is 15.

* Statistical Breaking Point: Modern tests effectively cap at around 160 (+4 SD), as there are not enough humans above that level to provide an accurate comparison.
* Theoretical Position: A person with 60x the processing capacity would likely land at +10 to +20 standard deviations above the mean.
* The Probability Gap: A 10-sigma event (+10 SD) has a probability of roughly 1 in 1.3 sextillion (10²¹). For context, only about 117 billion humans have ever lived in total.

II – Is Near's CPI necessarily 60X that of the average human?
Not necessarily. CPI measures position relative to others, not a unit of work count.
If you can process 60x more data, your raw score on a subtest like Symbol Search or Coding would be 60x higher than average.
However, because the scale is ordinal (based on rank), a CPI score of 200 is not literally twice as smart as 100, it just means you are rarer than 99.99999% of the population.
Mathematically, if we attempted to plot Near on the distribution, his CPI would likely be calculated at 250 to 300+, though such numbers are purely theoretical as no human test currently exists that can measure him, but it serves as a good heuristic as it really puts things into perspective. Just the idea alone that this feat cannot be scaled accurately on any existing human system, goes to show just how far beyond human Near’s lies in his CPI.

You could make calcs, find the time frame, estimate the number of operations he'd be doing, and get actual numbers, but I'll leave that to the other scalers.

I just find quantifying him with statistical rarity to be more accurate.

Common Questions:
To address a common criticism against Nears Monitor Hax,, I'll first steelman the skeptic's argument. It usually goes something like this,
* “It’s possible that Near wasn't processing all the monitors in his periphery at once. Since he was shown shifting his eyes back and forth around the room while using his monitor ability.”
* “ This would suggest a change in attention between monitors.”
* “Therefore he CANNOT possibley be processing all 60 monitors at once.”

Rebuttals:
   1. The first response would be that Nears HTR statement alone suffices as fact of his having a Monitor Hax. This in and of itself refutes any claims of impossibility.

   2. A second response would be that the conclusion doesn't follow from the premise. Near shifting his attention between different monitors wouldn't necessarily mean he can ONLY ever focus on specific monitors at a time. He could just as possibly be changing his focus between SETS of monitors as well. Thus by shifting his eyes, he could simply be changing the scope of his periphery and the subset of total monitors in view, such that he'd be processing information from a completely different set of ~60 monitors.
A moving of the eyes wouldn't necessitate that Near can only ever process a single monitor.

   3. It can also be true that Near simply chose to narrow his range and focus to one monitor, without it meaning he cannot perceive multiple monitors, if he so willed. (Again he is already stated to have this ability. Hypothetically, even if one could truly isolate an instance and prove Near couldn’t possibly be using monitor hax at this time. It still wouldn't be reason enough to falsify the fact that he has the ability at all.)

The only other counter argument would be to reject HTR and the Monitor Hax statement itself. The skeptic has to take this mode of argument, every other argument is either contradictory to the statement in some way, is inconsistent with the narrative or logically flawed.
