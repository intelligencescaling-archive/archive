The flaws in Light Yagami’s plans & strategies P1

Bus Hijacking Plan: The plan where Raye Penber, an FBI agent, doesn’t keep a fake ID on him—unlike former FBI agent Naomi Misora—which makes zero sense. The agency never changed; if she was trained to use a fake ID, he would be too. It’s purely for plot convenience—a plan that Light was only able to pull off because he had a magical notebook that can influence a victim’s actions before death, once more for plot convenience—and a plan that didn’t account for something as simple as predicting the possibility that one of the passengers, or even the bus driver themselves, would attempt to stop the hijacker, with no backup plan if that happened, which would cause Raye to jump in to stop the fight without having to reveal his ID to Light.

Potato Chip Plan: He wouldn’t be able to dispose of the potato chip bag in his house without L hearing the TV thonk, and trying to dispose of it elsewhere would immediately raise L’s suspicions. The timing of the criminal’s deaths, starting exactly when Light sits down to do his homework while eating, is suspicious even with the potato chip deception. And what if the name of a criminal is long enough that it takes more time inside the potato chip bag to spell it out on the piece of paper? It would be suspicious if he kept his hand in the bag, staring at the opening for ten seconds—hell, even seven seconds would catch L’s attention, especially if Light had only just started eating from the bag. But my biggest problem with this plan is Light’s behavior: he would need repeated visual confirmation, his hands lingering longer than normal, and focused attention on the bag opening—all of which would definitely be suspicious to L. The thonk after tossing the bag away is just the finishing touch. Another less obvious problem with this plan is what would happen if someone else accidentally found the potato chip bag, opened them, and saw the piece of paper and TV? He didn’t hide them—he just put them in the above kitchen cabinets. There are three family members besides himself. At least one of them would likely have checked the cabinets when they got hungry.

Isolation Strategy: He reveals that he has access to NPA information, which allows him to create distrust between the NPA and L—another strategy that he could only pull off because he had a magical notebook of death. By doing this, he not only destroyed his one advantage over L—knowing L’s every move through his hidden NPA connections—but also ruined his chance of remaining unknown. L had already narrowed Kira down to Kanto using the Lind L. Taylor trap, and Kira revealing that he had access to NPA information narrowed it down even further. From the Lind L. Taylor trap alone, there were still forty billion possibilities for who Kira could be. Light intentionally narrowed it down drastically to people who specifically had connections to the NPA. What is rarely discussed in this plan is that Light’s father revealed to him that Kira was being narrowed down to a student, which caused Light to change his killing timings with the Death Note. That alone would make him more suspicious of being Kira. If his father had simply told L, “Hey, I told my son we were narrowing Kira down to a student, and then Kira’s timing suddenly changed,” it would have been obvious to L that Light was Kira.
