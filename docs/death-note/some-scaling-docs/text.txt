I - B’s Main Aim

Beyond Birthday, the man known primarily as ‘B’ and the true successor to L, wants to challenge and surpass his arch-nemesis through a match of wits, proving to himself, by any means necessary including his own life, that the greatest detective in the world is nothing more than a fraudulent character, fueling his hatred, malice and despair into accomplishing this very goal.

“It all boiled down to the entire case being a challenge for L, and he had killed people, and tried to kill himself, for that alone… but while murders could be dismissed as simple madness, committing suicide for such a stupid reason could not. Before he became like that, if only someone had stopped him… but that just shows how intent he was on his purpose. His own life was as meaningless as the lives of his victims, nothing but a tool in Beyond Birthday's quest to surpass L. It mattered more to him than his own life. Perhaps he was less intent than desperate. Nobody could have stopped him. That was his resolve. Which made him… so very strong.”

By asserting his superiority, B, the pale copy, would triumph over the person whom he was groomed to duplicate in vain as a poor facsimile. For several years under the 1st Generation of the Wammy’s house, only insatiable rage and obsession would guide B’s actions. The world’s brightest mind, the greatest sleuth in the world, would grovel at the feets of the greatest criminal master-mind to ever walk the earth. B would hand L a murder case so infuriatingly difficult that he’d have no choice but to submit to B’s reign at the top, completely crushing L’s resolve and his motivation for investigation. Not only would B surpass L, but L would, by extension, see his intellect, pride and ideals destroyed.

(EQE)

B would engineer a complex serial killing linked to a sequence of intricate puzzles specifically tailored for L, which only he could successfully solve, whether directly or indirectly. L would meticulously unravel the riddles of B’s making, carefully taking in the clues which his successor would have left behind to finally catch and stop him in his state of madness. But this would be the very cause of L’s demise. Each puzzle, properly deciphered, would conveniently lead to another crime-scene associated with yet another puzzle. The streak would continue on up until its culminating point, an impossible murder case : B’s mind-breaking suicide.

B would orchestrate the perfect and ultimate crime which even L wouldn’t be able to solve : his very own death. Each sub-case prior to his ultimate crime would act as decoys – a long series of traps disguised as a seemingly coherent enigma. The preceding sub-cases would only serve a singular purpose : camouflage and concealment. L would fall for the illusion that the final crime would undoubtedly have a final puzzle accompanying it, something which he could solve to eventually retrace B and put him behind bars, having been conditioned with the idea that the puzzles were meant to be connected and coherent with one another. But the conundrum of B’s design would have no end, no solution which would allow L to corner and defeat B.

(Planning, Setting Traps, Concealment, Logical Manipulation)

Aware of L’s nature, B had inductively anticipated that he’d send proxies, his loyal public faces, to catch him instead of moving by himself – a factor upon which he had based his entire plan. And what better way to irreparably entrap L into losing than by using his own tools, resources and intellect against him. Feeding information about the messages he had dispersed over the crime scenes to the pawns, the latter, and by extension, L, would fall on the contradictory parameters of the final murder which would have conflicted with the very perception B would have keenly manipulated them into adopting.

(Anticipation, Direct/Indirect/Logical Manipulation, Inductive Reasoning)

With no pathway to a solution, L would be condemned into chasing after the mirage of B who would no longer exist. With no other hope, he’d be forced into obsessively reinvestigating the other murder scenes, looking for a hint which had been missed or a warning which would have been cleverly hidden, but in the end, no such element could be found. In fact, B had meant for the difficulty between each puzzle to escalate drastically, making the possibility of a mistake more likely. L’s hands would be tied by the contradiction of B’s design : a logical puzzle suddenly turned illogical, a mind-mire impossible to solve.

A problem with no missing link would mean an answer without a solution, but this very idea would contradict the ‘coherence’ displayed in the preceding cases. There would be no killer left to catch, and thus, B would claim his victory over L, as a ghost who, even dead, would haunt L for the rest of his life. B was perceptive enough to understand that, due to the gap in their abilities, he’d never be able to outsmart L by conventional or direct confrontation. So, B chose the most optimal and novel route possible towards victory : pitting L against himself, manipulating him into falling for a trap only possible because of his deductive skills, precautions, and proxies – one which would inexorably lead him towards his demise.

(Creativity, Perception, FRI, Setting Traps, Indirect/Logical Manipulation, Inductive Reasoning)

II - The Wara Ningyo Murders

B had made it so that his crime scenes would leave the LAPD detectives in an intellectual stalemate. Absolutely nothing prominent could be retrieved from what had been laid out by the killer. Or so they thought. What never obviously occurred to the police investigators was that they were purposely kept at a loss. Their only role in B’s plan was to collect photographs and evidence of the scenes; things which B could then use to skillfully steer L’s pawns into the right directions. He had engineered his murders in a way which required him to juggle between the perspective of the LAPD detectives, leading them completely astray via the natural difficulty of his cases, whilst making sure that he’d be able to leave hints only L and his proxies would be able to notice and factor in. In a sense, the LAPD only served as a medium between B and L, as additional pawns which he would indirectly use to provide L with the necessary information required to make vital deductions which B would use against him.

(Information Control, Concealment, Indirect Manipulation, Tactics)

III - The 1st Murder Strategy

At the original conception of his plan, B had been able to make two vital predictions which were crucial to a smooth execution of, not only his plan, but also of this strategy :

1-) B concludes that L knows of his ‘off-the-grid’ disappearance and that, in consequence, L will anticipate that he will offer him a challenge : an uncrackable mystery. Therefore, B expects that L will do anything to hunt him down once he spots a case which seems to fit B’s profile and motive.

2-) L will not move alone. He’ll send proxies, at most three, to investigate in his place (which is likely a trend which B would have inferred based on L’s previous cases). B would brilliantly foresee this and, as previously mentioned, structure his entire plan on that nigh-certain eventuality.

(Anticipation, Inductive/Deductive Reasoning)

The 1st murder strategy relied on two main objectives respective of his aforementioned predictions :

1-) Get L to recognize and pinpoint his murder case.

2-) Get L to engage into the battle by putting his proxies into action so that he can successfully meet them, allowing him to guide them across the murder scenes.

1st Tactic : Media Manipulation

B had created a coherent disposition to his three murder scenes, a connection which allowed for the investigators to link the killings to one another. The ‘killer’ had purposely left Wara Ningyo behind and created his own atypical locked rooms which, keep in mind, are supposed to make murders look like suicides. But here, the deaths clearly occurred to the LAPD detectives as murders, because of the methods which had been used. The inevitable conclusion to arrive at : the killer is undoubtedly very abnormal. Nothing of the sort had ever been recorded in the police’s criminal files, let alone in the public. This ‘oddness’ combined with the sheer difficulty and nebulous nature of the killings would fuel intrigue and curiosity among the masses, personified by the American public, the media, and the police departments. Naturally, the case would grow quickly in prominence, which it did on August 13th : the time where B’s murders were being called the Wara Ningyo murders or the Locked-doors murders. Through perfect affective forecasting and a keen understanding of mass psychology, B is able to amplify the coverage of his murder cases through the media by making them extremely odd.

(EQU, Mass/Indirect/Logical Manipulation)

B had been calculating this response from the public. He wanted to drastically increase, or even guarantee, the chances of L being able to notice the killings. B had anticipated that L would be looking for a sign of his activity, one which he could then link back to him, not only because of the suitable level of difficulty for an nigh-impossible mystery, but also because of the emotional signals which B had laid out within the crime scenes which he would have expected L to lump into patterns (we’ll go over these cues in the 4th Murder Strategy, since they’ll be much easier to understand by then).

For now, simply retain that L would be able to link the psychological profile he’s made of B to the aspects of the murders. B would foresee L’s thinking and give him exactly what he had been looking for tirelessly : an outstanding murder case the police could not even fathom solving which would inevitably attract his attention and, thus, set off the battle between the two of them.

(EQU, Anticipation, Inductive Reasoning, Indirect Manipulation)

2nd Tactic : The Crossword Puzzle

Let us now take a look at the second larger tactic making up the 1st murder strategy : the crossword puzzle. B had designed and made it incredibly difficult, so much so that it was reasonable that several police officers couldn’t have worked through the hints successfully. Naomi Misora confirms this specific quality of the puzzle later on in the novel. She first believes that, when L had first mentioned it to her, he was simply bragging out of ego, exaggerating the difficulty of the puzzle. However, once she is able to get a first-hand perspective of the puzzle, she completely discards this hypothesis and posits that it looked unfathomably complex and frustrating.

(VCI, FRI, Inductive Reasoning)

On July 22nd, B sends the puzzle to the police station via an untraceable forwarding system. If completed correctly, the answer to the puzzle would provide the detectives with the address of the eventual first murder of the LABB murders. In other words, B would provide the officers with the means to prevent his killings, willingly tipping off the location of the first murder as a preemptive warning. But of course, all according to B’s design, the police detectives wouldn’t be able to solve it, irrespective of how much time they would spend on it. Anticipating this very eventuality, B expected them to throw it out, dismissing it as some sort of pretentious prank out of frustration.

However, this is exactly what B wanted. Once the case would become broadly discussed through the media or the police, L would notice it, just as previously mentioned above. Being aware of L’s influence and his attention to detail, B anticipated that L would be looking for any sort of additional evidence pertaining to the Wara Ningyo murders and that he’d scan the state hoping to find other clues through his extensive information gathering network. L would find the puzzle, solve it, link it to B, and fall for the first little bait of B’s immense trap. Without acting directly, B had been able to keep the investigators in his control, forcing them to throw away the puzzle by having made it infuriatingly difficult so that it would eventually find its path to L, which would in-turn allow him to influence L’s thinking.

(Tactics, Indirect/Logical Manipulation, EQU, Anticipation, Deductive Reasoning)

But there was more to the crossword puzzle than meets the eye. The fact that B, the killer, had been willing to leave a warning, a notice to the police detectives which would have allowed for the prevention of his murders would show to L that he might be leaving other imperceptible preempts, other messages on the crime scenes. B would anticipate that L would pass down this thought to his pawns at the appropriate moment. Indirectly, they’d have been puppeteered into abiding a general rule of B’s design, a preconception which would steer their perception in a direction favorable to his plan.

B’s eventual investigation methods when he’d be on the crime scenes alongside the pawns would be thereby justified, since he had also anticipated that L would have asked his delegates to thoroughly reinspect the scenes, looking for hints which the police would have missed, based on the general rule which B had induced into their minds. The starting point of B’s final trap would start to slowly close in on L who, naturally, would have expected that finding the killer’s hints would allow for his investigation to progress. What he wouldn’t perceive however, according to B’s vision, was that this would lead to his doom in the future.

(Indirect/Logical Manipulation, Tactics, Anticipation, Deductive/Inductive Reasoning)

Not only that, but B’s multi-layered crossword puzzle tactic would also allow B to compel L into putting his pawns on the move. B has made it clear to L that the clues he’d be leaving behind wouldn’t be detectable by an amateur. In fact, this, right off the bat, would prevent L from acting through the LAPD, since it was made very clear that they didn’t possess the intellectual capacities required for the task at hand. L would need to send a proxy whose intelligence and deductive skills he deemed superior, somebody who’d be able to see things keenly, someone perceptive enough to stop B in his tracks. But yet again, this move would benefit B as well, courtesy of his preparations. He also needed L to send a highly clever person to investigate since, to remain concealed as a random third-party to the pawn, he’d have to limit his obtrusiveness, meaning that B couldn’t be seen solving everything by himself otherwise even the proxies would start harmfully suspecting him.

(Indirect/Logical Manipulation, Tactics, Concealment)

From the perspectives of Naomi and L, the puzzle also reflected the killer’s pride and his desire to be superior. Why make a warning, something that would allow the police to counter his plan, whilst actively making sure that they wouldn’t be able to access that warning in the first place? The answer : ego and over-confidence. The very essence of this part of the crossword puzzle tactic had been to display the killer as an attention-seeking and boastful maniac who’d do anything to assert his superiority, to mock those who are ‘below him’. But this is only the surface of B’s ploy, as there were other aspects which pointed at the killer being a condescendent freak. Excluding the crossword puzzle, the dolls, the locked-doors and the fanatical fingerprint wiping all also suited this trait. They showed that the killer was neurotic, obsessive, childish and arrogant. Though, as previously mentioned, this is only the surface of what B’s plan entails. The 1st murder strategy did indeed serve as a means for B to engage with L and to calibrate the thinking of his proxies, but more important is the fact that it would also prove to be crucial in the execution of his 4th Murder Strategy which we will cover later on.

(EQU, Psychoanalysis, Tactics)

Through this wide array of methodological preconceptions, B is able to indirectly guide Naomi to the idea that the killer is a prideful maniac with delusions of superiority and that therefore he’ll leave other hints behind, proving that the ones attempting to catch him are ridiculously inferior to him despite making the job easier for them. B had anticipated that Naomi would thus be able to figure out the toppling principle of the killer’s methods : the detectives should have been looking for things which should be there, but that aren’t if they wanted to solve the case. He knew that Naomi would link this modus operandi with the idea that the killer is extremely neurotic and that his actions are, at first glance, apparently meaningless, which suits with the fact that instead of leaving direct clues tied to a missing link, the killer had taken things away from the scene to formulate a message.

(Indirect/Logical Manipulation, Anticipation, Inductive Reasoning)

Naomi does indeed pick up on this after making her psychoanalysis of the killer and, when she heads to the scene of the first murder, she also notices that Believe Bridesmaid, the 1st victim is missing and that therefore, he suits the general disposition of the game the killer seems to be playing at. With this in mind, Naomi heads up to Bridesmaid’s bedroom where his dead body was found and then moved away by the police to look for additional evidence. The key is the bedroom, since that’s where the body of Believe Bridesmaid should be, but isn’t. Naomi then takes out the picture of the corpse and notices the strange cuts covering Bridesmaid’s body. She starts surmising about their use, discarding the theory that they were just the object of a grudge held by the killer against the victim, since the former had likely taken off the shirt of the victim to injure the corpse before putting it back on when someone with a true grudge would have simply cut through the fabric.

What occurs to her is that the cuts might be part of the message, which she starts looking for, observing the things left around by Bridesmaid. A thought then suddenly strikes her : beneath the bed was perhaps a certain blind-spot where the police missed something, something that they wouldn’t have been able to observe having been left ignorant of the killer’s logic (which had been exposed to Naomi through L’s deductions instilled by B). What never occurred to Naomi however, was that there’d be someone expecting her at the location. B had been waiting carefully, beneath the victim’s bed anticipating that Naomi would indeed find him there. He had guided her to him, visualizing her movements throughout the house and anticipating her exact thoughts, all thanks to the crossword puzzle and the preconceptions which B had managed to indirectly stow in her mind.

It is left unknown in the novel at which point B would have slipped inside the first murder scene to hide himself. The author makes the express point that the only eventuality which had left B guessing, was when L would put his pawns on the move. But obviously, that timing had been covered by B’s contingent thinking. The fact that L had unlocked the murder scene could be a possible theory as to how B knew when to lay in the bedroom. It’s plausible to assume that B had noticed someone unlocking the house on the 14th and that he had been lurking around the location, expecting something like this to happen, which is feasible considering he knows that L needs to give access to the scenes to his pawn somehow.

(Indirect/Logical Manipulation, Anticipation, Predictions, Deductive/Abductive Reasoning)

3rd Tactic : Meeting Naomi Misora

Before Naomi can finish peeking beneath the bed, B raises his hand out, seemingly reaching out towards Naomi, which fills her to the brim with fear and paranoia. What made this even more effective is the fact that Naomi had been conversing with L prior to scrutinizing the room, a factor which B would take advantage of eventually. If information concerning L would leak because of her, her career and future would be seriously compromised, which induces guilt and vivid panic in her mind. B crawls out and extirpates himself completely from beneath the bed and greets Naomi as Rue Ryuzaki, a private investigator.

(EQU/EQM, Emotional Manipulation)

Once they had moved out of the bedroom and into the living room, Naomi starts questioning B (who we’ll now mention as Rue Ryuzaki). She asks him if his name truly is Ryuzaki, which he confirms bluntly and calmly, whilst sitting in an awkward and child-like position which strikes Naomi as strange. When Naomi subsequently asks whether he is a private detective, Ryuzaki answers that he isn’t as he feels that the term carries excessive neurotic and egotistical connotation, calling himself an ‘un-private detective’ (which sounds absurd to Naomi).

Naomi then decides to ask Ryuzaki what exactly he was doing beneath the bed to which he replies that he was doing some investigative work, in the same unruffled tone and with an unsettling glare. Ryuzaki further adds that he was hired by the parents of Bridesmaid to conduct his own investigation. It's also revealed that the pressure which had been instilled by Ryuzaki lurking beneath the bed had gotten to Naomi, hence why she had asked him if he heard her conversation on the phone. He replies that he couldn’t make out what she was saying and that the bed had muffled the sound of her voice, further exacerbating her feeling of paranoia and doubt, since Naomi wouldn’t be able to tell if he was lying or not.

(EQU/EQM, Fabrication)

Naomi then tells Ryuzaki that she is also a detective investigating the deaths of Bridesmaid, Queen, and Bottomslash, to which he excitedly replies that they can cooperate if that’s the case, breaking away from his silent and unsettling persona. Naomi then proceeds to ask him if he found anything useful to the case, to which Ryuzaki answers that he was merely hiding once he heard someone enter the house, expecting someone like the killer returning to the crime scene having forgotten something.

Naomi instantly perceives this as an improvised lie, but that was also expected by B who would turn that into his advantage. Ryuzaki reiterates his will to cooperate with Naomi into investigating the case together, which she declines out of suspicion and doubt. However, he further persuades her by saying that he’ll give her all the information he’s gathered without any obligation to disclose her own. Naomi thinks of this as another lie, something that he couldn’t genuinely believe or want, further increasing her wariness and doubt regarding Ryuzaki, who had calmly spewed lie after lie.

(EQU/EQM, Fabrication, Emotional Manipulation)

Regardless, Ryuzaki decides to show her a copy of the crossword puzzle which the killer had sent to the police on July 22nd. Misora is instantly surprised at the fact that someone as strange and insignificant as Ryuzaki could have obtained such evidence. Surprised, Naomi starts showing signs of her knowledge of the crossword puzzle, which B instantly picks up on, confirming and deducing that the woman whom he had met is indeed the pawn sent by L to catch him.

What’s also important to note is that the copy which B had given Naomi was solved entirely. Keeping the level of difficulty in mind, Naomi is completely stumped as she’s only able to notice that the man who she found so suspicious had been able to complete the puzzle alone. Ryuzaki then decides to give Naomi a little ‘show’ of his deductive reasoning skills, arriving at the exact same conclusions which L had passed on to Naomi, namely that, since the puzzle had served as an unsolvable warning to the police, then it's likely that this attention-seeking killer would have likely left other messages behind.

Of course, B had already anticipated and knew that L would have made such deductions having created the very situation they were in, but the essential is that, naturally, Naomi couldn’t possibly dismiss him as a random amateur. The man had been able to stay on the same length as L, the world’s greatest sleuth.

(EQP/EQU/EQM, Anticipation, Inductive Reasoning)

Ryuzaki then apologies and heads towards the kitchen, exhibiting strange body language to Naomi. He then returns to the couch with a jar of jam in his hands, which he then opens. Having stuck his arm inside, Ryuzaki then scoops up jam with his hand and licks it off his fingers. After a few handfuls, he then starts slurping out the jam directly from the jar, making obnoxious sounds in the process (💀). Naomi is completely and utterly disgusted by this behavior, further pronouncing her disdain towards Ryuzaki and his odd demeanor. He senses this based on her expression, asking her if anything is wrong, obviously aware that his little habits were mentally torturing Naomi. After finishing his jam, Ryuzaki then invites Naomi to go investigate the scene with him. However, he ‘abruptly goes down on all fours’ and starts crawling all over the scene like a true freak. His posture further appalls Naomi, who, shocked, rejects Ryuzaki’s propositions of ‘joining him’. Ryuzaki replies that it's such a shame, sadly shaking his head in concern, which further puts emphasis on his childish and prideless behavior in Naomi’s eyes.

(EQP/EQU/EQM)

Although he had used very awkward tactics, this was all part of B’s strategy. He had acted out a persona so disturbingly sinister, that Naomi couldn’t help but feel as if she should stick around the odd character who was Rue Ryuzaki. B uses his amazing skills in affective forecasting to predict Naomi’s behavior which would result from his act, namely that she wouldn’t be able to set her gaze away from him. After all, who would? Rue Ryuzaki, the ‘un-private’ detective with a freak-ish, blunt, suspiciously deceptive, and appalling character who had been able to arrive at the same conclusions as L. Naomi couldn’t possibly fathom letting someone like that investigate the crime scenes alone.

But now, B had carefully engineered a situation where Naomi, L’s proxy, would follow him at all times. The trap had been activated. He had lured Naomi into focusing on him, which, in-turn, would allow him to lead her directly to the clues left behind on the scenes. A brilliantly cunning setup from B, one which was absolutely vital for the proper unfolding of his bigger plan since, without the pawns being as efficient as B required them to be, he could never achieve his goal. Now, he could regulate the soundness and effectiveness of Naomi’s deduction to his will, masterfully leading her in his accord, having indirectly influenced her into following him.

(Acting Skills, Tactics, Indirect/Emotional Manipulation, EQU/EQM, Setting Traps)

IV - Rue Ryuzaki

Let us take some time to describe the fake identity which B had adopted. As Ryuzaki, B would approach the families of the victims which he himself had murdered. With extensive persuasion, he’d be able to convince relatives of all three victims to hire him as a private investigator to solve the tragedy which had been inflicted upon them, bringing them truth and justice regarding their losses. We remain unaware as to how B would have exactly done so, but, whether it was through guilt-tripping, mere persuasion, or whatever form of smooth-talking, the novel points out that his manipulation on Naomi, in comparison to his manipulation on the families, was a simple walk in the park, meaning that his exploitation was extremely potent and difficult to pull off.

(EQU/EQM, Direct Manipulation, Charisma, Social Skills)

Regardless of the means, B had now been able to obtain a certified mandate from the actual relatives of the victims. Also, it's important to note that the initials of the name which B had purposely picked out (R.R.) are synonymous with the code of L (R’s become L’s in Japanese). This was not by chance : it was a cue given by B to L – a signal that he had come into contact with Naomi. Obviously, when Naomi would have been exposed to Ryuzaki’s behavior, she’d contact L, telling him that a mysterious and seriously disturbing investigator called Rue Ryuzaki had shown up on the scene. L would immediately deduce that it was B, the man he was searching for. But B would expect that L would instruct Naomi to let him do as he wishes, especially since he’d later confirm that a certain Rue Ryuzaki had indeed been hired by the families.

In essence, B anticipated that L knew what he was planning to do, and that, for him to win, he needed B to be at liberty to do what he wanted, otherwise both of their plans would fall apart. Naomi would have no choice but to let Ryuzaki do as he wants albeit without ceasing to suspect him, allowing him to execute his plan without any problems. Even if L was aware of B’s manipulation, he’d be forced into letting him do as he pleased since he ultimately needed more proof to close out the case and defeat B, hence why in either scenarios, it was highly beneficial for B that Naomi stayed in his grasp without suspecting the true nature of Ryuzaki.

(Tactics, Anticipation, Indirect Manipulation, Deductive Reasoning)

Let’s look a bit more at Rue Ryuzaki’s appearance and his habits, since that aspect is also quite interesting. The novel describes him as a young man with natural black hair, a plain white shirt with faded jeans, severely-pronounced eyebags and a curved or slouched position. Ryuzaki also displays a certain love for sweets. This sounds very familiar to another character doesn’t it? B had been able to pose as L, adopt the same exact appearance as him, without ever even meeting him in the first place. Not only somehow aware of his appearance, B was also able to replicate L’s habits and his thinking to virtual perfection. The novel makes it very clear that B, at his natural form, and L are actually radically different in looks. But with multiple layers of makeup, B is able to act exactly like L.

What’s also quite impressive about this disguise is that, despite incarnating the man whom he hates the most in the world, B funnels that vivid hatred into performing the act, swallowing his sense of superiority and his pride into incarnating the very essence of L. Quite ironic. B wanted to escape his destiny at Wammy’s, straying as far from the idea of becoming like L. He wanted to be different and above him in his own way, and despite that, he’s still ready to pass as and copy L, whom he despises to his very core if it means he can succeed. Now, what’s also important to note is to point out a misconception which some people seem to have. B specifically posing as L has virtually no relevance to the development of his plan. His ego and anger is what pushed him to do it, nothing more since all B needed was to be disguised and covered by several layers of makeup in front of Naomi (we’ll discuss that reason later on).

(EQU/EQM/EQE, Acting Skills)

V - Believe Bridesmaid

After putting on his act, Ryuzaki starts looking around the room, hoping to find additional clues, truly putting on the persona of a detective. Naomi tells him that it's unreasonable to do so since the police had likely already thoroughly inspected it, however, he replies that since the police had missed the importance of the crossword puzzle, then it's almost guaranteed that they could have missed other messages, other things which the killer could have left behind. Naomi then agrees and expresses her desperate will to have some sort of clue, a basis to make progress in the investigation.

Ryuzaki exploits this and asks her if there is anything which had struck her when she first entered the room. Obviously, having been anticipating her exact thought process in advance, Ryuzaki is aware that Naomi has inspected the photograph of Bridesmaid’s body which contains the first part of his riddle as, otherwise, she wouldn’t have been able to find him without having been exposed to the mental suggestion of the killer’s modus operandi. B is already starting his work and setting up Naomi to decipher the roman numeral sequence which he had carved in the victim’s corpse.

(Deductive Reasoning, Direct/Logical Manipulation)

All according to plan, Naomi thinks about the cuts on Bridesmaid’s body. But she starts wondering if she should really show them to Ryuzaki, since his act had already fortified a pejorative portrayal of him in her mind. Nonetheless, she decides to lay a trap for Ryuzaki by showing him the photograph of the victim, hoping to read his reaction at the sight of the cuts as he had done to her with the crossword puzzle. But this was also part of B’s expectations, who knew that Naomi would end up showing him the photograph with a certain degree of skepticism.

When Naomi mentions the photograph to him, he, again, intentionally displays strange behavior and then picks the photograph straight from her fingers, intensely staring at it and nodding, thus preventing her from reading any of B’s intentions covered up by his unconventional acting. He congratulates Naomi on her work and asks her why exactly is the photograph of importance. She tells him that the killer likes abiding by the rule that the missing link on the crime scene is an object that should have been there at the time, but that had been taken away subsequently. Ryuzaki tells her that this is quite clever and proceeds to inspect the cuts on the body portrayed by the photograph, positing them as a carefully cut-out sequence of roman numerals which he proceeds to quickly decipher.

Pretending to unravel the letters, Ryuzaki shocks Naomi with the sheer speed of his work and how keenly he had been able to detect some form of message far before she could even begin to understand what the cuts were supposed to mean. Naomi takes a step back, obviously stunned by Ryuzaki’s efficiency which is reflected in her tone of voice and body language. She then tells him to excuse her for a moment since she wants to go fix her makeup. B sees right through her and obviously knows that this was her pretext to go contact L, a factor which he had also anticipated. B knew that the proxies would be continuously calling L, reporting their progress about the case but also about his presence. So, B slowly follows Naomi up to the bathroom where he posts himself right across the door. After finishing her conversation with L, Naomi opens the door and finds Ryuzaki standing right beside her, which further renews her anxiety concerning the possibility that he could have been intentionally eavesdropping. Like the psychopath he was, B took dear pleasure in seeing Naomi struggle and becoming paranoid about his unsettling actions.

This is not in an attempt to outwit them, but rather the emotion that he feels from the idea of outwitting them… he is taunting and mocking them. This is a form of subversive behavior. To go out of your way to commit such horrible crimes for the sake of mockery and pride? He's trying to prove his superiority, not to the world or on a large scale, but to a personal level. Fueled by inferiority and a need for approval… to fulfill a sense of emotional depravity.

The unnecessary levels of fingerprint wiping play a significant role too. While it is true that fingerprint removal was a common form of trail erasure by criminals, the extent to which the perpetrator had committed them in his murders were beyond absurd, and were needlessly extensive. He could have escaped undetected without having to wipe every page of every book or unscrew the light bulbs to wipe them. But he still did it, which displays a sense of neuroticism, and obsessive behavior. As he is again, going to unnecessary lengths to prove his superiority, as if he is playing just a game… a game. This also links back to the fact that for all 3 scenes of the murders the doors were locked. In Mystery Novels, locked doors are used to make murders look like suicides, but in this case it was quite clear that these deaths were really just murders. So this was another example of the killer displaying neurotic behavior by going to unnecessary extents…Not to mention the Wara Ningyo symbolizing the number of victims, another taunt.

"I'm not done yet… come and catch me if you can! If you have the intellect to challenge me that is… You are all far inferior to me!”

He would anticipate that L would link this obsessive and prideful behavior with his understanding of the man he knew as ‘B’ who would have laid out the entire crime scene in this absurd, yet extremely telling manner. L would be able to discern the emotion behind the killer's methodology and link it to B, cementing the Wara Ningyo Killings as B's doing. Everything would seem to fit with L’s conception of B’s prideful and condescending nature and, as such, B would lure L into investigating the case.

(EQU, Anticipation, Psychoanalysis, Inductive Reasoning)

So, this is all well and beautiful, but what exactly is the purpose of letting L pick up on his own ego and prideful traits? Before going over that, we need to cover the actual meaning behind the crossword puzzle. As it is known, L would have been able to retrieve a copy of the puzzle itself through his extensive state-wide information gathering network and then successfully solve it, providing him with a warning of the 1st murder : the address of the first victim. B had deduced and prepared for that eventuality, it was an imperceptible trap for L for the following reason : B knew that L would be passing on this information to his pawn (who’s involvement had been anticipated preemptively).

He’d use L’s thinking and reasoning skills to calibrate the perception of the pawn into adopting the exact same psychoanalysis that L had portrayed of B. Therefore, when Naomi ends up being briefed and put up to speed on the case’s details by L, she instantly notices the neurotic behavior of the killer which B had fabricated, prominently through the crossword puzzle, which served as an unsolvable preempt, but also through the print-wiping, the dolls, and the rooms. This was all according to B’s design. B knew that, not only L, but also his proxy would end up profiling him as a boastful and childish murderer with cravings for superiority. Not only that, but B would also be able to indirectly manipulate Naomi into meeting him by using the crossword puzzle as a tool to induce the killer’s methodological preconceptions into her mind. The crossword puzzle was, on the surface, primarily a means for the killer to send a warning to the police. But to Naomi and L, the puzzle would also strike as a direct message, a deliberate hint left by the killer that he’d be leaving other such messages on the crime scenes, other clues that would have led to the prevention of the subsequent murders.

Ultimately, Naomi falls for the trick and starts to think that the killer would have taken objects away from the crime scene to conceptualize ‘missing links’, preempts which would have connected the victims together. Abiding this notion, she goes up to Bridesmaid’s room (who is missing) and meets B, all according to his thoughts. B introduces himself as Rue Ryuzaki, an ‘unprivate detective’, someone free of excessive ego and neuroticism. Sounds very familiar right? B dissociates himself from the preconception of the killer he had carefully entrenched in Naomi’s psyche. This is further emphasized when B adopts the personality of a freak-ish and appalling creepy detective who seems to have a complete lack of manners. Naomi is very confused at the sight of B’s unconventional acting and, despite finding him quite suspicious, can’t possibly fathom that he is the killer, since this prideless and blunt persona which B puts up completely contradicts the preconception that she has of the killer.

Furthermore, the hints of doubt induced by B into Naomi’s mind because of his act and demeanor would come to serve him directly. This is another very neat trap designed by B, who wanted to lure Naomi into keeping her watch over him so that he can then successfully and unobtrusively guide her through each crime scene of his making. Through this specific tactic, B is able to set himself up in the perfect position to execute his plan and, in this particular case, his 4th murder strategy. So far, this is exactly the same as the aim of his 1st murder strategy, but bear with us and keep reading since this is where it will begin to get interesting and transcend into the 4th murder.

Essentially, B is able to abstractly create two preconceptions of the killer. On one hand, Beyond Birthday, B, the true killer, an egotistical and condescendent maniac, fueled by an innate sense of superiority. On the other hand, Rue Ryuzaki, the shameless ‘unprivate detective’, characterized by his uncivilized, prideless, and strange behavior. Concerning the 5th tactic of the 4th murder strategy, the locked-rooms, we’ll mostly be looking at the first preconception of the killer. This is the key to everything : the ego, pride, condescendence, the drive to assert a sense of superiority, all of which are reflected in the components of the murders exposed to Naomi, the pawn.

Everything that the killer did was overwhelmingly connotated with these traits, whether that was the crossword puzzle, the doll sequence, the print-wiping, and so on. It would only seem fitting to Naomi that the locked-rooms, another random element of the murders, were just another trivial game the killer was playing at, since they suited the general disposition of the crimes : excessively conceited and neurotic. Even though Naomi had a hunch that the rooms might have another purpose, she’s easily swayed and seduced by the idea that the rooms are just the product of the killer’s arrogance and mockery, like everything else had been laid out. What better way to conceal the very core mechanism of his plan than through such an intricate tactic? B would have prepared carefully for this very lengthy and comprehensive misdirection to occur, something so unpredictable that his trap would slowly but surely close in on Naomi and, by extension on L.

(EQU, Anticipation, Concealment, Misdirection, Fabrication, Indirect/Logical Manipulation, Tactics)

IX - The Anti-Feat

We believe it is important to unravel and cover the process behind B's ultimate loss and to chase away the misconception that his plan was mediocre because he ultimately failed at its apogee. As we are aware, B had engineered his plan in a way which would deliver him victory if it abides by his preconceived notion of what it means for L to win. L's idea of victory and dismissing a case as being solved is to find the perpetrator, nail down concrete evidence against their guilt, and sentence them to prison. How B had originally intended the 4th murder to go was for Naomi Misora to run down to B's apartment (404) when he fails to answer his phone as scheduled, only to find him to have been burned to death with the doors locked and one Wara Ningyo pinned to the wall, concluding the Los Angeles BB Murder Case.

But of course this did not happen as Naomi managed to solve the locked door trick before Beyond Birthday successfully managed to commit suicide. But B had used layers of deception and psychological manipulation to cover and misdirect those working on the case from discovering the true nature of the locked doors, so what went wrong?

The little detail and slip up that turned the tables against Beyond Birthday was Capoeira. When B and Naomi were making the necessary preparations to head towards their respective apartments to catch the killer, B had "accidentally" slipped up to Naomi about knowing of her mastery of Capoeira, which only 2 people could've known : L or her assailant in the valley. Naomi soon manages to connect B to the assailant, deduces that he is the killer and then manages to arrest him just on time after extinguishing the flames he set on himself.

Now there are many problems with B's loss which we think are illogical such as, for example, how completely out of character it was for B to slip up about Capoeira, how he managed to survive the burns, how he managed to not die of smoke inhalation, how he managed to survive a trip to the hospital based off how the aftermath of his suicide attempt was described, and so on.

Not to mention, had Naomi been a couple seconds late, B would've died and for L, if he does not manage to catch the perpetrator and sentence him, then it would be a loss. In the end, we believe that there are just too many simultaneous elements which would cause B’s demise magically aligning at the climax. The author of the novel himself cloaks up this fallacious narrative by saying that Naomi had only been able to discover the locked room trick with a streak of good luck and inspiration.

Note : All aforementioned feats also fall under planning for obvious reasons. Also, although not explicitly mentioned, most of B’s feats also fall under abstract reasoning and general thinking skills.
