Fake notebook plan flaws explained (How Light SHOULD have done it to win against Near and even MT Near)

A common misconception is that fake notebook is a good plan. Memory Loss Plan is much better and low diffs the fake notebook plan. Fake notebook is flawed even if we ignore the fact that it relied on Near acting incautious, revealing his face, showing up with only 3 guards who could easily be sniped off leading to his death, risking his life by gambling on Mikami not having an extra scrap of the notebook, not having any hidden preparations such as people hiding in case all four of them die, etc.

But even if we ignore everything from Near's side and assume that Near does *exactly* what Light wants him to do, the plan is still flawed.

Main flaw: Light did not have Mikami keep an extra scrap of the notebook on himself. Rather than depending on an entire fake notebook, an extra scrap is enough to succeed against Near. This is a major flaw because of how simple and easy it is for any normal person to think of this idea, especially since Light had 23 days to come up with the plan. At one point, Light even had Mikami rip off 5 entire pages to send to Takada, and I think he did this multiple times, not just once, he could have made Mikami keep one of them on himself as a contingency measure (similar to how Light keeps a note inside his watch). This is a simple additional contingency layer but it makes the most sense because only a personal page on himself cannot be tampered with by the opponent. Automatic victory. This one is a major antifeat.

This flaw was so simple to fix, I thought I was tweaking and forgetting something, because there's absolutely no way Light overlooked this obvious tactic to bring his success rate up to almost 100%, but then some of the most ardent Death Note defenders confirmed to me they also think it's an antifeat, so now I'm certain it's a valid antifeat.

Additional contingency: Light knew the name of 2 people on the enemy side, Lidner (whose actual name Mikami knew with his shinigami eyes since she publicized herself), and Mogi who is the big buff task force member. Both were standing on the side of Near, which gives Light the opportunity for a surprise attack by controlling them. On his own side, he knew the names of everyone, so he can control everyone with the death note. There is also a possibility he knew the name of Gevanni too, because Near mentions at one point that only Rester is unknown to them, since Gevanni stalked Mikami and acted suspicious on call when Mikami killed someone publicly on the bus. Thus, the only people whose names Light does not know definitively are Near and Rester, but possibly Gevanni too. With 23 days of planning, Light could have come up with so many methods and contingencies for using these people in case the notebook failed. For example he could make the woman shoot the ankles of her own allies, or smash their faces, and he could make Mogi tackle them, kick the men in between their legs, etc., and Light could even make his own allies join in on the scuffle.

The death note prevents them from killing, but shooting ankles, tackling, punching, poking eyes, etc., is possible. Once they're in a scuffle, Light only needs to shoot and kill the people whose names he doesn't know (Near, Rester, and possibly Gevanni if Mikami never noticed him). They are too busy getting attacked on all sides, so Light would easily be able to finish them off himself with a pistol.

This way even if Mikami never shows up, Light has the opportunity to eliminate all his enemies. Literally everyone else (other than Near) had a gun I think, so it wouldn't be suspicious at all for Light to have one on himself, because everybody else does. It would give him the chance to survive even if Mikami *somehow* lost his scrap or died or didn't show up for some reason. He could even get Mikami to bring a gun on that day to help him take care of the opponents, if SOMEHOW the notebook and scrap both don't work. (The scrap should definitely work though, the main flaw is way too big.)

It's risky but at least it gives him a possibility of surviving without needing to rely on his persuasion skills, which didn't persuade anyone. After taking care of anyone whose names he doesn't know (in this case it's Rester and possibly Gevanni too) only Near, Light, and Mikami are left. The rest died because Light either wrote their name in the Death Note beforehand or shot them while they were busy dealing with the surprise attack from 2 people on their own side along with the task force. Near doesn't have a gun, so easy to take care of him. Light knows Mikami's name and Mikami is devoted to him, easy win. It's a simple additional contingency layer, but simple plans can be very effective, especially since this would also work against MT Near if Mikami is being controlled and refuses to write on the scrap paper (contingency 1).

Extra: A moment of desperation that Light faced was when he turned his back and tried to write Near's name in the watch scrap to take him down. An additional pathway to the previous method is that Light could have added ANOTHER contingency, where in case everything fails, Light can pretend to die along with the task force members (whose names are written in the notebook beforehand). This way everyone is dead other than Light (who is pretending to be dead), along with Near, Rester, and possibly Gevanni. That way even if Light's aim sucks and he couldn't shoot Near or Rester during the scuffle (which honestly should be easy with the surprise attack), he can pretend to be dead while writing their names down in his watch, or if he wants, he could just keep a gun on himself and kill them when they approach him like Jose did when he pretended to be dead. This could easily be made more realistic in many ways such as by setting the control of Mikami so that he suddenly starts laughing a few seconds after the moment Light set their death times at, making it look as if Mikami wanted to betray Light.

Summary: Basically the biggest flaw in the fake notebook plan is that Light didn't spam contingency upon contingency. You might think I'm exaggerating or expecting too much, but he had 23 days to come up with the plan, I'm literally only spending a few minutes coming up with these ideas he could have layered on, rather than gambling everything on Mikami and a fake notebook that Mikami doesn't even watch over. Especially flaw 1 (the scrap page contingency), even the biggest Death Note glazers on the planet agree it's an antifeat which says something. With that alone, Light would have succeeded. The only exception is against Matsuda Theory Near which Light would need the other contingencies I mentioned just in case Mikami ends up getting controlled or he dies or doesn't show up for some other reason (for example if Near prepares a sniper and shoots Mikami before he can write in the note). If Light used all those 23 days of preparation wisely rather than just the one convoluted notebook swap with no additional measures, he could have survived against almost every possible iteration of Near, whether it's normal Near, Matsuda Theory Near, or some other possible theory people use to glaze Near. I honestly can't believe how uncreative Light was with the plan, he had a notebook that could have controlled almost every single person present in the warehouse including 2 people on the enemy side (and possibly even 3 if Mikami noticed Gevanni on the bus) and he never utilized it. I only mentioned one of the ideas he could have added as a backup plan, but there are so many ways the notebook could be used given that he could control so many people's actions. The fake notebook plan is genuinely atrocious and his other feats are just so much better. I never understood why people rate the plan so highly, it might be because people think a plan that is "hard to understand" is automatically better, but if you analyze it, it's clear the author intentionally had Light throw in several mistakes into the plan so he can end the story, otherwise Light could have used the 23 days to add several backup plans like what I mentioned. Light losing here is similar to Takuya's loss honestly. It's just the plot forcing him to come up with a bad plan.
