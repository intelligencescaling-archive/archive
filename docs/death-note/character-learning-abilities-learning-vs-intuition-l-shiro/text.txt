Character learning abilities

Well, this topic has been on my mind for quite some time. Learning ability is one of the main criteria for a character's ability to adapt. However, I often find that people misunderstand what learning ability is and try to include the character's intuition into this criterion. I will now explain what I mean.

Let's start with a definition: **learning ability** is a person's individual capacity to acquire educational information, perform educational activities, including memorizing material, solving problems, and performing educational and self-control. It is also divided into general (the ability to absorb any material in any form) and specific (the ability to absorb specific material in a specific field), and includes the following indicators:

\- the speed of information assimilation;

\- ease of learning (lack of stress and fatigue, satisfaction from the process);

\- flexibility to switch to new methods;

\- durability of preservation of the mastered material;

Now it is important to note the following point: **what is the difference between learning ability and intuition?** The difference between learning abilities and intuition is that learning abilities are related to the acquisition of knowledge and skills, while intuition is related to decision—making and assessing a situation without explicit rationale.

Now that we've discussed what learning is and how it manifests itself, let's move on to the characters:

**1.** **L Lawliet (Death note)**

I want to highlight two things right away: the stock market and the helicopter.

**1. 1.** According to the feat with the stock market L, at the age of 8, he was able to increase the money invested in investments by 20,000 times in 2 years. The problem is that there are too many questions about this feat: how did the company that L invested in increase its capital so much in such a short time? How did L, who had no knowledge of how the market works, manage to invest so successfully (it is important to note that purely mathematical knowledge is not enough; the human factor plays a significant role)?

The problem is that there are too many "ifs" for this feat to work. So, let's say that the company L invested in had already experienced a collapse and was just starting to recover, but in that case, how could the company have survived such a severe decline and recovered in just two years?

L has a lot of knowledge about economics, but where did he get it from? He's 8 years old, and he lived in a regular orphanage, where he probably didn't have access to any serious books on the subject. Additionally, the internet was not widely available at the time, and an ordinary child would not have had access to it.

If L invested in several companies at once, then each of them should have grown a lot, which means that in the DN universe, such a sharp increase in company growth should be common.

I'm not a big expert in investment economics, so I recommend that you read the post by milk\_thirsty, which explains it much better: [https://www.reddit.com/r/IntelligenceScaling/comments/1mrkw65/debunking\_many\_claims\_around\_ls\_stock\_feati\_think/](https://www.reddit.com/r/IntelligenceScaling/comments/1mrkw65/debunking_many_claims_around_ls_stock_feati_think/)

**1.2.** In chapter 52, we are shown how L learned to fly a helicopter intuitively (actually, this point can be disputed, as L did not explicitly say that he could not fly a helicopter, but okay, let's accept the most well-known version). And it is truly absurd due to the sheer amount of details that need to be known and the impossibility of coming up with it on your own:

\- Lack of specialized movable surfaces for flight control, unlike aircraft. Helicopters are typically not equipped with rudders and ailerons that replace the controls.

\- The control stick determines the cyclic pitch of the main rotor, and the pilot uses it to control the helicopter's roll and pitch. Each action requires corresponding adjustments using the other controls.

\- The "step-gas" lever increases the overall pitch (blade angle) of the main rotor, which increases thrust. When the pitch is increased abruptly, the rotor's reactive moment changes, causing the helicopter to change course.

\- The pedals determine the pitch of the stabilizing ("tail") propeller, and the pilot uses them to control the aircraft's course. Sudden pedal movements affect the stabilizing propeller's reactive moment and have some impact on the aircraft's pitch.

And that's not even all the details. This is not a case where a person could have guessed something, as it is obvious. Operating a helicopter is extremely difficult, and every detail matters, so this feat has nothing to do with learning - it is pure intuition that is not based on anything. In fact, even L's phrase confirms this: *"You just have to rely on your intuition, and before you know it, you're flying a helicopter".*

**2. Shiro (No Game - No Life)**

So, I want to analyze Shiro's feat from Chapter 2 of Volume 1, where she learned a completely unfamiliar language in just 10 minutes. What's shocking is that she did it all on her own, just by reading a book.

In the novel, this is explained as follows: *"Since we could speak to everyone up to this point, the syntax and vocabulary of the language must be identical. In this case, we only need to remember the written representation of the language".*

And this is the problem, the languages may sound completely the same, but how does Shiro know how to read certain characters? In Japanese, there are no separate letters like in Russian or English, but instead, it uses syllabic alphabets. In the language of Imaniti (a language from the NGNL world), one syllabic character from Japanese may be represented by multiple characters, so how does she know that this is not the case? Again, she has not been taught how to read letters. Let me give you an example: take any English word, for example "comfortable," and now let me make a Russian word that sounds the same "камфэтбл". and here's the question: how can an abstract person who only knows how to read Russian letters know that the combination of letters "фэт" is the same as "forta"?

It also plays a role that the same letter can sound different or not pronounced at all in different words. For example, the word "fir" is pronounced \[fɜː\], while the word "fire" is pronounced \[ˈfaɪə\], even though it seems like just adding one letter at the end of the word "fire"! Or an example from Russian, we have the word "солнце" (sun) and the letter "л" is not pronounced, meaning we write it, but we do not pronounce it when reading. How did Shiro know all these things? So there's no actual learning ability in this feat, it's just ordinary intuition.

**3. Beth Harman (Queen's Gambit)**

Not a very popular character in SCD, but I want to mention her. In episode 1 of season 1, Beth fully understood the principle of playing chess by watching the game from the sidelines for a few seconds. Chess is a rather complicated game, the way the pieces move cannot be understood intuitively. Checkers is easy to understand - there are few rules, the essence of the game is simple, eat all the pieces, but chess is not about that at all, there is even a completely different goal - to checkmate. It's impossible to understand the whole rule, given that she comes in the middle of the party and leaves long before the end.

Checkers can be compared to arithmetic - just add or multiply numbers, it's simple. On the other hand, chess is like algebra - no matter how much you watch someone solve quadratic equations, you won't understand the essence. Beth didn't even see how all the pieces were moving (for example, how does she know that a pawn can move two squares ahead on the first move?), so she couldn't draw any conclusions, and I didn't even mention that chess has many additional aspects, such as padding, taking on a pass, and and so on.

I also want to debunk a popular myth that fans of the show often say: *"Jose Capablanca (the 3rd world champion) learned to play chess at the age of 4, simply by watching his father and his father's friends play".* I'm going to surprise many people, but this doesn't prove anything. Capablanca, unlike Beth, had access to dozens of games played by his father, and he could see the entire game from start to finish, while Beth only saw a few seconds of the game.

My main point is that many of the feats that are being presented to us as the character's insane learning abilities are actually just plain old intuition. Let me give you an example: we have an abstract person who is given a test with 100 questions, and when they read the questions, they realize that they don't know anything at all, so they start answering the questions intuitively. At the end of the test, they find out that they have answered all 100 questions correctly. So, according to these people's logic, this guy has an insane learning ability, right? So it's important to see the difference between learning abilities and unexplained intuition, so honestly, I would rank Koji higher than L in terms of learning abilities.
