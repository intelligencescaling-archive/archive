Death Note DEBUNK clarification post.

I know it seems like I'm beating a dead horse at this point but people keep misunderstanding my arguments against death note just because I don't spoonfeed them all the details.

**Argument 1:** "There's no narrative implication that L lied about being able to learn how to fly a helicopter with his intuition alone."

In the manga side story at Wammy's House, L explicitly stated that he is a "dishonest cheating human being who hates losing." Notice the word dishonest? Exactly. This should be enough narrative implication that he would lie to Light in order to assert dominance which is further proven by him stating to the task force earlier on that he is childish and hates to lose. Childish people love to feel smarter and assert dominance over their rivals.

Also assuming there needs to be a narrative implication for the possibility to exist is foolish. Which do you think is more believable if someone on the streets came up to you and told you a statement? "I ate breakfast this morning" or "This morning I woke up to see an elephant standing in my room" or "I got kidnapped by an extraterrestrial force in the morning and now I'm back"? If you think they are all equally likely, your IQ is very unlikely to be above 100. This is simple probabilistic reasoning. If something is less likely to occur, it is more likely to be a lie if someone says it did occur. In other words, the higher you scale L's helicopter feat, the less probable of an occurrence it is, and thus the more likely it is to be a lie.

It isn't a stretch at all to say that L who is Kira's biggest enemy chose to lie to the biggest Kira suspect to psychologically disrupt his thinking. Attempts to disrupt the opponent's thinking happens in all the time in psychological stories.

**Argument 2:** "But you don't believe the interpretation so it's not valid."

That's not how reasoning works. You don't abandon all alternative possibilities just because you think a particular possibility is the most probable. That would be stupid. This is the problem with people misusing Occam's razor... also after reading the manga side story where L confirms he's a dishonest person, now I'm starting to think this interpretation might be the likeliest, even more likely than my previous interpretation that L meant he *learnt* it through intuition.

**Argument 3:** "You think Light doesn't even know how to read in English."

Some people seriously think I think this. When I mentioned how Light struggles to read English at the start, I wasn't implying that he can't read at all, my argument was only a small subcomponent of my larger debunk against 2 arguments that Death Note fans use.

The first argument Death Note fans use is "Light gets high grades in X, Y, Z, therefore he automatically scales higher than another character who gets low grades for X, Y, Z (such as Yuuichi or a real life Japanese student)."

This is what I intended to debunk.

**Objection:** "But I never made that argument!"

Then my debunk is not for you is it?! If you don't believe that specific argument, you can ignore my debunk of it.

The second argument I intended to debunk was "The death note verse is more sophisticated than other fictional verses so given an equal scope of operation, the Death Note verse's character scales higher via a difference in complexity."

Someone else already made a post debunking the microcosm/macrocosm argument for Death Note so I won't go into detail on all that, but part of the reason for why I made this debunk against the sophistication of the death note verse was to prevent oversimplifications like the kind that overrates feats within the verse (such as stock market, Light manipulating the world, etc.)

The COUNTERargument from me against the above 2 arguments involves points such as:

Light getting 100 for his grade 12 mathematics subject despite doing basic algebra often learnt in year 9-10 in preparation for his exams (in other words 2-3 years behind our real life verse).

Light getting 100 for English despite struggling to read the Death Note in English at the beginning. (I'm not saying he can't read at all... I'm saying that being "top student of Japan" in the Death Note verse is not that special since it doesn't require fluent reading. Me > Light in VCI btw and it's not even close.)

Light getting 100 for Japanese despite showing no Japanese sophistication whatsoever in his speech, nor being able to notice the intent of L's grammatical trap with the hidden message after a few cognitive distractions. (The chapter 3 youth from 18LOH alone surpasses him in this domain in terms of VCI and with low difficulty).

Light in chapter 2 commenting on how his classmates aren't even able to publicly entertain a basic moral question in class nor are such questions even provided.

OBVIOUSLY this doesn't mean Light sucks at those subjects, it just means that getting 100 for those subjects in the Death Note verse educational system is much easier than getting 100 for those subjects in real life because the real world is more advanced than Death Note.

You could obviously make excuses for all of those occurrences such as by claiming Light did easy math because he wanted to go easy on himself, or that he found it a pain to read the Death Note in English because he just doesn't like the language subjectively, etc., but the moment this ambiguity appears and you start hypothesizing to refute my counterargument, the original argument (that Light scales above people of other verses such as Yuuichi in FSIQ due to a difference in grades) also falls flat automatically because the inherent ambiguity of the interpretation means that both the downscaling interpretation (that the verse is unsophisticated) and the upscaling interpretation (that the verse is comparable to similar verses like our own) cannot be confirmed. The only difference is that the downscaling interpretation actually has **more narrative proof** than the upscaling interpretation!

For example when Near reasoned out the mindset of the crowd that raided his building and there was literally no variability in their mindsets, despite the reasoning being an oversimplification of human psychology.

Even more definitive proof being the president having a nuclear button that he can press to start an all out global war (which Mello threatened him with) despite the protocols irl being far more complex with the authentication processes. The president in Death Note literally had someone tie him up so that he couldn't press the button that magically starts a nuclear war lmfao.

This is mainly to debunk interpretations that overglaze scopal influence (such as L's detective feats, stock market feat, Light's foresight, etc.) but it also gives evidence towards my interpretation of the verse and by extension the educational system of the Death Note verse being oversimplified caricatures of reality.

**Argument 4:** Light grows from his antifeats so you can't use them!

This might be the most important one. Some people say me debunking his Lind L Taylor antifeat is useless because he grows from his mistakes...

Firstly, there is no such thing as useless information. Information may relatively lack use given a particular context, but when it comes to scaling characters in narrative scaling, you need to have a comprehensive understanding of their narrative and everything involved including information that people often ignore (the sophistication of the verse, the context, the subtleties of their actions, etc.)

Secondly, even if we pretend that "growing" automatically makes past information useless regardless of the context... Light literally stays just as stupid and there is no evidence he grows definitively from his past mistakes. Lind L. Tailor is honestly overexaggerated as an antifeat (it's still an antifeat but there are worse ones such as the sacrifice he made to have the FBI find L's name and also the final warehouse confrontation). For example, how can you say Light's emotional management or caution antifeats (such as shouting in his home) was overcome when he literally continues to do the same thing over and over again later on against L, Mello, and Near? Where is the growth? Do you really think I fixate on the Lind L. Tailor trap? I already detailed enough antifeats to make this clear within my many debunk posts, but if you reread Death Note, you'll notice I haven't even made any posts on like 80% of Light's antifeats because I got bored of debunking it.

Thirdly if you're going to ignore the character's antifeats because they "become smarter," does that mean you're going to ignore the character's feats as well whenever they become dumber? Or do you have a double standard here? If you're going to be consistent, Light becoming very stupid at the end renders many of his categories useless (especially memory and reasoning). L has an even bigger impact since his best feats happen at the start of the manga and even prior to the manga's start (childhood, middle age, etc.) and he continues becoming less and less intelligent over time in the manga with the worst being right up until his death, so L automatically scales below average human now since all his previous feats after his intelligence decreased becomes useless. So which is it huh? Keep your standards consistent.

I never scale a character based on their past antifeats alone btw. If they truly overcome their antifeats, I'll take that into account. If they only ***pretend*** to have overcome it, then you need to think more critically and stop deluding yourself into thinking they somehow magically destroyed the antifeat component of their mind. It's not always the case that they overcome an antifeat just because they *slightly grow smarter.*
