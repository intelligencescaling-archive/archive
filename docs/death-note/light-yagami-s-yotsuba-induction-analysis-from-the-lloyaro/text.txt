Light Yagami's yotsuba induction analysis (from Lloyaro vs TertyWater debate on cord)

Early into the manga when he was still Kira, Light mentions that if an adult had the power of Kira, he would use it for monetary purposes which actually strengthens the soon to come predispositions.

Light had used a system which allowed him to look at police, media and public data from all around the world which is an immeasurably massive amount of data. L had previously concluded that Kira was in Japan so he started with that. However considering the ambiguities of what has happened so far, Light wants to test the strength of this proposition

First he observes the on-going deaths caused by heart failure for the criminals in Japan and compares them to the names of criminals broadcasted within Japanese media sources and is able to notice that they are analogous.

General conclusion (1) - Kira is in Japan

It's been established that Kira kills using heart failure thus he looks for heart failure victims that they had unable to pin on Kira yet, so he planned to carefully examine every person who died of heart failure in Japan including non-criminals over the span of the last 5 months (roughly the time since when Light was first confined).

Light was able to notice a vague statistical pattern among just 3 victims, all dead for the same reason of cardiac failure.

• Roppei Tamiya - Sekimaru Corp: Vice president of development

• Kouji Aoi - Aoi industries: director of integrated systems

• Takeyoshi Moriya - Yotsuba: Former vice president.

What's consistent among all 3 of them based off Light's analysis is that before their death they were at one point holding important positions in the Japanese business world.

General conclusion (2) - All 3 of these deaths are people who held important positions in their respective companies.

Light decides to head into the stock market and do data analysis on market trends for all 3 of those companies. In this case he would be laying out significant points of comparison for non-verbal data analytics to observe complex dynamic activities and patterns on the 3 different companies aforementioned. he is able to make the following conclusion:

General conclusion (3) - Yotsuba's stocks gradually rose across the time period whilst Aoi and Sekimaru's stocks declined.

Light begins to notice a pattern in the business world of unusual death that seemed to be congruent with Kira's killing method and stock bias towards a certain business party.

General conclusion (4) - There may be more relevant data leading to Kira in the business world.

Light then proceeds to investigate deaths strictly within the business world. He looks at deaths of businessmen, observes their background information and company, and seeks to observe how the deaths of these businessmen influences Yotsuba where he reenacts the data analysis prior to the 3rd general conclusion.

Of all the deaths in the business world, Light is able to observe 13 different deaths across the span of 3 months and each of those deaths caused a positive shift in the analytics depicting Yotsuba's stock value. Though from the perspective of other companies, only 2 or 3 were beneficial to Yotsuba.

General conclusion (5) - Kira is in support of Yotsuba

Furthermore Light observed that these deaths were not just heart attacks. Besides the first 3 observed heart deaths that set the foundation of the induction, 1 died by suicide, 3 were killed by Kira after being indicted for corruption and the rest died in accidents or by

It was previously established as an inductive rule of belief that Kira kill only through heart attacks. However the patterns up until now can only be consistent by deriving that Kira’s means of murder aren't limited to just heart failure.

General conclusion (6) - Kira can kill in ways other than heart attacks

Light is a discernment of 3 among 42,000+ deaths (Going from May to July). The impressiveness of this is obvious based on how induction is not solely causality but that massive datasets have correlatory obstruction where noise distorts this process.  The basic concept of spurious correlations which make determining causal relationships exponentially difficult.
