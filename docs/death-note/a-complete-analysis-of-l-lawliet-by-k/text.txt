Many readers comment that Mello provides two opposing perspectives on L's role in the case - either he was

source material details though, it becomes obvious.

Mello is initially referenced as potentially suggesting Naomi's assistance saved L. But this isn't logically possible.

exceptional ego and drive to appear faultless. Moreover, L accurately predicted upcoming events that could only
logically be explained by deduction of the full unfolding scenario before to its happening. A deficient or
reactionary approach wouldn't allow such accuracy.

On reflection, Mello acknowledges the statistical implausibility of L Lawliet  being ignorant.

Contrary to common assumptions overlooking them, L's deep indirect manipulative gifts are hinted in LABB
through his subtle guiding the cognitive processes of others to unfold exactly as he intended, while preserving
the appearance of noninvolvement. Many readers themselves are unable to recognise his influence throughout
the story, which speaks volumes. L had singularly engineered the case's every progression and resolution from
outset, maintaining absolute authority through stealthy puppetry.

to find incriminating evidence. L was a far stronger perceiver (one of the best in fictional history) than an
initiator.

While it's easy to believe that, if you were given the abilities of Kira, it would be simple to keep L at bay, L's
psychoanalytic ability means he would have depicted a new "L's Kira" and crafted a plan to capitalise on L's
Kira's weaknesses. The comment that Light should have simply "not killed Lind. L. Taylor" is ignorant - it
ignores Light's character, and it ignored the method by which L devised the plan. L makes extremely abstract
connections between very minute details and observations, based on a miniscule amount of evidence, to create
extremely accurate artistic deductions that reveal to him the entirety of the criminal's behaviour and thoughts. It
would be virtually impossible for any individual to not have L eventually identify him as Kira due to L's
inhuman reasoning ability. He doesn't make lots of one-stop connections like Sherlock Holmes, for example - L
creates extremely complex, long strings of reasoning that twist together and bring about the truth.

Whereas Sherlock has vastly superior observational power and memory, L has supernatural reasoning, abstract
thinking, psychoanalysis and raw intelligence that are completely beyond that of Holmes. Patrick Jane takes a
completely different approach to detective work, utilising his extraordinary EQ to come about his solutions, with
a high dose of intuition to drive his investigations.

In all, I believe L to be one of the most intelligent fictional characters of all time, and the single most intelligent
character in T2 intelligence. He is, in my opinion, the greatest detective, with Patrick Jane and Sherlock Holmes
tied for second place.

Further Note: Usogui is hailed as one of the best manga to display written intelligence, and I strongly agree.
However, I think L in particular is incredibly underestimated due to the way the anime series was dictated - it
leaves out all the allusions that L makes, and it completely forgoes a lot of his elegant reasoning, making it all
appear as "plot driving" or "lucky guessing". When analysed properly, Death Note is a series that has an
incredibly number of mechanisms at play, and is comparable to Usogui in that regard - the main difference
being that Usogui explains all of the reasoning, whereas in Death Note, it is highly glanced over and simplified
for mainstream media, with many of L's deductions being ill explained. L makes near inhuman deductions based
on raw reasoning ability, and is only appreciated if analysing the manga in a distinct, perceptive manner. If
Death Note was not as mainstream as it was, I think L's, Light's, Near's and Mello's intelligences would be
much more highly understood, and the interactions and themes between the characters would be much more
rigorously explored.
