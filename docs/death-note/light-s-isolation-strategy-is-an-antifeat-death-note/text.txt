Light's isolation strategy is TRASH. Feat? More like ANTIFEAT!

**Light's goal:** isolate L from the police force and turn them against each other. The police will therefore investigate L, find his name, and unintentionally allow Light to kill him.

Interesting goal. It makes sense.

So how does Light try to achieve this goal?

After Light's dad reveals at the dinner table the top-secret information from the NPA (national police agency) that L knows Kira is a student from the timings of his killings, Light decides to kill 1 criminal every hour for 48 hours straight by setting the timings of their deaths.

**Light's justification for doing it:** the police and L don’t trust each other. Light exposing that he has access to information from the police force (the information his dad told him) will make L want to investigate the NPA members, the police get mad because they’re being investigated, so they’ll try to track L down, resulting in L's identity being exposed, which Light can use to kill L.

This reasoning for the goal being achieved is absolutely horrendous. The strategy Light implements is even worse because of how badly it corners him.

1: Light gives away his informational advantage, which is access to the national police agency (NPA) information. If Light did not reveal this immediately, he would have a significant advantage over L from the start throughout the whole manga, because Light would be in the dark, meanwhile L's top-secret actions as recorded by the police would be constantly accessible to Light. Instead, he intentionally exposes the fact that he has access to the information from the NPA.

2: Light gives away his even bigger advantage, which is being in the unknown. Kanto's population is massive (around 40 million in 2003 which is when it took place). Meanwhile Light revealing that he has access to the NPA information cuts it down from 40 million to only 141 people + their associates (such as family members). In other words, even if we assume that each of the 141 people placed under direct suspicion result in 10 people overall being suspected (so 1410 total suspects), it is still more than 99.996% of the 40 million population of Kanto eliminated from the suspect list through Light's actions. What kind of stupidity is this?

Counterargument: Light wasn't aware of the specific numbers.

First of all, you don't need to know the exact numbers to understand the basic common sense that cutting down the suspect list from THE ENTIRETY OF KANTO to merely the few people who have access to NPA information is a terrible move, because it eliminates almost the entire population of Kanto from being suspected and makes it easier to investigate and ascertain who Kira is. You might even argue that because DN is fictional, maybe the population of Kanto cannot be compared to real life, that still does not refute the essence of the argument. Also, maybe Light actually does have an idea of the total number of people who will end up being suspected, because he can literally hack his father's computer to access NPA information which he does several times. If you assume he doesn't know, then it's actually an even bigger antifeat because if the number was smaller (for example 50 people instead of 141), then he would get himself into even bigger trouble, and if it was a more closed meeting during which L gave out the information (such as between a few higher ranking authorities among the NPA), then Light would be reducing the suspect list drastically by taking the illogical risk that he took. Either way, he is massively compromising his own position by revealing the fact that he is connected to the NPA, regardless of the specific numbers.

3: Light gives away the fact that he can set the timings of criminal deaths. This is another useful advantage which L knew nothing about, because L assumed from the timings that Kira had to immediately kill, but Light decided to just give away this advantage to implement his horrendous isolation strategy.

Even putting aside all these compromising problems he caused for himself to implement the strategy, the strategy itself is idiotic and unreasonable, as I'll explain in the following point.

4: Light's goal was to make L suspicious of the taskforce and therefore investigate them. This does not logically follow at all. Firstly, if L is a genius (as shown from the fact that he could trick Light into revealing his region), then he's not dumb enough to openly investigate the NPA members to turn them against him. The assumption that L will make his suspicion so obvious that it’ll cause a rift between him and the police and make them turn against each other is foolish, since L is not supposed to be an idiot, so he has the social intelligence to keep his investigation under wraps. Giving away all the advantages I mentioned above, the small potential advantage of making L investigate the NPA so that they both become suspicious of each other is neither likely to succeed if you put aside hindsight bias and evaluate it rationally, nor is it worth it even if success is guaranteed. Even if you become attached to the goal, there are many ways to go about achieving it without giving away all the advantages (NPA information advantage, setting the timings of the deaths information advantage, cutting down the suspect list by 99.996%). This is especially true when you consider the fact that he has the names and faces of all the NPA members, as well as a notebook that he can use to control their actions for 23 days before their death. Also, Light already mentioned how the police and L do not trust each other, so the suspicion and investigation is already prone to occurring even without a lot of input from Light Yagami. He didn't even give a lot of input, all he did was freely give L the information that he's associated with the NPA, hoping L decides to investigate the NPA in an overt way that leads to him getting caught.

Counterargument: Maybe Light killed the criminals every hour for 48 hours straight to scare people off from going after Kira, so that they resign from the task force.

Firstly, there's no need to expose all the information that limits the scope of the suspects and abandons all his advantages if he wants to achieve this. Secondly, if you read chapter 4 of the manga, the police resigned by themselves after finding out that all of the criminals that died had their faces available on the news. They know that they themselves carry their police I.D.s, so they are out in the open, and Kira can kill them. This was the reason they resigned, and it's basic human psychology (self-preservation). Light didn't need to disadvantage himself to make them resign.

In conclusion, Light's isolation strategy is trash if you put aside hindsight bias and look at things from his own perspective. There were many ways to go about it without compromising his massive informational and situational advantages. Instead, it is an antifeat because he gave away all of those advantages I mentioned, just to take an unlikely risk that he could have achieved in much better ways. The whole tactic is an antifeat, and probably one of the biggest antifeats Light commits in the whole manga. It doesn't upscale Light at all, and actually downscales him instead, because even an average human wouldn't make such a horrible compromise.
