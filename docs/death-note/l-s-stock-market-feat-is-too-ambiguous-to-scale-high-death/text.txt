L's stock market feat is too ambiguous to scale high

**What we know about the feat is this:**

L was around 8 years old when he spent 2 years on the stock market with the end result of turning a million dollars into 20 billion dollars (20000X return).

First of all, I will be using Narrative Scaling for this feat, obviously, because it's not valid in scaling systems like Methodology, RFES, Stop Scaling, in the first place.

**The timescale:** L had 2 whole years. I'm not sure if he had a lot of work on the side, but he was a child, so he may have been jobless, and therefore had a lot of time on his hands to become proficient in the stock market. Childhood is also the best time to take advantage of your neuroplasticity for quick learning, which is why so many grandmaster chess players achieve grandmaster level during childhood or at least as a young man, meanwhile it becomes very difficult for people who begin chess at a late age to achieve the same result. This is 2 years of intense learning for someone who is already smart during his childhood when his learning ability is at its peak. Other SCD characters sometimes have a learning ability feat where they take a few seconds to learn something. Meanwhile L has 2 whole years (63120000 seconds) which is a lot even if you take away time for sleeping and other necessities. It's impressive for someone so young, but you can't even quantify the timescale. You can only give a lowball and a highball.

**Economic difference:** Some people have made the argument that the Death Note world is not too different from our world economically, due to the lack of economic collapse, etc., but this is completely ignoring the differences that are shown between our world and DN's world. For example physical laws (optics) which was disconcordant with L's supposed observation ability during the potato chip scene. Or the simplicity of the president having an ultimate nuclear button compared to the complexity of the authentication processes within our world. Or FBIs not being expected to let their superiors know their identity was compromised while on a top secret mission, unlike in our world where nobody would be as easy to dupe as Raye Penber. Many other factors also reduce the chaos of the Death Note world to make it easier for the characters to implement their feats. So the claim about there being no economic difference just because of a lack of collapse is delusional. Therefore, the economic difference of the death note world potentially makes it easier for L to do his stock market feat. We can't even measure how big the difference is though, which makes the feat even more ambiguous and unscalable.

**Luck and intuition:** Luck is always a factor. Even in the real world, there are people achieving higher returns on investment than what L achieved. Usually luck, sometimes years of practice, but luck is definitely a part of it. Intuition is more ambiguous. If you consider it part of cognition, then it is valid for learning ability. But if you think L's intuition is magically gaining knowledge and has nothing to do with cognitive processes, then it's invalid in intelligence scaling.

**How did he achieve the feat?:** We don't know. What companies did he invest in? Did he increase it by waiting for 2 years, or through reinvestments? For example if L increases his stocks by 52% (1.52X) every month for 2 years, that will also be enough to get him the 20 billion dollars. Assuming he distributed it well for the sake of overcoming barriers like liquidity, but still maintained the average monthly increase of 52% over 24 months, that is enough to achieve the feat. Increase the percentage, add in all the other factors like luck and economic difference, and that will decrease the time requirement even more. So it's not as supernatural of a feat as some people overrate it to be, when you take these factors into account. Therefore, there's another ambiguity here. What categories? Reasoning? Learning ability? Intuition? Judgement? All of them? How much for each one? You can't just ignore the other categories that played a part and focus on only a single category to scale as high as possible. You can't even decide on what categories or give enough proof that those specific categories are the ones it upscales significantly.

**In conclusion** L's stock market ability is ambiguous. We don't know the exact timescale, because we cannot quantify how much time L had outside of sleep and daily necessities, only that he spent 2 years. We can't quantify how much luck played a part which is often a very strong factor in the real world. The death note market can't even be compared to the real world because of the economic differences of an oversimplified fictional world vs our chaotic economic system in real life. The categories can't even be specified because of how ambiguous the actual feat was. All 4 of these points make the feat highly ambiguous and unscalable. It's clearly a feat because an average human can't replicate it. But it cannot be scaled very high because of how ambiguous all of the factors that contribute to the feat are.
