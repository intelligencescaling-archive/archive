**1: The Timescale**

Some people claim Light came up with memory loss in a few seconds. This is nonsense. After Misa was apprehended, L let Light do whatever he wants for 3 days straight, during which he came up with the memory loss plan.

Counterargument: but Light had to ask Rem questions after the 3 days passed, and the answers were the basis of his memory loss plan.

Light didn't ask questions out of curiosity just to gather information for coming up with a plan. He instead asked confirmative questions: \["What happens to the ownership of the Death Note after it is returned to the shinigami" **(the ownership reverts to the shinigami)** and "As long as you have one notebook, do you retain memories of all notebooks?" **(yes)**\]

The answer to the first question is common sensical. It's so obvious, even Ryuk guesses the answer on his first try despite not being that knowledgable about the Death Note compared to Rem who confirmed it for Light. And the answer to the second question is what the memory loss plan hinges upon, and Light confirms it by adding "that's correct?" to his question after asking Rem the Yes/No question, so he already assumed in his mind that the answer was probably yes.

So the claim that he came up with the memory loss plan on the spot AFTER asking those questions is irrational to conclude compared to the more logical alternative: Light came up with the memory loss plan over the 3 days at home when he was researching on his computer, and free to do whatever he pleased, meanwhile the plan hinged on some information he needed to know (the ownership of the death note, and retaining one's memories) which he confirmed with Rem after she finally went to him.

If you also consider the rational inference that some ideas about a memory loss plan may have formed in his mind even before Misa was suspected (due to the rules of the notebook), then he had even longer than 3 days to come up with it. So the timescale is ambiguous, but potentially quite high according to the narrative. This reduces the potency of the feat, since thinking time reduces how much intelligence is required.
