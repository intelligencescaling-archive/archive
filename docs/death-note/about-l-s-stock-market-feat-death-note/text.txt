About Stock market feat

L's stock market feat is one of the most debated feats in SCD. There are a lot of people who outright reject this feat or just don't find it impressive enough.

Today, I'm going to share my personal thoughts on this feat.

Quick heads-up: I apologize in advance if there is any misinformation in this analysis.

Also, keep this in mind while reading: I'm probably nowhere near the best at scaling L. So even if my takes are genuinely bad, this feat cannot be instantly invalidated just because of that. Like I said, these are purely my personal opinions.

People generally criticize this feat in two ways:

They think this feat is purely intuition/guessing.

They think this feat is inconsistent.

I will be addressing both of these points.

1) Is the Stock Market feat just intuition/guessing?

First of all, intuition and guessing are not the same thing. Let me briefly define both and give an example for each.

Intuition: Reaching a quick judgment or feeling about a situation without engaging in a conscious, step-by-step reasoning process. Intuition is mostly associated with past experiences, learned patterns, and rapid information processing.

Guessing: Making a prediction about what will happen in the future based on available data, observations, past experiences, or probabilities.

Let's give two examples.

Example 1:

You go out with a friend, and your friend says they are going to a nearby grocery store. After about 20 minutes pass, you think to yourself:

"They'll probably be here in 5 minutes."

And your friend actually arrives 5 minutes later.

Here, you made a simple guess. You made a deduction based on information like the distance to the store, roughly how long shopping takes, and the fact that your friend wouldn't keep you waiting too long.

Example 2:

While watching TV, suddenly, without any apparent reason, you think:

"A bird is going to hit the window in a second."

And a few seconds later, it actually happens.

Since you didn't make a conscious deduction here, we can point to this as an example of intuitive judgment.

Now, let's get back to L's stock market feat.

Financial markets are highly complex systems where massive numbers of investors trade simultaneously, vast amounts of information circulate, and outcomes involve heavy uncertainty.

Because of this, evaluating L's feat as just an ordinary financial success isn't easy.

But for the people saying "L did this purely through intuition," let's look a bit deeper.

Dual Process Theory

In the Dual Process approach, associated with the work of Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, thinking processes are generally explained through fast, automatic processes versus slower, controlled processes.

To put it simply:

System 1: Fast, automatic, and intuitive thinking.

System 2: Slower, conscious, and analytical thinking.

For example, if a friend suddenly throws a ball at you, you might raise your hand and try to catch it without thinking. Your reaction here happens very fast, and you don't do any step-by-step conscious calculation.

In financial markets, however, there are certain cognitive biases that can affect investors' decisions. Fear Of Missing Out (FOMO), loss aversion, and overconfidence are just a few of them.

Kahneman and Tversky's Prospect Theory also reveals that people might not evaluate gains and losses in a perfectly symmetrical way. For instance, people can psychologically feel a loss much more intensely than a gain of the exact same size.

The important point here is this:

This information doesn't mean "investing via intuition will absolutely fail."

However, it makes it incredibly difficult to explain L's feat using only intuition.

Because what we are talking about here isn't a single correct guess. At 8 years old, L asks Watari to buy 1 million pounds worth of stocks in Japanese Yen, and by directing this investment to specific companies, he multiplies it by exactly 20,000 times within two years.

Therefore, explaining this merely as:

"L's instincts were just really good and he got a feel for the right companies."

falls completely short.

Such an extraordinary outcome would have required the phenomenal management of multiple factors: choosing which investments to make, timing the market, assessing the risks to take, and figuring out how to process the obtained information.

That's why it is much more reasonable to explain L's feat through advanced-level analysis, information processing, foresight, and risk assessment abilities rather than simple intuition.

2) Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH)

Now let's move on to another theoretical framework that supports this: The Efficient Market Hypothesis.

The core idea of EMH is quite simple:

«If all publicly available information is already largely reflected in market prices, it is extremely difficult to consistently and systematically beat the market using only that information.»

Let's explain this with a basic example.

Let's say a hamburger at a McDonald's branch costs $5.

One day, McDonald's drops the price of the hamburger to $3, and this discount is visible to everyone.

You see this and say:

"The burger is $3! I should buy it right now because I gained an advantage by noticing this."

But at that exact same moment, other people are also seeing that the price is $3.

Therefore, this information is not exclusive to you. Other people know the exact same information and can act on it accordingly.

If we adapt this to the stock market:

Imagine a company announces some highly important and positive news.

The news comes out → Everyone finds out → Investors react → The stock price changes based on the new information.

When you see the news, you might think:

"Good news came out about the company, if I buy the stock now, I'll have an advantage over everyone else."

But the core approach of EMH is this:

"If this information is already accessible to everyone, market participants have likely already priced this information in. Therefore, it is not easy to constantly generate extra returns using only this information."

Bringing this back to L's feat:

L taking an investment worth roughly 1 million pounds at age 8 and multiplying it by 20,000 times within two years can be evaluated as a feat that requires vastly more than just "he made a good guess" or "he got lucky."

Because making a single correct guess and exponentially growing a massive capital over a long period of time are not the same thing.

From the perspective of EMH, if information accessible to everyone in the market is already reflected in the prices, simply evaluating general market information intuitively might not be enough to explain such a miraculous outcome.

Therefore, EMH provides a theoretical framework that weakens the simple explanation of:

"L intuitively picked the right companies and won because of that."

However, we need to make an important distinction here.

EMH does not prove that L's feat is impossible.

Real markets are not entirely flawless. Certain opportunities can arise due to market anomalies, investor behavior, information asymmetry, and various market inefficiencies.

So my argument is not:

"Because of EMH, it is impossible for L to have done this."

Rather, it is:

"It is vastly insufficient to explain L's 20,000x feat with just intuition or simple guessing."

Even if the manga doesn't show exactly how L pulled off this feat, considering the magnitude of the outcome, it is a much more reasonable interpretation to assume that abilities like advanced analysis, information processing, foresight, decision-making, and risk assessment played a major role.

2) The feat is inconsistent, and because it is inconsistent, it is invalid.

First off, I don't really agree with this claim either.

SCD characters have feats in a lot of absurd categories like CPI/VSI and AC. Of course, you could argue that since themes like reasoning, planning, strategy, and manipulation are generally at the forefront of these series, while categories like CPI/VSI aren't, we can't call those inconsistent. I get that, but L's situation is still different.

The main argument is this:

The reasoning L showed in the stock market is highly inconsistent with his feats in the manga, because someone capable of that level of reasoning should have performed much better in the manga.

Let me clear something up about this topic right now.

There are 2 scaling systems:

Normal scaling: Under normal scaling, we use all of a character's narrative or methodological feats to scale them.

Methodology scaling: Under methodology scaling, we only validate the feats where the logic behind the character's actions is explicitly explained.

If you are using methodology scaling, I can't say anything if you choose not to use or hype up this feat.

But if you are using normal scaling, you have to accept this feat as valid.

So saying, "It's inconsistent, so I'm not using it"—sorry, but I disagree.

Because L couldn't even show his full capabilities during the Kira case anyway. He was constantly restricted in his movements throughout the investigation. That is why we never saw his full potential during the Kira case, and why I don't buy the claims that "this feat is inconsistent and therefore invalid."

But to those saying, "L showed a very absurd feat in the stock market but was very lacking in the manga," I will say this: L's performance in the manga is totally fine. I think people need to reconsider just how incredibly difficult the Kira case actually was. And additionally, I'll say this: when making criticisms, put yourself in the characters' shoes instead of looking at it purely from a reader's perspective.

Characters in Death Note constantly explain the story to us, so some impressive feats don't get hyped up enough in people's eyes, and when you add Death Note's fast pacing into the mix, there are feats that get completely downplayed.

Look, a lot of series aren't like Death Note. Take COTE, for example. Ayanokoji generally never reveals his true thoughts or plans to us readers, so when he suddenly manages to execute a plan, some people get super impressed and heavily overhype the feat. I'm not trying to hate on COTE here, just using it as an example. And I'm not saying characters in Death Note never do this either—for instance, the memory loss plan hits hard specifically because we only find out about it right as the plan is wrapping up.

Anyway, long story short: if you use normal scaling, this feat is valid. I've addressed the other arguments too.
