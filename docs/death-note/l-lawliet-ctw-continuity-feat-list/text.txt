Introduction and Notes:

L Lawliet is the major antagonist in the Death Note Series, written by Tsugumi Ohba. Lawliet has made his initial appearance in the Manga being the original material published. The next releases which remained spiritually connected to the original material were the Death Note Film Series and the Anime shortly after. The Film Series represented a separate continuity as it deviated from the exact settings of the original manga to the point it created its own separate identity. This becomes clearer to the audience with the introduction of L: CTW to its separate timeline of events, hence why the CTW continuities are considered not “canon” to the Manga series.

L: Change The World is a Death Note spinoff made as a continuation of the Death Note Film Series I and II [2006] which came into the market in the form of a Novel [L: Change The World] and a sequel of the film series [Death Note III - L: Change The World] in the following years. One of the reasons for their existence is to reveal a more “human” side of Lawliet.
The Novel and Film are different from each other, to the point where they have a lot of details about them changed and even contradictory to each other. For this reason, they are considered SEPARATE CONTINUITIES. This means that for CTW Lawliet, there are 2 separate continuities with different feats aside from the already different “Canon'' version [Manga Timeline].

CTW Lawliet’s Scaling should therefore be specified and carefully considered. The most plausible one is to consider “Composite CTW Lawliet” which covers the feats of both continuities despite their contradicting character at times. This version is not to be confused with “Composite Lawliet”, which should cover all the materials displaying L, including the Canon continuity [Manga, LABB Novel]:

Death Note: Los Angeles BB Murder Case was created by a different author after the Death Note Manga, before the remastered version of the anime, and by contrast before the CTW Novel. The author of Death Note, Tsugumi Ohba, read LABB and approved of it, which is directly shown by how the remastered version of the anime name-drops the case directly canonizing it to that timeline. The CTW Novel and Movie do the same thing, where the case is mentioned by name and is unmistakably the same case we read about in LABB, the specific nuance in the connection between LABB and CTW is that since Mello is never mentioned, one might call the connection between them dubious as he was the narrator of LABB, but the better interpretation would be that the case still happened, but there was no Mello to write about it, making it so the existence of a physical book on LABB in the universe of CTW is negated, but the case itself still happened all the same. This is why LABB should be generally perceived as a “shared timeline” with every other main piece of Death Note media, rather than a continuity knot for the different timelines in the future.

(LN) — It should be noted that from here on out, what Lawliet does is completely improvised, working on the impossibility-turned-into-reality that Hatsune injected Maki with the virus, and now can’t rely on the contingencies he’s previously established to put the dagger in Blue Ship and save Maki’s life, so he has to move quickly.

Acting swiftly, L decides to send and deploy a hot-air balloon to transport Suruga and the crucial materials in a truck. With Maki showing symptoms, they urgently head to a laboratory to commence antidote production. Fortunately, the FBI intervenes, preventing Kujo from closing in on L, a testament to their shift in strategy after the recent events; L had already begun formulating his scheme by anticipating that the FBI would come to his aid as an extra barrier to defend against Kujo troops. Analyzing the intercepted intel, L deduces that the agent's capture was deliberate, intended to instill fear, and broadcast Kujo's next moves. Meanwhile, Maki takes a bold step by arranging a summit where she seemingly aligns with Kujo, only to execute a sacrificial plan to protect Lawliet and Matsuda. However, Kujo's betrayal unfolds swiftly when Matoba intervenes, leading to Maki being forcefully taken along with the antidote. L attempts to fight, even getting slashed apart by some of the personnel, but he still manages to keep his composure and fight, but alas, it isn’t enough to stop Maki from getting taken.

L expresses extreme guilt and desperation at Maki, someone who he has been working so hard to protect potentially at the brink of death, and even asks Light Yagami from the afterlife whether he’s truly done the right thing, and what he should do in the future.

Returning to Suruga, L receives a crucial file from Matsuda, one of his old agents during the Kira Case, swiftly comprehending its contents with his remarkable analytical prowess. L arrives at the meeting and presents the Death Note to Blue Ship, claiming he had bluffed about burning it earlier. Lawliet effectively created a convincing illusion of his death while simultaneously gaining access to the virus trigger and exposing Blue Ship's fake Death Note.

To evade police pursuit, L hijacks his truck's controls and creates a diversion by distributing sports memorabilia to a crowd.

The earlier death of a criminal during the presidential threat is explained by a detail from Misa's past actions, recorded in L's report which I explained earlier, showing that L essentially fabricated the existence of the Death notes from the very start, manipulating and stringing along people during all of this time.
The final confrontation occurs on the plane as Blue Ship’s last effort as Lawliet expertly cornered them into this situation, where L scales the aircraft using a harpoon and rope. He confronts Kujo telling her to let go of Maki as a hostage and stop her crusade against the world once and for all. In this scene, L absolutely sees through her and is able to absolutely destroy her reason for jumpstarting the entire novel. She first asks him that as someone so powerful and intelligent as him, why does he continue to save people after seeing just how terrible and self-serving humanity is? It is a valid question at the end of the day: why protect a species that ultimately harms more than to preserve or heal? But Lawliet immediately proposes an answer.
He states that humanity isn’t perfect, and neither are the laws that seek to punish the evil that humans are capable of committing — because at the end of the day, the law is a notion and purely abstract idea created by humanity. However, through the law, we can see that humanity’s inner desire to heal and protect is displayed with utter objectivity. The law never needed to be created — it never once was something imposed on humans by a force of nature as much as religion or other divergent forms of philosophy appears to abstract — rather, the law is humanity’s inner wish to preserve, protect, and value good rather than evil. Ignoring this objective truth is nothing more than deliberately misinterpreting the inner machinations behind humanity and what drives people.
What L represents is the collective wish for humanity to pursue justice through the law as diligently and relentlessly as possible; for as long as he exists as “L”, and for as long as at least ONE human wishes for justice in a cruel and unforgiving world, he will never relent.
Kujo is utterly baffled at L’s relentless pursuit of justice, and before she can finish rejecting his notion, Lawliet sees through her immediately and reveals a truth that immediately catches her off guard:
Kujo was from the Wammy House.

This also explains why L never aimed to simply kill Kujo until now, because by not killing her, Kujo now BELIEVES in him. Because now, going against this means to question why she continues to exist at this very moment. Effectively, L is existentially manipulating her.

She lost belief in humanity due to her failures of a covert operation in the Wammy House, which led her to leave immediately and mask her identity. Above all else, she loathed herself because she thought she failed Watari — the man who trained her to become a competent specialist. All of the countless emotions of regret, despair, self-hatred, and desperation was within L’s design. He wanted to remind her of this pain, because he didn’t want her to hide from it anymore, just as she hid from her duties and responsibilities years ago. But at the brink of it all…
He smiles.

He reveals one more layer of information to her; he informs her that she was assigned a letter directly by the Wammy House DESPITE her defecting. A letter from the Wammy House means that one who has graduated from there is one out of 26 people in the world who is tasked directly by Watari to change the world. Watari sent this letter to her just before she left, but she never received it.
Watari never once lost trust in her despite her failure. To him, Kujo was “K” — signifying “KEEP YOUR WAY.”
At this moment, Kujo breaks if she hasn’t done so already, completely at the will of L who has expertly concealed this information from her this whole novel and reveals it to her in this critical moment to completely destroy her reason for doing all of this if it wasn’t destroyed already. He asks her one question:
Will she continue to spit on Watari’s trust and faith in her even after his death?
Instantly, Kujo lets go of Maki, completely having a change of heart purely due to Lawliet’s words in a conversation that only lasted for a span of a few minutes at absolute most, turning against Blue Ship and everything she worked hard to accomplish over the months.
