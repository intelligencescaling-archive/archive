**For example, here is some research that shows execution can deter people from committing crime.**

1: Understanding the correlation between homicide rates and execution

[https://archive.md/20010713071806/http://my.voyager.net/\~jayjo/capdeter.htm](https://archive.md/20010713071806/http://my.voyager.net/~jayjo/capdeter.htm)

2: Does Capital Punishment Have a Deterrent Effect? New Evidence from Postmoratorium Panel Data.

[https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5207057\_Does\_Capital\_Punishment\_Have\_a\_Deterrent\_Effect\_New\_Evidence\_from\_Postmoratorium\_Panel\_Data](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5207057_Does_Capital_Punishment_Have_a_Deterrent_Effect_New_Evidence_from_Postmoratorium_Panel_Data)

\[We examine the deterrent hypothesis by using county-level, postmoratorium panel data and a system of simultaneous equations. The procedure we employ overcomes common aggregation problems, eliminates the bias arising from unobserved heterogeneity, and provides evidence relevant for current conditions. Our results suggest that capital punishment has a strong deterrent effect; each execution results, on average, in eighteen fewer murders—with a margin of error of plus or minus ten. Tests show that results are not driven by tougher sentencing laws and are robust to many alternative specifications.\]

3: Deterrence versus Brutalization: Capital Punishment's Differing Impacts among States

[https://scholarlycommons.law.emory.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=faculty-articles](https://scholarlycommons.law.emory.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1142&context=faculty-articles)

This one is actually very interesting because it shows that capital punishments can even increase crime rates. But once the executions increase above a threshold, the crime rates are then decreased by the executions. This makes sense if you think about things from the perspective of criminals and society at large. It's not just capital punishment laws that matter, but moreso the public being aware of the punishments being enacted. If there are only a few punishments, this brutalization may influence criminals to commit more crime, but when the punishment becomes more certain, it has the opposite effect. This is very relevant to Light Yagami because of how rapidly he was executing criminals to show that he's serious about the punishments and it isn't just for show. Many of the deaths were even publicized on mainstream media outlets to the point where the whole world became aware of Kira, unlike hidden background executions in real life that the public doesn't hear or care about.

Also, since Light's identity was anonymous, there's no interference from things like nepotism (he was willing to use the death note on his own family) or bribery (since Light didn't use the Death Note for profit the way Yotsuba did). This cold portrayal and decision making increases the effectiveness compared to the justice system of random example countries you can cherrypick.

**Here are some interesting excerpts from the last article I linked:**

\[On average, the states where capital punishment deters murder execute many more people than do the states where capital punishment does not deter murder. I show that a threshold number of executions exists, which is approximately nine executions during the sample period. In states that conducted more executions than the threshold, each execution, on average, deterred murder. In states that conducted fewer executions than the threshold, the executions, on average, increased the murder rate. Perhaps each execution contributes to brutalizing the society and increasing murder. However, if a state executes many people, then criminals become convinced that the state is serious about the punishment, and the criminals start to reduce their criminal activity. When the number of executions exceeds the threshold, the deterrence effect begins to outweigh the brutalization effect.\]

\[First, if deterrence is the objective, then capital punishment generally succeeds in the few states with many executions. Second, the many states with numbers of executions below the threshold may be executing people needlessly. Indeed, instead of deterring crime, the executions may be inducing additional murders: a rough total estimate is that, in the many states where executions induce murders rather than deter them, executions cause an additional 250 murders per year. Third, to achieve deterrence, states must generally execute many people. If a state is unwilling to establish such a large execution program, it should consider abandoning capital punishment.\]

Interesting isn't it? If the capital punishment is there to only execute 1 or 2 people and keep their deaths private from the public at large, it doesn't do much and can even have the opposite to the intended effect. But once it reaches a threshold and people become cognizant of the punishments the way they did when Light and Mikami executed criminals en masse, it becomes an effective deterrent.
