COMMENT: The creators successfully crafted a chess game, but that is where the achievement ends. Numerous questions arise regarding the board position; it is difficult to comprehend how the pieces transitioned from one configuration to another (as depicted in the photo below) in mere seconds. The film fails to illustrate or audibly convey any significant movement of pieces. Now, turning to the board: the black knight inexplicably occupies C1, and the queen's pawns have vanished. While these may seem like minor details, the primary concern is: "What was the previous move of the white pieces?" Not only are they facing a potential checkmate in one move, which could easily be overlooked due to human error—such occurrences do happen in real games—but how can one rationalize the positioning of the white queen directly opposite the black rook, seemingly awaiting its own demise? There’s nothing to analyze seriously.

SECOND BOARD ENCOUNTER (BONUS)

L plays chess with himself again, after the burden of events in the film. Next following line: Nf6, d4, Neg4, f3. (Yes, black’s King & Queen are swapped, I checked that several times.)

Position on the board… strange, yet still possible, until you look at White’s rook at H3, and it’s black’s turn, any amateur chess player will simply exchange a Bishop for a Rook because it’s intuitive that in this exact position it’s the best choice, you are trading Bishop (3 centipawns) for a Rook (5 centipawns) a +2 profit. But L decides not to do so.

[2:10:45] – L plays with himself.

NOTE: Point here I’m trying to make is that, if L really played against himself, in a pretty much balanced game - chess, position would have not been +2.3; rather +0.5 at max.

CONCLUSION: In the original manga, Yagami Light and L Lawliet are depicted not merely as intelligent individuals but as intellectual prodigies. However, the footage presented reveals a distinct lack of chess proficiency. It appears that the filmmakers could have simply instructed the actors to replicate a game played by two random grandmasters while referencing a written record of moves. For those unfamiliar, this approach may seem acceptable, but we must contend with the reality before us. Can we regard this as a notable feat or an anti-feat? Considering the film's budget and its earnest attempt to forge something original, I would advocate for overlooking these shortcomings. Failing to do so undermines critical concepts such as: PSI (Problem Solving Intelligence), Faster Thinking, Lateral Thinking, Critical Thinking.I would estimate Light and L's elo rating to be around 600-700 | Max: 850, albeit with a sense of frustration and disbelief.
