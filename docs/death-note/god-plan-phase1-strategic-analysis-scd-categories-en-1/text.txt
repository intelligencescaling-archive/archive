This report re-analyzes the complete GOD PLAN — Phase 1 document as a strategic artifact: what it says, how it is built, and every category of intelligence it puts to work. By explicit request, it contains no scores of any kind — no ratings, no weights, no baseline comparisons. It is a structural reading followed by a complete map of the SCD categories the plan exercises.

The object of this analysis is the GOD PLAN — Phase 1 document in its entirety: a first-person strategic doctrine written by Light Yagami after the defeat of Near, in which Kira's rule stops being a regime of punishment and starts becoming a project of permanent world order. The text is organized as a vision statement, a four-stage deployment plan, an objective system of twenty-three codified items, five legislative instruments, a financial engine of USD 52,000 million per year, and an eight-country territorial campaign — closing with a self-diagnostic in which the author corrects his own project. It is, in the most literal sense, a governing document.
Two decisions define this edition. First, it is score-free: the previous report evaluated the plan and measured its impact on Light's matrix; this one deliberately drops every numeric judgment and asks only structural questions — what the plan is, how its parts connect, and which capabilities it requires. Second, the SCD mapping in chapter 4 is an inventory, not a ranking of points: it identifies all sixty-six of the one hundred and twenty-five taxonomy subcategories that the plan exercises, explains how each one is used, and states count-based shares of engagement. No 1-100 rating, evidential weight or score delta appears anywhere in this document.
All quotations are the author's own words from the source document, translated from the Spanish original. Where the source uses shorthand — 'the Fund of the Americas' for the Fund of Security of the Americas, 'T1/T2' for the Tipo 1 and Tipo 2 populations — this report keeps the document's own vocabulary and glosses it on first use. One editorial assumption is declared up front: 'the complete plan' is taken to be the GOD PLAN — Phase 1 text as supplied, whose doctrine germinated in the second half of Gotnw's chapter 27 and is here analyzed at full maturity.
2. The Plan at a Glance

The plan opens by naming its own horizon: to build a world where crime no longer needs to be punished indefinitely, because the conditions that produce it have ceased to exist. The terminal objective follows immediately: make the order established by Kira survive me, transforming itself into a political, institutional and cultural structure capable of sustaining itself. Victory over Near removed the last immediate threat, and the document treats that victory as precisely the moment when the real problem begins — the world keeps producing criminality, corruption and conflict that can erode the new order. Phase 1 therefore exists to convert a personal deterrent into a civilization that no longer needs one.
The answer is an architecture, not a list of enemies. Three complementary fronts divide the work of the new order: SANCTION contains the threats that persist; STATE modifies the material and juridical conditions that produce criminality; SOUL works on the ideas, values and generations that will decide whether the order outlives its founder. The document states its central design rule in one line — each front must compensate for the limitations of the other two — and then proves the rule negatively, showing three partial victories (military, economic, institutional) that all fail alone. Every component of Phase 1, from the five laws to the Tipo 1 movement, is a part placed to close one of those failure modes.
Deployment is staged. Stage 0 consolidates control, executors and resources; Stage 1 converts principles into laws and majorities; Stage 2 converts laws into territorial and institutional results; Stage 3 removes the founder from the dependency chain and hands the system to institutions, culture and a prepared succession. The phase declares its own exit condition: it is complete only when authority, institutions and culture have stopped depending exclusively on one single person. 3. The Strategic Plan Explained

   1. Design philosophy: from deterrence to permanence
Every strategic document answers a question; the question here is unusually honest. The God Plan could have stopped at deterrence — the Death Note already works, criminals die, the world obeys. Instead the author asks what deterrence cannot do: it cannot make itself unnecessary, it cannot survive its own author, and it cannot stop the machine that manufactures new criminals. The document's famous relapse logic — 'If I recover a territory but do not change the conditions that allowed crime to settle there, I will have achieved a temporary victory' — is not decoration; it is the plan's founding observation, repeated at the territorial, economic and institutional levels.
This produces the plan's governing distinction between temporary victory and permanence. A temporary victory removes an actor; permanence removes the reasons an actor can exist. Accordingly, Phase 1 spends almost none of its text on punishment and almost all of it on the conditions around punishment: tax design, juvenile justice, port controls, agricultural alternatives, school programs, the pricing of masculinity in a generation of young men. The Death Note still operates — Sanction is one of the three fronts — but it is deliberately scheduled for obsolescence inside the plan's own Stage 3, where stability must survive without depending indefinitely on Kira's intervention (objective S3).
The design philosophy also explains the document's tone. Light writes as a head of state drafting a program, not as a vigilante keeping a ledger: he names budget shares, article numbers and review clauses. The plan is self-consciously an institutional object — meant to be read, audited and inherited. That is why the artifact is best understood as constitution-building: the notebook founds the order, and the GOD PLAN — Phase 1 is the founding charter that intends to outlive the notebook.

   2. The three-front architecture
The three fronts are not departments; they are a single mechanism viewed from three angles. Sanction answers what must be contained, State transforms what can be corrected through institutions, and Soul works on what can be prevented by forming future generations. The document's design rule — each front must compensate for the limitations of the other two — is elaborated in its closing argument as three paired failures: a territory recovered without changing its conditions is a temporary victory; economic improvement left under criminal dominion is an opportunity captured by the organizations; institutions built without educational transformation reproduce the same problems in the next generation. 'I cannot allow myself that contradiction' is the sentence that locks the three fronts together.

The compensation logic also assigns each front a time horizon. Sanction operates in the present — it is the only front that can act tonight. State operates on a legislative and budgetary cycle — years. Soul operates on a generational cycle — decades. By combining the three, the plan always has an active instrument at every time scale, which is exactly what a deterrence-only regime lacks: it has no future tense. This is also why the identity of the front matter: the plan does not ask whether Kira is feared; it asks whether Kira is still needed, and arranges the world so that the answer converges on no.

   3. The phase engine: Stage 0 → 3
The stage engine is the plan's answer to a classic coup problem: new orders fail either because they institutionalize too early, on paper without power, or too late, when power has hardened into a personality. The document sequences the crossing deliberately. Stage 0 touches no institution at all — it secures the executor, the control mechanisms, the money and the diagnosis. Stage 1 writes the order into law while deterrence still backs it. Stage 2 makes the state perform: laws become plans, plans become recovered territories, and recovered territories are defined as incomplete 'while their inhabitants continue to depend on the same conditions that facilitated the criminal organizations' dominion'. Stage 3 removes the founder: the test of the whole construction is that it keeps working when he stops.

Objective O1 is the political master key, and the document states its mechanics without embarrassment: fund the campaigns of the most apt profiles — center and social-democratic figures who reject extremist ideologies — with Near's money, selecting candidates 'willing to accept the agenda in exchange for greater funding in education and security', and binding them to the standardized national plans and the five laws. It is the L-operation of the Matsuda episode scaled to presidencies: the target is given a choice whose favorable branch has been built by the planner. One drafting inconsistency is worth flagging on a close reading: O2 speaks of passing the laws in 'the six priority countries', while the territorial chapter defines seven priority countries plus Colombia as coordination center. The mechanism (majorities in every participating state) is unaffected; the count is simply not synchronized between sections.
The protection triad is what elevates the document from program to system. P1 maintains the separation between the identity of Light and the figure of Kira — the concealment that keeps a decapitation strike impossible. P2 forbids institutional continuity from resting on any single individual — including Light himself. P3 names the four risks that P1 and P2 cannot dissolve: succession, differences of interpretation, conflicts of interest, and the intervention of Ryuk — the one variable in the document that does not respond to human motivations and therefore cannot be engineered, only accommodated.

   5. The legislative core: the Five Laws
The State front's legislative core consists of five laws, and the document drafts them with real normative technique — numbered articles, quotas, exemptions, review clauses and transition regimes. Their selection is itself strategic: none of the five is a 'Kira law'. They are water pricing, juvenile responsibility, child protection, a security fund and sentencing reform — the legislative surface of an ordinary, defensible social program. The new order smuggles itself into legitimacy wearing exactly the clothes a democracy would expect of a serious government, which is precisely what makes the package passable by real majorities (O2).

   6. The financial engine: the Fund of Security of the Americas
Law 4 is the plan's treasury, and its first line contains the whole maneuver: the Fund is financed by an allocation equal to 6% of the United States defense budget, a cut enacted 'after making the new president win'. The reference estimate is USD 52,000 million per year. Read as a sequence, it is a complete capture operation — install a head of state (O1), redirect a slice of his military budget (Law 4), and convert the world's largest war treasury into the operating budget of the new order. It is also, characteristically, an act of poetic accounting: the campaigns that elect the president are themselves financed with Near's money, so the adversary funds the election, the election funds the cut, and the cut funds the order.

   7. The territorial campaign: eight differential strategies
The territorial chapter is where the plan stops being doctrine and becomes campaign. Latin America is read as one interconnected structure — production, transit, financing and distribution cross borders, so national results depend on regional cooperation — and the author rejects isolated operations explicitly: 'If I weaken the organizations in one territory but leave their connections to neighboring countries intact, they will simply find another way to continue'. Eight countries therefore receive eight different strategies under one common framework, each with named priority zones: Tijuana, Petare, El Callao, Chapare, San Matías, Rosario, the Buenos Aires conurbano and Chile's northern border.

Colombia is the keystone and receives the largest share (30%) for a stated structural reason: its position makes it the observation and coordination hub of continental narcotraffic, so it is funded to become the strategy's main support center — financial investigation, regional analysis and neighbor cooperation — rather than merely another front line. Mexico (26%) concentrates on fentanyl, fuel theft and extortion, with financial investigations aimed at decoupling communities from criminal payrolls. Brazil (22%) fights the PCC and Comando Vermelho on two synchronized objectives — weaken the organizations and prevent anyone from re-occupying the emptied spaces — because detention alone demonstrably does not dissolve structures that outlive their members.
The remaining five allocations are each a different theory of failure corrected. Venezuela (8%) is treated as an institutional-collapse case: nothing holds unless the state itself works, so institutional discipline is a precondition for any advance. Peru (6%) carries the plan's sharpest self-discipline clause — police corruption must be investigated and corrected before results are demanded, because executors captured by the organizations would weaponize the Fund against the project itself. Bolivia (1%) is deliberately cheap because its cure is economic transformation — agricultural alternatives, services and border cooperation — rather than permanent presence: 'If I eliminate that source of income without offering alternatives, the same conditions will end up favoring its reappearance'. Chile (4%) and Argentina (3%) are subsidiarity cases, where capable institutions receive reinforcement at the margins — border technology in the north, and anti-recruitment work in Rosario and the conurbano — instead of substitute governance.

   8. State-Soul integration: the security-development-formation triad
The plan refuses to let 'security' absorb the whole meaning of recovery. A region counts as recovered only when three elements coexist: security — institutions able to guarantee the law; development — basic services, education and labor opportunities; and formation — an education that builds intellectual capacity, responsibility and comprehension of consequences. The document derives the triad from its own causal model: eliminating an organization does not guarantee that another will not take its place, so the economic, educational and cultural conditions that favor their appearance must change as part of the same operation.
Two design consequences follow. First, social and educational programs are declared essential to territorial continuity, not secondary complements — every recovered region must receive concrete improvements (schools, drinking water, sanitation, employment) so that the population can recognize the new order in its own life rather than identify the state with a force that only appears to impose restrictions. Second, the territorial front is explicitly bound to the Soul front: the state holds the ground the culture will inherit. This is the quiet refutation of the Death Note's original logic — fear can clear a territory, but only conditions can hold one.
   9. The cultural front: manufacturing permanence
The Soul front is the plan's long game: a culture capable of reproducing the order's principles without imposition. It is organized into five objectives. T1 formation strengthens the people who produce knowledge, theories and interpretations of reality; T2 diffusion manages how those ideas travel until they become shared cultural references. The Tipo 1 movement itself is designed as a community of brotherhood, critical thinking, discipline and personal growth — an explicitly masculine formation project meant to raise a generation of Type-1 men as the social nucleus, confronting and cancelling 'repudiable and obscene attitudes' and building 'a sound, pragmatic masculinity that rejects violence and perversion'. Individual formation applies positive expectations — the Pygmalion effect, operationalized in A3 with classifications that can be revised when evidence justifies it — and A4 demands that moral principles be transmitted 'through comprehension and reasoning, not through repetition'. A5 secures the relay: a new generation formed to preserve and develop the legacy.
The document is candid about the front's darker instrument: the cancellation of channels, podcasts, influencers, politicians and people who act to denigrate or hold a mentality of hate. This is mass manipulation elevated to state policy — the same figure who once deleted individuals now edits the public sphere, replacing episodic terror with curated permanence. It is the plan's most openly authoritarian component, and the text does not disguise it; it simply embeds it inside an otherwise persuasive welfare narrative. A complete reading of the plan must hold both halves: the genuine long-horizon social engineering and the censorship that guards it.
What makes the front notable as intelligence rather than as ideology is its reproduction mechanism. T1 produces the ideas, T2 amplifies them, the movement embodies them, the schools standardize them, and the generational relay (A5, plus the heir contemplated in the continuity section) re-seeds them. The order stops needing its prophet because it has built an clergy of teachers. That is the precise sense in which the Soul front compensates the other two: it converts what Sanction enforces and State builds into something people transmit voluntarily — the cheapest and most durable form of enforcement there is.

   10. Protection, succession and the limits doctrine
The protection layer treats the plan itself as the asset to be defended. P1 keeps Light and Kira as separate figures — structural concealment, so that no investigation can connect the order to a man. P2 forbids the system from depending on any single individual, founder included: institutions, not persons, are the durable components. P3 enumerates what cannot be prevented and must be absorbed: succession, differences of interpretation, conflicts of interest, and Ryuk — described with precision as 'an external variable that does not respond to the same human motivations', the one actor in the document who cannot be recruited, funded, educated or cancelled.
Alongside the risk list stands a doctrine of limits, unique in Light's recorded thinking: people interpret principles differently; 'obedience does not guarantee comprehension'; 'loyalty does not guarantee that decisions are correct'; institutions can make mistakes; social classifications can be incomplete. The conclusion is architectural rather than sentimental: 'the stability of the project will depend on its capacity to conserve its principles and to recognize its own limitations'. The plan therefore builds feedback into itself — revisable classifications (A3), public evaluations (E6, Law 4 Art. 7, Law 5 Art. 7), and a self-correction the
author performs on the page in real time.
Succession completes the layer. The heir is contemplated as 'a possible continuity of identity and tradition', with the explicit caveat that his formation does not imply immediate assumption of the founder's functions — continuity is designed to flow through institutions and culture first, through blood last. It is the final inversion of the chapter-27 insight that loyalty is not understanding: the plan no longer needs to trust any individual, because it has been engineered to survive the absence of all of them.

   11. Victory redefined
The document closes its argument by redefining victory. Influence, the author writes, will begin to extend differently — no longer depending only on fear of punishment, but present 'in the institutions that administer their countries, in the communities that recover their opportunities, and in the education that the coming generations will receive'. Latin America is chosen as the demonstration region: not merely territory where Kira holds authority, but 'one of the first regions where he can prove that his new order is capable of transforming the living conditions of millions of people'. The fall of the criminal organizations is declared only the first result — 'the truly important thing will be to prevent others from taking their place'.
And then the final line, which is the plan's whole thesis compressed into one sentence: 'If I achieve that, I will have proven that Kira can do something that the Death Note, by itself, could never have achieved'. The weapon that founded the order is scheduled to become unnecessary to it. In objective terms, Phase 1 is the plan by which the God of the New World abolishes the need for a god — retaining the worship, the institutions and the values, while the killing becomes an emergency power instead of a governing system.

   12. Self-correction inside the document
Unusually for the genre, the document watches itself think. The closing pages are a first-person diagnostic — 'Now I see more clearly what the project is missing' — in which the author concedes that governments accepting Kira's authority is not the same as territory actually controlled, identifies Latin America as the proof case, and corrects the plan's own architecture: from treating nations as separate problems to a continental effort; from equal distribution to marginal allocation; from security spending to the security-development-formation triad. The GOD PLAN — Phase 1 is thus not only a plan but a record of its own revision — the strategic loop of diagnosis, design, error and redesign, closed inside a single artifact.

5. Closing Synthesis

Read as a whole, the GOD PLAN — Phase 1 is the point where Light Yagami's intelligence changes genre: from the detective-killer who wins finite games to the constitution-writer who refuses to play finite games at all. Its core idea is the mutual compensation of three fronts across three time horizons — enforcement now, institutions for years, culture for generations — and every component, from the twelve-percent water levy to the Tipo 1 movement, exists to close one of the three failure modes the author himself formulates. The plan names its own exit condition and schedules its own founder for obsolescence.
The category map shows the same shape from underneath. The plan's mass sits in Cyan — Strategy (28.8% of the demonstrated set) and Light Green — Thinking (22.7%), with Dark Blue and Purple supplying the reading and steering instruments; Cognition and Orange — Foresight are saturated completely; and everything that requires a duel, a clock or a face is deliberately absent. It is the signature of an architect-systems-builder: the least personal exercise of intelligence in the entire file, and the one with the largest design surface.
Its honest boundary has been stated throughout: a doctrine demonstrates design, not performance. The document can prove that Light can imagine a self-sustaining order to the last budget line; only its execution can prove that the order survives contact with the institutions it must capture and the one variable it admits it cannot control. Within that boundary, Phase 1 is the most complete artifact in the file — the plan in which Kira attempts the one thing the Death Note, by itself, could never have achieved.
