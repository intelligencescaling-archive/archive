To begin with, I want to point out the unpredictability and the precision of the action that L undertook after several events.

Over the course of several days, Light manages to kill twelve FBI agents, thereby depriving L of part of his operational team. This limits his control over the situation, since after the killings L had only a few members left in the investigative task force.

In such a situation, one might assume that L would shift into a defensive stance, attempting to keep his identity hidden. However, what L does is practically impossible to foresee. L decides to personally reveal himself to Light in order to observe and analyze his reaction.

At first glance, this appears to be an extremely foolish move, since from the very beginning L tried to conceal his identity from Kira in order not to become his next victim. Here, L seems to discard one of the most important factors—anonymity—by revealing himself to Kira.
But the situation is not that simple. First, we must understand what is required to commit a killing (specifically in Kira’s case):

1. A name
2. A face

A killing is impossible if one of these factors is unavailable. In L’s case, not only does he deprive Kira of the ability to kill him, but he also sets a trap that Kira may fall into if he does not anticipate it in advance.

How did L deprive Kira of the ability to kill him? And what is this trap?

Let us analyze it step by step.
Earlier, we established that two factors are required for a killing. L’s revelation is not ordinary, because even while revealing himself, he conceals his real name. He not only hides it, but instead gives the name of a famous actor—Hideki Ryuga.

Since Light can see L’s face, the only remaining factor is the name. Therefore, Light must obtain L’s real name. In this situation, Light appears to possess both a name and a face, but there is a complication: if he attempts to kill L using the name “Hideki Ryuga,” he would most likely end up killing the famous actor rather than the detective. This would confirm L’s suspicions toward Light, worsen Light’s position, and allow L to narrow Kira down from billions of suspects.

The next layer of protection lies in the possibility that the individual who presented himself to Light might not be the real L, but a hired agent. Light cannot be certain that the person who revealed himself is the true detective. If we assume that L hired agents and revealed himself to each suspect, this would be logical, as it would allow him to control the situation from all sides without exposing his true identity.

If we assume that Light were to obtain the Shinigami Eyes in order to learn L’s real name and kill him, L’s system would automatically direct suspicion toward Light. Such a system completely neutralizes Kira’s advantage—an absolute weapon whose nature has not yet even been fully understood.

Now I will recount and clarify the elements that demonstrate the unprecedented nature of this strategy, its effectiveness, and the system’s perfect adaptability to changes, to Kira’s actions, and to the emergence of new phenomena and variables.

After Light Yagami officially joins the investigative task force, the world encounters a new anomaly—the appearance of a second Kira. This unknown individual acts with fanaticism, broadcasting videotapes containing messages understandable only to the true Kira. In one of the messages, this person claims to have already found Kira—even though, in reality, they have not yet met.

Soon afterward, a girl known as Misa Amane suddenly appears at Light’s house. To cover his tracks and conceal the fact of their personal contact, Light creates a new videotape on behalf of the “second Kira.” In it, Misa supposedly announces that she is ceasing her activities and transferring the investigation into the hands of the police, remaining only an external ally of Kira.

The strategy also enables L to observe Kira almost 24/7, which serves as an auxiliary advantage, since he can monitor Light’s behavior while remaining physically close to him and investigating the case alongside him.

This is excessively effective, as it allows L to immediately eliminate Kira’s advantages while simultaneously appropriating certain advantages for himself.

L is forced to construct highly effective and sophisticated schemes while operating from such a disadvantageous position. Strategy resilience & shielding are demonstrated through the protective layers that neutralize Kira’s advantages.

Earlier, I showed how, through his strategy, L constructed a trap and executed a counterattack in response to Kira’s actions, anticipating a plan that had not yet even been implemented.

He deprives Kira of the ability to use the absolute weapon that would allow him to kill L at any time. He strips Kira of absolute anonymity and freedom, restricting his options (as evidenced by the constant surveillance and the joint investigation with Kira). L even accounts for his own death, creating an additional layer of protection: even if Light were to kill L, it would inevitably lead to Kira’s capture. L foresees such an outcome in advance and prepares a countermeasure. He publicly declares that if he dies in the near future, Light is responsible. By stating this directly to Light and to the task force, L once again restricts Light’s freedom and induces internal panic (arising from the impossibility of eliminating L, who strongly suspects him), thereby completely neutralizing the possibility of using any concealed means to carry out the killing.

Strategy logistics are manifested in the fact that L commands an entire operational unit, controlling their actions and the potential outcomes that require their involvement.

As an example, one can note the moment when Misa was apprehended. Such an action required a precise operational system through which the arrest was to be executed. The essence lies in the fact that the strategy encompasses every single day spent with Kira from the moment of identity revelation, which in itself testifies to a high level of logistical coordination.
