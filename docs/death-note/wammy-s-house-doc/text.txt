Wammy’s House

 Introduction

Wammy's House is one of the numerous orphanages created by Quillsh Wammy (also known as Watari). It was established in Winchester, England, as a training institution for exceptionally talented children. L Lawliet was the most accomplished of these children. Following L's success as a detective, Wammy's House's goal became to nurture talented children to become a successor to L. This article will contain information regarding Wammy's House, from its finances to its education. Its main goal is to provide information on the orphanage and end with you forming your own opinion as to whether or not it’s better than institutions such as the White Room.

FINANCE

To understand Wammy's House's funding, we must first examine its founder and primary source of income: Watari (Quillish Wammy.) Watari was not only L's handler and the Japanese Task Force's logistical provider during the Kira Case, but he was also a well-known inventor. Watari used his riches as an inventor to construct an orphanage that educates and sends exceptional youngsters out into the world. While it is unknown how much money Watari possessed prior to meeting L, it was sufficient enough to establish an orphanage for numerous gifted children to grow and learn, as well as supply their needs (he easily granted L's request of filling his room with servers and a few computers). Keep in mind that Watari bought this for L when the internet wasn't as popular as it is now, and being able to afford all of that would have been prohibitively expensive. The most crucial aspect occurs when an eight-year-old L requests that Watari purchase a million pounds with Japanese yen, followed by investments in various firm stocks of L's choice. Watari accomplishes this, and it is stated plainly, "I kept investing as L said, and two years later my assets had almost reached 20,000 times the original amount."

So, to find out how much Watari profited on L's investment, consider compound interest. Using a historical investing calculator to determine the Rate on Return (ROR) between 1987 (when L is 8) and 2004, it indicates that the Dow had an average ROR of 7.6% over that time period. As a result, the initial 20 billion investment will be worth around £70 billion (adjusted for inflation) in 2004. That would have already made Watari the richest person on the globe at that point in time, and things only get better for him from here on out if you thought 70 billion was a large sum of money that might be used to fund an institution like Wammy's House, consider how much L made throughout his career as a detective.

 "L, on principle, never got involved in a case unless there were more than ten victims or a million dollars at stake," said Mello, who narrated the majority of Another Note, a canon novel in the Death Note series, "and this is the real reason why he belatedly, but aggressively, involved himself in this little case, which only ever had three or four victims." The novel also claims that L has solved over 3,500 cases. Using basic multiplication (3,500 x 1,000,000), the total comes to $3.5 billion.
So we already know that thanks to L's investments, Watari had £70 billion to freely invest into Wammy's House, and that's not even taking into account how he was most likely very wealthy prior to the investments, given that he was willing to gamble a million pounds like it was nothing on an eight-year-old's stock market intuition. Remember that Mello claims that "in the public record alone, he solved over 3,500 difficult crimes." The number of cases L has solved may be far bigger than what the public is aware of, resulting in much higher earnings than we are aware of.

EDUCATION

Wammy's House "invites university professors, researchers, and top specialists in their fields from around the world to give individual instruction to the children based on their abilities and potential." This includes psychology, business/finance, microeconomics (L never guessed at random when it came to stocks, investments/finance require knowledge of microeconomics), computer science (which includes programming LANGUAGES), criminal investigation, politics, and multiple languages (L is fluent in Japanese, Chinese, English, German, French, and Russian.)

All of this is being taught to the already exceptional minds of the youngsters at Wammy's House by the top university professors in their respective fields. With Wammy's House training, L also received the same score as Light Yagami on the 2004 To-Oh University Entrance Exams. It was even more amazing that he still received a high score despite having to sit normally while taking the exam (it has been said that if he does not sit in the unique way he does, his deductive skills will decline by at least 40%). With this training, Wammy's House students can acquire a tremendous quantity of information at once. This skill is most noticeable in L, Mello, Near and even Matt. L is portrayed in the books to be able to monitor and analyze data from 50+ monitors at the same time. This achievement is still canon because it is claimed in the databooks that children from Wammy's House have the ability to absorb vast volumes of information at once, allowing them to view many monitors at once. Near demonstrates this skill twice when he sees various news reports followed by Mikami speaking on Kira's Kingdom. Mello has also shown this ability, first by watching a laptop with four displays track Sidoh and the Task Force's whereabouts, and then by performing the same thing with a much bigger configuration of 12 monitors for the same reasons described above.

Wammy’s house also holds its own library which Watari funds for. The literature is very diverse and includes books such as “The Catcher in the Rye”, ACD’s “Sherlock Holmes” and Doctorate level books. These are referenced in LABB and CTW. What makes this so brilliant is that this means the children of Wammy’s house are free to read from a library that is funded by the richest man on Earth.

Extracurricular Activities

In terms of extracurricular activities, Wammy’s house is no slacker as it sends out students to solve missions and cases. When Aizawa informs Light of the existence of Wammy’s House he states, “Apart from the normal schooling, each of them were given extremely advanced work to do.” An example of this would be a former student known as “K” who in the novel “L: Change the World” was given a mission to stop a suicide bomber. This is once again repeated in DN HTR 13 when it goes into a brief description of Wammy’s house and the specialized education given to them.

INFLUENCE

Wammy's House has a lot of influence because it was funded by Watari, who was previously stated in this document to be the richest man on the globe; that alone would give him a lot of status. Watari's impact was so enormous that his death hit the top page of a newspaper, and Matsuda described him as "world-renowned" after hearing information concerning the newspaper by Aizawa. As previously said, even before Wammy's home, Watari was a world-famous inventor, which implies that when Wammy's House or what is known to the public as "multiple orphanages" was established, Watari's influence and reputation would follow him.  Not all of the children who grew up in Wammy's House were destined to succeed L. The children that graduated from Wammy's House went on to achieve amazing things and acquire even more recognition for their individual efforts. Linda, a minor character in DN, is an excellent example since she applied the skills she learned at Wammy's House to become a successful artist. So, with their founder being the richest man on the planet, a world-renowned inventor, and a well-known philanthropist for "orphanages around the world" and students who go on to do great things even if they don't want to succeed L, it's no surprise that Wammy's House's influence is already enormous.

GENERATIONS/”HAPPY INDEX”

Wammy's House has several generations, all with the intention of producing a successor to L. The first generation of Wammy's House was known as BB's (Beyond Birthday) generation. While we might presume the curriculum was similar to that of subsequent generations, I believe it was much more difficult than that of subsequent generations. So difficult that everyone in Wammy's first generation was considered a prototype. It was so extreme that another child in B's (short for B.B/Beyond Birthday) generation, only known as "A," committed suicide. B, on the other hand, did not yield to the pressure and was able to complete the curriculum and graduate before becoming a serial killer that faced off against L in Death Note: Another Note.

When compared to B.B's generation, the fourth generation of Wammy's House, or Near/Mello's generation, was found to be significantly more calm. Children are depicted in the anime and manga as laughing, playing, and having a good time while being a part of the fourth generation. Aside from Near and Mello, there were successful pupils from this age who went on to achieve great things, with Linda being one of the most recognized from the fourth generation in the eyes of the public. There was also Matt, a friend of Mello's who assisted him in the Kira case, but little is known about him other than he specializes in technology, which is nothing to dismiss given that all children from Wammy's House, regardless of generation, have the unique ability to absorb large amounts of information and apply it (the most notable example of this ability being used is through the Monitor feats for Near, L, Mello, and even Matt).
