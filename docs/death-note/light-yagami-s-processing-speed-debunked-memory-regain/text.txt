**Part 1: The Meaning of "Return"**

Firstly what does the Death Note mean when it says that "When regaining ownership of the Death Note, the memories associated with the Death Note will also return"?

The Japanese text of the manga says **記憶が戻る** which means **memory returns**. In the Jisho Japanese dictionary, **戻る** means to return, revert, resume, restore.

If you search **記憶が戻る** on google, it often refers to amnesiac patients recovering their memory. For example one of the top search results (https://sanyokai-clinic.com/kokoro/6003/) is a Japanese clinical website that says "解離性健忘の**記憶が戻る**ことで、記憶や時間感覚の連続性が取り戻されます。 その結果、自分が自分であることに整合性をもち、再び自分の足で人生を歩めるようになるのです。" which translates to "When **memories return** that were lost due to dissociative amnesia, the continuity of memory and the sense of time are restored. Resultantly, a sense of coherence in one’s identity is regained and one becomes able to walk through life on their own again." Notice what the second sentence says? A sense of coherence in one's identity is regained and one becomes able to walk through life again. Thus memory can return in the manner that the vocabulary implies without causing them confusion, disorientation, or brain damage.

If we go by the English wording of the text instead, what does it mean for the Death Note to return your memories? Does it mean that it causes an "info dump" where the information is forced into your brain and processing the information relies entirely on your own processing speed ability? Or does it completely restore those sections of memory to its original state, such that you do not need to have high processing speed to have the information naturally stored in your mind? The latter is more fitting for the vocabulary. Assuming that the return only info dumps his mind and Light will need to process all the memories with his own cognitive ability is the speculative claim that diverges from the immediate interpretation. It is the claim that requires proof here.

**Part 3: "Misa Did it Too" Should not be Disregarded**

Even though it doesn't contradict the claim that Light can process so much information in such a short timeframe, Misa restoring her memories almost instantly does provide some evidence that it isn't a faculty of the mind that handles the processing of memories, but rather the Death Note's own power restores it to its processed state.

It is part of the rules of the Death Note itself, which highly implies that it is expected and normal for the people of the Death Note world to be able to have their memories returned without being "confused, disoriented, brain-damaged." The Death Note even contains a rule about not being able to kill people over the age of 124, it would be weird for there not to be a clarification that the Death Note only info dumps the character and they have to spend time processing it themselves to not be brain damaged.

From the parallel case and also there never being a counterexample of a character actually getting brain damaged, you can infer that it is NOT tied to individual intellect to be able to restore memories the way Light did. Misa also being able to do it is just another example.

**Part 4: Light being able to process 100 days worth of information in a few seconds contradicts his actual ability to process information.**

I will give an example, with a really good tactic from L. In chapter 21 of Death Note, L shows Light 3 images, which can be rearranged to form the phrase "L 知っているか... 死神は... 林檎しか食べない" which means "L do you know, gods of death only eat apples." A complete sentence. However, if you arrange it in order of the numbers that L gave, it forms "L知っているか... 林檎しか食べない... 死神は..." which is an incomplete sentence because it ends with は which is a particle. In English it would be something like "L do you know, gods of death that only eat apples..." This automatically implies there is another picture to finish the sentence. Anyone who understands basic Japanese will be able to tell there's a 4th picture needed to complete the sentence. However, Light was unable to process the information quickly enough to realize this because of all the other information L was giving him to occupy his cognitive processing, and thus made such a basic mistake.

If Light can process 100+ days of memories about the death note completely in only a few seconds, then why is it that he can't even process a basic Japanese grammatical trap within a few seconds? He should be able to process all the information L gave as well as all the traps and their intricacies in just a few milliseconds if his processing speed is that high.

The trap by L was spectacular. Even a random Japanese off the streets would be able to tell there's a 4th picture because they wouldn't need to process a lot of information due to not overthinking, it's just obvious the sentence ends with a particle and therefore needs to be completed, unlike Light who had the assumption there were 3 and thus would need to process more information to eventually come to the realization of where the trap lay. To complete the sentence, adding in L's fake 4th photo, it becomes "L知っているか林檎しか食べない死神は手が赤い" which means "L do you know, gods of death that only eat apples have red hands." The incomplete sentence becomes fully complete.

This scene shows L's creativity, being able to come up with such a simple but effective tactic using the information that Light himself gave him, thus increasing the probability in his mind that Light Yagami is Kira. But at the same time, someone who can quickly process the verbal information and notice the grammatical discrepancy would NOT fall for it.

Therefore Light couldn't process the information fast enough to realize the obvious implications of L's test. Given how much time he took to realize L's intention with the pictures, it would be inconsistent to assume he can process 100 days of information in a few seconds. The direct inference if we go with the most fitting reasoning for the data so far is that the Death Note gives complete restoration as I mentioned in point 2.

**Part 5: Processing Priorities**

Even if we assume the Death Note only dumps the information into the mind of the character and they have to process everything with their own brain (unproven), that still does not justify Light having such high PSI. Light Yagami does not need to process everything to become Kira again.

First of all, he already suspected and questioned if he was Kira. Thus it would not require as much cognitive dissonance resolution as it would have if he completely flipped personalities.

Also, not every memory was related to the Death Note, so it isn't exactly 100 days.

Most importantly, there is the assumption that he needs to process all the information while it is being restored, and that he can't just prioritize processing the important information whilst leaving the less important fluff to be processed in the background or during other times.

You gave the analogy that Light has 100 cards dropped in front of him. The backside of one contains an even number, and every other card is odd. Your interpretation is that he has to check all or most of those other cards, since he has no way of knowing which is odd or even until he checks them. However, this analogy only does the opposite of what you made it for. Light does not need to go through every single memory individually. Sometimes humans can notice anomalies that bring important stuff to the forefront. For example if you have 200 cards on the ground and 199 of them are blue and 1 is red, you don't need to go through all of them to notice the red card. You will instantly recognize the red card and it will be at the forefront of your mind. Sometimes it is harder to filter out the noise and find the discrepancy, for example if 199 of the cards have 1 digit numbers and 1 of them has a 2 digit number, that is harder to find because colour has a stronger contrast than digit count, but still easier than completely processing the number on every card. Even with odd and even numbers, you only need to look at the final digit and automatically mentally ignore the 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. In that case this feat would show Light has good judgement, decisiveness, noise filtering, etc., more than something like processing speed which would be of the assumption that he goes through every memory in detail rather than just noticing the best choices and zoning in on it, just like the card example.

**Conclusion:**

In conclusion, it is an emotional management feat for Light to quickly recover from the shock of having his memories restored. It almost definitely is NOT a processing speed feat because the most natural and reasonable interpretation that fits both the vocabulary and the narrative is that the Death Note restores everything completely with its power over the mind. This is the most logical interpretation and the Death Note's ability being demonstrated in other areas of the manga makes it more fitting even narratively. If we go with a more open interpretation, then it could potentially be a dissonance resolution feat, noise filtering feat, and judgement feat as well, which is a bit of a highball but you could still argue for it coherently. However, the worst interpretation of all and the most unreasonable explanation you can give for the feat is that Light Yagami has the ability to process 100 days worth of memories in detail within a few seconds, which is a stretch of the vocabulary, a poor logical inference from the narrative ignoring the likelier alternatives, and it would also be inconsistent with Light's actual processing ability shown during the manga when he failed at some points to make the best decision due to time constraints, indicating he doesn't have such a highballed proofless processing speed.
