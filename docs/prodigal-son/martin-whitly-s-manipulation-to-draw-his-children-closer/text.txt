Martin Whitly's manipulation to draw his children closer to him explanation

Context: Martin Whitly was a world renowned surgeon who saved thousands of lives, living in New York with his wife Jessica, and children Malcolm and Ainsley. However, as it turned out, he was also a serial killer with 23 known victims, arrested in 1992 by officer Gil Arroyo in front of his family. Over the years, Malcolm would talk semi-regularly with him as his interest in criminal psychology grew, as did his relationship with Gil, the man who saved him from his insane father. Gil too had at one point dated Jessica Whitly, and in effect tried to insert himself as Malcolm's new father figure to replace the one he separated from him. Eventually, when Malcolm was accepted by the FBI, roughly ten years prior to the series, he cut off all contact with Whitly, and stopped seeing him entirely. Of course, Martin did alright for himself, and was good enough a surgeon that he was even allowed to consult for other doctors or give his professional advice, enough so to be allowed his own collection of books to keep himself occupied, and even private interaction with his patients.

–One of Martin’s patients is a wealthy man, a dominatrix too, whose wife cheated on him. Martin can see in him that this man is no normal dom, but rather recognizes a true bloodlust inside of him, and manipulates the man based on his sadistic nature and need to exert control to convince him that he too should become a killer, even directing him exactly how to do so in Martin's own methods, and giving him one of his own illustrations. This means that the NYPD would classify this as a copycat of The Surgeon. Martin knows well about Gil and Malcolm's relationship, he can predict perfectly that as Gil wants to be a father to Bright and replace Martin, he would all but jump at the chance to enlist Malcolm's help against a killer copying Martin, which he does. Malcolm himself is forced to return to Martin, asking him for his insights when Bright sees the illustration he recognizes as his father's work found in one of the killer's crime scenes. Once there, Martin would be able to play on Malcolm's own hidden longing for his lost familial relationship, and manages to negotiate a relationship where in exchange for aid and advice, Martin and Malcolm would maintain semi-regular interaction, as Martin would refuse to talk to any other cops. –Martin's other child, Ainsley, grew up to be a reporter who, upon hearing of Malcolm's newly cultivated relationship with their father, decided to get her own benefits from him by interviewing him live, father and daughter. Martin found another inmate who was brought in at 17 named Tevin, and can easily discern this man, having been brought in so young, is vulnerable, but moreso craves notoriety and would be easy to use. He exploits his immaturity and becomes a mentor figure to him, gaining his trust and obedience, imploring him that he needs to be able to tell his story on TV, and that when Ainsley, shows up, Tevin should stab his therapist and come for an interview. He does so, escaping his room in the asylum and come after Ainsley. Martin then convinces Ainsley to keep filming even while Tevin is on the loose, causing Ainsley's cameraman to be stabbed, creating a situation in which they would have no choice but to let Martin operate on him on live TV, painting himself as the hero gaining back trust and altering Ainsley's perspective of him, as she barely knew him beforehand and thus would be easier to mold.
