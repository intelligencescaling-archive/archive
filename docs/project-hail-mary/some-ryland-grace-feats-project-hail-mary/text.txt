Some Ryland Grace feats

Grace is actually pretty smart in the novel ngl, maybe I'll make a doc on him.

Context: Grace suffered some pretty notable brain damage (pretty much none of his memories remained intact; he didn't know he was in space, he didn't remember his own name, he didn't recall his own nationality, employment, etc.)

Grace figures based on his intuitive usage of the imperial system (when describing the height of a ladder) that he's either American, English or Canadian. To try and further narrow it down he asks himself the distance between L.A and New York, when his gut answer is 3000 miles rather than kilometers he figures that he's likely not Canadian and is instead either English or American (or Liberian, since Grace apparently is aware that Liberia also uses the imperial system)

Later in this same page, Grace fails to climb the 10 foot ladder (due to being weak from just waking up from a coma + the stronger gravity (which he obviously doesn't know about yet)) and falls (being caught by the robot arms), during this, he feels as though the way he fell felt "wrong", however, he falls asleep immediately afterwards. Later on, he experiences a similar incident and this time makes use of the lab equipment he finds (a stopwatch, a measuring tape, and a plastic test tube). He used the measuring tape to check the height of a table in the lab (found the distance from top to bottom was 91 cm) then pushed the plastic test tube off the table and timing how long it took for the test tube to fall all the way to the ground, he repeats this test 20 times in total to account for his error margin and ends up getting an average of 0.348 seconds. With all this he was able to do the math in his head despite the brain damage (0.91 = 1/2 a \* 0.348\^2 or a = 2 \* 0.91 / 0.348\^2) (to which he noted that "these formulas come easily to me. Second nature. I'm definitely skilled at physics") and with all this he was able to figure out that the gravity is 15 meters per second per second, Earths gravity being 9.8 meters per second per second and gravity being a static, unchangeable force led Grace to the conclusion that he must not be on Earth. He briefly considers that he could be inside a centrifuge instead but comes to the conclusion (with more math stuff) that he's unlikely to be on a centrifuge because of another experiment he performed:

He grabbed a pendulum and used it on the higher level, timing it with the stop watch and counting each individual swing, once it stops moving Grace found that it had a total of 346 cycles in exactly ten minutes. Afterwards, he went to the lower level and repeated the same experiment, and after 10 minutes passed, the result was replicated. This was done to test if there was any difference in centripetal force ("in a centrifuge, the farther you get from the centre, the higher the centripetal force will be") given the pendulums behaved the same on both levels, it seemed like there was no difference in gravity. He wondered if he was in a really big centrifuge and therefore would make any differences in gravity undetectable, after some more math stuff, he figures out that this potential massive centrifuge would have to have a 700 meter radius and be spinning at 88 m/s. He realizes the unlikeliness of this given 1: It would be the largest centrifuge ever built and why would anyone do that? and 2: At such a scale (both in size and speed) there'd certainly be some turbulence and noise. So he wonders if maybe he's in a centrifuge located in space, he knows that (due to a lack of gravity helping out) the centrifuge would have to be bigger and faster with more math he discovers that the centrifuge would have to have a radius of 1280 meters, he also finds this unlikely since it would dwarf anything else humanity has ever thrown into space.

With all this he's able to conclude fairly solidly that he must be in space after all (he also says that he knows he's not on another planet, moon, or asteroid since none of them have gravity comparable to 15 meters per second per second)
