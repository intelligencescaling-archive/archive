Rankings

🌟 Deception 🌟
Concealment >= Fabrication > Misdirection >= Sophistry > Acting Skills
Throughout the second half of the series, information control and concealment can be found in the majority of Honami’s purposeful actions, to the point where everyone has a different idea of not only Hona’s strengths (with Kakeru, Kiyotaka and Arisu/Miyabi all having vastly different positions due to operating on widely different sets of information), but anything going on around Honami, while being perfect at concealing her footprints. Amplified tenfold the grave communication issues and misunderstandings between Kiyotaka & Kei and Kiyotaka & Miyabi.
Honami’s concealment is also her greatest weapon in strategy-orientated special exams; SG and S&D to a lesser extent serve as showcases of this.
This, combined with her lies capable of deceiving the most perceptive characters in the series and her misdirections, Hona’s fabricated narratives are strong. Not only do you need abductive reasoning, the information gathering required is the real challenge, as she can take advantage of other people’s distrust and conflicts.
Hona can perform good bluffs and believable lies, best shown in Y2V9 and Y3V1.
Finally, Honami has good acting performances, such as Y2V8, Y2V9 (phone call) and Y2V12.5. Beside mannerism and other physical expressions, Honami can also use objects, devices, technology and the surrounding environment to transmit false emotional information to her audience.

⭐ Planning ⭐
Intricacies > Applicability > Coverage > Invincibility > Preparations Process >  Accuracy > Acclimation > Contingency > Versatility
By the start of the second year’s second half, Hona grew into quite a formidable planner. To bind Kiyo to herself, she constructed a seven-layered plan, overcame severe informational + resource limitations + setbacks, accounted a minimum of ten greater-scope complications, eight more within the Y2V10 Meeting and eight more within one of the contingencies she had to draw up well ahead of time for the promised day (Y2V9.5 tactics also showed an attention to details & variables, albeit they were opportunistic rather than planned long beforehand). Hona’s trap on the promise day was for all intent and purpose a contingency. Factoring this with the other contingency she set up in case she had won the EoY exam as well as precautions for and made by her moves around the time of the second year’s winter break, she excelled at this aspect of planning.
Hona’s preventative measures blinded Arisu (and other risky third parties such as Ichika) from seeing what Honami was up to and thus, Arisu’s attempt at ruining Kiyo’s neutralization plan was “countered” and nobody else with a connection to his mysterious past could’ve interfered intentionally. In addition to this, Honami demoralized Miyabi completely, killing his fighting spirit without him realizing, and stopped him from truly messing with her or Kiyotaka. The Nori misdirection from the Y2V10 Meeting was a blinder that concealed her intent to coerce Kiyo.
Accuracy is #5 because of her bullseyes on her plan’s abstraction; Ryuji reaching out to Kiyo in the cultural festival, her disappearance at the end of Hokkaido Trip causing a panic and Kiyo finding out about it and looking for her, all the domino effects from her student council resignation (as in, her classmates reaching out to Kiyotaka to make him gather intel on her state, the resulting KiyoKei fracture, Miyabi’s rumor spreading along with Kei receiving a photo from one of the gym trip with Kiyotaka, etc) down to all of their EXACT timing. Then there’s the Nori plan in Y2V10 and its required preparation/foresight with Norihito between the Hokkaido trip & the following events prior to the winter break.
Honami’s plan was also practical - in Y2V9 she moved the needles on numerous programs and completed several tasks simultaneously; Rumor Mills 1.0, breaking the ice with Kiyotaka and fracturing his relationship with Kei, the neutralization of Miyabi, engineering his friend nest within her class, setting up the Nori plan’s misdirection through her manipulations of Norihito for the coercion long-term contingencies to ensure that the promised meeting went as planned and gather info on Kiyo throughout his actions and her time with him; Honami managed to do all of this without risking the invincibility of her plan.
Despite being ranked last, her Planning Versatility was strong. The way she used Mako, Ryuji and especially Norihito were multifaceted & laced with details. Hona also made the plan in a manner in which she could use any opportunity with the special exams and gaps in time to strengthen its success rate. So while her plan had an exact structure, Honami also used new developments to fortify it further.

⭐ Manipulation ⭐

Hona makes people do stuff. They do stuff. Y2V8-Y2V10 Norihito for Emotional, Miyabi & his year & KiyoKei & her classmates & school throughout Y2V8-Y2V9.5 for Indirect + Mass. Y2V9 Kei, Y3V1 Kei and Y3V1 Ichika for Direct. Y3V3 Kakeru for Logical. Blackmail contingencies for Coercion.

✨ Emotional Intelligence ✨
EF > EE >= EU > ER > EP > EM
EF is top because Miyabi, Ichika, Kiyotaka, Kei and Norihito ahhh shit.
She can override her moral values she had been hiding behind for years to make schemes out of pure EE and utilize her feelings to enact deceptive tactics, which also has some implications for ER.
Speaking of which, Hona has way too much emotional self-recognition for a side character, having moments in Y1V10, Y1V11.5, Y2V4. Y2V7-8, Y2V9, Y2V10, Y2V12.5 and Y3V1. Honami can use her self awareness & feelings to foresee the strategies and tactics of other people they use against her or to use her (Y2V9, Y2V10).
A big caveat for ER is Y2V9, specifically its two short stories; while “Jealousy” was if anything a feat given the situation (seeing Kiyotaka and Mako chat amicably) is novel and she seemingly saw through it quickly, Honami was clearly in some sort of denial (regardless of the contradiction regarding her reason to meet with Kiyo as described in the characterization section.
For EU, Hona’s main feats are 12.5 Kiyotaka analysis and Y3V1 Ichika profiling; has other feats like seeing Arisu’s love for Kiyotaka, Kakeru’s fear of Kiyo and so on. Between her feats on Ryuji in Y2V12 and Y3V3 as well as her feats on Ichika, Kiyo and Miyabi, we can determine that she can comprehend anyone else’s emotional states in the present with near perfect accuracy as long as she possesses enough auxiliary information to assess their current situations or current knowledge of what those people know, even if she’s nowhere near them at the time.
What may be enough will vary; for most characters it isn’t too much, as shown in the Y2V12 exam, but the deceptive people with fully concealed and extraordinary backgrounds like Kiyo obviously required more time and information gathering.
Honami is strong in EP too. During the battle against Suzune of the second year’s end-of-year exam, she discerned faint emotional cues of five people over twenty students (most of whom Honami hardly ever interacted with) in a single round of discussion. Honami can also spot other people having eye conversations.
She can see emotions in eyes and sense intent in microexpressions, as shown in the Y3V1 scenes with Kei and Ichika respectively. She also has instant mind reads based on general demeanors like Y3V2 on Kiyotaka.
See EM section for EM feats. TL;DR: the (increasingly lesser) composure hiccups are offset by Honami’s impulse control (Y2V9, Y2V10 & Y3V1), ability to somehow hide the emotions in her eyes (Y2V9) and promise scene (Y2V12.5).

Prediction > Preclusion > Anticipation
It’s a near certainty Honami predicted the Y2V1 exam strategies besides the ones unrelated to the White Room (i.e. the bounty, Ichika’s knife and Takuya), Miyabi’s election, all the stuff that Kiyotaka was going to tell her along with his emotional reactions to her choices during their promised meeting in Y2V12.5.
Honami took the necessary steps to avoid disaster in the partner exam. She saw many steps ahead to control the effects of her manipulations on her classmates, Miyabi & Kei before the Y2 Winter Break to hide from Kiyotaka the rumors about him circulating around ANHS + avoid a proper reconciliation between him & Kei.
Anticipation feats: Nailing the timing of the gym photo, Class A traitor during the S&D exam, and social behavior and development across the school with ANHS’s schedule (admittedly broadly) months ahead of time - especially the flow of the rumors and Kiyotaka’s movement before the winter break.

Characterization

Before Y2V9, nobody in or out of universe thought Honami was anything but a girl selfless to a fault with an unchanging generosity. After Y2V9 was released, new information re-contextualized Honami’s story and people in real life tend to be split between those who believe she’s still a good person and those who think she is not anymore. Y2V9’s epilogue is a reveal of her true color (or lack thereof, since she is constantly transforming). At the end of the day, Honami always acts and is driven by her desires. They usually revolve the love Honami has for those she holds dear. Always has been, always will be. While most characters, including the more perspective and distrusting ones such as Suzune (Y1V11.5) and Kiyotaka (Y1V10, maybe a case of misleading narration), thought otherwise, this was never a mystery to her.

"I've never really thought of myself as a good person, never thought of myself as a bad person. I suppose I have managed to become an honest person, just like my mother would have wanted." (Y1V9, Honami’s Soliloquy)

Honami always acted on her desires. So she was never particularly good or bad. Being someone they want her to be is a desire. If those she cares for want her to be good, she’ll be good. If they want her to be bad, she’ll be bad.
Pretty straightforward.
Children raised by single mothers, especially from a low-income household like Hona’s, can take on more responsibilities at an earlier age. This means children like Honami can develop desires to help their mothers. She studied really hard for her scholarship and she is often described as a hard worker.
Honami, who acted on her desire to lessen the burden on her mother and on her desire to fulfill her sister's wish, took drastic measures to satisfy this desire. This being the shoplifting incident, when she stole an expansive hairpin.
This backfired. Hona’s pillar of support was only her mother, so being scolded as harshly as she was for committing what her mother saw as a moral transgression by her was a massive blow. Because of this deep trauma, Honami, someone who remodels herself to suit those she cherishes the most, reshaped herself to create a facade, where she recognized moral transgressions as something to repel at all cost. Honami entered ANHS with this complete makeover.

One could say Honami’s real personality was on display throughout the first six books (Y1V1 to Y1V5), though it’s ultimately impossible to know with any level of certainty. Hona’s class was neither especially successful nor was it falling apart; they were essentially going through the motions, riding the waves.
Y1 sport festival could be seen as something of a win (Arisu and Honami lost 50 class points whereas Kakeru and Suzune lost 100), but it goes without saying it didn’t impress anyone, since it was a product of the bottom two classes tanking each other. Her character was never challenged since nothing special happened to her and those around her, less you count Manabu rejecting her candidacy for the student council as such and Kakeru’s wounded gazelle gambit with Satoru.
Or so you’d think, but something special began to happen inside Honami. There was a certain boy she began to notice at first sight when she entered the school (Y1V1 SS). A mysterious oddball who rejected her offer to play pretend boyfriend, her purpose of said being that she would not have to hurt the feelings of Chihiro Shiranami, a girl in her class who intended to confess to her class leader (Y1V2). He advised Hona to be honest towards those who express their honest feelings, those who left themselves vulnerable to a heavy crushing blow.
Not long after, after Ken’s trial had been dealt with, Hona witnessed this strange boy running at a blazing speed that she couldn’t keep up with (Y1V6 SS) to rescue Airi from the unnamed store clerk stalker. It may have been then Honami would begin to feel certain feelings she had never felt (though as previously mentioned there signals from the first volume, just in Y1V2 it became more obvious).
After confronting Airi's stalker (Y1V2), Hona uses her warning about Kakeru as an excuse to tell him: “So if anything ever happens, you can come to me for help. Talk to me whenever you need it.”
Earlier in the conversation, i.e literally one page before, Honami teased the same boy for telling Airi the exact same thing. It is not the most subtle of subtexts. He had shown the kind of behavior and moral standing we, the readers, later learn Hona is/was obsessed with because of her own mother.
The boy is Kiyotaka Ayanokoji, a student from Suzune’s class and her series’ main character. Most people failed to notice anything special about him at first glance other than good looks due to deliberately keeping a low profile.
Unlike everyone else minus Suzune, Hona had two opportunities to see Kiyotaka might be special before the island exam.
Fast forward to the island exam, Chie, the teacher in Honami’s class, interrupted Sae’s class while she was explaining the rules of the special exam.
Chie specifically speaks to Kiyotaka and no one else:
"Summer is the season of love. If you want to confess your feelings to a girl you like, perhaps it'd be more effective while standing before the beautiful ocean?"  (Y1V3)
As a byproduct of Kiyotaka having only encountered Honami, Chihiro and Ryuji of her class by then on-screen, Chihiro’s romantic feelings for Honami and Ryuji not being a girl or LGBT (?)Chie could only have been referring to Honami. Being the homeroom teacher, it’s not far-fetched to guess Chie learned of the dramatic events around Airi’s stalker and perhaps even as much as Kiyo’s involvement with Chihiro’s confession. With Chie’s interest in people’s love affairs, taking notice of Hona’s crush and flying the idea out there would be in character. If anything, it'd almost be strange if it were anything else.
As previously mentioned, Kakeru and Satoru pulled a wounded gazelle gambit on Hona’s class and correctly identified their leader. Despite this, Honami finished ahead of Kohei’s and Kakeru’s class in 2nd place. So there was no real pressure on her to change her class and her methods, though she began to be more wary of everyone regardless.
Following the events of the island exam, in the middle of the Zodiac exam (Y1V4), Kiyotaka discovered when spying on the teachers that Chie explicitly made sure Hona would be in the Rabbit group with Kiyotaka. Kiyo jumped to the conclusion Chie sent Honami there to spy on him (Y1V4), but the latter was actually unaware (Y1V4 SS) as to why she was not in the dragon group with the other class leaders. This makes it unlikely Kiyo’s guess was on the money. Making it even less likely is that Chie only started to take a genuine interest in Kiyotaka’s skills after directly witnessing a glimpse of his potential against Arisu in the Y1 end-of-year exam. Do note neither Kiyo nor Honami were VIPs; Sae and Chie were free to pick their groups and there weren't any sort of mysterious limitations they had to conceal.
Even after the Zodiac exam, there were hints Hona had a crush long before Y1V9 even if she wasn’t entirely aware, such as in Y1V4.5, Y1V4.5 SS, Y1V5 SS and Y1V8.
But being a class leader who takes her role seriously, Honami wasn’t really worried about love affairs at the time. As previously mentioned, because of the outcome of the island exam, she began to be wary of everyone outside her class. Needless to say, Honami knew Kakeru was untrustworthy with her knowing his involvement in the Ken trial and his usage of Satoru in the previous exam, which in itself meant this distrust had to be extended to everyone else in his class, not just the obvious lackeys like Ishizaki or Albert.
It doesn’t stop there; based on the point total of the island exam, another class definitely figured out their leader and wrote their name. The same applied for Kohei’s class; two classes correctly identified their leader.
Considering Suzune’s class won by a large margin and nobody correctly identified their leader whereas Suzune’s and Kakeru’s named Kohei’s class leader, it was entirely within the realm of possibility they were the second class who wrote down Honami’s leader.
This was made worse by Rokusuke, a student in Suzune’s class, betraying the VIP in the monkey group who is a student of Honami’s class. It goes without saying that Honami remained polite and maintained a diplomatic tone, but she began to act more deceptively afterward. Nevermind the Zodiac exam; from that point now, she never divulges confidential intel about herself or her class to anyone (including Kiyotaka by the way) ‘less she’s essentially forced to. The only time she spilled the beans about secrets to outsiders is when she lacks any other option.

Shortly after, around the time of the Paper Shuffle exam, someone spread rumors about Hona getting a large amount of private points through illegal means. That person being Kiyotaka, who signed those rumors on Kakeru’s name. Honami had to explain she’s essentially her class’ private point bank, her classmates entrusted their private points to her in case of an emergency.
Despite Kakeru not denying any responsibility in the matter, Honami suspected Kiyotaka had something to do with the rumors anyway (Y1V8 SS). This is because Honami remembered he saw her phone’s screen for an instant when it showed her number of private points, all the way back when they were dealing with Ken’s trial (Y1V2).

After Miyabi succeeded Manabu as the new student council president, Honami tried once more to enter the student council. Despite Honami’s attempts to get around having to do this and putting up some resistance, Miyabi eventually got Honami to reveal her secret - the aforementioned shoplifting incident - to accept her candidacy. Unfortunately, this burned her badly when Miyabi sold her secret to Arisu, leading to a chain of events in which Kiyotaka ended up revealing this to the whole school (with a number of lies by Arisu & co) to go along with it, stomping on her biggest weakness.
It’s her biggest weakness not necessarily because the crime is a sin or immoral as much as how far her mother, the one she loved most, held it against her. It was a constant reminder of how much she disappointed her mother back then.
Struggling to face her past, she retreated and stayed in her room for nearly a week. She gently pushed away everyone who expressed their concern and tried to assure her she could confide in them.
Everyone included Kiyotaka, but he didn’t care.
Despite Honami’s attempt to repel him just like everyone else, he ate lunch with her throughout the week and talked about meaningless things. It’s less about saving Honami in a normal sense. Honami, much like everyone else, can preserve through difficulties of that nature on her own, as proven later on. But having someone who’s actually there to change her mind a little from darker thoughts by talking uselessly, especially if it’s someone she already took a liking to, is different. Especially when you tried to push them away to not burden them, yet they persisted in caring.
Kiyotaka eventually told Honami he knew what kind of crime she committed, though he didn’t appear to particularly mind. More importantly, instead of trying to lessen the gravity of the act, he told her that anyone could be forgiven, which is something her mother could not bring herself to.
The love that already sprouted within her bloomed.
Both exams following this event were also immensely challenging. Kiyo helped big time in Y1V10, when he helped her reach 20 millions private points in the next special exam to avoid either being stuck in a relationship with Miyabi and taking a ~4M private points debt or expelling a classmate via the lottery.
This was followed by a disastrous Y1 end-of-year exam which had significant implications for class points, in which Honami lost 5-2 against Kakeru.
Then before the second year, Suzune’s class ended their alliance with Honami.
On the same day, March 30th, she stood in the rain after she felt lost. Kiyotaka found her and convinced her to come to his room.
It led to an important meeting for the both of them.  Throughout the meeting, Kiyo was making suggestive advances on her and essentially took the lead. He suggested they promised one another to meet a year from now, which Honami accepted. Following this, Honami regained her fighting spirit.
This promised meeting will have drastic implications for Honami’s character arc. It would take 16 volumes and 1 canonical year for everything to come together, in more ways than one.

Source: feno_edu (X/Twitter)

This scene pushed Honami towards a new direction. Much like everyone else in ANHS, she hadn’t seen her family for more than a year by then; Hona’s fabricated personality to satisfy her wish to be the type of person her mom wanted Hona to be, surrounded by people who enabled her facade, were now outweighed by her inability to satisfy her other desires that grew stronger: this being the leader her precious classmates need her to be and end the hesitations she felt with having to fight the man she long realized by then she likes.
“What if, what if he and I were in the same class… Then all my worries would disappear and I could fight without any hesitation.” (Y1V11.75)

The corruption, so to speak, was already fully in place, transforming her.

She continued to be hunted by internal conflicts for long after and struggled to come to terms with who she is, but that begun to gradually fade and Honami’s lack of care for the wellbeing of outsiders, those she didn’t have any love for, already existed before the 2nd year had begun at bare minimum.
More importantly, Honami was completely aware of this side of herself, but she had to learn to engage with it instead of suppressing it. This is shown in the aforementioned soliloquy, but it’s best demonstrated in the epilogue of Y2V9. Notice the similarities between the quote from Y1V11.75 with this:

Consider “fight without any hesitation” and “won’t choose a means to an end” to be synonymous. This proves then without a shadow of doubt that Honami, while singularly protective of those Hona hold dear, cared not one iota about the well being of those pesky outsiders long before Y2V9, or even Y2V1 and perhaps more importantly, Hona was, without a shadow of doubt, entirely aware of this side of hers. That is, even if no one else did or, with regards to some people like Suzune (Y1V11.5), they stopped believing it could exist.

So what exactly happened? Or to be more accurate, what stopped Honami from removing her chains before Y2V9? Throughout the second year, she had virtually no monologues or screen time before Y2V8, meaning her thoughts were hidden from the readers, but she slowly began to remodel herself as early as the start of the 2nd year, slowly becoming more and more blunt about her willingness to act on her desires. Abandoning her group in the Y2 Island exam to warn Kiyotaka of the danger he was in regarding Tokinari & Shiba was only one of many examples. As a matter of fact, the last time Hona truly acted in a way that was detrimental for altruistic reasons was when she didn’t report Kakeru after Y1V11.
So for the 2nd Year, she tried to steal or close the gap between her and Kiyotaka (though at that time, only from his class) through subtle social maneuvering.
Honami’s actions in this regard became more bold and courageous over time.
Other than her being a bundle of insecurities, she restrained herself from being too bold because it’s very likely Hona had reasoned back then Kiyo had a vested interest in helping anyone in need, even to the detriment of his class and he was a very much of a mystery.
This creates reasons for Honami to do what she did best: doubt herself, lack confidence and lose courage. This suggests Kiyotaka wouldn’t necessarily like it if she were to lose certain qualities she gained when remodeling herself, much like her mother. The same goes for her class - perhaps even moreso. They clearly liked her the way she was in the first year even through the ups and downs.
Does Kiyotaka care about Honami? Do her classmates? If so, is it because of the same perception everyone else has of her, as an altruistic goodie two shoes?
If so, she needs to hold back or perhaps more accurately, she has an excuse to back off. At least, Honami needs to use the old facade, the one she created for her mother as a front.
Upon realizing Kiyotaka already had a relationship with Kei, everything turned upside down. Being rejected for your first time is pretty bad as is, but it also means she had to make some difficult decisions. Honami couldn't really be sure what she should do anymore, her love for Kiyotaka and desire to snatch him away didn't conveniently fade even after knowing he had a girlfriend in his class. To Honami, who thought she had to keep her mask to not commit any moral transgression, it was a contradiction.
To steal Kiyo away requires tossing aside the perception of Hona being a good person, but how would Kiyotaka feel about her if he no longer thought she’s a good person? This contradiction was difficult to handle.
For months, Honami struggled to even look at Kiyotaka face-to-face (Y2V6), though she might have tried not long after the island exam (Y2V10.25 SS).
Of course, her suffering didn’t only revolve around Kiyotaka. Doubling the pain was, as her class’ leader, letting down her precious classmates as well.
Honami’s class was sinking.
Going from an already fragile Class B position, she slipped to C after Suzune’s class stumbled into 150 class points in the unanimous vote special exam and then Class D following the events of the Y2 sports festival. Then in the cultural festival, all the other classes gained 50 class points over hers. Rock bottom.

When they began their conversation, Kiyotaka told Honami that he was there to serve as her Kaishuku - much like he said he would in Y1V11.5 if she were to fall. This term refers to the act of delivering a finishing blow to a suffering person, so as to finish them off quickly without any more pain. It is most often used in relation to when a dishonored Samurai accepts responsibility for their failures by cutting their own belly open - and their Kaishakuin then strikes them down afterwards, to spare them of their pain. It is a form of an execution.
But, the term also has a second meaning, which is to attend to someone. To lend them a hand, and help them out of a sticky situation.
Kiyotaka’s expectations were that Honami would be upset at hearing this term, as he assumed that she thought he meant the deadly kind - which he did.

She had failed, and must accept responsibility by “dying” - and he was there to send her off on her way. Honami did not automatically jump to that conclusion, and specifically asked him which of the two meanings he was referring to. Was he here to strike her down, or to support her?
He told her that she will soon find out.
It was the other way around - rather, Kiyotaka was the one who would soon find out. Kiyo believed he was there to “execute” Honami, but she already knows that he’ll instead be by her side going forward. Going by Honami’s Y2V12.5 short story and Kiyotaka describing her response as “quick”, we know she saw through him completely and well ahead of time - she knew everything he was about to say.

The Execution of the Rumor Mills

Hona dropped out of the student council not long after the Hokkaido trip, during the scene where Miyabi revealed the elections. As mentioned many times before, this is a decision she claimed to have made before the trip.
Honami’s explanation in the moment was that she didn’t think she was worthy of such a “title” (being the SCP). Dropping out of the student council obviously had nothing to do with a lack of self-worth. If anything, it was the complete opposite: she had more confidence in herself than before. Leaving aside her strategies and planning, the decision in itself evinced it, especially as Hona didn’t consult any of her classmates before making this daring choice even though it was on her mind for some time. It directly concerned her classmates since Honami’s presence on the council provided a passive source of class points. It’s even more illogical in a narrative sense as it was meant to come across as the 1st noticeable step in the right direction to be more courageous. No one in the room, which included two of the most perceptive students in ANHS, is shown doubting this explanation in the volume; understandable as nobody there could’ve known in the moment that she kept everything to herself. As it’ll be explained throughout this section, this act played a role in nearly every layer of Honami’s complete plan. Directly, it was the catalyst for the date and the demoralization/manipulation of Miyabi.
(Verbal Deception, Tactical Potency, Tactical Management)
As Honami didn’t (and could not after her resignation as Miyabi asked Honami to stay quiet about her resignation since the procedures being that the SC needs to have the replacement officially introduced beforehand - Takuya’s case obviously being an exception to this rule since he was expelled on the spot) explain herself to her classmates at any point before the weekend, it caused confusion, so they asked Kiyo to investigate Hona for them, even though he was in a different class which could go against what would’ve been their better judgement under normal circumstances, but he has a special position for a multitude of reasons. To bring context to establish the feasibility of the strategy in regards to getting the timing of the date right: It was a near guarantee that Kiyo would ask Honami to meet up in a public space; the situation between them was still very awkward at that point and when you add Kiyotaka’s still-existing-at-the-time relationship with Kei, it was borderline impossible that Kiyo would ask Honami to meet up in either his or her room. The only plausible time given all the aforementioned factors is when Miyabi announced the elections, when Honami & Suzune had to consent to taking part of those.

There’s the matter of Honami reacting to the word shoplifting, but this isn’t very strong counter evidence. Rather than proving Honami didn’t expect Ikuta to try something ahead of time, it’s possible, as Kiyo said in his monologue, shoplifting in general is a trigger for Honami due to her backstory and it was unpleasant for her to listen to Fuka’s disgust of being framed as one. That being said, outside of everything in Y2V9 seemingly going as Hona planned and the aforementioned lack of reaction to Fuka’s (abrupt) entrance, there isn’t a wealth of (or one hard) evidence that she expected Ikuta’s actions. It also wasn’t necessary for Honami to have this specific foresight. But it’s kinda probable.

C-D Alliance Strategy

Preliminaries

This is effectively an extension (+ result) of the CKA plan, but this was separated from the rest for clarity’s sake and will be relevant for at least the first half of Y3.
Before reading this section, it’s important to mention this description will draw comparison to a variety of game theory terms and models, we believe them to be pertinent in explaining the strategy. Those being the Nash equilibrium (NE), Law of Requisite variety aka Ashby’s law, Prisoner Dilemma (PD), the Folk theorem, Tit for Tat (TFT), Iterated Prisoner Dilemma (IPD), evolutionary stable strategy (ESS) and Zero-determinant (ZD). Those are theoretical concepts used to interpret the C & D Alliance Strategy. Meaning, many of the words and abbreviations used will not refer to in-universe characters, environments, devices or concepts.
For the sake of conciseness, explanations for the relevant theoretical concepts used to analyze the C-D alliance strategy were split off from the rest of the doc, though they can be read here.
Using Game theory to interpret this alliance is a form of hermeneutic, or even an explanatory/structural, reading. Such an approach carries the risk of producing interpretations of the text that may not be fully compatible with how the same actions, events, and decisions are perceived in-universe. But those aware of the details, such as Kiyo, regard the alliance highly, though some skepticism remains from people like Ai. Furthermore, even if the author doesn’t know those specific terms, it doesn’t mean he doesn’t get the overall idea the concepts entail, so they can still be relevant.

Another detail is that using Game theory for text interpretation isn’t a niche. It might be a stretch to call it mainstream, but it is a recognized approach.

Y2V12.5 preparations

As described in the plan section, Honami, in all likelihood, intended to bring Kiyo to her class before the Y2 EoY exam. Therefore, everything around this strategy was an adjustment and there’s no evidence it was considered before Y2V12.5.
This interpretation of the alliance only requires Honami to recognize that Kiyo’s goal wasn’t solely to graduate from class A, so it can survive under unfavorable interpretations for her.
After the Y2V12 exam, Honami’s class was 519 Class Points behind Class A. Also, Honami didn’t want to abandon her zero-expulsion policy and other idealistic policies in her class. It follows from Kiyotaka’s evaluation in the Y2V12.5. In the same scene, Honami confirmed it. Furthermore, Honami herself reaffirmed her commitment to these policies during the meeting with her class in Y2V12.5.
In practice it means the following. Closing the gap (519 points) is already a tough, complex task. Having self-imposed restrictions such as 0 expulsion complicates it even more. In essence, it’s about diminishing variety in outcomes.
According to Ashby’s law, only variety can beat variety. In application to this case, this means that Honami needs to increase the regulator’s variety.
In this context, the regulator refers to Hona and her class. If she can’t magically increase the strength of her classmates, then she must use something external, i.e., team up with another class or classes or recruit someone.
Arguably, there is no single exam that would allow her to close such a gap. This is even based on the unrealistic assumption that other classes would score zero.
That assumption is unrealistic. The statement implies that a one-time alliance can't solve her problem. It should be something long-lasting. This immediately raises a question regarding the PD and IPD.
Focusing solely on class points implies that "game" is closer to PD. This means that she should propose something other than class points (in addition to) to a likely ally that may change the nature of the game, making it more akin to IPD.
Another problem is her damaged reputation in terms of leadership. Setting aside readers' evaluation of her strength/weaknesses, it’s likely that, for an increasing amount of peeps in-universe, she appeared to be an incapable or barely capable leader who had ruined her own class and relegated them to class D, even though Honami still had her own class’ support. Consequently, Honami needed someone who could serve as a "bridge" between classes, who understood her strong sides, and who could agree to cooperate with her.
However, having someone who could serve as a "bridge" does not solve the main problem. One of the most important concerns is to prevent possible defection as much as possible. One way to do this is to form an alliance with a payoff profile that fits the folk theorem, thus building a credible reward-punishment mechanic alongside a discount factor indicating patient players. In this case, both factors depend on Honami's understanding of Kiyotaka's goals (when looking at it from her POV). That's why for Honami it was critical to confirm her image of him and learn as much as possible about Kiyotaka during the Y2V12.5 promise meeting, as much as possible regardless of external influence in these events.
That’s why she tested with some of her behavior, as explained in Y2V12.5’s “the method” promise meeting division of the CKA plan section.
Additionally, this understanding serves as a prerequisite for countermeasures against possible ZD extortions from Kiyotaka's side.
For Hona, it doesn’t matter whether Kiyo expected to use the alliance to achieve another goal or if it serves as a checkpoint. For Honami, it’s important that Hona could bring her class back to the class race without losing students (minimizing this risk as much as possible) using the alliance.

There is another interesting detail related to the application of Ashby’s law. Actually, convincing other classes for not-trivial long-lasting cooperation is quite difficult, especially for someone with a not-good reputation as a class leader.
However, instead of convincing the whole class, Hona only had to convince Kiyo, the leader of the previous Class A.
It's about destroying variety; one may argue that this is a byproduct of Kiyotaka being the main character, it still can be counted because Kiyotaka obviously was going to take the leadership role sooner than later. Hona framed it as Kiyotaka’s idea, most likely to ensure the other class gets on board; it was explicitly shown to be Hona’s idea during the Y2V12.5 scene.

The core idea is that the C-D alliance as a strategy has the properties required for a successful, difficult-to-exploit strategy. This was evaluative and required reviewing the meeting between Honami, Kiyotaka, and his classmates in Y3V1.

Scene with Masayoshi and Ai (Y3V1)

During the meeting between Honami, Kiyotaka, Ai, and Masayoshi in Y3V1, the latter two were quite skeptical about the alliance proposed by Hona and Kiyo. Masayoshi even attempted to take advantage of Honami's class.
Essentially, Ai and Masayoshi's argumentation could be summarized as follows. Let's consider two common situations.
The first scenario involves a special exam in which Hona's class faces Kiyotaka's. In this case, the class with structural/contextual advantages may be incentivized to unilaterally defect to secure victory. Moreover, any class might try to exploit the alliance and turn the situation to its own advantage.
This defection strategy will reduce the gap between the defecting class and the top-ranked classes while also widening the gap with the lower-ranked ones. Therefore, exploiting the alliance looks like the most profitable solution.
The other scenario involves a situation in which both classes need to cooperate to prepare for exams, e.g., by forming joint study groups. In this scenario, one party could exploit the alliance by contributing less while receiving additional support from the other. It makes sense considering that these classes are likely to compete against each other in future exams. Once again, defection appeared to be the most profitable solution. In other words, they argued that the alliance was unlikely to succeed because defection offers a higher payoff.
These scenarios are, basically, the prisoner’s dilemma. Therefore, at first glance, the alliance isn't possible or reasonable, because defect is the optimal strategy.
However, the crucial aspect is that it is not a one-time collaboration but rather a recurring process. Thus, it represents an iterated game; the cooperative strategy may provide a more profitable solution. It depends on how it’s played.

First of all, the condition for dissolving the alliance.
One condition for a cooperative strategy to be effective is that there should not be a predetermined last round. If the last round is known in advance, defection becomes the optimal strategy for both parties. If both parties choose to defect, cooperating in the round prior to the final round is not a reasonable option, and this reasoning continues for earlier rounds.
The condition "until the four classes are competing equally" helps resolve the issue by turning the game’s horizon stochastic rather than fixed.
Essentially, it solves the issue with “infinite horizon,” which “unlocks” NE.
Masayoshi tested the signal for potential deception. He perceived the alliance as a covert collusion when there is a hidden romantic subtext. The alliance may just serve that romantic goal; it might not align with the collective goal.
Consequently, Hona's and Kiyotaka's explanation of C-D sought to prevent "false defections." Their transparent explanation may prevent observers from wrongly inferring a side deal. They aimed at convincing them that the alliance was not an opportunistic romance pivot and expectations of both classes aligned. It mainly fits the Tit for Tat’s ”Don’t be too clever” criterion.
It indirectly fits into the "Nice" criterion because it highlights the initial choice to cooperate rather than defect. It’s about the ”Don’t be too clever” criterion.

Masayoshi claims that it's a zero-sum game. In this, one party's gain is directly equivalent to another person's loss. The counterargument broadens the context of the alliance. In this extended domain, the "game" becomes non-zero sum. Therefore, cooperation and collaboration lead to better outcomes for both.
It establishes the alliance payoff profile in a way aligning with the folk theorem. The payoff is structured to be feasible for the players and individually rational.
The passage is about TFT’s Nice and Don't be too clever conditions. "…we have agreed to concede victory to the class that is even one point behind in Class Points" shows commitment to cooperate from the beginning & values cooperation more than a single-exam win. The articulated rule is simple and understandable and leaves minimal room for misinterpretation & consequently exploitation ("more detailed arrangements").
The rule can be a grim trigger or incite retaliation in tit-for-tat (TFT).
Indirectly it touches on TFT's other two conditions; Retaliating and Forgiving. Since the general rule is simple, the class violating it could demonstrate their willingness to cooperate next time. Since the rule is detailed, it clearly codifies who must concede. Any violation is easily verifiable and leads to punishments.

Again, it touches the folk theorem. “The alliance formed with Ichinose's class is also one of the reasons for my transfer.” Kiyo forced his new classmates to start with cooperation; otherwise, he would retaliate. Here Kiyotaka sounded like a “grim-trigger” (retaliating by defecting in all future rounds).
Honami’s public image is an explicit foundation for the alliance (from her side). Masayoshi and, apparently, Ai are looking for a possible mechanic to retaliate. Kiyo's hypothetical scenario shows that Honami's actions result in a significant loss of reputation, which serves as a punishment.

At the same time, it deterred defections. The reputational logic allowed earning redemption by returning to cooperation outside her class as a respectable class leader (sort of a “rise and fall” of Honami's reputation). This serves as yet another confirmation that the payoff profile aligns with the folk theorem.
Dissolving alliances in early stages can serve as a retaliation mechanic. This can reinforce the concept of the alliance as a non-zero-sum game.
Honami also explicitly points out (but not in IPD language) the endgame problem with backward induction. Implied "no betrayal first."
Honami’s understanding (to some extent) of Kiyotaka's motives, along with his abilities, serves as the foundation of the alliance, just like Honami’s credibility.
It's about the C-D alliance being a non-zero-sum game and destroying outcome variety by adding variety (two classes act as one in certain situations).

After a while, Masayoshi tries to get more out of the agreement, wanting Honami to pony up some private points, leading to her making an important statement.

First, Masayoshi's private points demand creates two scenarios.
In the most extreme scenario, his proposal could alter the dynamics of the game by modifying the payoffs for defection and cooperation for both classes.
It's hard to predict, but it's fair to say that the payoff Prisoner's Dilemma “matrix” might become asymmetric in favor of Kiyo's class. In some cases, cooperation for Honami's class might become impossible or even the worst option.

The game might stop being an Iterated Prisoner Dilemma.

In the more skeptical scenario, Masayoshi's proposal complicates the retaliation mechanic for Honami's class. This could lead to situations where retaliation isn't an option at all. For instance, let's consider the equivalence between a low chunk of Class Points and Private Points.

There is an exam in which Honami's class is expected to win based on the rules. However, Kiyotaka's class defects, wins, and earns 20 Class Points.
On the other hand, Hona's class has already spent several million Private Points. Retaliation might be a flawed option (depending on the current class rankings).
It’s Masayoshi's attempt to force the extortion of the zero-determinant strategy. For Hona, it’s obviously important to avoid such exploitation.
Therefore, Honami refusing this suggestion and her insistence on equal-footing cooperation are the only ways this cooperation can succeed.
In addition, the whole conversation was public. It was not a private conversation between Honami and Kiyotaka. All their classmates will, likely, learn about the alliance and the rules at one point, which is critical for “coordination equilibria”.

As previously mentioned, one of the main criticisms is that Nash Equilibrium and similar solution concepts are weaker in “noisy” environments. Considering the horizon of the long-term, clear uncertainties like unknown exam content and so on, and unpredictabilities related to social life, it's ideal to mitigate such risk.
The main tool used to increase robustness is to avoid rigid, inflexible rules or an overreliance on far-fetched guesses of what’s to come during the third year.

This is why they chose not to have a formal written agreement and instead relied on trust as the foundation of their C-D alliance. It was explicitly highlighted as a strength in the Y3V1 while pointing out how rigid rules may cause big drawbacks.
That wide range of strategic options allows them to make quick feedback-driven adjustments based on their observation. This process can be done iteratively. Presumably, monitoring the situations with his class shouldn't be the problem.
In addition, this point allows one to claim in addition to the other points that the alliance as a strategy is somewhat “forgiving”. It provides them options to return to cooperation after defection if they like. In other words, it prevents an endless cycle of retaliation after a defection or even an unwitting/accidental defection.
It can be seen as tough to exploit. The expected loss of the strategy's retaliatory phase cancels any extra rewards the exploiter may earn by defecting.

It doesn’t mean that the alliance is invincible. For example, unpredictable events  that change what Kiyotaka wants, his goals and objectives, may drastically affect the discount factor and cause defection, as the defection in a single round would outweigh possible future punishments.

But as things stand at the time of this writing (Y3V2), Hona has reasons to think it’s a strategy with a high success rate even with the aforementioned caveats.

Honami’s Leadership

From the very first volume, Hona has been the one and only leader of her class. Honami is the only leader of her year who can make this claim; Sae’s class never stopped being a carousel, especially early on when people referred to her class as either Suzune’s class or Yosuke’s class, then there is those in the know such as Kakeru who referred to it as Kiyo’s class before his transfer. And students in the class follow one of those three (if not Kei or Kikyo depending on the factions and circumstances) and during the end of the second year there was a debate inside the class as to who should really lead the class, especially since Y2V5. Kohei led the former Class A until Arisu won the power struggle. Kakeru lost control after Kiyo clapped him in Y1V7, until he made his triumphant return in the first year’s end-of-year exam.
It was Honami’s mentality that caused her whole class to adopt the cooperative mindset they’ve been known for from the onset. She has displayed outstanding skill at motivating and directing individuals to work together collaboratively and cohesively to accomplish her visions and purposes.
The quest to reach Class A hasn’t been smooth sailing: her class’ decline in class points began with a significant loss against Kakeru in the first year’s end-of-year exam and they fell from Class B to D. Even worse, she does have a real anti-feat in leadership in Y1V9: that being, her hiding herself in her room after the rumors began to spread because she couldn’t face her own past, leaving her classmates even more troubled and in the dark is obviously not good leadership.
But the fact that Honami’s class remained united under her banner throughout more than half a year of hardships and zero class win since they entered ANHS until Y2V9 (Y1V5 could be seen as one, but she tied with Arisu’s class in losing fewer class points; entirely because the other two classes destroyed each others) without any real internal opposition serves as a testament of not only Honami’s mental strength and perseverance, but also her abilities in guiding and inspiring her class towards achieving common goals or objectives through times many other would lose the respect of their comrades. Y2V12.5 took it to a whole new level. Even though Honami had just suffered an heartbreaking loss against Kiyo and the new Class A, she managed yet again to recover and rally her class behind her through their sheer belief in her, even pursuing the three people on Ryuji’s faction to give her time to think of a strategy for the upcoming year.
There’s no evidence she ever used coercive force to make her classmates submit to her decisions. She’s the only leader of Kiyo’s year with this distinction. Suzune, often considered to be the most democratic class leader, forced the rest of her class to spare Kikyo in the unanimous special exam, leading to Airi’s expulsion.
While a fraction of her classmates like Ryuji or Yuki have their own ideas on how the class should be run, Hona’s under no obligation to act on any demands they have, change her methods to suit their tastes or follow their advice and the rest of the class is within their right to believe in Honami’s vision if they want to.

Honami started at Class B and fell to D. She made her fair share of mistakes that played a part in leading into this (*ahem* Y1V3 and Y1V11), but in no way does it prove she is not the best leader. Her bad luck caused her to take multiple critical hits she didn’t deserve. While she should have been more careful regardless, the situation leading to Honami giving her secret (shoplifting past) to Miyabi to join the student council was created by the former president, Manabu, refusing her candidacy with the vague excuse that Honami was vaguely “unfit” despite Hona’s outstanding social contributions and academics. From a point of view, one could say, Manabu developed tunnel vision over having Kiyo as the first year’s member of the council instead of anyone else despite the latter not concealing his clear disinterest in the job. Had it not been for Manabu and (indirectly) Kiyotaka, Arisu and Miyabi would never have had the opportunity to hit her weakness.
The class poll exam was disastrous for Hona and her class specifically. Whereas the other classes are less unified thus less damaged by losing their own, having to pick a classmate to expel could’ve damaged her class’ best card which is unity.
This exam was entirely unprecedented and so, Honami had no way to foresee its announcement without being in the White Room loop. The only reason it even existed was because of the circumstances around Kiyotaka.

Without Masayoshi’s personal agenda around Kiyotaka and the rotation working out perfectly, both of which Kakeru was in no way responsible for. If Honami was successful, her strategy would have netted her a big win in Y2V10.

As previously mentioned, Masayoshi fell on Kakeru’s laps leading to a massive win in Y2V10.

Arisu's (now Kiyo’s) class had a big head start over the other classes by hundreds of class points (though in all fairness Hona’s class began at B so she had that over Kakeru). Even if Honami makes or considers making questionable decisions at times, Hona has proven throughout the series she possesses outstanding leadership qualities.

Manipulation Resistance

Hona was often dismissed by people, in and out of universe, as gullible, naive and everyone's victim when it comes to being manipulated/controlled. This used to be a valid criticism in Year 1, Y1V9 and Y1V10 being particularly ugly moments for her, but she had great feats in this category throughout Y2.

The situation with Kiyotaka is actually a very complicated case.
He definitely set the stage to make her fall in love with him. Kiyotaka began in Y1V6 when he fabricated rumors he signed under Kakeru's name about Honami illegally collecting private points to bait others (namely Arisu) into attacking her. Kiyotaka manipulated Arisu even further in Y1V7.5 to push her in that direction. This led to Arisu attacking Hona with Miyabi's assistance and then Kiyo rescuing her. Kiyotaka helped Hona at several other low points, namely Y1V10, Y1V11.5 and Y2V8. Nowadays, Hona is as “yandere” (for lack of better words) as any character can be in COTE's semi-realistic setting.

But even if Honami became more conscious of Kiyotaka after the events of Y1V9, there were indications Hona had a “thing” for Kiyo even before Airi in Y1V2, Y1V3, Y1V4 (see Characterization section), Y1V4.5, Y1V5 SS and Y1V8 (indirectly’ Honami freaks out when a NPC girl asked if she liked anyone and later in the story Hona said Kiyo is her first love). Five of the aforementioned volumes happened before Kiyotaka spread the Y1V9 rumors.

Honami already having feelings for Kiyo lessened the seduction as an anti-feat. It’s also worth keeping in mind he is an excellent manipulator.

Something Kiyo also has over Arisu and Miyabi is that, while he's just as capable of amoral behavior as the other two if not more, Kiyo at least has some respect for Hona’s intelligence. Unlike Miyabi, Kiyo made an infinitely more sophisticated attempt at covering his tracks. Kiyo successfully pinned the rumors about Hona's PPTs on Kakeru. Plus, Arisu was manipulated by Kiyotaka into attacking Honami. It's worth pointing out she did suspect Kiyotaka was responsible for the rumors (Y1V8 SS), but compared to Miyabi blatantly leaking her secret and Arisu thinking she could toy with her in Y2 after what she publicly did the year prior, it’s easy to understand why Kiyotaka had more success winning Hona over than these two; not everyone can pull off the white knight manipulation method with finesse.
But even if she knew Kiyo was the one responsible for the rumors in Y1V6 (which admittedly is likely), it's nigh-impossible to tie them to the events of Y1V9.
But even then, there's no concrete example of Kiyotaka manipulating Honami into doing one specific thing or another outside of being in love with him in Year One with all the implications this has on her goals, etc. In Year 2, even less. Honami's actions since the UIE veered Kiyo’s plans towards more unpredictable directions according to both Arisu (Y2V9.5) and Kiyotaka and he had nothing to do with her in Y2V1; this confirms Hona hasn't been manipulated by Kiyo in the conventional sense of the word throughout the second year (despite Kiyo indicating otherwise in Y2V12). In fact, every outsmarting character has been deceived at least once, with Kiyo being disproportionately on the giving end.

Honami dropped out of the council just as he was about to launch an election to compete against Kiyo, the worst possible timing for the former student council president. Hona considered leaving before the trip too, so it can't be credited to Kiyotaka's intervention of Y2V8. Miyabi’s attempt to manipulate Honami in the aftermath of the uninhabited island exam by using her broken heart amounted to one awkward lunch shortly before the sport festival.
Ikuta tried to use Fuka to guilt-trip Hona into dropping out of the elections, but since she wanted to quit the student council altogether beforehand, we have no way to know if it would have worked out the way Ikuta had intended to under normal circumstances. Judging by how she deduced his agenda very quickly if she didn’t foresee it ahead of time, the chances were slim to none. BTW She made more outta both of ‘em with her y2v9-v9.5 plotting than either ever did outta her.

(Y2V1) Partner Exam Tactic

One of the potential hidden plots of Y2V1 was Honami envisioning the uniquely stinky situation her class was about to get itself in and escaping this nightmare turn of events before it trapped them.
Right after the exam began, she made an announcement for a meet-and-greet with her juniors. It gave her a head-start to building relations with a large chunk of the new first years, which could have proved to be useful, since Year 2 had a lot of inter-year events (V1-4.5, 6, 7, 11). By thinking outside the box, you can come up with many ideas to involve classes from other years in your businesses; for example, in Y2V2 Sakurako and Riku suggested they could offer to Kiyotaka an unknown amount of private points to expel Kazuomi.
Unfortunately, the juniors that came to her meeting were on the lower end when it came to academics, so they didn't score much. Consequently, Honami and her class finished last with 621 points, 19 below Suzune's class.
However, this was a non-issue when you contextualize the hurdle Honami had to face. This exam came at the absolute worst possible time for Honami and her classmates: her class had next to no private points due to their decision to not expel anyone in the Class Vote Exam at the cost of all their funds. Making this even worse is that everybody in the school knew their coffer was empty, so not only Honami couldn't join in any bidding war, but she also couldn't bluff either. Negotiations would then be off the table as a result, since there's no private point to appeal any first year into negotiating.
Nothing stopped the other classes from letting all of their juniors know of their poverty (in PPT), in hope they would charge obscene interest rates from having all the leverage. Had she taken a different course of action, her class’ unfortunate circumstances could’ve put her not academically gifted classmates in jeopardy. Trying to bluff before the news came out would’ve backfired horribly - nothing could stop the first years they’d approach from bringing up their offer in their negotiation with Arisu and Kakeru’s class to jack up their own prices. Fortunately, while the timing was horrible for the aforementioned reasons, it's great that the exam came at the beginning of the school year because the juniors were obviously not going to be organized under any sort of leadership by then, Class 1-D being a freaky outlier.

Using her people skills, she took advantage of their disarray to pair up everyone in her class ASAP before anyone could take advantage of her class’ weakness to make unreasonable demands.
She was never winning the exam, but considering the measly 50-30-10-0 class points at stakes, it’s far from a big deal in the grand scheme of things. If it was not from factors widely outside of her control (Takuya's schemes around Kikyo and Kazuomi chasing the bounty), Honami most likely takes 3rd place, +10 class points. What really matters is the disaster she avoided; i.e., ending any chance she had of reaching Class A: Even if only one unremarkable classmate were to be expelled, there's zero mention anywhere in the Y2V1 that the -300 class points penalty per expulsion and/or drop out was waived: even one expulsion would have destroyed her class.

Looking at things from this perspective, Honami was able to foresee all of this as soon as the exam began. Outside of being a feat of problem-solving, this would also be an exhibit of her decision making, tactical and leadership skills.

(Formulation Speed, Field Safeguarding, Risk Assessment, Abductive Reasoning, Social Cognition, Critical Thinking, Abstract Thinking, Shielding, Fluid Methods, Tactical Management, Leadership, Anticipation, Preclusion)

Sidenote: While partnering up with the weaker juniors was a rather bad thing in the context of scoring points in this exam, they are easier to control or use by outsiders because they’re less valued by their classmates, thus having less power and more likely to be on the outs, making them more likely to betray their class or be convinced into taking other desperate measures. Therefore, it is arguably better to build relationships with weaker students outside your class depending on the circumstances and what you’re trying to accomplish.
(Y3V3) Survival Game Strategy

Thirty minutes before Kakeru’s attack on Kiyotaka, Honami noticed that the GPS signals for Kakeru’s class hadn't moved for 5 minutes. Hona immediately realized Kaneda had activated the GPS freeze option and that Kakeru’s class was about to ambush Kiyotaka’s. Considering the foresight for her first act, Hona must’ve seen something like this coming sooner than later from the start. The GPS tablets can not take screenshots, so Honami observed the anomaly because she memorized the position of all the students from Kakeru’s class 5 minutes prior. According to the neutral narration, Honami memorized the coordinates “down to the smallest unit”. The narrator also mentions, as part of Honami’s awareness of the situation, that the GPS has functional limits; satellite signal transmissions will always have some deviations, so even if people stayed completely still, there should still be a small drift in the signals’ positions. Additionally, Honami had zero reason to only remember the coordinates of Kakeru’s classmates on the first scan, meaning that Honami remembered the coordinates of all the students of all classes, proven by the fact that Honami also spotted that the GPS markers from Kiyo’s class shifted somewhat, but those were all confined to one area, suggesting they were resting or holding a strategy meeting.
Hona tried to signal Shimazaki with her eyes, but the latter simply exchanged a glance with her, did not get the situation and returned to staring blankly at his screen. No other means of communication - namely languages, hand signals or lip-reading, were allowed between the commanders.
In response to these developments, Hona began her strategy to defend Kiyotaka that will be described below.
(Crystalized Intelligence, VSI, Memory, Tracing, Critical Thinking, Anticipation)

She aimed to join up with the remaining players of Kiyo’s class to construct a force of 50 that Kakeru & Suzune would not recklessly attack. After the ambush by Kakeru’s class that almost destroyed them, Kiyo’s class spent the 2nd and 3rd days on the defensive in the southern area around the headquarters. Then, their attempt to recover even one single additional supply box to regain some ground ended in failure. Looking solely at the results, it was two blunders in a row.
But Honami understood Kiyo’s aim, at the very least all the way back to the time she noticed Kaneda turning off the GPS function and responded appropriately.
Both ostensible errors made by Kiyotaka were deliberate to force his classmates to join forces with Honami’s class and conceal the alliance from Suzune & Kakeru for the first stretch of the exam, so they wouldn’t team up to counter them.
The neutral narrator stated Honami tried more than anyone else to stay “in sync” with his mind, that her awareness was constantly attuned to him, so she counted on Kiyotaka to, soon after “mistake” #2, approach her classmates, so she notified them about this shortly before then. Honami instructed her classmates to follow Kiyotaka’s orders on the field. Between the two allied classes, Kiyotaka was by far the most adept student (physically) in the fight and a cut above Himaguchi, Ryuji, Masayoshi or whoever else was out there in decision-making as well.
Fundamentally, since Honami was in the commander role, the best choice was to let Kiyotaka make split second calls over someone less capable. Either way, given the structure of their alliance (Honami being Class D meant that Kiyo would have to concede the win) and Kakeru’s attack putting Kiyo’s class in a disadvantageous position compared to her class, it’s unlikely that Honami didn’t expect Kiyotaka’s class to concede the victory to her regardless of that, meaning there was not any real loss to letting him make the calls.
(Redirection, Responsiveness, Field Safeguarding, Adaptability, Holistic Thinking, Critical Thinking, Psychological Insight, Anticipation)

There was another potential, albeit speculative, advantage from letting Kiyo take commands. Realistically, the C-D alliance won't last forever and it’s not meant to.
Kiyo is now a clear yet new leader. Observing Kiyo from the sideline while having the opportunity to research him through her classmates was logical.
He will undoubtedly be the biggest threat, ultimately, and Honami will eventually have to fight him for Class A. Studying how Kiyo operates from up close through her classmates who’ll report to her everything he did around them could serve a purpose beyond this exam. But again, this is very speculative as of Y3V3.
(Information Gathering (?))

Before their merge, Hona launched an operation to ensure that the merge would happen in the second half without alerting Suzune and Kakeru’s class while also keeping her own class, as well as Kiyotaka’s, safe.
Once the exam started, every class moved to the north region. Unlike the other classes, Hona had her classmates move east and collected all the rewards there.
Honami already expected that Kakeru would be busy staying close to Kiyotaka’s class and Suzune’s class would move straight north as Class A’s starting position gave them a massive advantage to dominate all of the northern areas. It resulted in Honami’s class having a small advantage in supply over Suzune’s and Kakeru’s class (10 boxes over 9).
On the note of her class management, based on how her class moved on the 2nd day and Kiyo’s monologue, Honami must’ve forecasted Rokusuke as a derailment from the onset and managed her classmates’ movement to stay far away from his vicinity in case he decided to go rogue. It kinda ended up happening although he attacked Kakeru’s class instead of hers.
Additionally, since Honami stayed around the same area with little movement for a full 24 hours, her class could conserve stamina - an extremely valuable resource to maintain as constantly reiterated throughout the volume. It’s to the point that it isn’t worth listing every time Kiyo mentions this factor throughout the volume.
Honami needed her classmates to not tire too much, since she intended to make them move a lot during the middle portion of the special exam.
(Strategy Forecast, Strategy Logistics, Shielding, Prediction)

Another advantage that stemmed from the maneuver was how everyone else was going to be at a long distance from Honami’s classmates as a result of traveling in different directions for a while.  What this meant is that any class that wished to charge at them had to telegraph their attack; they would have needed to travel a long time to move to their spot, which was suicidal since it would have given her classmates plenty of time to prepare their own defense, even with a GPS freeze.
At first, it appeared as if Honami originally wanted to spend the four days in the eastern side and avoid fighting as much as possible, but this was unfeasible since Day 2 when the groups closed in. Honami ordered her class to circle around the mountain to the south (note: both scans are important to support this claim).
The decreasing space for the 4 classes left any class that attempted to pursue or intercept their navigations wide open to be blown up by another class.
Predictably, Class A still occupied the northwest regions, but they were moving southward and Kakeru’s class was closing in on the other side, meaning if either classes tried to strike Hona’s class in the middle, the other classes would learn of this quickly through their commander and their active students were not too far away to capitalize on the aggressive opponents.
Additionally, Hona could keep track of everyone's movement on the GPS, so she could have adjusted if the other classes hoped to attack her classmates. As such, like on the first day, hard committing an attack against Honami’s was going to be a tall order to both execute on its own and avoid massive retaliations.
Most importantly, it facilitated the merge with Kiyo’s class. Honami’s “collecting supplies and keeping a safe distance” method was a ploy. It essentially created a massive area where Kakeru could not hit Kiyotaka’s class to finish them off even before they merged. This is due to the fact that her classmates could easily show up and ruin his attack in accordance with their movement around them nearby. Kakeru could’ve ended up in a confrontation with Honami's class as well, putting himself at a big disadvantage even before Rokusuke wiped out nearly a quarter of his force, even more so since Kakeru already suspected a C-D alliance.
Even before Day 2, Hona had her class not too close, yet not too far from where Kiyo and Kakeru’s classes were. Kiyo’s class was more vulnerable since she would have to make her class travel a longer distance to reach them (Kiyo was at G12 on Day 1, 5 PM). Still, if Kakeru intended to pursue them further, he would’ve needed to merge his two forces to have any chance of success; one was approaching the B9 area and the other (that Kiyo suspected to have the least but more physically capable combatants) was moving towards H9, the latter of which happened to be a lot closer to Honami’s classmates stationed at N12.
Her class being well defended was vital to assert control over the situation. Hona made it so nobody had realistic options to stop the merge even if they suspected an alliance between Class C and D. There were alarming risks to attacking either one of Honami or Kiyotaka’s class, they wouldn’t interfere with the strategy.

It was too late by then to build a truce with Suzune’s class for this exam - Class A had all the leverage over Class B via having more resources and their GPS freeze still being unused, meaning that Class B would’ve had to forfeit the W or promise to give Class A another sort of significant concession.
Even if Kakeru agreed to or offered anything else, Suzune had reasons to distrust him; they were Class A and Kakeru wasn’t the most trustworthy individual.
(Strategy Building, Strategy Complexity, Strategy Flexibility)

When Ryuji stressed out over the spaces shrinking, Hona understood how he felt even though she was miles away. Despite the narrator’s earlier confirmation that Hona was already executing her strategy to merge with Kiyotaka’s class, Honami told Ryuji that she needed time to think about her next moves, likely to suppress any potential leak or tipping off the other two classes if they crossed paths in the meanwhile if they sensed something was “off” with how her classmates behaved. For what it’s worth, it’s a fairly standard procedure for most class leaders. Kakeru and Kiyotaka have kept their classmates on a need-to-know basis multiple times (including in this very exam) and according to Tachibana, Manabu was similar as a class leader in this regard. The only difference is that no one in her class seems to have caught on to the fact she does this, not even partially, though this can be written off as her classmates not being observant and characters like Kakeru not not making any effort to deceive his classmates like that, rather than Hona being an exceptional liar compared to them. Regardless, Honami might have kept Ryuji and co. on a need-to-know basis to ensure the strategy went as she intended.
Perhaps in relation to this, Hona could sense her classmate Kobashi “frantically” making hand gestures to Ryuji through the audio, after she gave her command to merge with Kiyotaka’s class. Honami’s classmates, especially Ryuji himself, were hesitant when it came to working closely with Kiyotaka and his class, something Honami was aware of.
(Emotional Understanding, Verbal Deception, Sensory)

This strategy ensured Hona avoided casualties and any skirmishes for the exam’s first half while keeping the veil on the C-D alliance, to conceal her intent to take the veil off to merge her forces with Kiyotaka’s in the 2nd half. The narrator said Hona planned to do so since Kiyotaka’s class was attacked by Kakeru on the first day. But if one only observed the coordinates, the manner Honami executed her strategy made it come across as if Honami was acting independently of Kiyotaka or anyone else to be nonconfrontational for its own sake.
At first, it was as if Honami intended to park in the east for all four days, but then she was forced to adjust to the shrinking area when it was no longer sustainable. Always in the hope of not fighting at all throughout the SE. But as written earlier, she acted to secure as many supplies as possible at first and to protect Kiyo from Kakeru’s intense pressures and ease the merge afterward. (Y2V10) Description of the Strategy to Win S&D ◈

Colored by: Andreev (Twitter/X)
In Y2V10’s special exam, Honami offered 25 points to Kakeru in exchange for not hitting any of her vulnerable classmates (two wrong answers). Honami’s deal was based on her expectation of Suzune's attack plan against her: build up a block of five people on the brink. Hona understood everyone knew she wanted to protect everyone and that, unlike Arisu and Kakeru, Suzune did not care about expelling anyone in particular, so she would try to take advantage of Honami’s mentality to score points. Honami used this to negotiate with Kakeru.
(Negotiation, Prediction, Psychological Insight, Hot Reading, Metacognition, Inductive Reasoning, Emotional Realization, Emotional Understanding)

The max number of points a class could score in the S&E special exam is 100 points, and 50 in each half. This is because every half consists of ten rounds in which the attacking class nominates five students of the defending class. The class will score a point every time a student answers correctly or be successfully defended. Basically, 1 * 5 * 10 * 2 = 100 possible points.
You can not score points when attacking and class alliances were discouraged by the exam’s reward: Only the class that finishes first earns class points, the other three lose points and last place loses 50 more class points and will have to expel one of their own. As such, the class leaders were restrained to a limited number of strategies to win this special exam, increasing its difficulty.
You can try to tank other classes by carefully selecting the subject you’re hitting their students with and ensure your attack pattern remains unpredictable thus harder to defend, but the exam’s rotation will prevent every class from directly interacting with one other class: Honami & Arisu and Kakeru & Suzune will never attack nor defend against each other. This approach also eliminates any chances of dealing with the other classes. You can make a deal with someone of another class, whether that be their leader or find a way to entice a traitor. As long as the other side holds their end of the bargain (which can be ensured in a number of ways), this is much more reliable than any other approaches since you can secure a fixed ceiling number of points.
The rewards of the exam discouraged this by harshly punishing last place and giving a large boost in point differential to the class in 1st place. The class leader can instead simply rely on and hope their classmates to answer correctly. This is not a terrible idea if the class leader creates an advantageous playing field, but it relies on mixing up a few tactics. She chose this option. She used her protection slots "ineffectively" during the first half when Suzune attacked her.
This completely fooled Arisu and Suzune. During the lunch scene, the two class leaders thought that she was recklessly trying to protect her classmates without thinking things through. However, Honami's goal was to win.
The narrator states that one of the reasons she wasn't going to offer more than 25 points to Kakeru is because any more than that would jeopardize her chances to finish first. Intentionally being inaccurate when defending by wasting defense slots on people unlikely to be attacked appear suboptimal, but the alternative of trying to predict Suzune’s attacks normally would not provide a significant boost in accuracy. Without a spy, Honami had no way to know with any certainty who she had to defend and knew Suzune didn’t care who would be expelled; as long as she wins, nothing else mattered to her. Meaning, in practice, trying to predict Suzune’s targets every round would have been a fool’s errand and an exercise of luck. Suzune’s strategy may seem like the best way to ensure the defending class scored less points, but it had a big flaw Honami wanted to exploit. If Kakeru and Suzune never attacked anyone with 2 mistakes, they were attacking the students with 0 or 1 mistakes. Logically speaking, these people were more likely to answer correctly. That’s common sense; those who already made mistakes were likely to keep answering incorrectly versus those who did not. Then if, during the second half, she didn’t have to worry about protecting vulnerable, less capable students, Honami would use her protection slots on the other potential targets, giving her a much higher rate of successful protection.
This arrangement indirectly may have given her class a lot of points.
(Divergent Thinking, Strategy Logistics, Misdirection)

Giving 25 free points to Kakeru's class is no big deal - exceptions like Hiyori and Kohei aside, overall they’re by far the weakest class academically. Their score of 19 points in the first half reflected this. It's also better for Hona’s class if Class D wins or avoids last place than A or B. Arisu and Suzune were clearly not teaming up; had Suzune won, her class would become the new Class A. If Kiyotaka hadn't ensnared Masayoshi in Y2V9.5, he wouldn’t have betrayed his class, and it's more likely Kakeru would have taken Hona’s deal. If he didn’t, chances are Arisu takes the W and that his class would’ve ended up last - the absolute worst outcome for Kakeru. In Hona’s own worst case scenario, she would’ve lost 100 class points but avoided any expulsion due to Suzune’s attack pattern and her deal with Kakeru.
(Anticipation, Prediction, Field Safeguarding, Risk Assessment)

To shade her strategy, Hona forestalled information leakage. Hona sent her text to Kakeru right after the exam began, knowing he would not work with Arisu, to guarantee he and anyone else wouldn’t have the time or opportunity to decipher her intentions. For good measure, Honami had her class hide during halftime.
While they might have used the time to study, it kept her classmates from saying anything unnecessary.

Her class never had an opportunity to buy Masayoshi's betrayal - a byproduct of the rotation set up by the school. Those factors were out of Honami’s control, so straight-up bad luck.
