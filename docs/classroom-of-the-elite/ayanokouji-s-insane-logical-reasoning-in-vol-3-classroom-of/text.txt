Ayanokouji's INSANE logical reasoning in Vol 3

So I don't normally make these sorts of posts, but I have to after just finishing Vol 3 of COTE, the island exam, which is apparently when Koji's feats start getting good, and being subjected to the plot bending over backwards to make Ayanokouji look smart.

**Ibuki as the liar deduction:** The context is that someone stole Karuizawa's underwear and planted it in Ike's bag, and Ayanokouji figures it isn't Ike based off him only getting scared halfway into checking his bag after being told to do so instead of instantly as the culprit would. Ibuki is a girl from Class C who apparently had a fall out with their leader and has been taken in. Ayanokouji also skulked through her bag and found a digital camera.

He concludes that she is the thief after a simple conversation with her. How so? Well, normal people typically make eye contact throughout a conversation but tend to look away when lying. Ibuki is the opposite. She is aloof and hardly makes eye contact with anyone, but he notices that she makes eye contact when he tells her he trusts that she isn't the thief and she states she isn't the thief, seemingly indicating that she has the opposite pattern of unconsciously making eye contact when lying. Sound, right? **Wrong.** Introverted people, and I say this as someone who was aloof like Ibuki when I was younger, may make eye contact in such situations for all sorts of reasons. Ayanokouji is canonically a good-looking boy, and he's expressing trust in her, who from his POV hitherto that deduction is a poor outsider in both her class and his. It could've been attraction over him showing kindness to her, it could've been appreciation, or it could've simply been for emphasis on her part against the possible attack on her character.

**Ibuki's radio deduction**: The context is that Ibuki had hid a radio underneath the spot where Ayanokouji, Yamauchi, and Sakura had found her sitting there with a bruise on her cheek, and Ayanokouji sabotages that as part of his plan later on. Now this isn't actually specified within the story, as he states he simply "saw" Ibuki hide her radio in the ground, but I've seen people make the case that he immediately figured out that she saw something based off the clues given when the scene of the group's discovery of her actually play out: he notices traces of dirt underneath her fingernails and a slight disturbance in the soil underneath where she was sitting.

I've memed on this before, but dirt underneath her fingernails is an odd thing to pick up on IN AN ISLAND IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FOREST, let alone deduce that she had been digging. Based off context clues, there's several reasons she could've had the dirt there. (1) Maybe she tripped and fell on her ass like Sakura prior to the scene. (2) She was sitting on the ground, so perhaps she simply got it there when she had her arms on the ground in a relaxed posture. (3) She got bruised. Perhaps she fell to the ground when she got struck and the dirt got there that way. (4) She could've gotten it when she was helping set stuff up in her camp. The disturbed soil is also an odd thing since, if it's soft soil that can easily be dug up, any disturbance in the soil, granting that the soil would even show signs of that, would more soundly be attributed to her planting herself to the ground there.

It's far more likely that after his leap of logic to determine Ibuki was the traitor, he simply kept an eye on her, followed her, and saw her mess with the radio as he states to Horikita i.e. he wasn't lying to her about it.

**Class A's leader deduction**. Context: While scouting with Sakura, Ayanokouji had stumbled onto a path that led to the entrance of a cave. He quickly ducked behind cover when two guys - Katsuragi and Yahiko - stepped out. Katsuragi is holding the key card and announces that they have to be cautious. His deduction is this: Katsuragi seemed smart and cautious in his interactions later in the story, but claiming the spot immediately is a very risky move that only greedy, short-sighted people would do. Therefore, it had to be the guy right next to him, who seemed stupid in their later interactions, and Katsuragi was simply covering for Yahiko's failure.

Now the problem is this. It's actually a fairly simple deduction if it was just written smarter, since after the Class A students leave they check the cave and see that it was claimed about 5 minutes prior. With no one else in Class A spotted, it had to be Yahiko or Katsuragi who claimed the spot with they key card. Katsuragi is obviously (and very badly) lying when he steps out of the cave, because he says in the same sentence that the two of them have to be cautious with everything they say and do since there could be spies from other classes watching them, AND THEN HE IMMEDIATELY ANNOUNCES THAT HE IS THE LEADER FOR THOSE POSSIBLE SPIES IN QUESTION TO HEAR. Obvious lie -> He's covering for someone, and by process of elimination... -> Yahiko made the claim. Ayanokouji needlessly complicates it by trying to game theory the points system, stating that claiming it instantly is a stupid move, but it isn't.

The spots reset every 8 hours to and have to be reclaimed, granting one class point per claim. Assuming one finds a spot instantly and renews it on the dot, you could get 3 points per day, so delaying a claim could cost you points in the long run for. It's 1 point for not doing it ASAP, and then an additional point for each delay of 8 hours. A shrewd, intelligent person would reasonably quickly claim a spot upon finding it. And there's the obvious incentive of simply claiming it before anyone else, because neither Ayanokouji nor any possible leaders he comes across knows exactly how many spots are on the island or how far off they are, so they don't know when next they might find a spot. Furthermore, if the duo delayed claim and a larger group found the cave spot at the same time, they're in a very bad situation. The larger group could either force the duo to leave and claim the spot, hide their leader and claim the spot, or wait until the duo leaves in a standoff situation, wasting both sides' valuable time. And the duo's options in return are to either claim the spot and tell them to fuck off with their right of exclusive possession granted by claim (but revealing the leader) or give up the free points. It's better to avoid that entirely by quickly making a claim when the coast is clear. Furthermore, he sees a lone group of two out of a class of presumable dozens. They're obviously employing some sort of spot scouting strategy, which naturally incentivizes covering as much ground as possible. The point is that quickly claiming the spot is by no means a dumb move.

**Some actually decent reasoning**: Now I will give Ayanokouji for a deduction that actually impressed me. He figures out the Class A/C alliance from Ryuuen's plan involving a camera. Now, such explicit proof would likely not be required if she was sharing the info with Class C alone - her word of mouth would be good - but other classes would naturally not be so trusting, especially with a class with Ryuuen. So that proof is likely for another class. Since Class A's points hadn't changed at all (besides the initial 30 point loss from Arisu's absence), then they had to be getting their resources elsewhere. Not Class D for obvious reasons, Class B had also lived frugally so it couldn't be them, and it was most likely Class C since they had used up all their points. Good job, Ayanokouji. Ofc it's not air tight, he even admits it's intuition on his part, but it's a far more logical process than his other conclusions.
