Timeframe Fallacy: The 15-Second Claim

The argument that Takuya had 15 seconds of tablet access ignores visual and narrative pacing. The scan evidence (Y2V4) clearly illustrates:

Takuya is physically shoved back immediately after grabbing the tablet.

The next frame places him visually far from Tsubaki, indicating no prolonged dialogue with tablet in hand.

Thus, the 0.25–0.5 second range remains the most canon-consistent interpretation, aligning with human visual processing latency (~250 ms) and Ayanokoji's real-time perception benchmarks. Inflating it to 15 seconds contradicts both pacing and physical action continuity in the LN. You cannot interpret still frames as an extended scene when action cues directly contradict it.

Map Scope and Visualization Are Critically Undersold

Claiming that Takuya only referenced a “50-square-inch map” rather than visualizing the 35 km² terrain ignores the structure of the data displayed:

The tablet renders dynamic GPS overlays, not static maps. These overlays must be mentally reconstructed into geographic space.

Inferring Kitou’s precise route and speed necessitates the spatial mapping of moving vectors in real-world scale. You can’t process route optimization using an abstract symbol set, in which you need to interpret terrain implications (elevation, obstacles, etc.).

This demands VSI (Visual-Spatial Intelligence) well beyond “checking a map.” It's an act of near-instantaneous 3D modeling from a transient glance.
