Suzune lost to Ichinose 7–0. Some say it was 10–0 because of the final display, but that isn’t accurate. To begin with, it isn’t a score—it’s a life counter. Since it’s a life-based system, each life Suzune lost is effectively a point. In other words, Ichinose scored 7 points.

In my view, from 1 to 100%, Suzune’s chances of winning were around 1–3%. Not just for Suzune, but for almost any low-tier, mid-tier, and even high-tier participant—though each with different probabilities. The situation itself was simply too unfair.

The exam doesn’t measure academic knowledge or physical strength; it measures the ability to read gestures, microexpressions, and everyday social dynamics. (EP + EU)

Ichinose has spent two years deeply connecting with every student in her year. She knows their habits, their looks, and how they lie. Meanwhile, Suzune spent 10 years of her life completely isolated. Although she has improved, she has only been socializing for a few months. She is essentially playing in Ichinose’s “home field.”

If Suzune and Ichinose had exactly the same level of skill in reading people, Suzune would lose 100% of the time. Ichinose enters the board as a General with 10 lives, while Suzune, as a Mid Guard, has only 7 lives. To win on equal footing, Suzune would need to be about 30% superior to Ichinose just to compensate for the life gap—something impossible in her weakest area.

Suzune burned her “elite group” to secure victory against Kanzaki. Against Ichinose, the rules force her to use her less capable students—those harder to read or more prone to making mistakes for personal gain (like Shinohara).

Ichinose’s class is cohesive, intelligent, and team-oriented. Suzune’s class is filled with individuals who prioritize private points over collective benefit, giving Ichinose “easy targets” to quickly wear down Suzune’s 7 lives.

Even if a miracle occurred and Suzune, through intuition or luck, managed to individually outperform Ichinose and corner her, Ichinose could use her Traitor privilege—allowing her to communicate with her classmates and gain additional flexibility.

By doing so, Ichinose’s class (which is highly proficient in this type of exam) would effectively become a perfect radar, revealing Suzune’s class roles each round. Suzune would be bombarded with precise information against her own group, destroying her remaining lives.

It was a situation where if she didn’t reach Ichinose’s level, she lost. If she reached Ichinose’s level, she lost. And even if she surpassed her, she still lost. The only ways Suzune could win were:

Betray Kiyotaka and use the Traitor right, reducing his chances of winning if she lost (counterproductive to her main goal: winning the exam).

Stall rounds until she could reuse her elite group—people she knows well (she didn’t have enough time because Ichinose was too strong).

Play with Ichinose’s psychology using information extracted from Kanzaki (Ichinose already knew, making it useless).

And her best strategy:

Reduce Ichinose’s lives as much as possible so Kiyotaka would have an easier time (the path she chose).

Which ultimately meant her defeat.

Her best strategy to win the exam was, paradoxically, to lose.

Holy man…

I estimated a 1–3% chance because it represents chaotic variables beyond strategic control, such as:

Ichinose suffering an unexpected mental or emotional breakdown mid-exam.

Ichinose’s classmates making extremely obvious behavioral mistakes, allowing Suzune to identify Honor roles by sheer luck.

In conclusion, under the exam’s rules and respecting her constraints, Suzune was essentially designed to lose that matchup. Her true “victory” wasn’t winning the battle, but wearing Ichinose down enough for Kiyotaka to step in and finish the exam effortlessly.

And all of this without even considering the many previously mentioned limitations that didn’t even allow her to fight properly or comfortably.

Such as

Many people use the following to belittle Suzune, so before discussing the confrontation, I would like to pause for a moment to list all the limitations and adversity that Suzune is currently facing:

Like everyone else, Suzune faces an inability to plan a strategy; the specific content of the matches is completely unknown. Without being able to narrow down information, it is frankly impossible to devise a strategy or prepare countermeasures in advance to ensure victory. This is something completely fatal for Suzune, who is not a genius like her competitors. Even so, she managed to formulate a battle strategy to face Ichinose. However, despite all the measures and precautions Suzune took to keep Kiyotaka’s role a secret and use him as a surprise factor, the information that he would be the general was leaked to the opposing class due to a traitor within her own class, ruining her strategic advantage before the exam even began. In other words, in a situation where strategies could not be formed, she managed to create one, and it was leaked by a traitor.

Suzune’s class has exactly 38 people. Subtracting the 3 representatives leaves 35 participants. This means that if even a single person is absent due to illness or any reason, they fall below the minimum and will automatically receive a penalty of 5 class points per missing person. Unlike her class (and Sakayanagi’s), the classes of Ryūen and Ichinose have 40 people. This means Suzune’s opponent starts the exam already gaining 10 additional class points simply for existing (thanks to the surplus participant benefit), so Suzune is already losing the exam before they even finish explaining the rules.

With Kiyotaka as the general and intending not to fight, Suzune is forced to assume the role of middle guard. This drastically reduces her margin of error because she must fight with a massive initial disadvantage against Ichinose’s class, being forced to win quickly and take many lives from the opponent to prevent the turn from reaching Kiyotaka. (7 lives vs. 22 lives)

The rules stipulate that the same group cannot be used consecutively; they must rest for one round. This limits Suzune’s tactical options when it comes to repeating lineups that work. This is problematic because, unlike the elite group selected by Suzune, the rest of her class prefers to prioritize their personal gains (the private points granted by executive roles or winning discussions) rather than actively helping Suzune win the special exam.

The representatives cannot leave the waiting room. This restriction on movement between floors and the confiscation of communication devices is intended to cut off any flow of information to participants. Since they are closely monitored, Suzune cannot risk performing any suspicious actions, meaning she cannot communicate with her classmates—leaving them completely on their own.

Suzune was forced to use her best pieces immediately to secure her plan. To face Kanzaki, she had to burn one of her best groups in the first discussion (calm, poker-faced students capable of reading the atmosphere and thinking quickly, such as Sudou, Miyake, or Matsushita). This implies that for subsequent discussions against Ichinose, her available groups will consist of less prepared students.

The content of the participants’ discussion is meaningless to the representatives. Suzune’s only task is to discern roles (such as honor students) based purely on microexpressions, everyday gestures, habits, and suspicious glances through a monitor. This need to evaluate the natural behavior of her classmates is Suzune’s weakest area, as she spent 10 years of her life isolated and only began interacting and socializing with them a few months ago. In contrast, she faces Ichinose, who is widely recognized for mastering this area perfectly.

Originally, the right to designate and interrogate the “Traitor” (which forced a confession under threat of expulsion) was the only path and key weapon Suzune had to fight strategically and balance the scales. When Kiyotaka demanded exclusive use of this resource, he stripped Suzune of her only chance to win through her own strategy, leaving her disarmed and forced to rely solely on her limited individual ability to identify her classmates.

And she wasn’t facing a normal Ichinose. She was not the friend she had met two years ago, not the same person she had formed an alliance with, not the same one she had overcome the Mixed Training Camp with without expulsions, nor the one who had been harassed by rumors. She was a new person thanks to Kiyotaka—someone unknown to Suzune, something Suzune had not yet realized. On the other hand, Ichinose knew her perfectly.

Not to mention her mental state. With no class points to lose, the exam seems safe, but the enormous number of points at stake (250 points if they win) is vital for Suzune’s dream of reaching Class A. This creates nervousness, concern, immense pressure as a leader, and a moderate level of stress that forces her to seek sugar to keep her brain functioning. And this pressure increases with Kiyotaka’s conditions—she would lose her best asset if she didn’t perform perfectly.

And this was not her only mental burden.

The representatives arrived at the building at 8:00 a.m. and had to endure two hours of isolated waiting, absorbing complex rules under an atmosphere of absolute silence before the exam began at 10:00 a.m. The sudden call to action after being confined in the waiting room without information (other than watching their classmates’ lives decrease) created physical strain and an even greater mental burden on her.

Although Suzune defeats Hamaguchi and Kanzaki (12 lives) while maintaining her 7 lives, the flow of the exam is a repetitive and continuous cycle of 5-minute rounds, 1-minute tablet decisions, and short 10-minute breaks. Suzune must use those brief rests not to relax, but to clear her mind of unnecessary thoughts, review the rules, and calculate the risks of her moves—accumulating mental fatigue before the final confrontation.

Not to mention the emotional strain and anger caused by Kanzaki, who humiliated himself by bowing deeply and repeatedly begging her to concede victory due to the desperate situation of Class D.

In short…

She couldn’t plan any advanced plans for the actual special exam.

What little she managed to plan was leaked to the opposing class by a traitor.

If even a single person from her class was absent for any reason, she would automatically receive a point penalty.

Her opponent began the exam with an automatic gain of ten additional class points simply for having more students.

She was forced to assume the role of Mid Guard with a drastically reduced margin of error (7 vs 22).

She had to win quickly and take many of the opponent’s lives to prevent the turn from reaching Kiyotaka.

The rules prohibited her from using the same group of students consecutively.

Most of her classmates prioritized their personal gain of private points over helping her win.

Absolute isolation and the confiscation of phones prevented her from communicating with her (incompetent) classmates to guide them.

She was forced to exhaust her elite group of participants in the prior match against Kanzaki.

She had to face Ichinose using remaining, less prepared student groups.

She couldn’t expect direct help from participant discussions because the content of the discussions lacked meaningful value.

The exam required her to evaluate natural behavior and microexpressions through a television monitor.

She was forced to fight in her weakest area due to her ten years of prior social isolation.

She was facing Ichinose in an area where her opponent possessed absolute and flawless mastery.

She was stripped by Kiyotaka of the “Traitor” right, which was her only strategic tool to balance the scales.

Her opponent perfectly understood her way of acting, while Suzune was unaware of Ichinose’s current true nature.

She carried immense pressure and stress due to the critical need to obtain 250 points to reach Class A.

She bore the mental weight of potentially losing Kiyotaka’s future support if she failed to achieve a perfect victory.

She suffered significant physical and mental exhaustion after enduring two hours of isolation and complete silence before the exam.
