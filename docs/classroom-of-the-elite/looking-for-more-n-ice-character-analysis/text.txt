Character Narrator: To further engage the reader into the environment of the story, the character, and the Aesop of a story, what some do is to have the story be narrated by one of its own characters. That character does the narration, and everything the reader takes in comes directly from his own Point of View. The character is usually also the protagonist, although there are exceptions (such as a First-Person Peripheral Narrator telling the story of a Non P.O.V. Protagonist). This can make the story more challenging to understand since you only get to know what this character thinks: if there are other characters, and there is tension between them and the narrator, not only will you be in the dark about their purpose or morality, but all the info you get about them will be biased. Similarly, for a person who the narrator especially admires, they will be portrayed as much more heroic and generally better than they really are. In some cases, the narration could be even delusive, and the challenge is to understand the actual situation behind your referent's words. Ayanokoji Kiyotaka is the Narrator of Classroom of the Elite. But what's more, while we can see and understand more of him, he's a type of character who gets only harder to understand once you understand more and more since now his actual goals and motives remain a mystery. Cognitive Biases

All the characters within COTE show different forms of cognitive bias. Ayanokoji shows (as stated previously) cognitive bias for example survivorship bias. What Is Survivorship Bias? Survivorship bias (or survivor bias) is a cognitive fallacy in which, when looking at a given group, you focus only on examples of successful individuals (the “survivors”) in the selection process rather than the group as a whole (including the “non-survivors”). In which we can relate to him choosing Kushida over Sakura, his own friend, due to Kushida being more capable. He also didn't mind some people in his class being expelled, so there'd be fewer problems. He also has a self-serving bias. The self-serving bias is defined as people's tendency to attribute positive events to their own character but attribute negative events to external factors. It's a common type of cognitive bias that has been extensively studied in social psychology. For example in Volume 7 when Atsuomi, his father, said that Matsuo died because of him enrolling in ANHS, he immediately in his mind blamed it on the Whiteroom and his father, seemingly also trying to convince himself he's innocent and it's not his fault.
Horikita has the Dunning-Kruger effect, an effect which occurs when a person's lack of knowledge and skills in a certain area cause them to overestimate their own competence. By contrast, this effect also causes those who excel in a given area to think the task is simple for everyone, and underestimate their relative abilities as well. We see Horikita constantly thinking she's special and superior to others, to the point of arrogance and uncooperativeness and even carelessness.
Ryuen shows the Gambler Fallacy (in his consequentialism): which is a mistaken belief about sequences of random events. Observing, for example, a long run of “black” on the roulette wheel leads to an expectation that “red” is now more likely to occur on the next trial. With this, we can easily tie in the "X" search, by which he not only thought he's got the advantage and sets up the scene, he also thought he knows how Ayanokoji thinks and that they are even birds of the same feathers. Just to in Vol 7 have this belief completely shattered by reality, yet he thought if he tried again, and again he'd win one day, and to prevent that, Ayanokoji planted fear into him.
Hirata shows Loss Aversion/Sunk-Cost Fallacy. What is Loss Aversion? Loss aversion is a cognitive bias that describes why, for individuals, the pain of losing is psychologically twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining. The loss felt from money, or any other valuable object, can feel worse than gaining that same thing. Just an example of Friends is easy enough to tie this to Hirata, as Hirata has an enormous amount of friends and classmates and while he is glad to have them, when Yamauchi was expelled he was absolutely saddened by this despite not even being well acquainted with Yamauchi. Of course even more, so we can think about Hirata's childhood friend who is now brain dead as well.
Karuizawa represents Aversion Cascade or also regret aversion, is something that occurs via fear of either commission or omission. In other words, it is the prospect of committing to a failure or omitting an opportunity that we seek to avoid. This obviously is tied to her fear of being bullied again and doing anything to save that, even becoming a parasite living on other's protection.
Kushida represents The bandwagon effect is the tendency for people to adopt certain behaviors, styles, or attitudes simply because others are doing so. More specifically, it is a cognitive bias by which public opinion or behaviors can alter due to particular actions and beliefs rallying amongst the public. She always wants to stay clean and positive for others and doesn't want to go against their needs or wants, opinions etc. She always supports it to not let her image crumble.

A self-insert is a character who is meant to represent the author or the readers, most often in a very idealized sense. Firstly, the more shallow reason: He wants to get back what he missed due to being a part of the White Room.
He feels like he lost something, and he wants to get it, to feel a semblance of balance within his life that had, up until this point, only been dedicated to the White Room. It’s like FOMO (fear of missing out). In a way, it’s not really that he truly desires for the quiet life but that he wishes for it. This is something he laments to himself in Y1 V7, though it is a metaphorical lament:

*Ayano (Narration): The first 'winter' I experienced. It's this cold and somewhere deep down inside, I never thought I'd be able to see this view. There's a song about a dog that saw snow and excitedly ran about. I can empathize.
If it ever snows, I wonder if I'll be just as excited.*
(***Note: Borrow Saadisfy’s words but Snow in Japanese literature symbolizes ‘innocence’ so this means that he wonders if by continuing to try to live this quiet life that he thinks is this innocent life, will be excited about it? Basically his way of asking himself whether or not this is something he’d really be able to enjoy.)
*Ayano (N): I exhaled and recalled the events that transpired today. Meeting with my father, the existence of Chairman Sakayanagi and that the policies of the school don't mean anything. I also achieved much in seeing through Chabashira-sensei's lie. Because with just this alone, I'll be able to make significant progress forward.

Ayano: "...shall I put an end to it?".

Ayano (N): So far I've mostly kept it behind the scenes but the way the exam results are announced, the more Class D continues to prosper, there'll be no avoiding malevolent attention. Inevitably, the surveillance would intensify and you'd be able to investigate who it is that's at the center of it all. As a matter of fact, although I set Horikita as that person in the center, Ryuuen realized that it's faked. Sakayanagi also knows my past and it's only a matter of time before Ichinose starts doubting it as well. If I want to turn back, now's my only chance.*

Notice that in this monologue of his, he’s rationalizing. He’s telling himself what he believes will be the case if he does continue to be ‘X’ and operate behind the scenes, almost in an attempt to prod himself, despite the fact he already knows what he should be doing to ‘win,’ and to get that quiet life. But why does he need to prod if he knows the ‘right way’ to go about the showdown with Ryuuen? Because he’s hesitant and indecisive. And that’s why I call this reason the shallow one.

It’s something he believes he should have been able to experience, untainted by the White Room, not something he truly wants at this moment in time. The fact he ends up saving Kei over preserving this quiet life is proof of this. Because if he did value it over everything else, he wouldn’t go save her, but he does.

Anyways, as I said before, this quiet life was sort of a wish more than a true desire. And the thing about wishes is that we long for them even though they may not be within the realm of possibility for us. So why does he wish for this quiet life, where does the FOMO come from? It comes from his desire to be normal. The fact he fakes his personality in the earlier volumes (especially in Y1 V1 and V2) extends from this; here’s an example:

*Ayano (N): Conclusion: I’d pretend not to hear her. I had dubbed this the “Sleeping Strategy.” If she (Horikita) were a kind girl, then she would say something like, “Aw, well, there’s nothing to be done. I’d feel bad to wake you, so I’ll forgive you. ★” “If you don’t get up, I’ll kiss you!” would also be okay.*

He’s trying to conform to the other students in his class by acting like what he thinks is normal (Note: this is definitely not how a high schooler typically acts but he did have zero anecdotal experience before this so it’s a given he would think based probably on stereotypes he’s seen).
This is his way of trying to live that quiet, ordinary life, by acting in a way that he thinks is normal. “I should feel this way, right?” It’s a question like that that drives him on. But the fact of the matter is he doesn’t actually think these really cringey thoughts like the one I show above. It’s all a facade, something he forces himself to think (probably trying to become this normal high schooler by ingraining habits into himself).
That’s why, despite this quiet life where he doesn’t do anything troublesome, he contradicts himself and helps people. Because at the end of the day, he’s experienced the horrid place that is the White Room. He says it himself, almost in an accepting and self-realizing way (Y1 V7):

*Ayano (N): I am a first year high schooler. I am 16 years old. However, my knowledge far exceeds the amount learnt in a lifetime. I realized it, was made to realize it. That humans have an endless amount of curiosity.*

It’s undeniable that his personality has been morphed by the White Room. It has certainly been a large part of his life, taking up around 14 years of his time and turning him into the “masterpiece” that we see glimpses of time and time again in the story. His time in the White Room may be an impossible feat to forget, and the memories essentially branded into him by it will probably never truly fade (due in part to how much it traumatized him). I’m saying some cruel stuff, but it’s true, I believe.
And it is truths like these that he struggles to bring himself to accept, as he begins his school life (though he does end up overall doing it, as seen in the quote above. Hindsight is 20/20).
In Y1 V1 and V2, he helps people out despite disliking trouble and wanting to live this quiet life. But he rejects this in Y1 V2, affirming to himself that he knows himself (in an instinctive, almost habitual manner) and that he's a terrible person. He doesn't even humor that there could be some truth to what Horikita says, or that there could be more to why he wanted to help people like Horikita.
And then he tells himself so many times he’ll never get over what he experienced within the White Room, like this monologue in Y1 V7.5:

*Ayano: I’d left the White Room physically, but some part of me would always be stuck there, where we lived in a state of constant self-defense. You shouldn’t need your guard up at all times in normal, everyday life. Going out with Satou would’ve let me experience normal joys, a normal relationship…but I couldn’t visualize what “normal” might look like for me yet. I’d worked to protect myself against any opponent who might arise; I didn’t know how to stop. No one else mattered, so long as I won. Maybe I’d be this way until the day I died.*

So how could he enjoy living such a quiet life, ignoring his experiences and what led him to becoming the masterpiece of the White Room? How could he truly want to think, act and feel in a way that he thinks he should be, rather than based on what has morphed him into the person he is, as he enters the school?
This probably seems sort of fucked up of me to say, ‘cause it might sound like I’m trying to denounce him of his ability to become a regular highschooler. But I don’t think he can bring himself to blank out that portion of his life (which the monologue above supports) and live like it never happened (even if it has fucked him up deeply). At least, in the way he thinks he should be living his high school life, by living this quiet life.
This is something that fortune-teller he went to in Y1 V4.5 talks to him about, indirectly telling him to decide his own path (so to not force this quiet life onto himself) and not to be fake:

*Fortune-teller: “Fate tenchuusatsu is certainly rare. However, that doesn’t necessarily mean that your entire life will be marked by misfortune. It’s true that the overall outlook is bad. There are negatives: you won’t have the blessing of your parents or your family. However, the rest is up to you, individually. You alone decide what you can do and can’t do.”
Ayano (N): She had a grim expression on her face, but I could see compassion in her eyes.
Fortune-teller: “You don’t need to be pessimistic, and you don’t need to act like you’re starring in a comedy, either.”*

As I said before, if he genuinely valued such a life, he’d refrain from throwing it away, right? But it's clear from Y1 V7, when he chooses Kei over that quiet life: he truly didn’t want this. He’d rather save someone who he describes as being ‘an irreplaceable existence’ to him over that life. Almost as if a life with such a person, connections with people like Kei, is what he wanted. But I’m getting ahead of myself.
Now for the second reason, which isn’t one that is quite evident unless you fish for clues:
He wants vengeance against his father.
You might be thinking: ok, Mr. distilledeyes, pump the brakes. He certainly says in Y1 V7 that he came to this school out of a self-described “adolescent rebellion” and to choose his own path, as he calls it. And he does show a desire to want his old man to lose by being buried by people such as Sakayanagi or Ryuuen. But that doesn't mean it's also the reason he wanted a quiet life...
But I think it is, at least, up until around Y1 V4.5.
One of the biggest hints is in Y1 V1. As Ayano reflected on the class situation post them losing all their class points and them having to work together for the midterm, he pondered his father's words:

*Atsuomi: "Only a fool wouldn't use his innate abilities."
Ayano (N): These words stuck in my head.
Ayano: "A fool, huh? I wonder if that's what I am after all."*

Why do these words of his father stick in his head at this time in specific? Why does he feel like a fool? (Note: And let me remind you, immediately after this, he ends up helping out Horikita and Kushida to get the class past the midterm, by getting answers from an upperclassman and saving Sudo, using his abilities to help.)
As I mentioned earlier, in Y1 V1 and Y1 V2, Ayano constantly affirmed to both us and Horikita that he wanted a quiet, peaceful life. This manifested in that first excerpt I pulled out, where he said he's getting annoyed because of Horikita's pestering. And then there's the part immediately after that argument, where he told himself that he knew himself better than anyone else, that he's a fool, and that he's a terrible person.
Because of the use of the word fool twice, it sort of clicked to me. He believes his father's words. He thought himself a fool for wanting to hide his abilities (Note: And let me emphasize it was only for that reason, not for wanting to come to the school or any other reason. After all, even later on in the novels, he never really showcases regret for wanting to come here (besides feeling guilty about Matsuo the butler)).
But he still pursues this quiet life despite believing he's being a fool. Why? It is his way of rejecting his father.
By not using the gifts burned into him by his father, by the White Room, he rejects his father's wishes. It would explain why he gets so defensive and irritated with Horikita when she stipulates that he doesn’t truly want this quiet life he says he wants, more so than the first, shallow reason I talked about, though both are valid.
It would also explain why he instinctively told himself things like “I know myself. I’m a terrible person.” After all, he’s his father’s son. Even though he doesn’t see him as a father in anything besides paper, they are genetically linked. He might not be able to disregard his father’s existence completely when assessing how he is as a person, even though they don’t see eye to eye on everything. And as shown from how he thinks about him in key moments (like Y1 V5 when he meets Sakayanagi for the first time where he desires for himself to lose so his old man would, or Y1 V1 for the quote I brought up just now), that man’s existence certainly weighs heavily on him.
His desire for vengeance against his father, by not using his powers as the masterpiece of the White Room, I believe is a key motivator into why he wanted that quiet life, even though it really isn’t explicitly stated. And thus why he comes to the school.
(Note: As to whether or not he hates his father, I think he definitely does (though even that morphs as the series goes on). His rejection of his ideals by wanting himself to lose so his father would lose, him calling him ‘that man’ which is probably his way of not seeing him as a father, his unforgiving nature which I hypothesize could be a byproduct of this hatred for his father, and so on.)
However, I need to clarify. These are his motivations before he enters the school, which hold true up until around Y1 V4.5, up until the scene with the fortune-teller (I think).
After that, things change.

Section 2: The Changing of His Motivations throughout Year 1 and realizing the path he wants for himself (occurs from around Y1 V4.5 to V11):
I said there were two reasons for why he came to this school, but the second one is a bit more of an intangible one. But it’s also the bigger reason why he came to this school: Being able to decide his own path.
Now, a good question is: how is this different from him wanting to live a quiet life? As I tried to illustrate in the first section, Ayano’s desire to live this quiet life was something he thought he wanted. So, at first, he compounded these two reasons into one (meaning he thought that being able to decide his own path = living a quiet life).
But the line differentiating the two shows up more and more as the story progresses, until we reach Y1 V4.5. I’ve already harked on and on about Y1 V1 and V2 showing off his desire to help people, but I really need to emphasize that this desire contradicts that wish of his to live a quiet life.
It depends on the person but some may say that helping a person, especially without any gain to had, is troublesome or not worth the effort. So the fact he does so means he didn’t see it as much of a bother in the moment, or maybe that his desire to help these people around him overwhelmed him and that he may have not been thinking about that quiet life of his during the moment.
So going off the assumption he doesn’t want to live the quiet life, being able to decide his own path… what does that mean for him?
There’s a lot to consider here, because, well, there’s a lot of uncertainty. For starters, let’s talk about his phone call in Y1 V11.5 with Chairman Sakayanagi.
He says, verbatim, to the Chairman that he’s accepted his fate and will go back to the White Room. Now, let me ask you: when did he start to think this? When did he decide that he would not struggle against the White Room? Well, he literally tells us directly:

*Ayano: “After graduating, I will return to the white room and take up the role of the leader. That man has trained me for all these years up to now for this.”
Ayano (N): The moment I step out of this school, the walls protecting me will disappear. If I lived in a cheap apartment, he would attack me at night or use some other method to take me back to the white room.
Chairman Sakayanagi: “So you’ve accepted your fate… And on that basis, you came here.”
Ayano: “That’s the reason why I want to protect these three years to the end.”
Ayano (N): In simple terms, it is like being in a rebellious phase. To reject my father’s orders and do what I wanted.*

So what this means is that, from the start, before he came to the school, he came on the assumption that he would inevitably return to the White Room.
This means that when he says he wants to decide his own path (when he has this basis of returning to the White Room in mind)… It’s not something as simple as being able to escape or defy his father (after all, he’s accepted that he can’t for the foreseeable future) or being able to choose a career path.

So what does being able to decide his own path mean for him? And it comes back to the biggest draw to the character that is Ayanoukouji Kiyotaka, and that is, his real personality. Not the fake shit he puts on or the White Room side, but the one that lurks beneath all that, his genuine thoughts and how he genuinely wants to act.
One of my favorite monologues in the entire series comes in Y1 V4.5, when he's talking to Kei. Here's an excerpt, and the highlighted section is just… well, just read it:

*Ayano: “I want to ask you something. What would you say is the specific difference between me right now, and the usual me?”
Kei: “Usually, you’re this gloomy guy. You don’t talk at all. But now, you’re assertive. You’re being direct. Those traits are polar opposites. The way you talk is different, too. What’s your deal, anyway?”
Ayano: “What do you mean? Don’t people simply act differently depending on who they’re with? Don’t you do the same thing?”
Ayano (N): That was probably the best possible rebuttal, although it didn’t fit exactly. The truth was that it felt like I’d just been born as an individual. When I enrolled in this school, I wasn’t fully formed. I was like liquid, or moldable clay. Developing into a full person took time, and I had yet to completely understand how to interact, or the correct way to express myself.*

You know how people say "The first step towards change is awareness"? This monologue radiates that energy like the sun. What I mean is that this might've been his first step towards discovering himself. He begins to desire to learn more about himself.
(Note: This monologue takes place after the fortune-teller scene, implying that the fortuneteller may have affected him and caused him to change his views on how he thought about himself).
Before this moment, he constantly tried to write himself off as being one way (like how he kinda instinctively calls himself a terrible person), dismissing what might actually be the truth about him. It's weird, especially given how much he puts into trying to understand other people or how he never really ever called anyone a terrible person (he definitely does showcase frustration and cruelty towards others like Sae but I'm not sure if that's out of him genuinely thinking they're terrible people and that they deserve it, especially as that vengeful behavior fades as the story progresses). It's unfair to himself, and it's dehumanizing.
Little mini theory: I think part of the reason why Ayano comes off as an unreliable narrator is because he doesn't like to face the truth, in other words, he lies by omission to the reader and himself. Rationalizing to avoid seeing what may actually be the case. As I stated in my preamble, he’s not a perfect human being. His rationalizations are certainly well-thought out but they may not be fact.
So yadda, yadda, yadda, Ayano starts down his path of self-discovery. Nice, but why do I think that correlates to him being able to decide his own path? Well, because to him, this path of self-discovery is done for the sake of self-actualization. In other words, he starts to want that path of his to go like:
Understanding himself as a person → Figuring out what he wants out of these three years.
By being able to understand how he is as a person, he frees himself from the shackles of the White Room, in a way. Then he can find out what he wants to do at this school, or what impact he wants to leave. It's an idea that builds very quietly, as we see Ayano start to do things that contradict what he thinks he should be doing. The first step of which is saving Kei in Y1 V7.
Now to bring up something I haven't addressed, why exactly does Ayano like to help people? Because he likes to see them grow. Something which he brings up time and time again. But why? Well, there's a couple of reasons for this. First is because of the fact he's trying to achieve a certain goal: to make his old man lose. Excerpt from Y1 V7 incoming:

*Ayano: "I don’t know whether there is salvation for you or not, but I have no plans to give up learning. The methods may be different, but it’s true that this school is raising talents. That’s where my expectations are."
Atsuomi: "What gibberish. You don’t understand what kind of place this school is. This is nothing more than a shack for the mobs. There should be some in your own class I’m sure. Lowlifes without any chance for salvation."
Ayano: "Lowlifes? That’s not true. This is a place where I can find out whether people are equal or not. It’s a pretty interesting policy I think."
Atsuomi: "So you think even worthless people can grow up to stand in the same ring as geniuses?"
Ayano: "That’s my wish."*

So by helping people to grow, he can see whether or not anyone can stand up to other geniuses, like Sakayanagi. In other words, proving that geniuses are formed only as a direct result of being subject to a good environment and not things like genetics, and acting for the goal of winning.
The second reason (which is a bit more speculative) is because this sort of is the impact he wants to leave on people. He wants to be able to help them develop into people capable of taking on the world in a sense.
The reason I think so is because of that same moment in Y1 V1, where he thinks himself a fool for wanting to hide his skills. Why does he think himself a fool? Just because his father may think him to be one by hiding them? No, he must also think so himself. Why? Because being able to be an honest person is something he values. Here’s an excerpt where he echoes that belief in Y1 V6:

*Ayano (N): It was better to be a genuinely kind, helpful person who made mistakes than someone two-faced who bragged about being perfectly good. A kind but flawed individual was more honest than a saintly liar.*

So by choosing not to hide his skills in those first couple of volumes, he desires to express those skills that have been honed into him through his years in the White Room by impacting the people around him. It’s like an artist to a canvas but instead of a canvas, it’s the people around you. It’s both self-fulfilling as it could convince him that maybe that time in the White Room wasn’t worthless, if it led him to this moment where he could help people, and kind, as it is an expression with the purpose of being able to guide someone towards success.
Of course, guiding someone on a path towards any end could be seen as manipulative, but if it’s only done for the sake of putting them on the path to their own success and fulfillment, I dunno if it truly is so bad. After all, by just existing, you impact the others around you, as they have to think about you and respond to you. Just by something as simple as one conversation, you can move a person.
So he slowly starts to help people to grow, prioritizing that over that quiet life (specifically after the Rooftop incident). From helping Kei, to Ichinose (in Y1 V9, when she’s writhing in guilt over her actions), to Hirata (as he moved around aimlessly in Y1 V11). And of course, Horikita, throughout all of that.
And this desire slowly builds up, subconsciously. And it manifests beautifully into one of my favorite scenes in the entire series, which occurs in Y1 V11, in Chapter 5, Part 5, as he converses with Horikita Manabu. I’m just putting the whole thing because very it’s great:

*Manabu: “But, if…”
Ayano (N): The elder Horikita stopped walking for a moment and fixed his eyes on me. For some reason, I found myself getting caught up in his powerful gaze and stopped walking as well.
Manabu: “If Suzune could stop chasing after my shadow, break away from her dependency, and become honest with herself…”
Ayano (N): A Spring breeze blew through the air.
Manabu: “She would surpass me altogether, and probably become someone you wouldn’t be able to ignore.”
Ayano (N): He wasn’t saying this just because he was doting on her as an older brother. He truly meant it. In a lot of ways, I also admired Horikita’s high potential. Why was that, though? Was it because of what he just said?
Ayano (N): Suddenly, a thought crossed my mind. What was I supposed to be doing here, at this school? No, what did I want to be doing? I felt like I had suddenly found the answer to that question.
Manabu: “But in the end, it all depends on whether she can make the change at all.”
Ayano: “She will change.”
Ayano (N): I responded to him confidently.
Ayano: “Or, no, let me rephrase that.”
Ayano (N): But then, I chose to correct myself.
Ayano: “I’m going to make her change. Not in the same way I’ve gone about it so far, but for real this time.”
Manabu: “…Oh? I never thought you’d say such a thing.”
Ayano (N): It felt like this chance encounter with the elder Horikita would leave a large impact on my life. It would be a very long time before I knew whether that premonition proved to be true or not.*

And this is the moment where he consciously realizes what is the path he wants for himself. In other words, what impact he wants to leave on this school. And he confidently verbalizes this to Manabu.

He wants to help people grow. As to why, I’ll be getting into that in the next section.

Section 3: Enacting his path (Y1 V11 to Y2 V7):

So this section is going to be quite speculative as we still don’t have clear answers as to how exactly he plans to help Horikita (and the others, I’d presume).
But even so, there’s still a lot of information we can go off of.
So, before I get to that open-ended question I left at the end of the last Section, let’s start off with another question: Why does he want to help people grow?
Before, I speculate he does so out of a desire for vengeance for his father, and a form of self-expression. Is that still the case? Kind of.
I do think it is still out of a desire for a form of self-expression and fulfillment. But I think the vengeance thing might not really be a factor, or that it fades throughout Year 2. Why?
Well, cause, firstly, he sort of doesn’t really talk about his father much in these volumes or his ideas. This might be a little bit of a cheap answer but this could be an intentional move from the author.
So the fact he doesn’t think about his father’s opinions, wants to reject them and doesn’t think of parallels between his father and himself anymore is the author indirectly telling us that hatred he has for his father isn’t what is driving him anymore. To make a comparison that is easier to understand, let’s say you had a toxic ex.
They fucked you up, you guys broke up, and you can’t stop thinking about them, both because of how they fucked you up, and also because you probably wished for things to be different or for them to have expressed things differently. But then, you start to do more things, experience more things, and you come to appreciate what exactly these things mean to you, and that your ex’s opinions don't define you and shouldn’t restrain you. And at the same time, you probably analyze your ex, realizing what may have led them to think the way they did. You start to hate them less, viewing them not as some terrible, irredeemable that can never be forgiven but as another human. Even if the shit they did felt inhumane (And well, if it was genuinely inhumane, they should probably be jailed). It’s all for the sake of your own growth, because you start to value yourself more, not exactly more than them but just enough that you feel like you are also another person that exists within the world. Who deserves to be able to find things that matter to you.
So what am I trying to say? Well, basically I’m trying to compare Ayanopapa to a toxic ex… God, I let my mind run wild sometimes.
Anyways, the summary of it all is he’s found something more important than vengeance, than his father. Inciting hope rather than despair and hatred.
You can sort of sense this by viewing his interactions with people who he has directed his vengeful nature towards in the past and seeing how his behavior around them has changed. Namely, Sae-sensei.
To say the least, throughout most of Year 1, he was pretty merciless towards her. But that changes in Year 2, and you can see glimpses of it, especially in Y2 V5 in the last chapter, where he conceptualizes her growth, and recognises her as his teacher:

*Sae: “If you’re interested……would you be willing to listen to my confession?”
Ayano: “The Sacrament of Reconciliation it is. I’m not much of a religious person, but if that’s fine with you then go ahead.”
Ayano: “No problem. Then, I’ll leave now, Chabashira-sensei.”.
Ayano (N): I’ve been calling her that lately, but I said it with emphasis. She will be fine now. Through this special exam, she has grown as much as the students. Her mind, which had been stuck in the third year of high school, had quickly begun to catch up to her current age. Her mind, which had been stuck in the third year of high school, had quickly begun to catch up to her current age.*

So his vengefulness fades. But he still wishes to deny his father of his ideals, indirectly. Indirectly, as in the story, it’s solely because it’s an idea he doesn’t like. So he wants people, people who have had a normal childhood, to beat him. Which would thus deny the White Room’s existence, in an ideological sense.

My evidence for this is his monologue at the end of Y2 V6:

Horikita: “Of course, you’re one of them. I don’t know what you’re thinking, and you’re uncooperative in many ways, but... you’re indispensable to the class and to me.”

*Ayano (N): I’m like training wheels on a bicycle. At first, it’s indispensable, but then it comes off, falls down, and shakes repeatedly, and eventually you learn to ride it without difficulty. There is no single person who can support your back as you pedal your bike. Your classmates will support you. And after watching you grow for a little while longer… I’m leaving your class. I’m not going to say why yet, but I’m sure Horikita will find out why sooner or later. And then… There will come a time when the class you thought invincible will meet a reality you can’t beat. I will teach you that. For myself, and no one else. I’m fine as long as I’m winning. If I decide to be the enemy and defeat Horikita, that’s a done deal. But I’m leaving because I want to be defeated. There is a future that I hope is uncertain. The answer is already there, but there is a contradiction within me that I deeply want the answer to be different.*

So by guiding them, helping those individuals to grow, he plans to create a classroom capable of defeating him.
Of course, he says stuff like “If I decide to be the enemy and defeat Horikita, that’s a done deal.” But his line after, “there is a contradiction within me that I deeply want the answer to be different” is clearly his desire, his dislike for his method to victory: a meticulous way of winning no matter the cost. So that would be his reason for why he wants to help people, and why helping people grow is both the way he is able to leave his impact within the school (and by extension the world) and the way he can achieve his goal of proving this ideal he dislikes wrong.
So he goes along with this idea in mind, helping key members who are essential to the class to grow so that the class can become capable of beating him and also so his impact is felt. Pretty epic. And this also results in the heartbreaking scene that occurs within Y2 V5, where Sakura is expelled.
I bring up this scene because I think it is like a “point of no return” sort of moment to his plans. In that, although he’s been following through with this idea to build up the class to being amazing, he may not have been sure of how far he would go. This might have been why he had never opted for people who were “holding the class back” to be expelled before, like Sudo. After all, he certainly believes in the students of the class's potential, even Sakura’s (here’s an excerpt from Y2 V7):

*Ayano: “It's true what I said about you being protective; Airi may indeed have been a handful, but she started growing at an incredible rate after she was expelled from school.”*

So why does he switch gears and start to adopt that policy of “cutting off the weakest members of the class” that he talks about? Because he begins to fully embrace the idea of letting the class grow, grow into a way that is true to all of them.
