COTE UIE Analysis

(Beware the big wall of text coming up.)

The Y1V3 island exam is one of the most well known and most analyzed arc of COTE.

However, some aspects of it are sometimes misunderstood. So, in this post I will try and offer some insights into the Y1V3 strategy and also how it affects the future volumes as well.

The first thing, is that people often assume Kiyotaka got lucky throughout the volume. Such as when he encountered Katsuragi and Yahiko coming from the cave spot, which allowed him to identify Class A's leader. While, you can make an argument that the timing was a bit too convenient, chalking up the whole thing to luck isn't completely correct either.

Because, Kiyotaka had also picked up on the clues the school had given and made his way to the cave spot almost immediately after Class D had decided on searching the island. This is also shown in the volume itself -

>“Yeah. First of all, I volunteered to join the search for the base camp. Able to move freely, I planned to search out spot locations **ahead** of everyone else.”

The timing might have been too convenient, but it makes complete logical sense, since both Katsuragi and Kiyotaka identified the importance of the cave spot and made their way to it as quickly as possible. So, it would only make sense if they ended up encountering each other, which was exactly what ended up happening.

Another thing that was often seen as something Kiyotaka lucked out on, was Horikita getting sick. Kiyotaka had planned on swapping out the leader by having Horikita retire from the exam. This happened because Horikita was too sick, and after her fight with Ibuki, was so exhausted to the point of collapsing unconscious.

But is that really the case? We know that retirement doesn't necessarily have to happen because the person is sick. Koenji retired even though he was fine. Ryuen's entire class retired, even though they were partying about. So, a student can retire, even if they are completely fine.

This might seem a bit too lenient. However, the LN clearly points out that the theme of the exam is freedom. The only restriction was the 30 point penalty applied to the class. This would be more than enough of a deterrent. But, it ultimately depends on the students. If they want to retire, they have the freedom to do so. If they don't want to retire, they also have the freedom to do so.

So, Kiyotaka can still make Horikita retire, even if she wasn't sick. Which means Horikita being sick or not doesn't affect the success rate of the actual strategy.

And, this is where we get to the most overlooked part of the Y1V3 strategy (imo at least). Kiyotaka had around 3 ways to take first place by an overwhelming margin.

1)The first method was what he did in the canon. He allowed Ibuki to find Horikita's leader identity, provided her with the opportunity to both steal the key card and escape. Horikita, in turn, chased after Ibuki. But due to her condition, she ended up collapsing at the end, allowing for Kiyotaka to retire her from the exam.

This method allows for Horikita's growth. Horikita, till this volume, believed that her placement in Class D was a mistake and that she was a superior student who didn't need others. That was why she never admitted the fact that she was sick to anyone - because she believed she can win on her own and didn't need anybody else. That was also why she didn't entrust the key card to anybody else while taking a shower in the river. And why she ran after Ibuki on her own to "rectify" the mistake she believed she had committed.

So, when Horikita ultimately failed, she would start to realize her flaw. That perhaps if she had someone to depend on, this whole situation could have been avoided. However, despite this realization, Horikita was entirely uncertain on how to make allies. That's why Kiyotaka had reached out to her, offering to be her first ally, allowing her to grow by guiding her.

>“That’s difficult advice... No one would want to be with me.” It sounded like she’d resigned herself to unhappiness. Perhaps there was a trace of masochism in her. I laughed.

>I looked down at Horikita, who lightly groaned against my chest. “If you can’t fight by yourself, it’s better to fight with a partner. I’ll lend you a hand.”

However, this method also results in an issue. This issue being the fact that it gives Ryuen enough evidence to conclude with high possibility the existence of an mastermind behind Class D.

How? Because, Ryuen saw with his own two eyes that Horikita was the leader of Class D. However, the results show that Class D had come out on top by an overwhelming margin, while both Class A and Class C had done far more poorly than anticipated. So, Class D's leader must have been changed sometime before the exam had ended.

With how everyone is praising Horikita and crediting her with the victory, the obvious conclusion is that Horikita is the one who came up with the idea to perform a leader swap. But, Ryuen noticed many things that conflict with this idea -

* If Horikita was aware of the leader swap, then why would she chase after Ibuki and put herself in such a dangerous situation? If she wanted to deceive Ibuki into thinking she had pulled one over Class D, then merely chasing after her for a while and pretending to lose track of her would be enough. Especially given the weather conditions and environment. So, if Horikita was aware of the leader swap, she put herself in danger for absolutely no reason.
* Also, Horikita due to putting herself in danger had ended up collapsing. But to retire, at the very least she had to go back to the base camp to inform Chabashira or directly go to the starting point. However, in her physical condition, that would practically be impossible. She would need the assistance of someone else. If Horikita really did come up with the leader swap, why would she put herself in such a horrendous position, where she needs someone else to help her retire in the first place.
* Finally, when Horikita was chasing after Ibuki, she didn't immediately address the fact that Ibuki stole the key card. This was because Horikita wasn't a 100% certain Ibuki was the thief. Because, if she was, why would she need to steal the key card? Just taking a peek would be enough. Besides stealing is an heavily punishable offense and would immediately alert Class D. Also, when the manual was burnt, Ibuki was just as surprised at the fire. If she set it up, there would be no reason to continue staying there. Due to these reasons, Horikita wasn't completely sure Ibuki stole the key card. So, she kept it vague, asking for Ibuki to return what she stole, instead of specifying what she stole.
* Ryuen knows that he was guessed as the leader of his class, because his class ended up with 0 points. And since Class D came out on top, they must have guessed him as the leader. Since, Ryuen kept himself hidden on the island extremely well, the only way they could have known he was the leader, is if they knew Ibuki was a spy. After all, Ibuki was in contact with Ryuen the whole time. So, whoever was behind Class D's win, knew Ibuki was a spy far ahead of time and intentionally set that fire to help her escape. But, Horikita wasn't certain Ibuki was the spy until Ibuki slipped up and admitted to stealing the key card.

From the above points we can see that the main reason for Ryuen suspecting the existence of a mastermind behind Class D, was because Horikita chasing after Ibuki makes absolutely 0 sense if she was indeed the mastermind.
So, this first method allows for Horikita's growth, but allows Ryuen to identify the existence of the mastermind.

2)The second method was to simply NOT let Horikita chase after Ibuki. Kiyotaka would have to convince Horikita ahead of time, which can be done easily. For instance, he can bring Horikita to the place where Ibuki had hidden the walkie-talkie or even lay out the idea behind the leader swap. At this point in time, Horikita was already aware that Kiyotaka was quite competent. So, with enough evidence he can easily convince Horikita not to chase after Ibuki.

As established before, a person can retire from the exam even without needing a genuine reason. So, Horikita's condition doesn't even have to be severe enough to retire.

What changes would happen, if Kiyotaka had followed this method over the canon first method? The first method would have be far more effective to help Horikita grow compared to this one. However, a big change would be, Ryuen finding it far more difficult to identify the existence of a mastermind.

He wouldn't have had the necessary clues/evidence to conclude whether a mastermind was behind Horikita or not. He would have no reason to think Horikita couldn't have come up with the leader swap. Especially, since from his perspective she was also the person that came up with the fake camera move to force his class to back off during the Sudo trial in Y1V2.

3)The third method would have been to NEVER even let Ibuki find out who the leader was. The entire reason why Ibuki found out Horikita was the leader, was because Kiyotaka intentionally dropped the card, catching her attention. If he had NOT done that, Ibuki wouldn't have been able to identify Class D's leader.

What would be the results in this case? Since Ryuen wouldn't have never learnt Class D's leader identity, he wouldn't have incorrectly guessed Horikita. The same applies to Katsuragi. So, both Class A and C gain +50 points. However, Horikita wouldn't have had to retire either, so +30 points. Besides, Kiyotaka had to miss the roll call at 8PM in the canon, due to having to retire Horikita. This wouldn't have happened here, which means the penalty would never have happened, so +5 points.

Ultimately, the results would have been -
Class A - 170 points
Class B - 140 points
Class C - 50 points
Class D - 260 points.

In the canon, the results were -
Class A - 120 points
Class B - 140 points
Class C - 0 points
Class D - 225 points.

Class's A and C only close the gap with Class D by a mere +15 points in this third method. While, Class D would have gained more class points in general and would have had a more dominating win too (with a 90 point difference with the 2nd place compared to 85 point difference in the canon). So, Chabashira would have pretty much just as, if not more, impressed with Kiyotaka.

Another benefit, besides not changing the class point results all that much, is that Ryuen wouldn't have been able to detect the presence of Kiyotaka. Since he wouldn't have had the necessary clues to conclude the possibility of an hidden mastermind.

But, in this method, again Horikita wouldn't grow as much.

Of course, this is assuming that Kiyotaka would have been able to convince Horikita regarding his knowledge of the leader's of Classes A and C. But, I believe he can pretty easily do that. Because, Sakura had been at the cave spot alongside Kiyotaka, to verify his story (though their conclusions were different). Besides, Kiyotaka can still show the walkie-talkie that Ibuki was hiding or even the camera in her bag. So, convincing Horikita would be easy enough.

So, from this analysis we can see that the 1st method's main gain is making Horikita grow. While the 2nd and the 3rd method, would have be better in hiding the existence of a secret mastermind. However, an argument can be made that both the 2nd and the 3rd methods are superior because making Horikita grow could happen even in the future and through various other means aside from  just breaking her down. While, the moment Ryuen grew suspicious, reversing his suspicions would have be very difficult, if not impossible.

Now, why did I do all this yap for? To show that Kiyotaka had other methods, arguably better methods, he could have used? Is this a Kiyotaka downscale? Actually, no. It is the opposite.

Because, there is a bigger picture at play here - the X strategy. The basic idea of X strategy was to paint the idea that the mastermind is extremely paranoid and avoids any level of attention. By planting this idea in Ryuen, Kiyotaka can make him use some wacked up strategies (like the rooftop), which would have been effective against the mastermind (based on the idea that Ryuen had), while actually not so effective against Kiyotaka.

But to achieve this, first Kiyotaka has to make sure Ryuen even knows a mastermind exists. The 2nd and 3rd methods, prevent that from happening. So, for the X strategy to work as effectively as it did, the 1st method is the ideal method to follow. It makes Ryuen realize a mastermind exists, creates the idea that they dislike attention (which is developed upon in the future volumes) and also aids Horikita's growth, all in one go.

This, in turn means, the UIE is part of the X strategy. In other words, contrary to what most people think, X strategy actually begins from Y1V3, not Y1V4.

Another bonus of following the 1st method is that it causes a lot of problems to Class D (on the surface), like the manual burning incident or Horikita's retirement. These issues had caused Hirata to grow very disoriented, seeing Class D unravel right in front of his eyes.

This is important. Because, once the exam ends and the results are revealed, Hirata realizes that Class D came out on top solely because of Horikita (from his POV). So, this would make him realize that getting Horikita on his side, as one of the leaders of Class D, is the only way forward to keep Class D afloat.

Since Kiyotaka had planned on making Horikita grow, which includes getting her more allies (like previously mentioned above), getting Hirata to work together with Horikita achieves that goal. This is why we see at the start of Y1V4, Hirata wants to immediately get Horikita on his side.
