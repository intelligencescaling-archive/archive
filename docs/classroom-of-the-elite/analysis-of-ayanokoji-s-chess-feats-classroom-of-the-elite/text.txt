Analysis of Ayanokoji's chess feats

I still often hear: "Ayanokoji's chess feat is one of the most incredible in CPI", "Ayanokoji has calculated several billion chess moves", "Ayanokoji has an ELO of more than 3000 and destroys Magnus Carlsen, Garry Kasparov, and Bobby Fischer in chess", and so on. For some reason, many people are convinced that Ayanokoji's feat in Volume 11 is an indication that he is some kind of super-genius in chess. Today, I want to delve deeper into this topic and explain why this feat is not particularly impressive.

To begin with, I want to note something for everyone who has not read COTE, AyanoKoji fans often say: "Koji was able to beat a supercomputer in chess." First, he never beat a supercomputer, in the 11th volume during a chess match with Sakayanagi, he simply made a move that the computer could not calculate. Second, the novel never mentioned the word "supercomputer", it simply said about a chess engine that the computer could not calculate. And thirdly, some of you might have heard from Koji's fans: "In Volume 0, he was able to beat a chess engine that could think of billions of moves per second," if you hear something like that, know that the person telling you this has not read COTE. There is no mention in Volume 0 that Koji played against an extremely powerful chess engine.

Some people might say, "Okay, Ayano-Koji didn't actually beat a chess supercomputer. But he was able to make a move that the computer couldn't predict. He had to consider millions of different moves, which is impressive." But... no. While this is impressive, there have been instances in history, especially recently, where a chess player has been able to make a move that a chess engine couldn't anticipate. For example, here's a video of a puzzle that humans can solve but Stockfish can't:

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5AbNBY0GfOc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5AbNBY0GfOc)

In my opinion, a chess player doesn't need to calculate millions of different combinations to do something like this.

A huge problem is that the novel doesn't describe the chess match between Sakayanagi and Ayano-koji, so we're told that they made moves at an incredible speed (once every 5-20 seconds), but it doesn't feel particularly impressive. If you watch a few blitz games between grandmasters, you'll realize that this feat from COTE isn't that extraordinary. Some people might say, "But there's an anime!" In it, we were shown a game between Sakayanagi and Ayano-koji!" Yes, the game was shown, but it was created in a terrible way. Instead of consulting professionals or using an existing game between grandmasters, the anime creators decided to create their own game, and it turned out to be terrible! There was a moment where Sakayanagi could have defeated Kiyotaka in three moves. Here is an analysis of the game, if anyone is interested:

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AB7P0-Qe1Ic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AB7P0-Qe1Ic)

If you want examples of humans beating computers, here's a link to a game between Hikaru Nakamura and Stockfish. Hikaru exploited a bug in the program, which refuses to accept a draw (the only problem is that the video is in Russian):

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mO1BbTOhDZQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mO1BbTOhDZQ)

Or here's a video of Magnus beating a cheater in chess:

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rMTcawGzbOk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rMTcawGzbOk)

In conclusion, I can say that Ayano-koji is not a chess genius! He would lose to any modern grandmaster, let alone geniuses like Magnus, Kasparov or Fischer.
I understand that the analysis is not very detailed. Most of what I wrote is something that many of you already know, but I just started getting interested in chess again and decided to analyze COTE.
