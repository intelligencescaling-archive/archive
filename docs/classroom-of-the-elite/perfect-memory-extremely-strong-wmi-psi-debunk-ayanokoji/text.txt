PMH = extremely strong WMI & PSI debunk

Many people argue if PMH is valid or not, we'll go by the assumption that it is true and valid.

I'll go over some points and debunk the notion that PMH= broken CPI (PSI+WMI) as it doesn't hold true.

To map out the perfect memory of kiyo we'll have to use the closest real world equivalent, which is Hyperthymesia or Highly Superior Autobiographical Memory (HSAM)

First, let's go over how regular memory works:

1. Encoding: Information enters the hippocampus where it's temporarily held there, what effects how strong the memory there will be is the emotional salience (how much emotionally it matters to you) and attention. This is short term memory.

2. Consolidation: The process of consolidation is the transferal of memories from the hippocampus (Short-Term memory) to the neo-cortex (Long term memory)

3. Retrieval stage: After this process, the memory is essentially "crystallized" into your mind and can be remembered for a long time.

Now what does hyperthymesia or HSAM do? In short, they process it differently, they have differences in the caudate nucleus and temporal lobe which are crucial for memories, they also have stronger emotionally connection to their personal experiences (Emotional salience plays a huge role) and way stronger attention as they often obsessively rehearse memories (which by consequence makes them more vivid and less forgetful) and also have automatic memory encoding as even without trying, they encode even mundane details with extreme fidelity.

Why It's NOT high WMI:

Working Memory is vastly different (Not to mention that people with HSAM or hyperthymesia don't usually have strong WMI), working memory is the ability to hold independent information and variables in your working memory and in direct line of your attention while also having the capacity to manipulate them.

WMI has a limited capacity (Not 7±2) of 3 to 5 (on average) and only lasts a few seconds to minutes, it can of course be consolidated into long term memory, but not always, I can remember a 6 digit number but I'll likely forget it the next day.

Not to mention WMI relies on the Pre-frontal Cortex while Perfect memory more so relies on the standard memory consolidation (encoding-consolidation-retrieval).

Why it's NOT high PSI:

first, PSI refers to the ability to process visual or symbolic information efficiently and accurately, under time pressure, to test it you typically have to do:

Symbol Search: Quickly identify if target symbol appears in a set.

Coding (Digit-Symbol): Match numbers to symbols as fast as possible.

And amongst other things like psychomotor speed.

It actually more correlates to stronger WMI (Better PSI≈better WMI) not long term memory because it relies on a fundamental different process, the information is processes in a different part, it's stored in a different, it bears no significant correlation.

Remember, in HSAM and Hyperthymesia, only Autobiographical memory is exceptional, WMI and everything else isn't like that. (Citations for this statement at the end)

And even if kiyopon could also perfectly remember information (semantic memory), it still wouldn't necessarily mean extremely high PSI & WMI, because they are simply too different.

Overall I hope I did a decent job at explaining it, PMH to my extent of knowledge does not equal to high CPI (WMI+PSI). That's not to explain kiyo doesn't have really high CPI, it only means that PMH is irrelevant to those subcats of FSIQ.
