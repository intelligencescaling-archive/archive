VR OBSERVATION FEAT

Ayanokoji is inside a full 360-degree VR Tokyo with moving crowds, layered audio, depth, and motion, while suffering motion sickness that disrupts vestibular balance. Despite that, he must detect unnatural points, meaning errors subtle enough that they are not obvious visual glitches but violations of realism, behavior, physics, or timing. Because pain is the correction method, failure is not allowed. This forces nonstop observation without rest, blinking minimization, and sustained precision.

What he is doing cognitively

He is running three processes at once.
Global scanning. His eyes sweep a 360-degree environment while maintaining a mental map of everything already seen. Humans normally hold 3 to 5 tracked objects. He is holding dozens. Local detail inspection. He zooms into individuals and objects to check micro-features like gait rhythm, posture shifts, reaction delay, and movement smoothness.

At age fourteen, this observation feat becomes fundamentally different from the same task at age five, not because the environment changes, but because the way his brain handles information has been radically compressed through nine uninterrupted years of White Room conditioning. The key shift is that he no longer perceives the scene as a collection of separate details. Instead, he recognizes structured patterns instantly. Human movement, spacing, posture, timing, and environmental consistency are no longer processed one by one. They are processed as unified packets. This drastically reduces cognitive overhead while increasing accuracy. On top of that, his attention is no longer evenly distributed. He anticipates where irregularities are statistically most likely to appear, meaning most of his processing power is focused on high value zones rather than wasted on neutral background information.

By fourteen, his reaction window also tightens. The fear conditioning, punishment based correction, and repetition push perceptual decision making well below average adult reaction times. He is not reacting after the fact. He is recognizing and validating anomalies almost as soon as the visual input stabilizes. This allows him to scan dense urban scenes in fractions of a second without panic, hesitation, or loss of detail. The result is not superhuman vision, but superhuman interpretation speed. His eyes remain human. His brain is simply no longer bottlenecked by hesitation, indecision, or sequential processing.

What makes this feat extreme is that it is sustainable. This is not a burst of focus or a lucky moment. It is a trained baseline state. He can repeat this level of observation continuously under stress, motion sickness, and threat of punishment. That consistency is what elevates the feat from impressive perception to elite level cognitive processing. By fourteen, the same task that once required conscious effort has become automatic, faster, and far denser in informational output.
