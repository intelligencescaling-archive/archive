1. Visualization part
1.1 Zones
The island is shown in Y2V3 as being 24,850,000m², Y2V3 p60: "Overall, the map has been divided into 100 zones, each extending 500 metres vertically and 700 metres horizontally." Map below:
So 500 * 700 * 100 = 35,000,000m², but there are 29 zones in the water, and 100-29 = 71, so 500 * 700 * 71 = 24,850,000m². On this island there are 3 school years, each with 4 classes of a maximum of 40 pupils (there are a few expulsions), so 40 * 4 * 3 = 480 pupils. There's also a lot of jungle, mountains, etc... on this island... "see the island in three dimensions by making realistic projections in your mind" an island of 24,850,000m² with 480 people on it and features such as a dense jungle and a huge mountain.
Let's calculate more precisely how many zones Arisu would need to visualize.

* 21 black zones are completely covered by water.
* 17 yellow zones are not completely covered by water. ~3 zones in total cover land. So, 17 - 3 = 14.
* 6 red zones cover mountains where students wouldn't walk due to the high risk of death.
* 6 red-yellow zones cover mountains and land.~2 zones in total cover land. So, 6 - 2 = 4.
* ~1 zone covers other impassable areas (like lakes and additional mountains).
To sum up, that's 21 + 14 + 6 + 4 + 1 = 46 zones Arisu does not need to visualize.
Therefore, she would need to visualize about 54 zones, or 18,900,000 m².
1.2 GPS signals
Now, let's correct the number of students she needs to track.
Wanoxx considered every student as an independent person moving on their own. This is why he consistently refers to 480 students or 480 points on the map.
In reality, most students walked in groups and rarely split up. This is because a student can only see their own position on the map for free. Locating group members after splitting costs 1 point. Furthermore, Wanoxx claimed it was nearly impossible for students to intercept each other even within the same area, making splitting even more dangerous. Additionally, some tasks required the entire group to participate. Consequently, most groups moved together to zones and tasks throughout the exam. In the novel, we rarely see a single student from a group acting alone (exceptions are students like Ichika or Hosen, who operated independently while still belonging to the same group).
There were 157 groups at the start of the UIE, but some merged in the later days, meaning the effective number of groups was even lower.

(example of 128 groups of students)
This means we are not dealing with 480 individual points on the map, but with less than a third of that and it will be clusters of points representing groups. This significantly reduces the mental tracking load. Therefore, calculations involving GPS data should be reduced more than threefold.

Let’s analyze the sentence about the one "who was behind the overall strategies as they moved about the map" more deeply (let’s call it S2).

So, Arisu wants to find the leader by comparing positions before and after her 30-minute break. The thing is, the previous sentence about "who and how they were moving" (let’s call it S1) is almost unrelated to that task. For S1, she has to make two GPS searches in a short time (that's the only way to identify who was moving and how). But to complete the task in S2, she only needs one GPS search before the break and one after. Then she simply compares the two GPS results to find possible leaders. This means S1 and S2 have little in common and should be considered almost different methods for achieving her two goals: blocking the first-year groups (1) and finding the leader (2).
Let’s summarize. To block the first-year groups, she needs two GPS searches made within a short period. To find the leader, she needs one (any) GPS search from S1 and the GPS search after the break.
She can have access to the students' previous positions (i.e., from before the 30-minute break), so she doesn't need to keep them in her mind for half an hour. How?
Well, is it time to show the alternative way Arisu could analyze paths and find the leader?
Screenshots
Before Kinugasa presented Arisu’s strategy and her ability to realistically visualize, he also mentioned Kiyotaka using screenshots to track student movements and patterns (like Ichika's or Nagumo's).
“After running the GPS Search, I took a high-quality screenshot of the results and saved it. This way, I can study the images during my spare time and slowly analyze the movements groups made that day whenever I want.”
While we couldn’t send messages or photographs to anyone during the exam, saving screenshots to our own device was a built-in feature of our tablets, so it was only natural that we could make use of it. By repeatedly scrutinizing the screenshots, it was possible to keep a detailed, persistent record of every single student’s position.
“By thoroughly cross-referencing the images, I’m left with what’s essentially a record of an entire day’s worth of movement that can be viewed at any time.”
(Of course, Arisu should use not her own tablet for that, but someone else’s. However, this isn't a problem, as she told Kiyotaka that Class A's points are under her control and some groups can spend points on a GPS search.)
Let’s summarize what this feature can change:
1. Memorization of GPS points. Arisu could have saved earlier GPS screenshots, reducing the need for perfect recall in any mentioned process, such as constructing paths or finding the leader. While she still needs to associate points with identities, the visual data could be stored externally, not in her mind.
2. 3D visualization of the island. She still constructed a 3D mental model of the island, so nothing changes here.
3. Visualization of paths. Arisu can use screenshots she took over one or several days, analyze them, and only then design possible paths in her mind.
4. Comparison of positions to find the leader. She can simply compare two screenshots (before and after the break) to identify students who didn't move.
Conclusion
The text doesn’t state exactly what Arisu used to visualize paths or find the leader. Kinugasa only mentioned that Arisu visualized paths and can see the island three-dimensionally. So, there are two possibilities:
1. She used her mind to remember every GPS search over one or several days to visualize paths. She used her mind to compare two GPS searches to find the leader.
2. She used screenshots and designed possible paths in her mind. She used two screenshots to find the leader.
As we can see, the second option is significantly easier to execute. It still requires considerable VSI to visualize paths and predict possible student movements, but nothing unnatural. This second interpretation doesn’t require assumptions about Arisu having insane VSI or SPI.
The existence of screenshots almost nullifies the feat if we choose this option over Wanoxx’s.
If we still follow the first option, we must consider the corrections made earlier that limit the feat’s scope in Part 1 and Part 2. Also, we would have to admit that Arisu has insanely poor resource management, because comparing two screenshots with hundreds of data points (positions, names) directly and doing it in her mind are completely different tasks requiring vastly different amounts of mental resources. That's like using a sledgehammer to crack a nut.
While Arisu’s feat is undoubtedly exceptional, Wanoxx likely overstates it by relying on mistranslations and ignoring plausible simplifications.
Which interpretation to choose is up to you.
