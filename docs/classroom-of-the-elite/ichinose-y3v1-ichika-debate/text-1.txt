"Let’s start with Ichika’s performance. It was terrible.

Xorpaw tries to argue that it was good because "she was perceptive enough to realize that Honami and Kiyotaka had engaged in sexual relations before their third year," but that is incorrect.

The narrator explicitly clarifies that Ichika had no proof—and didn't care whether it was true or false—and that she threw out the accusation at random, purely as a crude provocation to see if Ichinose would falter and drop her "good girl" persona. In fact, Ichika is genuinely shocked when Ichinose doesn't deny it. So, Ichika was not at her best here; this diminishes the feat, as her opponent wasn't even taking her seriously.

There are also many strange interpretations. For instance, at one point, it’s suggested that Ichinose’s failure to deny the physical relationship might have stemmed from a moral commitment to honesty. Yet, just seconds later in the same conversation, Ichinose lies blatantly, claiming that Ayanokouji told her everything about the White Room. (Lol)

This implies that she didn't deny the sexual encounter because she had decided to stop being "nice" and instead confront Ichika’s hostility head-on—not because of some moral code.

Another example is the claim that Ichinose understood how sensitive and taboo the subject was (citing her scanning of the surroundings as evidence). Yet, shortly thereafter, Ichinose’s internal monologue completely contradicts this, stating explicitly that she is entirely ignorant about the matter.

Moving past these oddities, if we look at the over-interpretations...

Xorpaw suggests that Ichinose’s blatant scanning of her surroundings was a non-verbal way of signaling to Ichika that she understood the gravity of the White Room. This creates a chronological issue—look: Ichinose glances around before saying, "There are no longer any secrets between Ayanokouji-kun and me"—even though neither of them had mentioned the White Room yet. What does this mean?

She simply looked around as a matter of common sense, ensuring that no nearby students would overhear her discussing Ayanokouji’s private secrets in such a crowded place.

Furthermore, the account describes the conversation as if Ichinose had manipulated every one of Ichika’s reactions, step-by-step, ever since Ichika brought up the topic of sex. In reality, the text itself makes it clear—even from an initial reading—that Ichinose is acting purely reactively: she first tries to respond without hurting Ichika, then decides to stand up to her once she realizes Ichika’s malicious intent. As I see it, she only improvises the ruse about "secrets" after Ichika arrogantly claims that no one at the school knows Ayanokouji better than she does.

Not to mention that it fabricates an entire line of reasoning for Ichika regarding a "respectful waiting period after a breakup" and Ichinose’s attitude toward Kei. In the actual text, Ichika’s suspicions are based solely on two direct observations: Ichinose’s rapid emotional recovery following the second-year final exam, and the two of them being close together in the cafeteria after the entrance ceremony.

Moreover, one can use the text itself to challenge some of the claims presented as "great feats." For instance, there is talk of Ichinose’s "brilliant" concealment—yet the conversation itself proves otherwise.

The fact that Ichika failed to anticipate Ichinose’s cunning does not mean that Ichinose masterfully hid her abilities from her. It merely highlights Ichika’s own arrogance and indifference; she openly admits to having looked down on Ichinose, reducing her to nothing more than "a busty *senpai*." Ichika was monitoring Ayanokouji’s surroundings for external threats or agents from the White Room—a point established some time ago—rather than analyzing the psychological development of the third-year class leaders. That was her objective during the conversation, and she succeeded in obtaining the information she sought.

In short, while it is true that Ichika was unaware that Ichinose had heard the term "White Room" back on the uninhabited island, Xorpaw overlooks the real reason for Ichika’s miscalculation: ego.

Ichika fell for the bluff because her need to be the only "special" person who knew Ayanokouji’s true self clouded her judgment, leading her to believe that someone as calculating as him would reveal his greatest secret simply because he had slept with a girl.

In other words, it was more of a blunder on Ichika’s part than a feat by Ichinose, who already possessed privileged information beforehand."
