X Strategy

People are nothing but tools to me

Introduction: X-Strategy is the strategy employed by Classroom of the Elite protagonist Kiyotaka Ayanokouji against the first major antagonist of the series Kakeru Ryuuen. It encompasses four volumes and serves as a scheme centered around misdirection and manipulation.

Disclaimer: An appeal to possibility fallacy is the fallacy that  because something is possible means it’s probable or likely. In the context of analyses, debaters often use this fallacy to downplay characters. For example, suppose Bob plans to rob a bank by slipping a note to the teller and revealing a gun. It would be an appeal to possibility to criticize his plan for not accounting for the possibility of him being struck by a meteor or lightning. Both events are possible, but they’re so improbable that it’s unreasonable to criticize the lack of preparation there as a flaw in the plan. It’s not an appeal to possibility if you criticize Bob for not accounting for the possibility of a silent alarm since silent alarms are fairly common and worth considering in a high stakes robbery. I explain this because I seek to avoid that in this analysis, but a lot of criticisms later on will be fairly basic strategic maneuvers that even average people like me could think of. For these supposed geniuses to not consider that or that their opponent might consider that they might consider that is thus a flaw.

Disclaimer 2: Like SAW, Classroom of the Elite makes clever use of foreshadowing. Kiyotaka frequently makes innocuous observations that pay off much later down the line. A good example of this is when a group of people note that Hirata and Karuizawa don’t seem that close in Vol 3, which pays off in Vol 4. I take this into account by filling in any possible gaps or explicating on unspoken strategic aspects of plans if the hints are there. BUT I won’t fill in any gaps if there are no hints. For example, a point I make later on is that, from Kiyotaka’s POV, his plan was essentially to have Horikita lose to an average girl in a fight. Since there’s no indication that Kiyotaka accounted for Ibuki’s martial arts skill or even noticed it, I don’t just make the claim, “Ayanokouji must’ve known Ibuki would win because he presumably noticed her skill via his great BIQ (despite no hint of that in the text).”

Strategic Aim: To maintain Kiyotaka’s anonymity, to build Horikita up as a major player within his class, to increase his influence over Class D, and to deal with Ryuuen.

This strategy has many benefits. Building up Horikita is important since she is, like him, pragmatic and intelligent,  and his closest friend. By increasing her influence over the class as a leader, Kiyotaka can essentially puppeteer his class from the shadows through her. Ditto for increasing his influence through other means expanded on later.

* Each class gets 2 tents, 2 flashlights, a box of matches, unlimited sunscreen, and the girls would get tampons and such.

* 300 points for each class + a manual that explains how to get points, good spots on the island, etc.

* Each class with get however many points they have left added onto their class total the next semester

* Wristwatches will be distributed amongst all students that tracks their pulse, the time, their position, etc

* No pissing/shitting outside of a toilet

* Spots on the island grant rights of exclusive possession that may be invoked for up to 8 hours after discovery. A special key card is needed to claim that spot. Only a leader can use the key card. Leaders can’t be changed without justification. One bonus point per claim.

* Any student who commits burglary, causes damage to, or  is violent toward another class will immediately disqualify their class.

* At the end of the week, you guess the  leaders of the other classes: you take 50 pts per correct guess from them, and you lose 50 pts per incorrect guess. The classes who have their leader discovered loses all bonus points from spots.

 Ryuuen’s plan:

The aim is fairly simple: info gathering on the other classes.

>Bruise Ibuki (and the other guy), and then send them as spies
>Strike an alliance with Class A
>Bank on Class B and D taking them in out of pity plus the point incentive of roll call absence
>Use all 300 points initially given before sending the class back to the ship. Ryuuen further uses reverse psychology to make it seem like he doesn’t care whether the spies return or not, offering to take them back in
>Ryuuen would stay behind on the island
>The spies would use cameras to record the key card interaction and then rendezvous with him, and Ibuki would drive division in Class D by pitting the girls against the boys.

        We can see that it’s decent in these aspects. It’s believable that Ryuuen’s class would have defectors since he had the reputation of being a tyrant, so he played into that even more by bruising the “defectors.” He further displays a nonchalant attitude about whether they return or not, deflecting suspicion away from them. Ryuuen orders them to bury their radios outside of the camps they end up in so they can communicate with him secretly. If it worked, it would have nabbed them 100 class points and impressed Class A, while putting down both CLass B and D, and it would also have maintained his hold over Class C by giving them a way to both have fun and win on the island.

But the plan also has numerous holes. The spies just enter the classes with the cameras in their bags for anyone to see. Despite Ryuuen’s bruising of Ibuki, people (of average intellect) would still naturally be suspicious of her given the nature of the game and Ryuuen’s reputation for underhanded tactics. A simple baggage check is something he should have anticipated and planned around, perhaps by having her bury the camera outside the camp as well. Ryuuen carelessly lets the radio be spotted when the Class D duo come to see them. There’s no benefit to this and it doesn’t fit in with the facade that Class D didn’t give a shit about the exam and was just partying, and it further telegraphs him as their leader. Even if Ryuuen successfully guessed the other classes' leaders, he made it extremely obvious that he is the leader. So his entire plan is negated if the other classes write his name down, causing a net gain/loss of 0 for any mutual exchange involving Class C. Ryuuen could have easily planned around this by just having some NPC in his class under his control be the leader who renews the spot, anticipating that he’s the obvious first guess since he’s the “tyrant” of Class C. This is the strategy I actually thought he was employing to make himself a red herring, but no, he just banked on no one guessing the obvious answer. When all is said an done, he later explains to Horikita his plan and the various deductions he made.

Ayanokouji’s Counter-Plan

        The aim is primarily to switch the key card so that the information Ryuuen acquires and passes along will be incorrect

>Drop the key card during the discussion with Horikita where she reveals it to him, catching Ibuki’s attention
>Convince Yamauchi to put mud on Horikita so that she has to go bathe, allowing Ibuki to make her move
>Sabotage Ibuki’s camera, forcing her to physically grab the card
>Set a fire that makes Ibuki panic and flee, as people would naturally suspect her, being the outsider when so many bad things were happening. (Prediction)
>Horikita chases after her, gets in a fight, and collapses, allowing Ibuki to pass along the information (Prediction)
>Retire the exhausted Horikita, either through persuasion or just dumping her off to the staff
>Class C’s plan fails. Make the correct guesses of the other classes’ leaders. Let Horikita take all the credit.

Pros - This plan is much more intricate in its multifaceted benefits and precautions. It plays into his long term strategy of misdirecting attention away from himself and building up Horikita as a powerful figure within the class. It proves to Horikita his superiority. By using her as a pawn, though he could have likely convinced her to play along on her own, he demonstrates his superiority to her and inclines her to respect his capabilities, and since he did this seemingly in service of Class D Suzune also respects him as an ally. Setting fire to the manual is an impressive, on-the-spot frame job against Ibuki. The underwear incident had already caused a lot of tension in the class, and while that could be interpreted as perverted internal trouble, the manual burning seems like a malicious external act of sabotage. The outsider Ibuki would naturally be the culprit, and Ibuki realizes this and flees. Ayanokouji had also made sure to weaken Horikita as much as possible by manipulating her into bathing in the cold water, worsening her condition and causing her to lose the fight to Ibuki and collapse from exhaustion. It’s likely he improvised much of this plan on the fly when the group went foraging, as he did mentally note that peeling Horikita’s name off the keycard would be impossible, implying he might’ve considered that beforehand, before dropping the keycard. Since Ibuki had been there for a while by the time Kiyotaka formulated a plan, he knew she likely knew the leader by then. Thus He had to retire Horikita, but that can’t be done without “suitable justification” per the given manual, and he surmised that sickness would be a suitable justification. It’s mentioned early on in passing that one student in Class A would stay on the ship due to sickness, so it’s likely he used this information to make the connection.

Cons - But it has flaws just like any plan. First, it relies on Horikita not being able to use the shower. There’s a long line there when they return. Kiyotaka never hints that he accounted for this somehow in his narration. There’s no mention of him seeing people clamoring to use the shower before he heads off to forage, so he just gambled that Horikita would both be unable to use the shower and impatient enough to knowingly bathe in the cold. He doesn’t know Ibuki is a capable martial artist. From his POV, Kiyotaka is betting that a random girl will be able to beat up Horikita when she is sick. This might not seem too bad, but Horikita does put up a fight against her, throwing multiple blows and landing one that’s described as “agonizing” to Ibuki. It’s only after Horikita got slammed to the ground, tired herself out throwing multiple attacks, and was forced to block/evade multiple hits from Ibuki that she collapsed from exhaustion. An ordinary girl, even against a weakened Horikita, would most likely have gone down fast. And this is something Kiyotaka should have known. He’s seen her manhandle Yamauchi and complimented her striking power in Vol 2 when she, while holding back, hits him. He got lucky Ibuki was trained. Most obviously, the fire tactic is both clever and contrived. I’ve already explained why it’s clever, but this is why it’s stupid. No one in Class D actually makes the logical inference that Ibuki was the likely culprit. (It’s true Kiyotaka actually was the perp, but from their POV this would be it.) Even Suzune, who doesn’t have the excuse of being a screw up or an idiot, just goes “One thing after another” after she returns from bathing, just dropping whatever suspicions she had for Ibuki despite knowing both that the keycard was missing and now that there’s a fire. She only gets seriously suspicious when she notices that Ibuki is gone; i.e. somehow Ibuki is able to make the inference but no one else in Class D is able to do so. If anyone else did or if Horikita put two and two together while Ibuki was still there, they could’ve either confronted her the camp or multiple people would’ve followed her out of the camp to confront her, confounding his plan which requires the keycard to be stolen and shown to Ryuuen.

Reasoning - His deductions are as follows. He deduced Katsuragi wasn’t the leader for Class A because he was too careless (pretty obvious) in that instance, but he was otherwise cautious, and so it had to be the guy next to him. Occupying a spot immediately after finding it is risky, and Katsuragi pretended to be the leader in case anyone had caught sight of Yahiko’s making claim to the spot.He figured out Ibuki was the thief (and thus a traitor) when he noticed that she didn’t make eye contact normally, but did when he expressed trust in her when she said she wasn’t the thief, and this is in comparison to normal people who do ordinarily make eye contact but look away when lying, so perhaps her eye habits are inverted. Most impressively, the digital camera in her bag led him to suspect that there was an alliance at play, because if Ibuki was merely giving info to Class C her word would likely be good, but other classes wouldn’t be so trusting. Class A’s final points (270) confirms the fact since that’s only the 30 point deduction from Arisu’s absence. It’s likely Class C lent them resources. This is actually fairly impressive.

Unsoundness - Besides the alliance deduction, his reasoning here is very sound and he is carried by his conclusions being fairly obvious. His reasoning that only short sighted greed would prompt someone to claim a spot is flawed. Hypothetically, if you immediately find a spot, claim it, renew it every 8 hours on the dot, then you’d get 3 points from it per day. Delaying just reduces the points gained in the 7 day period from that spot. So a shrewd, intelligent person is just as likely to immediately claim a spot to maximize points. And more obviously, someone might just want to claim a spot before a person from another class stumbles onto the site to claim it, and then you are forced to either reveal the leader to hog the spot or give up the free points. But since there were only two possible leaders there, no matter what he said he had a decent chance of guessing right. For all it matters, he could’ve just as easily said “Katsuragi is taller than the other guy, and taller people are more likely to be leaders,” and it would’ve been just as likely to be right. His deduction about Ibuki is also flawed, because people, even the aloof and introverted types, also tend to make strong eye contact during emotional moments or significant conversation points. Within the conversation he made the deduction, he stated that he trusted her. Her making eye contact could just as easily have been from shock at his gesture of good will, attraction, or appreciation.

VIP Exam (Vol 4)

It’s been 3 days since the island exam, and Kiyo is lonely. He’s under pressure from Chabashira. Hirata interrupts his daydreaming to invite him out, even canceling his outing with his girlfriend, Karuizawa, to do so. Kiyo briefly wonders if he’s gay, but he suspects an ulterior motive. He is correct: Hirata now thinks highly of Horikita and wants to coordinate with her. He could act as a bridge to deliver Hirata’s thoughts to her and pretend they’re his. Kiyo notes how Hirata seems to have, in doing this, lost his nerve and showed unusual uncertainty. Karuizawa and her friends arrive shortly after, push Kiyo away, and chat with Hirata.

A message from the school interrupts a conversation between Kiyo and Kushida, urging students to arrive at a certain room at a certain time. He notices the times for girls and boys are different. He meets Karuizawa, the Professor, and Yukimura at the meeting. The test is this: all first year students will be divided into groups with designations based on the Zodiac calendar. It’s a test of thinking. Why exactly only four of them were called there when it would be more convenient to explain everything at once greatly puzzles Kiyo and the gang until the teacher explains that groups aren’t made up of members from just one class. The teacher hands them a slip of paper with their group (Rabbit) 15 total members.

The rules are as follows:

There is a VIP in each group. At 8AM on the test day, every student will receive a text stating that their respective group’s VIP has been chosen. The test begins the next day and ends on the fourth day. Each group should convene twice on test days to discuss for one hour. Answers will only be accepted between 9:30 and 10:00 PM on the final night. During that time, each group member must submit an answer as to who they think their group’s VIP is, but only the first submission will be accepted. VIPs can’t submit answers. Submissions should be only for a student’s own group.

There are 4 outcomes, as explained:

O1: If the answer is correct, then everyone in the group gets 500,000 private points, with the VIP getting an extra 500,000
O2: If there is no answer submitted, or if someone within the group who is not in the same class as the VIP answers wrong, then the VIP gets 500,000 private points.

O3 and O4 trigger for any answer given prior to the 9:30 to 10:00 time frame.

O3: If a group member not in the VIP’s class submits a correct answer early, then their class gets 50 class points and they personally get 500,000 private points. (The answer is null if the VIP’s classmate answers early.)
O4: If a group member submits a wrong answer early, their class loses class points, the VIP gets 500,000 points, and the test ends (Answer is null if the VIP’s classmate submits wrongly.)

The incentive for O2 is to simply prevent other classes from benefitting. The most beneficial outcome is mutual cooperation, but the built-in traitor system also fatly rewards betrayal. So it’s a prisoner’s dilemma. When all is done he begins creating his strategy. Later, as he meets some students from other classes he memorizes their names and faces. Hirata is in the same group as Horikita and Katsuragi. Horikita is now respected as a “puppet master.”

Karuizawa

Bad - The biggest flaw with this plan is how Kiyotaka gets Karuizawa into this situation. He has Hirata call her there. On a more nitpicky note, Kiyotaka states he was inspired by the Milgram experiment - an experiment where prisoners pretended to be shocked when the test groups administered a shock at the behest of an authority figure - but the two situations are similar only in the most tangential sense. Kiyotaka’s set up just relied on the fact that people are capable of violence in the right conditions, which is obvious. He didn’t actually subvert Manabe’s morals. Holistic Analysis

We can see that he has a few schemes running throughout his battle against Ryuuen at once since he’s also having to counteract Chabashira's blackmail for most of the run. This is evident in his plays to boost Class D’s ability overall. We see this in his usage of Horikita, strengthening her as a leader and building her connections with Hirata, winning the exams for them, letting Sudo get bullied during the Sports Festival to facilitate character development, and even more minor gestures like letting Ike be useful during Vol 3 by building a fire or raising Sakura’s confidence by being her friend. These acts contribute towards strengthening Class D and prove his worth. When Chabashira confronts him, he points something out to this effect and now she’s cornered into a situation where her coercion is nullified. If she exposes him or works against him in any way, that only spells doom for Class D without Ayanokouji’s guiding hand. He could, he suggests, even actively sabotage them if she overplays her hand.

Now that brings us to Ryuuen, and we’ll have to diverge since I don’t trust Ayanokouji’s words. I’ll first give an analysis of events based on his own words (the popular interpretation) and the one I feel is more sound, taking into account his status as an unreliable narrator.

Ayanokouji’s Description

        Based on this interpretation, Ayanokouji’s ultimate goal from the very beginning, at least so far back as Vol 5, was to dominate Ryuuen mentally and physically, coercing him into going after other classes. He deliberately led him on, predicting Ryuuen’s actions and then setting up a stalemate situation as described above. Karuizawa’s predicament, which he facilitated, would serve as a good excuse to beat the shit out of him.

        In light of this, it may be interpreted that Ayanokouji deliberately didn’t cover his tracks well in his strategy to recruit Karuizawa, knowing she would lead back to him. His actions could be interpreted as creating a false profile to lead Ryuuen on into believing the latter had genuinely ensnared him. His plans - the VIP switch in Vol 3, Karuizawa’s recruitment in Vol 4, the spy footage in Vol 5 - make Ryuuen think this guy sticks to the shadows at all costs. This sells the illusion that his ultimatum with Karuizawa was a genuine trap. Kiyotaka racing Manabu at the sports festival served to get attention on him as a possible suspect.

Kiyotaka knew, based off her previous reliability, that she would hold against his peaceful attempts at persuasion and that this would only stoke Ryuuen into violent measures, which would force the stalemate. He had also read Ryuuen perfectly, both knowing the latter would resort to such a stupid plan and also that he would resort to violence quickly based on his several assaults on Sudo and even his own usage of violence against Ibuki during the Vol 3 exam. His strategy can be outlined as this:

1. Pass the exams
2. Elevate Class D to counter Chabashira
3. Recruit key figures like Horikita and Karuizawa, strengthening the former
4. Allow Karuizawa to be discovered through the spy
5. Allow her to be violated
6. Beat up Ryuuen in “self defense” with witnesses

Was it actually all planned out? No. Alt. Interpretation

This interpretation takes into account his status as an unreliable narrator and I personally believe it makes more sense when you look at his actions throughout the war from a strategic standpoint. It wasn’t actually all planned out. Ayanokouji had genuinely fucked up in not covering his tracks well with his usage of Manabe and Karuizawa, but the plan to mentally break Ryuuen and Karuizawa had formed in his mind soon after the text from Ryuuen at the end of Vol 6 showing Karuizawa. This jives much better with his internal monologues prior to the showdown. For example, when he gets the text, he says, “Enough. I was fed up with his obsessive persistence. I closed my phone and steeled myself. Half-measures wouldn’t do here,” implying things weren’t actually going as planned (since he had no exact plan) and that his mind had shifted to employing a definitive solution against Ryuuen’s hunt, hence the foregoing of “half-measures.” Up to that point, he didn’t actually have any solution in mind for dealing with Ryuuen beyond the half-measures of just continually evading or outwitting him. Furthermore, while anything he tells someone is inherently questionable, his brief dialogue with Ibuki in Vol 7.5 does seem to confirm this interpretation as he states that he only fought Ryuuen after considering several options, alluding to his decision to forego half measures in Vol 6. This plan therefore served to break his spirit, as he says during the fight.

        From here, it’s largely the same as the other interpretation. The only difference is the shift in strategic goals. He now wants to break Ryuuen and use him against other classes while also end Ryuuen’s hunt for him. Ayanokouji allows her to be caught, tortured, and then forces a stalemate which enrages Ryuuen, allowing Ayanokouji to beat him viciously. The actual explanation Ayanokouji gives to Ryuuen is just fluff he made up. Given his goal is to break Ryuuen’s spirit, of course he not only physically beat the tar out of him, but he also exaggerated just how much was per keikaku to seem intellectually superior as well. He used his own deductions of Ryuuen’s actions (such as him discovering Manabe as a spy or tracing X through Karuizawa), which he predicted or reasoned out but didn’t really have a solid counter to, to bluff that he had puppeteered Ryuuen. When we consider his private introspection in his apartment where he says - “The only remaining problem was Ryuuen Kakeru. If he did figure out I was the class mastermind, he might create an uproar by circulating that information. It would have been ideal for my identity to remain completely concealed, though that seemed impossible now. Even if I cut all ties to Karuizawa Kei, we were still bound by an invisible thread” - this implies he adapted to a hole in his plan, being Karuizawa, rather than it all being planned from the start. In this light, his performance at the Sports Festival can be seen as a weak attempt to throw suspicion off of him by attempting to throw Ryuuen off by being out in the open, unlike the profile he’d formed of X.

        This is a decent change in goals. Ryuuen, of course, had proven to be a powerful, challenging figure to deal with. He’d genuinely gotten Ayanokouji on the ropes there, so he needed to bluff superiority and send him after other classes like an attack dog. Ryuuen’s hold over Class C makes him both a risky and valuable possible “ally” against the other classes, and, considering Arisu’s threat against him, it would be useful to have another card up his sleeve.

7. Pass the exams
8. Elevate Class D to counter Chabashira

Ryuuen closes in on him -> Adaptation

But how are we to interpret Ayanokouji’s external and internal dialogue that implies/states it was all according to keikaku? (1) The external dialogue is a clever lie and reframing of events that happened thus far, making Ryuuen believe he was just “dancing in his palm.” In that situation and considering his goal, he’s only incentivized to lie.  (2) The internal monologue in that scene is unreliable narration. We know (2) based off its conflicting with previous internal monologues and his tendency to lie and exaggerate. For example, Ayanokouji frequently exaggerates how much of a threat Horikita is to him, expressing fear for her. He even lies to the reader about having a normal upbringing in Vol 2. If Ayanokouji lies and deceives in his narration in one facade, it’s difficult to not consider that he would lie in another.

With that said, we can consider the categories:

1. Strategic Adaptability - Ayanokouji turns each test towards his advantage to evade Ryuuen. He adapts to Ryuuen catching up to him.
2. Strategic Alignment - Uplifting Class D both works out in his favor against Chabashira and Ryuuen, particularly in his usage of Horikita
3. Strategic Execution - Ayanokouji displays excellent martial arts skill and physicality in defeating four capable opponents. He maintains his cool against surveillance and bluffs convincingly during the showdown.
4. Strategic Resilience - Ayanokouji maintains his and Horikita’s place in ANHS despite Kushida’s bet
5. Strategic Shielding - The Paper Shuffle contingency. Setting up Hirata and Karuizawa as Horikita’s friends, increasing the pool of suspects. Manabu and Chabashira as witnesses. Deleting all conversations between him and Karuizawa.

Feat Framework

External Situation - One of his challenges is maintaining the facade of normalcy. While it doesn’t hold perfectly, Kiyotaka manages to fly under the radar and achieve his goals from the shadows despite increasing scrutiny and novel situations.

By far Kiyotaka’s biggest advantage isn’t his brain, it’s the stupidity of others throughout the novels. I’ve hinted at it before, but let’s run down all the instances he’s carried by luck or stupidity. In Vol 3, Ayanokouji’s plan relies on framing Ibuki by burning the manual. Ibuki knew she’d naturally be the prime suspect in that situation as it does seem like someone is trying to sabotage the class - people would naturally go for the outsider, so she fled… Except no one else in Class D makes this connection besides Horikita! And it’s after a while she does, so Horikita goes alone (Kiyotaka likely knew she would based off her pride) to confront Ibuki. But it’s not like Kiyotaka controlled for anyone else following Horikita or noticing Ibuki flee from her spot, where she was standing right beside the fire next to Hirata. You can make excuses for students like Sudo and Yamauchi, but even the supposedly “clever” ones like Karuizawa and Suzune don’t catch on as quickly as they should or at all to make the logical leap that Kiyotaka induced in Ibuki. Vol 4, Karuizawa, as mentioned, never once questions the contrived nature of her ending up in the lower decks of the ship. As I explained earlier, she realistically would’ve questioned where he ran off to, opened up to him about the incident with Manabe (or Hirata would’ve caught on), and Kiyotaka would’ve been exposed as a manipulator to Hirata at the very least.

There’s also something to be said about Karuizawa. She, alongside several other characters, are two-dimensional and sometimes comically easy to manipulate. Karuizawa displays absurd loyalty to Kiyotaka because she… had fun and felt protected, even though she just discovered the situation that begot his protection in the first place was orchestrated by him? Yamauchi just agrees to harass Horikita with mud because of a promise with no backing that Kiyotaka will give him Sakura’s email address.

 As far as stupidity goes, there is also an elephant in the room…

Opponent Quality – I’ve not been subtle about this. I think Ryuuen is, at best, written inconsistently, or a downright idiot at worst. He’s smart enough to figure out the group system in the Zodiac Exam and subtly map out the school’s cameras, but his strategies are mediocre at best. The best and funniest example of this is when Ryuuen leaves a phone on record after antagonizing Kiyotaka and Horikita in Vol 4. The story treats this as a serious trap, even specifying that he left it out in the open, funnily enough,  when it has no right to be. They realistically have no reason to not just throw it away, ignore it, or tell Ryuuen to eat shit. His ingenious strategy is to have NPCs spy on possible suspects to apply pressure and make X sweat. When X contacts him in Vol 6, he doesn’t press him at all when X claims Horikita saw through his strategy with Kushida. Even then, he has no reason to betray Kushida besides his own stupid fixation on making Horikita submit. If he just ignored X and gave Kushida the correct answers, Horikita goes down and Class D + X take a big hit.

Also, let’s talk about his big plan. He thinks X wants to preserve his identity and so he might come up to… reveal himself to stop Karuizawa from revealing his identity? This makes no sense as a plan. There’s no benefit to texting X about what he’ll do to Karuizawa on that day. In fact, considering the first interpretation, let’s assume Ryuuen doesn’t know he’s being played by Ayanokouji and thinks he’s got him trapped. He still has to consider numerous other plays X could make against him to get out of there. He could’ve sent a teacher there (almost actually what Kiyotaka does), he could’ve sent someone in his place (Albert takes little convincing to get past) to get Karuizawa out, and, of course, anything he does at that point with Karuizawa can be used against him. Even if X does show up, the most obvious and logical conclusion is that X would instantly have blackmail material against him, negating any threat of exposure. Even without that, he’d given X the time and location of Karuizawa’s torture. Ryuuen didn’t check for any sort of listening device or recording that Kiyotaka could’ve set up there. X could have easily turned his own plan against him if he just prepped the rooftop. Remember, from Ryuuen’s POV, X’s only goal in this situation is to prevent Karuizawa from betraying information. It’s a stupid plan, so let’s consider some superior ones that an average, not “clever,” person like me could come up with:

1. Once you’ve discovered Manabe as a spy and Karuizawa’s connection, simply have her spied on and tailed, record her schedule, and conceal any particular suspicion towards her by having others spied on as well (something similar to what he actually does). Have someone break into her room while she’s out and plant a bug or listening device and listen for a conversation with X. Given the “income” from Class A, he could’ve easily acquired the resources to do this. Discover X through this.
2. During the Kouenji interrogation scene, have a lackey outside of the area message or call X. Given that Ryuuen was perceptive enough to notice Karuizawa’s pupils dilating during the torture scene, he’d certainly notice Ayanokouji’s phone dinging or vibrating.
3. During the rumor stunt, simply deny the allegations and demand an investigation. Camera footage would show Ayanokouji rummaging through people’s mailboxes.

Obviously we can think of easy counters. Kiyotaka leaves his phone behind, he instructs Karuizawa to check her room, etc, but that’s exactly the point. Ryuuen never even considers these simple, average-human level albeit effective strategies, and Ayanokouji never even alludes to any countermeasures for these obvious tactics besides the possibility that someone might check Karuizawa’s phone. If we’re being objective without adding contingencies where none are mentioned in the story, Ayanokouji would’ve lost his anonymity against anyone even slightly cunning if they played their cards right at these points. It only speaks to his great emotional intelligence and theory of mind that he knew Ryuuen, capricious and inconsistently intelligent as he is, would be clever enough to make certain inferences but retarded enough to make the plans he did.

Of course, he’s given some leeway since he’s chaotic and motivated by fun, but that still makes for a poor opponent.

Context: Arisu has obtained information about Ichinose from President Nagumo. Nagumo himself allows this to break Ichinose’s spirit and have her as a pawn. Using that, she begins a vicious smear campaign by circulating rumors that Ichinose is a criminal. Ayankouji has also been tailed by Hashimoto, a Class A student who was tipped off by Nagumo that there may be something unusual about Ayanokouji. Arisu’s plan is ultimately to draw Ayanokouji out and provoke some sort of response. On the 11th of February, she even had one of her henchwomen visited Ayanokouji and reveal Ichinose’s past, concealing this as if it were betrayal since the Class A student in question was someone she had blackmail on, which would make it seem like a genuine betrayal to Ayanokouji.

Explanation

Ayanokouji’s primary goal is to save Ichinose. Since Ichinose is unwilling to take action that would force the school’s hand and shut down the rumors harming her, he figures the best way forward is to involve other students who wouldn’t be so reserved. To do this, he, on the 11th, calls Kushida to his dorm at night. He strikes a bargain with her: Kiyotaka will, moving forward, give half his points to her in exchange for secrets about all the first years’, secrets which he carefully chooses so as to not trace solely back to her and not so trivial as to be uncontroversial. This move, he explains, simultaneously disincentivizes her from expelling him, since he’s now a steady source of points and thus an asset to her, defending himself from Kushida, a looming threat in the background, while also pushing his main plan for the story forward.

But before all this occurred on the same day, Ayanokouji was accosted by a girl from Class A. She claimed that she was going behind Arisu’s back to talk to him, and to back this up she revealed her shady backstory. She was a frequent shoplifter, Arisu caught her early on in the school year stealing beer, and she’d been under blackmail ever since. To prove this, she even leaves the dorm and returns with a can of beer. She reveals Ichinose’s past and then leaves. Ayanokouji is already suspicious, as it makes no sense for her to approach him, ostensibly a random student from her POV if she’s honest. He inspects the beer, notices its sell date, and goes to the store and sees a very blatant discrepancy between the can she gave him and cans at the store, which are far more recent. Therefore, he reasons that Arisu had put the girl up to this. The can she gave him was the same as the can she first shoplifter and gave to Arisu when she was caught, and she either got it back at some point or quickly retrieved it from Arisu when she briefly left Kiyotaka’s dorm. He then knows that Arisu, who had already told him that she would go after Ichinose, knew Ichinose’s secret, as who else could this random Class A girl have found it from? And that secret was passed to her by Nagumo, who is on the council with Ichinose as her superior.

Next, he has to get rid of Hashimoto. On the 14th, he had predicted that Kei Karuizawa would give him a Valentines Day gift. He had already subtly encouraged her to break up with Hirata, and she in fact did so at the start of Vol 9. Knowing Hashimoto will be tailing him, Kiyotaka briefly visited Ichinose’s floor in the dorm building to pique his interest. It works, as Hashimoto sticks around for some extra time until six, when Kiyotaka leaves and meets Kei. He briefly inquires about a mysterious number that had called him (not really related to this strategy), and she gifts him chocolates when their meet is over. Finally seeing something interesting, Hashimoto decides to stir things up by revealing himself and flirting with Karuizawa, though Kiyotaka had prepared for that and reveals that he was just on his way to the library, which he proves by showing the books in his backpack. The cover story Karuizawa improvises is that he’s not really her crush, but rather that he was just passing on the gift to her real crush. Regardless, Hashimoto is thrown off his trail. Afterwards, he met with his reading buddy from Class D, Hiyori, and planted the idea in her mind that Class A wanted to do more than just spread rumors.

His next move was to meet VP Kiriyama (a confidant of Manabu) and explain his strategy to save Ichinose. He wants Kiriyama to post the rumors for him to every class’s online board except Class A. He explains that this has the double effect of both making it impossible to trace the rumors back to him. He also tells Kiriyama that, if someone really traces it back to him and presses him, that he could give credit to Manabu for the strategy, as the feud between Manabu and Nagumo is well known. In particular, he targeted the volatile Ibuki and Ishizaki, knowing they’d start a fight that would catch the school’s attention. During that fight, he also showed up, knowing Hashimoto there would bring up the rumor about his crush on Kei (which he had Kiriyama put forth), and let his classmates point out that those rumors had some weight to them, lending credence to his respective “secret” being only a mundane love story.

Finally, he moves towards repeatedly visiting Ichinose, who is skipping classes, during lunch when no one else is around the dorms. He knows that, since she is so compassionate, she’ll feel bad about his display of empathy, and she eventually does break down. He says he knows her secret, and she confesses it all to him. Why? Because he knew Arisu would confront her about it and he knew that getting her to be honest the first time would make the second time easier, so Ichinose was able to earnestly confess her secret in front of the class at Arisu’s coercion without mentally breaking down, resisting her demands to step down as class leader.

We have 2 plans:

To misdirect Hashimoto:

1. Prepare certain countermeasures (the meeting with Hiyori as a pretense, the visit to Ichinose’s floor to keep his interest)
2. Meet Karuizawa in his view
3. Show up at the fight and let his classmates bring up his closeness with Kei

To save Ichinose

1. Strike a bargain with Kushida
2. Use Kiriyama to circulate the information
3. Guilt trip Ichinose into spilling the beans
4. Allow her to stand up to Arisu

Strategically, this had several effects. (1) Ichinose is saved, countering Nagumo. (2) His relationship with Ichinose is improved. (3) Class A’s relations were destroyed. While they were focusing on B, Kiyotaka made it seem like they were attacking everyone; and since they started the whole affair with the first rumors, he knew they’d be unable to deny the claims convincingly, especially since they were the sole class whose board was left untouched. (4) He gets rid of the tail. (5) If anyone does bother to investigate the online rumors, it traces back to the student council, hurting Nagumo. (6) Kushida has less reason to go after him. (7) One of the rumors being about him brings him a lot of embarrassment, which makes it less likely for someone to suspect that he started them.
