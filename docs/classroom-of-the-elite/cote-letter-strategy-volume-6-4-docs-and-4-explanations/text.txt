2. To see how the school reacts to rumors, the rumors need to exist for a while. However, Ichinose literally went to the school the next day. So what was the school supposed to do? It confirmed her innocence and thereby prevented the rumors from spreading.

>**V6:**

>The next day, Ichinose did as she’d said she would. The school issued an official notice that there was no fraudulent activity. They cleared her of all suspicion.

3. Drawing parallels between the rumors in V6 and V9 ignores a fundamental qualitative difference. The V6 rumors concerned breaking school rules. The V9 rumors accused Ichinose of breaking the law. Drug use and robbery are serious criminal offenses, harshly condemned in Japan. There's no guarantee the school would ignore these new accusations (though ultimately, it did). However, a test under different conditions can yield different results.

4. The school didn't ban everyone from talking about Ichinose’s “illegal points”. Does that mean the school ignored the rumors? Absolutely not. The school confirmed Ichinose's innocence, and widespread rumors didn't spread afterward. So why would the school need to issue a kind of ban? One could argue the test worked because the school didn't interfere with the rumors in V9. Well, if you're testing something, the results need a clear interpretation. In this case, you could draw opposite conclusions. You could say the school was inactive (since Ichinose initiated the check herself), but you could also say the school prevented the rumors (by confirming her innocence). So, you cannot draw definitive conclusions about the school's actions from that test.

# Instead of a conclusion

Lately, I've been noticing an unhealthy trend of overcomplicating the strategies of some characters, making them more complex than they actually are. Complexity is now equated with effectiveness and depth. The more complex the strategy, the better. Instead of using consistent explanations, people add their own interpretations.
This approach makes readers feel stupid, though they are actually normal; it's just that some people try to add significance to things that don't need it. Attempts to explain something from new perspectives are praiseworthy, but sometimes they are based on conjectures or misinterpretations.
People forget that the author is an ordinary person, and writing is his job. Denying the author's right to make mistakes, they deny the author's humanity. Considering these strategies flawless and believing each has multiple layers pushes them further from reality. They continuously search for new evidence supporting their theories in ambiguous character phrases, which might have been added without such intent or simply by mistake.
It looks like a race of speculation. One assumption serves as the foundation for another, building an entirely new narrative that eventually starts being perceived as fact. They mix real reasons with invented ones, and then you see people perceiving the invented reasons as real. If initially it's just one person's opinion, over time it turns into an unshakable truth, and soon new followers of this interpretation strive to convince others of the validity of the opinion they heard.
I’m afraid that overcomplicating strategies will eventually lead to a loss of trust in strategies overall. If some explanations of a given strategy are mistaken, how can we trust other explanations of it, even the true ones?
