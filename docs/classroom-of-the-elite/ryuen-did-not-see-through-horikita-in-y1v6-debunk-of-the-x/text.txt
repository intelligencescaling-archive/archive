This is based on the off-screen negotiation.

If you remember, Kiyotaka’s strategy in the Paper Shuffle tells us that Ryuen gave X his class’s questions after Kiyotaka informed him that Kushida’s plan had failed.

After the contract, when Suzune submits the questions, both Ryuen and Ayanokoji act as if they don’t know anything.

Because of this, people downplay and undervalue Suzune’s strategy. So I’ll put some points on the table.

Point 1

If the reason Ryuen knew in advance that Suzune was going to win is Kiyotaka, this does not diminish Suzune’s strategy. In fact, it only makes it stronger—or otherwise reflects very poorly on Ryuen. Let me explain.

If Ryuen knew beforehand what Suzune was going to do and still did nothing to change it, that is a terrible debunk for him, because it shows that Suzune completely outplayed him.

Not only because he would be entirely dependent on Kiyotaka saving him and his class with that message, but also because, even knowing what would happen, he couldn’t do anything to stop her or gain an advantage despite having over a week to prepare. That would be extremely embarrassing and completely out of character.

What’s worse?

Being outplayed without realizing it, or being outplayed while knowing you can’t do anything?

Some might defend this by saying:

“But Ryuen wanted to test Koji, he didn’t care about Suzune or class battles.”

Yes, he tested Kiyotaka—but not because he had structured his entire strategy around that. It was more like a bonus gain resulting from his supposed victory over Suzune (using his strategy with Kushida).

Not tò mention that his goal was tò make x come out by beating suzune thing that koji intself confirms in his monologue before the submission of the questions

This is reinforced by the fact that, in Ryuen’s perspective, Kiyotaka was merely a pawn of “X,” especially after the Kiyotaka vs. Manabu race. Someone not particularly important.

For him, everything is a game. Hunting "X" is fun. Ruining Kiyotaka's plans is something he'd love to do. So if he could sneak up behind Suzune and ruin her plans, he would. Because that would be the most fun thing for him.

It’s true that he doesn’t care much about class battles at this point, but he wouldn’t throw away an entire exam just to see a reaction from someone who, in his mind, is just a pawn. It's too ineffective and silly.

Before moving to the second point, let’s briefly address Ryuen’s strategy for this exam:

In this theory, it is argued that Kiyotaka sent the message several days in advance. This is supported by the fact that Karuizawa was able to plant the cheat sheet with the exam questions into Kushida’s vest.

However, this creates a problem.

During all that time, Ryuen showed no signs of knowing anything about Suzune’s plan. Not only that, but Kei didn’t plant the cheat sheet immediately after it came back from the dry cleaner, because Kushida would have noticed in that case.

The purpose of Karuizawa’s “show” at the karaoke was to determine whether Kushida only had one uniform available (the school provides two, but Kushida kept one with Kiyotaka’s fingerprints). Kiyotaka didn’t know whether she had bought more, so he used this to verify it—not to plant a cheat sheet.

That action happens later, right before the exam, after Kushida makes a mistake. This is for two reasons.

First, because Kushida is not stupid. With her body movements, she would eventually notice that there was literally a piece of paper on her chest. If she had been carrying it for a week, it would have been even more obvious. Just by taking off her blazer, it would be visible, and the plan would fail. So placing it right before the exam was necessary.

Second, as mentioned before, the deal had not been finalized at that point, and therefore Koji did not yet have the questions to plant.

However, this alone is not enough to say that Kiyotaka didn’t send the message days in advance.

"If we look at the conversation between Ryuen and Kushida, we can see that Kiyotaka gave Ryuen two options, which led to three possible outcomes.

Ryuen chose the last option—letting Suzune win by changing the exam questions. But how could he change the exam questions? By the time Suzune executed her plan, there was no time left to do that.

This simply means that there was a time gap between when he received the message and the final submission—enough time to create an entirely new set of challenging questions."

That's the argument

But that’s not actually correct.

There are two factors that invalidate this interpretation.

First:

Kushida recognized the content of the cheat sheet and referred to it as something “only she and he should know.” This means that that cheat sheet is something she recognizes from somewhere.

What is something that only he and she should know?

Obviously, the product of the secret agreement that existed between them.

This means that the cheat sheet contained the same notes Ryuen had originally given Kushida so she could defeat Suzune.

So what was changed?

It wasn’t the exam questions, it was the material Ryuen gave to Kushida.

Ryuen didn’t follow the first option Kiyotaka gave him. He followed the second one: providing Kushida with incorrect answers.

This is reinforced by Kushida’s statement that the deal with Ryuen was only finalized when Chabashira confirmed the questions.

"Like I told you. I will force Horikita to drop out no matter what i have to do. Solely for this reason, I betrayed my classmates and secretly submitted Class D's exam questions. The condition was that I'd get the questions and answers for Class C's math questions in return. If you had kept your promise, Horikita would've voluntarily withdrawn herself from school by now. And yet, you betrayed me."

"What? Are you mad about something like that?"

"Something like that? You come out ahead against Class D, and you're just going to end it with that?"

"You're fundamentally misunderstanding the situation, Kikyo. The questions you made weren't used on the exam."

"Huh? What are you talking about? I turned in the exam questions as soon as possible just as you instructed. I also confirmed everything with Chabashira-sensei. There were no mistakes."

Something that happens on the final day.

Since Ryuen learned from Kiyotaka that he wouldn’t meet that condition, he simply switched the notes.

This requires no time and fits perfectly within the timeline of the series:

Suzune makes her move → Kiyotaka informs Ryuen → Ryuen gives incorrect notes to Kushida (and Kiyotaka) → Kei plants Kiyotaka’s cheat sheet → The exam takes place

Even if I were wrong due to translation issues, it’s not like Ryuen didn’t have a secondary question bank.

Classes could submit multiple question banks to test the school’s limits and create difficult exams. Suzune does this, and as Kiyotaka states, the questions Kaneda gave Kushida were of high level so logically, Ryuen did the same.

We know from a canonical standpoint that at least Ryuuen had at least two different question banks in reserve. (One with the fake questions and answers for Kuhida and another with the real ones). Which is evidence that Kaneda used that method.

So, within those final two hours, Ryuen could easily switch the questions. There was no need for several days to create a new set, because they already had them from previous attempts.
