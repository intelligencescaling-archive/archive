Honami > Nagito in Planning
By XorPaw
This is a short doc to make the case for Honami (COTE) being better than Nagito (Danganronpa) by feats, in terms of planning specifically.

I’ll use one plan from each - CH5 for Nagito and Y2V7-Y3V1 for Honami. Both have secondary planning feats (CH1 for Nagito and UIE, Ichika & Kakeru Alliance for Honami), but they’ll be excluded from this comparison.

It is recommended that you read the two characters’ respective analysis and their source materials.

First I will describe the definition of planning that’ll be used for the comp. Planning is the character’s blueprint to achieve their goal. It usually comes down to the conceptualization of predetermined events. Think of it as the character’s ability to write an instruction booklet with the steps-by-steps (preparation process) as well as warning labels and safety information (contingency, coverage, etc). For example, to make sure you wake up before 9:10 AM, you’d need to set an alarm on your phone to ring at 9:00 AM. You might be concerned whether it’ll be enough, so you buy and set up an alarm clock that’ll ring at 9:05 AM.

Contrast with strategy - the set of principles guiding the way you will try to achieve your goal. The character’s “instruction booklet” will be written with those principles in mind. For example, to wake up before 9:10 AM, you’d need someone or something to make loud noises around that time.

Planning Aim

I won’t scale “planning aim” given it’s too result based and subjective IMO, but it’s important to understand what the two characters were trying to accomplish with their plan and their context/emotions that drove them to formulate their plans. After all, it’s impossible to scale feats if you do not even understand the characters’ actions.

Honami: She wanted to “bind” Kiyotaka to her, make him support her and her class. Through her plan, Hona wanted to reconcile her feelings for Kiyotaka, who’s a student from an opposing class, with her duty to her classmates as their class leader. Another complication was the fact that at the time, Kiyotaka was dating Kei. Additionally, Honami’s class was slipping from B all the way down to D. Beside dating matters, this most likely translated to Kiyotaka joining her class, before Y2V12.

To that end, through various manipulation methods, Hona planted and watered seeds to grow a friendship nest in her class, took many actions to isolate Kiyotaka socially, engineered rumors about herself and Kiyo, loaded up coercion materials on him and prepared for the promise day, reworked her own relationship with Kiyotaka, all the while concealing her scheming from (potentially) antagonistic third parties or straight up demoralizing them into giving up.

Nagito: He loves hope very much & hates despair, so when he found out, after completing the FDR during the fourth Class Trial investigation, that every participant in this killing game except the supposed traitor were a part of something called “Ultimate Despair”, he wanted everyone except the traitor - the Future Foundation spy - dead. To do so, for the fifth class trial, Nagito wanted the Future Foundation spy to be the next blackened and make himself their “murder victim”, but ensure they win the Class Trial by making the group vote incorrectly.

To achieve this, Nagito set up the stage for the traitor to inadvertently throw the murder weapon - the one fire extinguishing grenade that had “Monokuma’s special poison” among many. In the factory, there were plenty of fire extinguishing grenades, but Nagito knew the traitor would pick the right one because of his luck.

As a disclaimer, I’ll note that Kinugasa, COTE’s author, rarely puts all his characters’ actions in retrospect. Instead, he’ll leave clues for the readers to piece up the hidden plots of the novels. The characters rarely explain their actions (and are not always honest when they do). Plus, COTE does not have anything analogous to a Class Trial where there’s a justification (in-universe) for having most of the process behind each plan explained.
In SCD, this translates to every COTE character being scaled based on the explanation which has a much more logical and empirical basis to them. Though this creates a bit of a hurdle as what’s considered “more logical” is obviously debatable, so it’s essential that you read the character’s docs if you’re not familiar with their scaling and make your own judgements. Unfortunately, to my knowledge at the time of this writing, no public document exists for Nagito’s CH5 plan though fortunately much of the process is explained on-screen. For the sake of keeping this comparison concise, I won’t go through literally every single thing such as the evidence supporting the interpretations for Honami when the aforementioned document does exactly that.

Planning Coverage

This is definitely the most commonly used category of planning and is the mainstay of any planning distribution.
Planning Coverage scales the character’s ability to put together a “To-Do” list. It is measured by how well a character can take into consideration various complications leading into the execution sequence - potential hazards, variables and outcomes.
A character’s ability to put together a To-Do list and respect it will maximize the success rate of the scheme.

While Nagito currently doesn’t have his own public document that breaks down his CH4-5 planning, enough process was explained or made obvious on-screen, especially during the class trials that we can reasonably scale his planning coverage without one.

Anyway this is based on the sum of Nagito’s actions:
* Buy as much time as possible for the initial preparations.
* Keep the group distracted while setting up the warehouse.
* Make it seem like you’re an actual victim of torture.
* Set up a contingency for when the cast will see through the torture fabrication. If the majority vote against Nagito as the blackened, the traitor wins. If the cast believe he committed suicide, the first impression will be that Nagito wanted to kill them all because he made it look like a murder. This would increase the chances they vote for him which would shield the traitor.
* If it comes down to that, the “crime scene” should create a sense of ambiguity about whether Nagito’s death was caused by the poison or suicide.
* Set up a way to transmit the instructions to the group without having to be physically present yourself, for both before the BDA and after the class trial.
* Properly mix up the poison into one of the fire grenades.

Planning Preparation Process

Planning Preparation Process is basically the A->B->C->D steps. The character’s ability to logically align different tactics over the course of the plan’s lifespan.

Nagito didn’t make any egregious blunders in his preparation process format.

Get out of the class trial ASAP -> Scout the island, set up the bomb bluff in the theater and hospital and the poison quickly -> Destroy the hotel to sell the buff and tell the rest he hid bombs with hints -> While they are busy looking for the bombs, get the warehouse ready for his eventual body discovery announcement; set up the domino with the monokuma panels -> Place a oil lighter in front of it -> set up the music drown the other noises -> hang a spear to the ceiling -> tie up one arm and both legs -> grab the tip of the rope hanging the spear to the ceiling -> slash slash slash -> Stab free hand into monokuma plushie.

There’s no fundamental fuck up and every steps logically align.

But there was no adversity to challenge this alignment. Honami’s environment is ANHS, where schemers are everywhere, there’s a lot more people in general (hundreds of students, many teachers, etc), the exams are somewhat random and it’s nearly impossible to predict everything. Yet, there are a few patterns that stick year after year and Honami’s preparations went in accordance to this. Everything within Honami’s plan to take Kiyotaka that could have been planned long ahead of time - her awkward scene with Ryuji during the second year’s cultural festival, disappearance in on the last day of the Hokkaido trip, nearly everything Hona did in Y2V9, the meeting in Y2V10, Y2V12.5 promise day and the Kei scene in Y3V1 - happened slightly before upcoming special exams or on set special days with the scheming revolving around those events being provoked by those events or using convenient attributes of those events - for example, the excuse Honami used for getting people like Ryuji involved in the Y2V10 meeting was to run “some checks” before the upcoming S&D exam. What this implies is that she could have used foreseeable events as “landmarks” for her alignment of tactics.

This, however, meant Honami had a limited amount of definitive opportunities to progress her plan. That is, even if opportunities could arise by luck in-between those set steps to maximize the succession of the plan.

Meaning, Honami also had to align more complex things at once than Nagito ever did. To tie up her tactics in Y2V7-Y2V8-Y2V9 to Y2V10, she had to manage the relationship between Kiyo and her classmates, Hona’s own with him (which back in Y2V7-Y2V9 was very awkward to say the least) while engaging in psychological warfare with Kei & Miyabi and stay under first years and Arisu’s radars. Each step had to progress multiple separate layers of her larger plan and they did just that.

Verdict: Honami Mid-High Diff. Neither have legitimate failure in this subcat, but her situation was more complicated and random over its duration while having a more layers to align to maximize every opportunities while also having planned more “acts”-steps ahead of time (12-30 > 7-11), so being able to have an A -> B -> C -> D that is logical and without failures is more impressive in Hona’s context than Nagito’s more fixed and simpler situation.

Planning Intricacies

Planning Intricacies is scaled by the character’s ability to create a detailed plan with many layers.

* Handling Discontents - Managing the situation with Ryuji.

Seven more could arguably be added: the Miyabi trap, if you do not consider it as a subset of the rumor strategy given it has its own set of individual acts during the resignation scene (like the Kei interception and the “see you later” bluff) and Kiyo analysis as many of Honami’s actions revolve around gathering as much info on him as possible. Planning Versatility

Planning Versatility is the plan’s options to be versatile with its resources and come up with new strategies during its process when preferable. Resources can be time, currency, tools, etc.

Honami’s plan was made in a manner in which there’s enough wiggle room to maneuver in-between the predetermined steps to apply the layers of her plan or make any adjustments should they be necessary in case of a failure in her forecast of the school’s schedule. It is also not dependent on the SEs after winter break being anything specific which allowed her to take advantage of them when possible. Honami used the opportunities to improvise during the winter break to take advantage of Kei’s illness with the gym fabrication and selfie tactics. Hona also took advantage of the circumstances during the S&D (the fact she knew Kakeru won before the exam began) to do the KiyoKei nomination tactics. Honami used her student council position to the fullest for the sake of her plan and she also used her social standings and insight in other people without damaging her reputation, usually by scapegoating Kiyo and/or Miyabi and through her communication skills. Hona’s usage of Mako and Norihito before & during the Y2V10 Meeting are perhaps the best examples, as it was multifarious: Mako allowed Honami to walk to Kiyotaka’s room inconspicuously + bait Nori into the meeting & Norihito made the meeting fun + predictably caught her & Kiyotaka on the act to coerce both him and Kiyotaka.

As for Nagito? From the moment he entered the warehouse to set up the crime scene, he had zero room for adjustment or control over the plan's execution. Even before that there was no real time gap between his prep process to have too much leeway, everything happened in a specific way and Nagito could not be opportunist at all about any changes in the environment of the killing game even if new opportunities opened up. Nagito also had (virtually) no way to adjust in the case of unexpected circumstances. The extent that could be argued for his planning versatility is the island scouting and his other work right after Class Trial #4. Given Nagito’s aim & situation, low flexibility is forgivable if he managed his resources properly, but Nagito’s blunders - specifically the errors in what he should’ve done to control his resources so they do not turn into the footprints of his plan but didn’t do, ultimately expose his inability to organize resources according to his objectives.

Verdict: Honami Very Low Diff. Nagito is 0 in Planning Versatility compared to Honami and any decent planners.
Planning Acclimation

The acclimation of a plan is determined by the character’s ability to alter a plan to be more befitting for their current environment, condition and potential hazards. Adapting the existing plan to the current environment as it changes or in the face of unforeseeable circumstances.

Verdict: Honami Very Low Diff. The C-D class alliance strategy to save her plan to bind Kiyotaka is a big planning acclimation feat for Honami. Nagito’s acclimation was virtually nonexistent - the islands’ scouting could be classified as acclimation if you stretch it, but it’s nothing remotely comparable to Honami reimagining how to bind Kiyotaka to herself following the end-of-year exam.

Planning Invincibility

Planning Invincibility is scaled by the planner’s ability to counter active threats. A planner may construct a plan with preventative measures that hinder their adversaries’ ability to hinder its setup. This has some overlap with planning versatility and acclimation as a plan with more leeway for adjustments can allow the planner to actively counter whenever necessary. Some may only consider preventative measures when scaling invincibility. Fortunately, in this comparison it doesn’t change the verdict all that much.

Honami’s actions to disguise and conceal her changes and growth as a schemer from Arisu, along with all that as well as the winter break rumors from this (from her POV) unknown “White Room” threat blinded literally every outsider. Honami also took effective actions to neutralize Miyabi, another potential threat. All of that allowed her scheming to move without concerns, aside from Kiyo revealing his “true color” during the second year’s end-of-year exam. But even with that, Honami’s countermeasures to prevent enemy tactics from hindering the setup worked as intended. The Norihito misdirection during the Y2V10 Meeting was the blinder to conceal her intent to later coerce Kiyo during the promise meeting from Kiyo himself. All of Honami’s preventative measures were efficient as they were only fractions of tactics that also advanced the other layers of Hona’s plan; the same can’t be said for Nagito.

Nagito’s countermeasure, the bomb bluff, was highly effective in distracting the rest of the cast. However, as previously mentioned, he did nothing to conceal his intent from the mastermind or set up some kind of protection that would prevent the mastermind from doing anything to interfere with his plan. If anything, a plan such as his that relies so much on indirect manipulations, foresight and entails virtually zero flexibility would be especially vulnerable to the mastermind’s whims. This is all in spite of the fact that Nagito was under the impression that he was working against whatever the mastermind was trying to get out of the killing game.
His complex layered trap could also be viewed as “invincibility”, though in the same vein all of his errors would lower his scaling.

Verdict: Honami Low-Mid Diff. Honami’s plan had better walls in its setup, was harder to discern during the execution specifically thanks to her better planning, involved better thinking regarding what is a potential adversary and she had more leeway to actively counter threats in case of an emergency.
Planning Applicability

Planning Applicability is for preparation what the cat “knowledge application” is to knowledge. Sorta. Generally speaking, you want your preparation to serve a purpose, or maybe more than a single purpose. In nearly all outsmarting scenarios, the opportunities to prepare are limited, so it’s essential to not only identify and act on those opportunities but to also make the most of them. Long story short, applicability asks how relevant the preps were to the aim.

Most of the tricks Nagito used during his preparations served one or two purposes at most - the bombs in the theater and hospital being an particularly noteworthy example - or lacked in synergy; factors lowering the efficiency of his plan. An example of poor synergy can seen in how the spear/domino arrangement and poison setup preps interfere with his torture/suicide preparations for many reasons (the warehouse’s one entrance, the panels being valuable evidence left behind, the blue seal, etc point toward the death not being directly related to any blood evidence) rendering the deception around his fabrications markedly weaker.

As previously mentioned, Honami’s preparations usually align and respect every other layer of its plan, so they’re not self-defeating and it is efficient. This might sound like glaze, but it really is just a simple fact; most of her plan’s acts, with the student council resignation and Y2V10 Meeting being the best displays of this.

But really, Hona’s actions in Y2V9 are enough for Nagito. The timing of her rumor mills #1 with Miyabi’s neutralization & the engineering of the friend nest Hona aimed to create for Kiyo with her classmates, as well timing it with the start of the KiyoKei fracture & break the ice between herself and Kiyotaka, in which she continued the process for future contingency to, if necessary, try to coerce Kiyo in the promise meeting via her manipulations of Norihito to make him rely on Kiyo for his personal woes ahead of time to prepare for the Y2V10 meeting misdirection while continuing to collect info on Kiyo to understand him better and his relationship with Kei all the while concealing any footprints of all that planning from Arisu and any possibly harmful kouhais / teachers, eviscerates anything Nagito has ever done in his whole life with regards to his preparations’ serviceability.

Verdict: Honami Low-Mid Diff. The key difference between Honami and Nagito’s planning applicability is that Hona’s preparations never cripple each other. Nagito also doesn’t have any performance for this subcat that is remotely comparable to Honami’s Y2V9.
Planning Contingency

Planning Contingency is measured by the character’s ability to create other plans or backup plans for future events that could occur for a potential outcome or to gain security or insight incase of a discrepancy.

Honami’s main contingency is her entire scheme in Y2V12.5. As a backup plan, it is drastically more complex as its own thing than Nagito’s messages post trial or the layer of within his traps, while the two are equal when it comes to containing discrepancies. The coercion Hona created during the Y2V10 Meeting in the event she had won the EoY exam is also important to note. Gathering the materials for her coercion contingencies in case she had won the exam and Kiyo wasn’t... enough into what she wanted him to do beyond just this and that involved a more complex set of mind games than for Nagito to put his own contingencies in place; he faced low adversity when it came to this facet of his plan - the messages are obvious, but Nagito could set up the warehouse as it pleased him when the others were distracted, admittedly because of his own initiatives.

Verdict: Honami Mid Diff. The process for Honami to both create and set her contingencies in place involved much more cognition. Nagito could have scaled higher in this, to the point of extreme diff if some layers of his trap weren’t undermined by his coverage.

Planning Formulation

Planning formulation is how quickly a character can come up with a plan in a relatively short period of time.

It’s not exactly clear when Nagito formulated his plan but it was before Gundham’s class trial had ended, given his talk of opening act and whatnot. His plan as a whole was more complex than Kei and Kiyotaka nominations in Y2V10 and Y2V9.5 gym fabrication. As written on the first page, feats outside of one plan can’t be used for this comparison, so no Ichika and partner exam don’t count.

Verdict: Nagito Low Diff. This is pretty simple. Nagito made his whole plan (or most of it) very quickly, possibly as soon as he left the FDR. Hona’s formulation speed feats revolved around some tactics and were in all likelihood less speedy as well.

Planning Accuracy

Planning accuracy measures how the abstractions of your plan translate into reality and everything connects together reliably.

There’s no use repeating the same things over and over again. The preparation process went along with Hona’s foresight of ANHS’s schedule and social engineering went as planned. Nagito never had to concern himself with any form of complex schedule and social cognition or at least never to Honami’s extent. Nagito’s plan had him deceive significantly less people, none of whom could be described as schemers or outsmarting characters with individual agendas, over a much shorter and simpler time span.

But that doesn’t mean Nagito is “meh” at planning accurately. Far from it. He anticipated everyone using the fire grenades to put out the fire and make his plan go smoothly. He also expected that the group would flip the Monokuma panels he prepared beforehand.

But Honami’s forecasting of everyone else’s actions were usually more difficult to nail down than Nagito’s. Some examples; Miyabi having her and Suzune compete for the SCP seat on the 2nd week of December, before its last day prior to the following weekend or him sending the photo to Kei via a third party after school but right before the winter break, nailing the timing with perfect accuracy. Honami’s anticipations of her classmates around the same time (mainly them asking Kiyotaka to investigate her discreetly as well as Kiyotaka telling her to decide where they would go first and the predictions of the Hokkaido trip grouping in the first place along with Norihito starting to see him as someone reliable) + Norihito in the Y2V10 meeting (that Nori was coming back to Kiyo’s room) are ridiculously strong secondary cases.

Verdict: Honami Mid-High Diff. Neither are bad at this and giving it to Nagito is not criminal, but I believe Honami definitely takes it, taking in factors such as foresight and the difficulty involved with getting things accurate.

As for Long Term Planning and Short Term Planning, obviously they should go to Honami and Nagito respectively.

Conclusion

Honami’s planning was significantly more complex and detailed than Nagito’s. She took much, much more in consideration without having his glaring holes when it comes to what she failed to consider. Despite these complexities, Honami’s planning had much more room to adjust and improvise when such assets were advantageous or necessary & it was efficient when it came to using her limited set of assets. Being an intricate plan for its own sake doesn’t mean much on its own, although Honami’s planning had a stronger structure with each step being timed & connecting nicely despite her disadvantages regarding her setting’s randomness, resources & limitations compared to her possible enemies. Thus, Hona takes every commonly used planning subcats from Nagito with the exception of Formulation Speed and Short-Term Planning.
