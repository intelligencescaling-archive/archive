Because in her situation she could turn to absolutely no one in order to fend off any sort of criticism towards her, as she was effectively done, the one way to defend herself effectively was to attack the others criticizing her with the aim to cause as much damage to everyone around her, enough to subsequently stain her name in malice even by the adults who have witnessed the aftermath of the scene.

The Weapon of Trust
Kushida Kikyo, the social butterfly, possesses a very powerful weapon, one more deadly than violence - Information.
With her social status as well as her ability to get close to individuals, Kushida is able to gain huge amounts of information about everyone’s own personal tales and secrets they decide to share with her. Because Kikyo poses as a brilliantly kind person who helps everyone in need, she is able to gain the trust of individuals very easily. Additionally, seeing from her goal to befriend as many people as possible, she is able to gain immense amounts of information about various people, creating a ridiculous web of secrets she possesses and knows of the people who look up to her daily.
When thinking of information which most people would like to keep private, matters which would affect one’s public image negatively are among the first things perceived. Such secrets told by others is information Kushida is no stranger to. The likes of tales she had grasped and gatekept throughout the time can cut through relationships and turn people against each other more effectively than any sharp knife will, and Kikyo Kushida knows this better than anyone.
Her narcissism thrives on possessing leverage over others, since it gives her a strong sense of superiority and defines her sense of self-worth given to her by the others who shower her in trust and compliments. The lustful side of her narcissistic desires is manifested in her perverted thrill of getting the key to messing with other people’s lives, the very ones who collectively decided to give her their trust.

When Kushida’s ugly self was revealed and she was faced with the strong response from the ones who used to love her, all she had to do was keep revealing more, just reveal people’s worst personal secrets to each other until they begin to prioritize one another and end up fighting between themselves, even when the cause behind their conflict is right in their faces.

FINAL CONCLUSION:
The incident which branded Kushida’s status as a defective student revolves around her causing a huge drama by leaking everyone’s secrets between themselves, and subsequently a fight which completely broke an entire class apart in such a violent way that it was an event recognized by Kikyo’s and Suzune’s entire middle school. She had effectively destroyed an entire class from the inside.

As he reconstructed Kushida’s past piece by piece, Ayanokoji had additionally comprehended the reason she had aimed for ANHS in the first place. Because this school cuts off contact with the outside world, it would allow Kushida’s past to remain unknown as well as permit her to start anew without looking back to her previous failure.

Ayanokoji questions Kushida about the level of stress built inside of her directly and how it affects her to which she responds that it is what she wants out of life however, because if so her existence has meaning.
Ayanokoji sees the emotional depth her response carries and concludes that it contains multiple truths and lies within it, all emphasizing Kushida’s distortion of her “real” self, one beyond any forced mask she is supposed to put.

- The stress is what she wants out of life is a truth because it’s supposed to give her existence meaning. -> The burden of having to deal with the constant strain on her mind from maintaining her fake perfect self is what she ultimately forces herself to strive for, because she pursues her ultimate happiness in the form of making herself liked by anyone, ultimately giving herself her internal validation and meaning.
- The stress is what she wants out of life is a lie but it does give her existence meaning. -> And yet the burden is taking a visible toll on her, she cannot stand the stress and she is forced into releasing it no matter what to protect her sanity, all for the sake of protecting her own meaning, which still in truth, makes it all worth it.
- The stress is what she wants out of life is a truth and yet it fails to give her existence meaning. -> Kushida strives for the constant strain for the sake of keeping her fake personality and bring worth to herself, and yet her own significance is devalued by her degrading mind in having to pretend to be what she isn’t while her stress remains something she can barely hold in and is at risk at losing herself in. The true Kushida’s existence fails to hold any meaning due to the internal struggle.
- The stress is what she wants out of life is a lie because it fails to give her existence meaning. -> Kushida finds herself a broken mess unable to ever come at peace with herself despite the constant drown in pleasure, as she also pays the price in a neverending pain. The impossibility to find herself at ease makes it impossible for her to even give her existence any meaning, as she is constantly in a chase to fulfill her desires without giving her own self more significance than that. It is because she realizes this struggle that she knows the stress is just a burden in the long term, rather than a small inconvenience.

Despite the eternal contradiction, all of the above are ultimately correct perspectives of Kushida’s psyche and acceptance of her internal struggles in the face of her desires.
It is because of this well understood eternal contradiction that Ayanokoji lays out - “I couldn’t tell WHICH Kushida was the real her.”
He knew that in truth, neither of the 2 versions which he had acknowledged of Kushida could be the real, “significant” self, there had to be something else.

Once Kushida switches to her angelic personality again and asks Kiyotaka to head back with her, he makes an association between her and the story of Doctor Jekyll and Mister Hyde. His comparison of Kushida to the themes of Jekyll and Hyde is a deliberate choice that further emphasizes his deep understanding of her mental state, and holds particular importance as a means of discerning Kushida’s “true” self.

The Plot of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde:
The personality of Jekyll refers to a man who publicly holds himself up to very strict moral standards, but "indulges in his unsated vices'' secretly, which makes him feel incredibly shameful. As a way to avoid his shame when he sates his desires, he figures out how to physically transform himself into the alter ego known as Hyde so he could indulge in his vices shamelessly using a serum he personally developed. Jekyll's alter ego, Hyde, is borne from the true desires that he always held deep down, and is let free through the existence of the serum.

While the symbolism for Kushida and J&H is seen in the form that both characters have seemingly two alternate personalities, it is rather stemming from this basic misconception left at face layer that Ayanokoji has used this association as a means to give detail to Kushida’s true self by “explaining the puzzle” through J&H, because it highlights an inconsistency by directly attributing Jekyll's and Kushida’s alter personalities with their true selves.
There's also to note that the rest of the story as well gives incredibly high relevance to Kushida’s personality, which is another big standpoint for Ayanokoji’s comparison.

Rather than simply associating Jekyll with Kushida’s shown side during the tantrum and Hyde with the angelic persona, Kiyotaka portrays that just like in the novel, Dr. Jekyll’s transformation into Mr. Hyde symbolizes the struggle between a public persona and hidden desires, and it is the same duality that is rather mirrored than directly transcribed by Kushida’s outwardly “angelic” appearance and her ugly nature.

From previous psychoanalysis induced information, Ayanokoji concretely reasons a connection to a generalization of Kushida’s version of herself she does not even know exist using already collected information and the literary analysis.
Starting from the assumption that Jekyll is supposed to represent her true “significant” self which is not normally affected by any sort of contradiction between her meaning and desire, the “unsated vices” are resembled in her narcissistic character traits which inherently make her to crave for appreciation and control over others, then Mister Hyde is without a doubt the created angelic personality Kushida puts herself to portray, with what can only be sustained through the key serum, which is also Kikyo’s obligation to vent her stress.
Since Kushida’s ugly side cannot be her true self according to both the book and Ayanokoji’s own psychoanalysis and observations of Kushida’s psyche, all that remains is an extra element: The consequence of the vices, what is openly deemed as bad by all society and what is to be digging Doctor Jekyll’s grave in advance, just as it will to Kushida Kikyo.

Kushida’s similar psychological conflict and the motivations driving her behavior resulted in the creation of the stress and malice burdened monster Ayanokoji saw on that night on the rooftop. In the end, it is just a part of an illness-causing emotional response disguised as a coping mechanism caused by mere convenience and self gaslighting, only worsened by her personality disorders.

The consequence of the uncontrolled desire has had a negative impact on Jekyll’s life all throughout the book, and it is the deeper layer of Ayanokoji’s association of J&H with Kushida Kikyo’s internal struggle, as the parallels suggest. Just as Dr. Jekyll’s suppression of his darker urges later leads to their violent emergence, Ayanokoji sees that Kushida’s relentless efforts to maintain her image are compelling her to keep straining further from and utterly dehumanize herself, consequently falling into a downward spiral which only ends in a spectacularly grim end.
Ayanokoji associates Kushida’s life with a number of events happening in the book:

- The Murder of Carew.

At one point, Jekyll involuntarily turns into Hyde in his sleep, and this causes him to stop his transformations altogether out of fear over inability to control them. This eventually fails, as Jekyll drinks his serum to turn into Hyde once again in a moment of mental weakness, and kills a character known as Carew under the Hyde personality.

What parallels this moment is the middle school incident, where her true personality was leaked across her entire school, and where she had caused the destruction of her entire class, with the consequence being Kushida enrolling into ANHS. This allowed Kushida to start a clean slate and make new friends to validate her existence once again, without the risk of anyone looking up information about her and finding out about the incident that happened the year before. This is why during her introduction on the first day, she specifically mentioned that nobody from her middle school followed her into ANHS, making her want to make friends with her new classmates.

- The Death of Lanyon

After this murder, Jekyll once again tries to avoid further transformations, but involuntarily does it again whilst awake. To transform back into Jekyll, Hyde calls a character named Lanyon to bring him the necessary ingredients for the transformation serum. After mixing all the ingredients that Lanyon brought up together and drinking in front of him, he transforms back into Jekyll. However, the sight of this transformation causes Lanyon to die of shock.

The rooftop scene where Kushida's tantrum is witnessed is what Kiyotaka parallels as the J&H plot. The “transformation” and serum is Kushida's venting of frustration towards Horikita, and the “death” of Ayanokoji would be the shattered image of Kushida's former self that he had held prior.

- Jekyll’s Suicide

After this event, the amount of serum that Jekyll required to stay himself increased and he decided to go into seclusion. It continually becomes more difficult for him to procure the ingredients to create that serum. Eventually, Jekyll ran out of the key ingredient, and killed himself as a final resort, fearing that he'd never return as his former self if he were to ever transform back into Hyde.

What Ayanokoji visualizes based on his J&H association is the future scenario where the “suicide” of Kushida’s fake personality is forced to happen. Because the J&H is rather a mirrored parallel where Jekyll himself is self aware of the point where one can go back, in Kushida’s case such a self does not exist, and what remains to push toward the crumbling of her angelic personality is the consequence of the vices, or rather the circumstances Kikyo will place herself in.
As Ayanokoji well gets the balance Kushida strikes between her need for emotional release and her fear of exposure, with the path he planned in mind for the conflict between Horikita and Kushida, he has already through his FORCED intervention of causing the tantrum, as the “Lanyon” himself who influenced Jekyll’s fate, laid the groundwork towards engineering a scenario of Kushida’s angelic personality “suicide” happens. This is yet another layer of Kiyotaka’s plan to use Kushida as a means to build Horikita until the very end of her “lifespan”.
With Kushida setting up the circumstances against herself, she is left no room of being able to escape her impending exposal inside ANHS caused by her constant interference against her own class through the existence of Horikita Suzune inside of it which she aims to get in the way of, as Ayanokoji had foreseen long in advance and plans to ensure will happen. Kushida Kikyo will suffer a spectacular implosion of her angelic character as she is in turn destroyed in front of everyone, with the “ugly self” revealing once again, this time for all to see.
Kiyotaka has in fact started setting up the circumstances for his plan through using Kushida’s own built “leverage” against himself as a way to further entrap her in overconfidence when it comes to handling him.
The one important aspect to handling Kikyo which Ayanokoji takes into account is reflected in Kushida’s past and perhaps as another reference in the J&H book, her “weapon of last resort” which happens to be her self destructing mechanism as well, the information she will possess on her classmates which she will try again to use as a means of defending herself during the outbreak of the conflict.

II - Ensuring “Jekyll’s suicide” happens in a scenario where Class D is mentally prepared to deal with Kushida and overcome the turmoil she tries to cause.

In the end, only with the “death” of Kushida’s angelic personality and its occurring consequence will a projection of Kikyo’s true self finally be released by freeing her of the monster created by the circumstances imposed on herself, only then will she finally truly embrace meaning in herself. With this, Ayanokoji had not just prepared for the important conflict laid down by himself in class D involving the start of his class battle all according to his will, but had also anticipated over a year in advance and planned over the end of such instigated long term conflict in the way the scenario sets it out to be for himself to adapt to. Ayanokoji aims to intervene and control the variables in the direction most desired by him, all for fulfilling his ultimate goal, while he was given this 3 year time period.
