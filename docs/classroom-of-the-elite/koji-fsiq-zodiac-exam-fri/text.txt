Explanation of the Phone Switch Strategy:
To start with, Koji stated that his phone strategy is something he had been planning since the start of the exam.
After Koji forcefully brought Kei over to his side, he took her phone and switched their SIM cards using private points, which would copy the email stating that the user was the VIP, Koji also replicated their call/email history to keep Kei hidden.
Next, he switched his phone that now had Kei’s SIM card in it with Yukimura's phone, and he replicated phone information on both of the devices, creating a double-layered trap.
The first trap would be sprung once Yukimura was revealed to be the VIP, which would be backed by the replicated phone information and Yukimura's knowledge of Koji’s password, which would force them to believe the phone belonged to Yukimura.
The second trap would be sprung if Yukimura were exposed, which would cause the students to believe that Koji was the true VIP and that he lied to them.
Both traps worked and Ichinose exposes Yukimura, claiming to the rest of the group that she had investigated how viable a phone switch strategy would be with her classmates. In her investigations, she mentions that the SIM cards can not be exchanged and that phone information could be replicated, which allowed her to “see through” the deceptions Koji created with the phone information and password.
Koji had banked on the student's not realizing that the SIM cards could be switched. The only way one can really see through that strategy is having the knowledge that SIM cards can be switched for private points
Morishige, a Class A student, had decided to input Koji’s name through an email, which caused him to unknowingly trigger Outcome #4, giving 50 class points to Class D and 500,000 private points to Kei.
Despite the fact that Koji’s phone switch had nearly ended up failing due to Ichinose pretending to go along with Koji’s strategy, he still had a backup plan that would ensure he'd be named as the VIP, which meant he had close to no worries. Because Ichinose couldn’t be 100% sure while exactly the VIP is.

In canon, the method Ryuen used to crack the pattern was by gathering the names of 6 VIPs in total, according to the rule mentioned in Y1V5 i.e. 2 VIPs from class B , 3 VIPs from his own class , and 1 VIP from class D. However, it was mentioned by Koji that the possibility of Ryuen cracking the whole pattern with just 3 VIPs exists, if you remember the details of the volume, leaving aside the method he told us about the way to crack the pattern with 6 VIPs by just collecting the VIP names. That means an alternative method to crack the VIP pattern with 3 VIPs exists, which Ryuen missed out on noticing.

The pattern of matching the Zodiac group to the alphabetical order of the students in a group's list, was according to the chronological order in which the Zodiac groups are arranged. Hence, in this case, what should be the inference? It's that certain Zodiac signs are grouped together in accordance to the "classes" they belong to, and they're considered as one entity when the pattern is concerned. Hence, the "allies" , "secret friends" or "conflict animals" are always the groups from another class, since they're treated as someone different from them. So the rules in this case are :
VIPs of groups from allied columns don't share a class.
VIPs of groups from the main column don't share a class with those in the allied column.
Same goes for the main column compared with the secret friend column and conflict animal column.

Get the lists of all the 12 Zodiac groups.

They get a total of 9 VIPs, 3 each from classes A, B, C, 3+3+3. They need to verify this hypothesis.
Group the result VIPs together in accordance to their classes , and input their classes into the chart they arrived at. That's it, boom. Pattern verified. They now have access to all VIP names in the exam.
