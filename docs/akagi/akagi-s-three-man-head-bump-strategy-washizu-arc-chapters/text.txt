# 3man Head Bump Strategy (Chapter 83 - 93)

In the second Hanchan, South 4th Akagi had executed one of his most potent schemes in Washizu mahjong.

In washizu mahjong the tiles are transparent with only one out of the 4 tiles of the same kind being opaque like usual. If I'm explaining a hand, a tile I list without the (O) sign assumes that it's transparent, if it has the (O) sign it means Opaque and that tile is a regular tile. When explaining tiles of the opponent obviously the only tiles visible are the transparent ones so any tiles that are opaque will just be written as (O).

In this round Akagi is winning in scores so if Akagi can just end this round he will finish this Hanchan with lots to gain but if Washizu gets more than a mangan Tsumo or a Haneman from Suzuki (one of his allies that is one of the players since mahjong is a 4 player game, Suzuki isn’t neutral and is only there to help Washizu) Washizu even though does not decrease Akagi’s score unlike a Tsumo or Ron from Washizu can still overtake Akagi in points as the Hanchan ends. If the Hanchan ends with Akagi’s point ranking below Washizu, Akagi will have lethal levels of blood taken from him.

Akagi in the previous rounds has refused to transfer blood back to his body even though he was slowly approaching death. And this pressured Washizu to want a quick Tenpai to finish Akagi off. Also Akagi’s apathy towards his own life was pressured Washizu further,with his insanity making Washizu feel as though Akagi is no ordinary human and has the capacity to take his life. Akagi at the start of Washizu Mahjong said to Washizu that he is coming for Washizu’s life saving which was basically his life, and with Akagi’s insanity this nigh-impossible possibility made Washizu scared.

At this point of the game though Washizu still has a lot of money and Akagi is not fully at the brink of death, so Akagi predicts that Washizu’s fear towards Akagi is minimal. Akagi accurately reasons that Washizu’s small subconscious fears manifested in his conscious thoughts as feeling of impatience or contempt towards Akagi as Washizu’s pride will not allow him to think as though his life is threatened by a random kid.

Akagi predicted that Washizu will misread his own emotions as the root of his emotions are still deep in his subconscious, covered by pride which he will not want to doubt. And so Washizu will stay oblivious to the fact that his psychology is being shaped by Akagi. Akagi also refused to get his blood back throughout the game so he was always close to death, which further strengthened Akagi’s image as apathetic towards death and he gave Washizu a goal post that seems close so Washizu will try to hastily chase to kill Akagi, applying pressure.

Now as the round starts Akagi’s hand is decent, forming:

(1man, 1man, 2man (O), 3man, 5man, 6man (O), 1pin, 4pin (O), 5pin, 8pin, 9sou, South (O), West, Haku)

Washizu’s hand was:

(Hatsu, (O), West, (O), East, 6sou, (O), 7pin, 3pin, 1pin, 4man, (O), (O))

And For Washizu’s hand no useful sequences are visible while his hand is filled with disjointed word tiles, both of these are indications for a difficult hand formation but Akagi realizes something. Washizu organizes his tiles in order and the opaque tile next to the West tile could be another West tile since if Washizu had 2 west tiles from his perspective he would line them up next to each other for a better organized hand to more comfortably play.

There is now a chance that Washizu has a West tile pair, and in Akagi’s hand there is a West tile that is disconnected from the other tiles and is thus a nuance to Akagi’s hand formation. Akagi understands Washizu as a person, and how he has a near divine ability to have the flow of the game benefit and bias him. No matter how many foolish plays Washizu did, the flow never sided against him and from this Akagi understood Washizu’s natural born talent of being lucky.

Akagi knowing the heavens will always side with Washizu realizes the West tile he has in his hand is likely a gift for Washizu from the heavens. This is because If Washizu does have a pair of west tiles and Akagi discards his tile Washizu can call pon for that tile. The west tile also happens to be the Dora tile and Washizu’s wind so calling on that is an automatic 4 Han to Washizu’s hand. If Akagi discards immediately Washizu can call pon and get 4 Han for his hand and if Akagi keeps it his hand progression will be permanently stamped while if he ever decides to discard it Washizu might call a Ron (if Washizu reaches Tenpai later on).

Of course this is all under the assumption that Washizu’s west tiles are forming pairs but Akagi finds the coincidence of Washizu’s wind being Dora, the fact that he has one of that tile as a nuisance to his hand, the fact that Washizu has god like luck and the fact that Washizu’s hand order very well indicates the chance that he has 2 west tiles tell Akagi one thing. The heavens are siding with Washizu in this round and he has the two west tiles.

Akagi with this decides to not discard the west tile and keep it in his hand, knowing full well it staggers his own hand progression. As the tsumo turns goes around, Yasuoka, the player next to Akagi and is currently trying to help Akagi innocently discards a 8pin, not thinking much about it but Washizu makes his move. He calls a Chi and reveals that his (O) and 7pin was actually a 9pin and 7pin and with Yasuoka’s 8pin completes a sequence of 9,8,7 pinzu. Washizu discard the 6sou and on Suzuki’s turn Suzuki discards a Hatsu (revealing that Washizu’s Opaque and Hatsu tile section of his hand was a Hatsu pair) which Washizu also calls Pon and now Washizu’s hand is:

(9pin, 8pin, 7pin) (Hatsu, Hatsu, Hatsu) (West, (O), East, 3pin, 1pin, (O), (O),)

This hand already has 1Han from the Hatsu Pon and with all the tiles visible being capable of being used in either a Chanta (where all melds and head involve at least one word, 1, or 9 tile, so tiles that the player can use are 1,9, word tiles and 2,3 tiles if it forms a sequence with a 1 tile and a 7,8 tile if it forms a sequence with the 9 tile) or a Honitsu (the hand being formed only with word tiles and one suit of tiles, a manzu, pinzu or souzu, for Washizu’s case the visible parts are all pinzu and word tiles).

Washizu’s hand rapidly progresses and Akagi recognizes the immense flow Washizu has in this round and understands how Washizu may rush to form his hand. Akagi knew from Washizu’s psychological state as well as the fact that his west tile is transparent and is thus visible to Washizu that Washizu will be in a hurry to form his hand even if his hand shape is not optimal, ruining his flow. Akagi knew he can only discard his west tile once Washizu gets hasty and locks in his hand in a poor shape so Akagi can discard his tile for Washizu to fully lock in a poor shape, which will allow Akagi to counter Washizu later more easily.

Akagi knew he could not just stop Washizu’s hand progression and just try to end this game in a draw or passing a cheap win to Yasuoka to end this game because the flow sided with Washizu and if Akagi didn’t force to a loss, he might do a Tsumo or a Ron from Suzuki with his luck and finish Akagi. Akagi knows that if he can get Suzuki to offer a tile to Washizu to get a Ron from, and Akagi can headbump the exact tile Suzuki discards (due to turn order, if both Washizu and Akagi does a Ron on Suzuki’s tile, Akagi’s Ron will be prioritized) it will perfectly counter Washizu's offense. This not only adds to Akagi’s score but it has a greater purpose of stopping Suzuki and Washizu from allowing Washizu to form a powerful hand. Yasuoka’s hand formation in this round is very bad and Akagi assumes he can not rely on Yasuoka to win a cheap hand to finish this round quickly.

Suzuki in this round is already preparing tiles so he can offer whatever Washizu needs and is disregarding his own hand development which is obvious since Suzuki was tasked to help Washizu and in this round Washizu’s hand is strong so it's a big opportunity for Washizu. Also Suzuki’s hand is not forming cohesively at all indicating a more supportive playstyle than going for his own wins. This however is information Akagi can use to read what Washizu and Suzuki is planning as some of Suzuki’s tiles are transparent.

Akagi continues to form his hand as Washizu does the same, and after a few turns Washizu calls Pon on a 1pin that Suzuki discarded, and discards a 3pin from his hand meaning if Washizu is going for a Honitsu he is likely in Tenpai.

With Washizu’s hand only having 4 tiles as the rest are all used for calls his hand was as such:

(West, (O), (O), (O))

Now Akagi knows Washizu has entered the point of no return where he committed to his panic and lost his calm. This gives Akagi the opportunity to continue his hand but Akagi knows its still not the right time to discard his west tile, he is in the phase of the game where he can discard it but the specific timing still wasn’t right.

The timing arises once Suzuki himself pulls a West tile, and because its transparent Akagi notices he has pulled it. But Suzuki does not discard it, and with this Akagi deduces what kind of hand Washizu has.

Akagi already predicted that Washizu’s hand had two West tiles but for the other 2 opaque tiles he knew they were not Pinzu [tiles.Suzuki](http://tiles.Suzuki) has so many Pinzu tiles visible as he has been hoarding them so he can give them to Washizu. From this, the fact that Suzuki doesn’t just give Washizu a West tile so he can call Pon and with the turn going back to Suzuki he can discard a pinzu tile for Washizu to call Ron, and with a HonItsu Yaku on top of a Dora Pon and a Hatsu Washizu’s hand will be expensive enough for Washizu’s score to overtake Akagi and kill him.

The fact that he doesn’t means Washizu isn’t going for a HonItsu means Washizu is going for another Yaku that can be formed from his current hand formation which is likely a Chanta, and because in the previous round Washizu called Pon on the 1pin, it means his hand is in a decent shape although not ideal. Akagi also knows its a partial formation of a sequence and not a pair since if it was Suzuki can just immediately deal the West tile and the pair can act as the head. Washizu also does not have any pinzu tiles so the 2 tiles that are not west tiles of his hand is likely parts to form one of the following sequences:

(1man, 2man, 3man) (7man, 8man, 9man) (1sou, 2sou, 3sou) (7sou, 8sou, 9sou)

In Suzuki’s visible part of the hand he already had 1sou, 9sou and 9man so Washizu could not be waiting around it, making the possibility for a partial (1sou, 2sou, 3sou) (7sou, 8sou, 9sou) or (7man, 8man, 9man) sequence obsolete. There was also no possibility of Washizu having the 2,3 or 7,8 part of the sequence and was waiting for a 1, or 9 to complete the sequence in which case Washizu will not have the 1or 9 tiles meaning Suzuki can’t discard it but still doesn’t eliminate the possibility of washizu forming partial sequences, but Akagi eliminated this possibility as Washizu already discarded 4 and 6 of both Manzu and Pinzu meaning due to 2,3 sequence and 7,8 sequence being a dual wait, Washizu will be in Furiten which is not ideal for his hand and thus Washizu will not call Pon on 1pin before if his hand is so poor in shape (Furiten is when one of your discards has one of your winning tiles even if they were discarded before you became Tenpai, this state makes you unable to win with Ron and only Tsumo. Because if this possibility of Washizu’s hand existed he will be in a double wait his hand will be in Furiten with his past discards).

This means Washizu’s remaining part of the hand was likely a partial formation of a (1man, 2man, 3man) sequence. Akagi already had an opaque 2man in his hand and because there is only one opaque tile of a kind, for Washizu’s hand to have two opaque tiles of the 1,2,3 manzu sequence his hand had to have 1 and 3man. With this Akagi knows Washizu has (West, West, 1man, 3man).

Now in Akagi’s turn, because Suzuki did not discard the West tile Akagi for his turn is safe to discard his West tile since Washizu is not ready to call Ron yet, Akagi knows how desperate he is for a win, especially because his life has always been that of winning, Akagi knows how much Washizu understands that securing a victory and being advantageous is two totally different things, and he knows Washizu will not intentionally force Suzuki from giving him the winning hand just so he can get a Ron from Akagi since the result will be the same.

Akagi however does not discard his West tile this turn and intentionally discards a North tile. This served to misdirect Washizu into assuming something about Akagi. If Akagi does not discard his West tile right now even though it's probably the safest chance he will get from an outsider perspective and from Akagi’s reasoning as well to Washizu it will seem as though Akagi is fearing a West tile Tanki wait and if it is so, it makes sense as to why Washizu did not make Suzuki discard the West tile since if the West tile which is also a Dora is his head, Washizu’s final hand will have one less Dora then compared to if he does have a triplet of them so Washizu will need a Ron from Akagi to make up for it.

To Akagi he does not fear that at all since from his initial reasoning, Akagi is confident that Washizu has 2 west tiles, but by intentionally making it seem to Washizu as if Akagi considers that possibility means to Washizu Akagi will be lacking the core premise of (Washizu has two west tiles) needed to reason out the rest of his hand. With this Washizu thinks Akagi logically can not reason out what kind of hand he is forming and is primed to fall for Akagi’s trap.

This would of course risk a certain possibility, which was that after Akagi’s turn of not discarding the west tile Washizu may pull either a 1man or 3man for his hand to be either (west, west, 1man, 1man)

or (west, west 3man, 3man) which would turn Washizu’s winning tile to a west tile, making Akagi lose his chances of winning and allowing Washizu to get a Ron from Suzuki. Akagi however was willing to take this risk since he knew the risk of Washizu winning with later draws were much bigger than this slim possibility.

Now in the next turn Washizu does not make Suzuki discard his West tile so Akagi discards it knowing he might call Pon but not Ron. Washizu does not call neither but Akagi does not discredit his read on Washizu’s hand and instead sees how cautious he is to get victory. Washizu even if he calls Pon will only limit his hand more and because Suzuki has a West tile he can call Pon when he wants even if he dismisses the chance to do it right now. This allows Akagi to assume that Washizu’s winning tile is not in Suzuki’s hand as Washizu is likely waiting for a situation where Suzuki draws his winning tile so after he calls Pon on Suzuki’s West he can immediately force Suzuki to discard his winning tile. This strategy allows for no Tsumo turns for Akagi as after Washizu calls Pon on Suzuki’s discard the turn goes back to Suzuki meaning Akagi can’t do anything about it, making Washizu’s vulnerable time period (having minimal tiles in his hand due to so many calls) irrelevant as he can immediately win afterward since Suzuki can immediately give him the win.

Akagi recognizes this thought process from Washizu and from this derives two insights, first it affirms his reasoning from before that Washizu does not have his winning tile inside Suzuki’s hand and secondly Akagi realizes how Washizu is confident that if he waits until Suzuki draws his winning tile his final road to victory will be untouchable by Akagi which Akagi aims to exploit with his head bump strategy.

Akagi needed Washizu to do his combo play with Suzuki using the 3man explained later, this was because Akagi knew Washizu if he pulls any of the 1,9 or word tiles Suzuki has, he can instantly win meaning Washizu has a very high chance of winning in the next couple of rounds. Akagi needed to prevent this so he planned to force Washizu into doing his final combo play with a certain tile regardless of Washizu’s draw in such a way that he can capitalize off of it.

Now Akagi reaches Tenpai and his wait can either be a 2man wait, a 1-4-7 man wait or a 3-6 man wait and Akagi chooses a 3-6 man wait and he declares Riichi too. Akagi intentionally ditches a 3 way wait, and discards his 1man for a 3 or 6man wait. Akagi chose the 3man since it was a tile Washizu already had and was also a tile he can confirm that Suzuki had. Akagi intentionally made a Riichi so he can use a 1000 points stick. With this consumption of a 1000 point stick Akagi’s score can drop to a point where as long as Washizu wins this round he does not need the Chanta to overcome Akagi in points. Akagi did this because he recognized how Washizu needing a Chanta is only a restriction to him because Akagi’s score if so high, so by calling Riichi it lowered the bar for Washizu to act, Akagi did this since he saw a transparent 3man inside Suzuki’s hand so if Washizu does not need a Chanta, on Suzuki’s very next turn he can tell Suzuki to discard the West tile then the 3man to win this round.

Akagi did Riichi not just to lower his score but to also apply time pressure to Washizu, making him think if he doesn’t win quickly Akagi might win before him. This time pressure coupled with it opening an immediate path to victory made Washizu force his win immediately, preventing any more further Tsumo from Washizu in which he might draw a tile to win himself or win from Suzuki in a different tile which would have ruined Akagi’s plans.

If Washizu wins with the 3man, because the 3man head will not be connected to any of the 1,9 or word tiles it makes Washizu’s hand lose the Chanta Yaku but will allow Washizu to win earlier since Suzuki already has the 3man. Akagi’s riichi makes Washizu want to win faster so Akagi gets no more Tsumo’s. From Washizu’s point of view Akagi doing a Riichi allowed Washizu to kill him without Chanta so Washizu will tell Suzuki to execute his finishing sequence. This was however beneficial for Akagi as he knew he could counter and capitalize off of Washizu’s planned final sequence with the headbump.

Akagi made sure Washizu won’t realize this is part of his strategy in multiple ways. First he misdirected Washizu from the fact that Akagi fully deduced Washizu’s hand by intentionally slowing down his discard of the West tile previously. Akagi also exploited Washizu’s psychology of panic and hastiness as well as his will to fully convert his advantage into victory. Akagi knew that from Washizu’s life filled with success he understands what differentiates a successful person and someone that is not, and one of those key traits is understanding how to cling to unrealistic possibilities is idiotic. Washizu with his superiority complex thinks Akagi’s Riichi was just a desperate attempt for a miracle such as Yasuoka pulling his winning tile miraculously, and Washizu projects all the losers he has saw in his life to Akagi to sooth his fear against Akagi which makes him blind to the fact that he is being manipulated, thinking he is superior to Akagi as Akagi wanted. Finally Akagi’s hand appears from Washizu’s point of view to not be waiting on a 3man.

This is because from Washizu’s perspective Akagi’s hand is like:

(5pin, 5pin, (O), 4pin, 4pin, (O), (O), 5man, 4man, 3man, 3man, (O), 1man)

Akagi is in tenpai and now assuming that Akagi ordered his tiles, which he always did and for this case he did too, Washizu can infer how Akagi is most likely not waiting on the 3man. This is because first the (O) tile between the 3man and 1man is definitely a 2man assuming the tiles are ordered. This is because Washizu has both the opaque 3man and 1man meaning the tile that can be opaque and between 3man and 1man is only the 2man. This fixes one of Akagi’s sequences to be (1man, 2man, 3man).

If the opaque tile between the 5pin and 4pin can either be a 5pin or 4pin since his hand is ordered, and if it was a 5pin the (5pin, 5pin, 5pin, 4pin, 4pin) shape forms so the two opaque tiles in the middle either becomes a (4pin, 6man) so the 4pin forms a triplet and the 6man forms a (6man, 5man, 4man, 3man) and needs to form one meld and a head, making it a 3man or 6man wait. If the two opaque tiles in the middle was (7man, 6man) then the 4pin will be the head and (7man, 6man, 5man, 4man, 3man) will need to form 2 melds, and the wait will be 2man, 5man and 8man. If the opaque tile between the 5pin and 4pin was a 4pin the (5pin, 5pin, 4pin, 4pin, 4pin) forms already forming one meld and a head. So the two opaque tiles in the middle can either form a (7man, 6man) like in the case before where the wait will be 2man, 5man and 8man.

There is also one more possibility for both scenarios which is that the middle part is a completely random formation of sequences but in that case because the opaque tiles comes after the manzi tile sequences, the opaque tiles are likely higher number manzu tiles or not manzu tiles all together meaning its not a 3man wait. The only way for Akagi’s hand to be a 3man wait is if the two opaque tiles are the 4pin and 6man but for Washizu this case is nigh-impossible. This is because when Akagi did Riichi he discarded his 1man tile, and for Akagi to intentionally chose to discard his 1man tile means that Akagi from this hand state (hand where Akagi can discard a tile to get to Tenpai) (the state when Akagi discarded a tile then declared Riichi):

(1man, 1man, 2man, 3man, 3man, 4man, 5man, 6man, 4pin, 4pin, 4pin, 5pin, 5pin)

From this hand Akagi will need to discard his 1man for the 3 or 6man wait to happen but in this state Akagi could have discarded the 3man so his hand forms:

(1man, 1man, 2man, 3man, 4man, 5man, 6man, 4pin, 4pin, 4pin, 5pin, 5pin, 5pin)

And form a wait of 1-4-7 manzu tiles. And due to Akagi’s misdirection from before Washizu thinks Akagi can’t predict his wait causing him to assume Akagi will definitely choose a wider wait of a 3 way wait than a 2 way wait for 3-6 man. Washizu’s superiority complex does not allow him to consider the possibility of Akagi reading him and intentionally choosing a lower wait hand and with Washizu’s projection of Akagi as a desperate loser, he will think Akagi would mindlessly chose the widest wait and because he didn’t Washizu assumes Akagi’s hand is not the 3-6 man wait and is something else. With Washizu’s assumption of Akagi not knowing his hand, this inference is perfectly valid and Washizu reasons that Akagi is not waiting on a 3man and is waiting on something else.

Washizu goes on to force Suzuki to discard a West tile, he calls a Pon on that, he discards his 1man and it goes to Suzuki’s turn again to which he discards the 3man to secure Washizu’s victory. Washizu calls Ron and gets excited that he won but as he tells Akagi that he is going to die Akagi reveals that he has head bumped Washizu’s Ron with his own Ron. Akagi’s Ron overrides Washizu’s Ron and Akagi wins the round and the Hanchan.
