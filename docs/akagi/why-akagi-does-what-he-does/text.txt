Why does Akagi do what he does 🀄

**Decided to Rant a little about Akagi as a character after seeing some YouTube and reddit comments low-key misunderstand him. There were also comments responding to some of them which all  brought up some great points and made me want to go in this spill. I'll be using some of those ideas** **and compiling them into this rant.**

**Facts about Akagi**

1. Akagi always strives for death. Akagi always puts himself or his opponents and "do our die" situations. He always strives to take everything or as much as everything as possible, always striving for the brink. By the end of the game either Akagi or his opponent must lose something important

2. The essence of gambling is a meaningless death. Akagi puts himself in situations of All or nothing. He himself says that he would do this until one day he fails, that one day he will lose and be broken. This is essentially a meaningless death, he is just walking towards death. Every win is just a postponement of death.

From these we can see that Akagi is a deeply existential character. He gravitates towards self-destruction, he resembles the mad samurai described in "Hagakure" who recklessly throws himself into battle.

**From what I've gathered throughout the story**

Akagi is aching to be understood, he is not a second-rate adrenaline junkie fool.. He is hungry for meaning. We don't know much about his background, but seeing he was born in 1945 japan, he was probably an orphan. I could even speculate a symbolic intent of the writer that akagi is a reincarnation of the spirit of kamikaze, since he was born the year the war ended. The comparison with hagakure is because it is mentioned in it that the way of samurai is in death "whether you are fighting one soldier or a hundred, it makes no difference" which is identical to akagi explaining to Ohgi that it didn't matter if he was killed by the Yakuza's because when it s time to die you should just die, whether your opponent is lunatic or whatever, just die.

Going back to the beginning real quick to when Akagi (13 at the time)  tells Nango he had to "die" if wants to win was him basically trying to tell him "i throw my life away in every move i make, that's how i can do moves that break the predictable flow of a game and make my mahjong hand pass through. Don't be controlled by death or you'll be its bitch and end up playing safe predictable moves".

5 years (Akagi is now 18) after the Ichikawa match Akagi started working in a factory because he lost the drive for gambling after beating Ichikawa. Akagi thinks of himself as one of a kind, something that is not human, or in his own words "A True Outlaw".  When he first met Ichikawa, I remember him saying "same kind of me? impossible, there shouldn't be. One of us is a fake". When he has beaten him he gives up on being understood by an equal because there are no equals to him, just fakes. Akagi is alone. Basically he was just drifting through those 6 years, working from factory to factory, getting in fights for stimulation "but generally that's 1960s japan so the streets are dangerous and he is always surrounded, he is not the petty kind that picks fights, he probably beats them in gambles and they try to get back their money. Basically the match against Ichikawa made him lose the drive to strive. He saw no value in pursuing meaning anymore, so he settled for living as a nobody.

He accepted the match against washizu because in his eyes this man may be the equal he was always looking for, a man to play a death match against, a man that can understand him.

How can akagi be understood? Simply by being FORCED into looking inside himself. How? By having someone who is his mental/spiritual equal to challenge him into confronting the parts in himself he never tapped into, and how can this be done exactly? Through the greatest force in life.. DEATH. How do you harness death without a 100% chance of dying? Gambling!

That's the kind of man he always wanted to encounter. A man who will give him a real encounter with his own self. That's what he meant when he has beaten urabe and then says "this man will never experience genuine anger, just as i will never experience a genuine match"
