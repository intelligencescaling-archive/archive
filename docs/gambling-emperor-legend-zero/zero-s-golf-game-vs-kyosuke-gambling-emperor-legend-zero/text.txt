Golf game. Gambling Emperor Legend Zero part 2 chapter 1-9

Before the game, Zero sent Suezaki to investigate Kyosuke, a rising golf star. Suezaki managed to catch Kyosuke cheating during a casual game and later taunted him in private. This unsettled Kyosuke’s pride as a golf prodigy, which became a key aspect of Zero’s strategy.

Suezaki proposed a golf match to Kyosuke’s father, with the seniors’ right to stay as the stake. The player opposing Kyosuke would be Zero, rumored to be undefeated in the world of gambling. Despite never having held a golf club in his life, Zero was confident in his victory, which fueled Kyosuke’s anxiety.

The match time was agreed to be "at 5 tomorrow." The manager confirmed that Suezaki had reserved a golf range at 5 PM.

A few hours later, Suezaki woke Kyosuke up with a announcement to start the game. Suezaki had said the game would be at "5" but never specified 5 PM. The reserved golf range was never confirmed to be the match location. In reality, the game would take place at 5 AM, when the green was frozen and solid like asphalt—terrain completely unfamiliar to Kyosuke.

On the way to the agreed-upon Hole 3, Kyosuke requested to play at Hole 7 instead, claiming Hole 3 was under repair and they should use a proper hole. The real reason was that Hole 3 had a green ridiculously close to the tee, something even a beginner could manage. In contrast, Hole 7 had a tee positioned so far from the hole that it was impossible for a beginner to clear the gap. Zero accepted the request.

Kyosuke’s first shot missed the mark due to the solid green. Zero’s shot seemed even worse, as his ball went over the cliff. At this moment, Zero recited the golf club’s house rules: unlike the junior leagues Kyosuke was used to, if a player’s shot went off the cliff, their next shot would be taken from a special tee with penalty strokes added. The special tee for Hole 7 was located near the green—where Zero had been practicing putting after they agreed to the match. This meant Zero had predicted Kyosuke would choose Hole 7 over Hole 3.

Thanks to his practice, Zero’s putt left his ball one stroke away from the hole. Due to the penalty, Kyosuke had up to three shots. His first landed on the green. Before his second shot, Zero provoked Kyosuke, saying his status as a golf prodigy meant nothing because Zero was the one experienced with putting on frozen greens. Kyosuke then tried to mimic Zero’s putting posture, only for his ball to barely move. Upon inspecting Zero’s club, Kyosuke discovered it was specially weighted to be heavier. The extra weight gave Zero’s putts more force than his posture suggested, meaning Zero had anticipated Kyosuke’s attempt to copy him.

Suddenly, Zero proposed raising the bet by an additional 10 million yen. Kyosuke, only one putt away from the hole, agreed. Humiliated, losing to a near-amateur, and pressured by both the money and his father’s expectations, Kyosuke began overanalyzing every detail of his next putt. In his panic, he failed to inspect the hole, which had a piece of ice secretly placed inside. As a result, Kyosuke’s putt was too strong, bouncing out of the hole. Meanwhile, Zero’s gentle putt landed perfectly, securing his victory.

When Kyosuke accused Zero of cheating, Zero shut him down, arguing that as a beginner, he should have had no chance of winning—yet Kyosuke, as a professional, had failed to uncover a single one of Zero’s tricks. Any one of them, if exposed, could have secured Kyosuke’s victory. With that, Kyosuke accepted the match result.
