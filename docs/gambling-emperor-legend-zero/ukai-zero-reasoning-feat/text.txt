Here is one of Ukai Zero's best Reasoning feats, known as the Kanji reasoning feat:
Brief context: Basically in the Anchor game round, Ukai Zero and 2 other intellectually average friends were tied firmly to an anchor floor where there would be a very sharp anchor dangling on top rotating like a pendulum, Ukai Zero and his friends were asked trivial quiz questions and some of them got unreasonably difficult and arbitrary; Ukai Zero and his 2 friends were FORCED to take turns to answer each question and they were not allowed to tell each other the correct answers, if they got the answer right, the anchor would go up until they win officially after finishing the quiz questions, if they answer the question wrong the anchor will go down bit by bit until it reaches low enough to swing the anchor blade that cuts the 3 men’s head off like a guillotine.
The game master asks a RIDICULOUSLY unreasonable quiz question:

“What is the Kanji character on the back of my head?”

Ukai Zero with his two friends pinned to the ground while the quiz master was standing on a platform way way higher than them just simply makes this question extremely seemingly unreasonable, however somehow Ukai Zero manages to very logically figure this out from very limited information alongside usage of very airtight explanatory power.

Reasoning Part:

P1 → The black man claims that he got his weird-looking hairstyle back in high school which was apparently very famous for its violent delinquents.

and…

The black man claims that he hung out with the baddies, and that during that time it was trendy for kids to write words with their hair which apparently was efficient in gaining respect the more you did it.

and…

The black man claims that he was the boss among the juvenile delinquents, and had absolute power alongside many underlings.

P2 → The black man’s appearance looked as if he wasn’t particularly good at fighting.

P3 → The black man has a bad personality.

C1 → The black man was actually the little runt of the group and was a massive bully victim.

P4 → The concept of the trend of “all the kids making out letters with their hair” which the black man claims sounds ridiculously stupid and it sounds dubious for anyone to want to do that.

C2 → The black man was badly bullied and forced to put on his stupid-looking kanji character hair-style all alone as a bully victim.

P5 → The bullies probably tried to shave words like “idiot” or “retarded dog” onto the black man’s head

and…

In order to prevent that, the black man would’ve done something to his hair first.

P6 → The black man still kept this dumb-looking hairstyle even up to this date.

P7 → The black man definitely wouldn’t have wanted to be marked with something of disgrace that reminded him of the times when he was bullied.

P8 → If the black man used his hair-style as a hidden message that only he himself could read, then that would be his only way to cope being bullied every single day and secretly laugh behind the bullies who didn’t understand it.

C3 → The kanji on the back of the man’s head is something he’s proud of and a sign of his triumph. It was a mark of his retaliation against the people who bullied him.

P9 → If the kanji letter 城 (shiro) is flipped upside down, the “shiro” in his name would be read backwards/reversed.

P10 → The shiro kanji read backwards would become “roshi”.

P11 → The black man’s name is “小太郎” （kotarou)

P12 → And if you read the black man’s name in hiragana pronunciation alone “kotarou” with “roshi” inputted correctly and coherently, you get “koroshitarou”, which turns to 殺したろう , which means “to kill”. This sounds like a very triumphant message that is very well hidden.

 C4 → The kanji on the back of the black man’s head is “城” (shiro) written upside down.
