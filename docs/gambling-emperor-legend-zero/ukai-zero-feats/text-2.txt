Ukai Zero Feat – Jack Room

Brief Summary of Jack:

Jack is the room master and the undefeated champion of his own game. He hides the right side of his face behind long hair due to severe burn scars that left his face disfigured — a source of deep insecurity. Jack has only ever lost once, which cost him the pinky finger on his right hand.

Equipment (Pic 1 + 2):

Chisel (used by The Attacker)

12 Iron Plates (used by The Defender)

The slots for the fingers (used by The Defender)

A Roullete with J and another Symbol

(The Roullete decides who goes first. If the pointer lands on J, Jack is the first Attacker; the other symbol means Zero starts.)

Rules of the Game:

There are 3 total rounds. Each round, the roles of Attacker and Defender alternate.

The Defender places one hand into the slots, while the Attacker uses a chisel to try to chop off a finger. The first to sever a Defender's finger wins.

The Attacker gets 2 strikes per round.

The Defender can hide iron plates inside the slots to protect their fingers.

Round 1: Jack Attacks First

Before the game even started, Zero saw a guy who had already lost a finger to Jack. Jack “accidentally” flaunted his left hand — the one he uses to bet — which still had all its fingers, and also showed off his collection of severed fingers to pressure Zero by implying he's undefeated in this game. Despite this, Zero remained calm and joined the game (pay attention to his hand) (Pic 3 + 4).

After understanding the rules, Zero quickly spotted the core challenge of the game. Since there are only 12 iron plates, the best a player can do is survive until the end of Round 2. In the final round, only 2 plates remain — meaning 3 fingers will be left exposed — making a win almost impossible.

(3 rounds = 15 finger positions → 12 plates = 3 exposed fingers)

Zero’s initial strategy was fairly basic: use 4 plates in this round. That way, he’d still have enough for the next two rounds, and it was a smart balance between protection and scouting the opponent’s habits.

→ If Zero can gather enough info about Jack’s attacking tendencies this round, he could then build manipulation or deception tactics and just repeat the 4-plate strategy in the remaining rounds. That would make the 12 plates just enough.

When the game began, Jack acted casually and annoyingly slow. He randomly blurted out that he “knew” Zero left his ring finger and middle finger unguarded, and somehow he was actually right. Lucky bastard.

But just as Jack was about to strike, he stopped 3cm short. Then he went on a monologue about how he could read people’s emotions to figure out their weak points. However, because of Zero’s solid poker face, Jack doubted himself and assumed those two fingers (ring and middle) weren’t the right targets after all (Pic 5 + 6).

Jack continued his analysis, saying that the ring finger is the least valuable compared to the index and thumb, which are more critical for hand function. But because he was wary of reverse psychology — and Zero’s stoic expression was too trustworthy — Jack ended up choosing the index and thumb instead… and failed.

Even when Jack said things that seemed favorable to him, Zero kept his expression completely neutral, not giving away whether it helped or hurt him (Pic 7 + 8).

→ Jokes aside, if you listen closely to Jack’s logic — that the ring and middle fingers are the least valuable, and so often used for betting — then his idea of fake-attacking them to bait a reaction and then pressing psychological pressure has some theoretical merit.

→ At this point, all Zero needs to do is play into Jack’s “strategy” — by staying silent and letting Jack think his “reads” are working — and he’ll safely make it to Round 2.

When it was Zero’s turn to attack, there wasn’t much worth mentioning — he basically held back, choosing not to actually try cutting Jack’s finger and only used the handle of the chisel.

However, what stood out was that Jack used up 5 iron plates in just the first round, which raised some eyebrows.

Round 2: Zero Attacks First

This time, Zero figured that with only 7 iron plates left, Jack would have to leave at least 1–2 fingers exposed, so that he’d still have at least 3 plates for the final round. But when Zero guessed wrong on both fingers, he realized Jack was still using 5 plates, just like in the previous round (Pic 9).

Jack Attacks

When they switched roles, Zero noticed that his own protective iron plate felt unusually cold. At this point, Zero started to form a theory and strategy to win (Pic 10).

During Jack’s attack turn, he revealed his trump card — a thermal camera hidden in his right eye, which he’d previously covered with his hair, claiming it was due to a burn scar and his self-consciousness. That was a lie.

All the delays and “emotional reading strategy” at the start of the game were just distractions. What Jack was really waiting for was a moment when Zero either looked away or closed his eyes in fear — a natural human reaction to danger (Pic 11). That gave Jack the chance to scan Zero’s hand and then check a screen connected to the thermal camera to find out which fingers weren’t protected by the cold iron plates (Pic 12 + 13).

But then, Jack chose Zero’s index finger — which did show heat on the camera — and got it wrong. That’s when Zero exposed Jack’s entire trick, from the thermal camera to the supposedly intact left hand — which was actually fake.

→ First, based on the earlier analysis, Zero had already begun to suspect Jack’s "emotional reading" was just a cover. He deduced the use of a thermal camera due to Jack’s unusually long periods of observation, and the fact that Jack used full iron plate protection for both of his own defense rounds, which seemed wasteful — unless he had a cheat that gave him full confidence in winning (Pic 14 + 15).

→ Next, Zero noticed Jack’s dominant hand was his right, based on how he held the chisel. That raised suspicion — who would ever risk their dominant hand’s fingers first in a game like this? (Pic 16).

→ Zero figured Jack must’ve bet and lost all his left-hand fingers in the past, then replaced them with fakes to deceive newcomers.

→ So, Zero baited Jack into choosing his index finger by pretending to look away, triggering Jack’s trap. At the same time, Zero crossed his fingers so the iron plate covered what appeared to be an unprotected finger.

→ When Jack struck, Zero quickly rotated his fingers to position the iron plate horizontally, completely blocking the attack, and then called out Jack’s deception (Pic 17 + 18).
