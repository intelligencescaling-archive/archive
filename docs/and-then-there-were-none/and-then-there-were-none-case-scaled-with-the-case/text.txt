And Then There Were None Case - u/Good-Fennel7417 scale

Inspired by the Johan post and obv the OG scaling post [https://www.reddit.com/r/IntelligenceScaling/s/M3eTMETDC2](https://www.reddit.com/r/IntelligenceScaling/s/M3eTMETDC2).

Contains spoilers about the book, I'll refer to the culprit as UN Owen but some spoilers are inevitable anyways. Since there is no detective on the island, this post will assume that the detective is solving the case from an outside perspective AFTER the crime has been completed, similar to the police at the end of the book. >!Also not counting the existence of the confession bottle because that's just goofy for this purpose lol!<

Lack of Evidence: High. The sequence of murders was designed to make sure that all ten people on the island could have committed any one of the murders while they were still alive. No real evidence pointing to the killer exists.

Information Uncertainty: Mid. Not a lot of info exists, but the info that does exist is definitely misleading. The order of the deaths is not accurate to what the victims recorded in their diaries/journals or whatever, which would make any detective trying to solve this have some trouble.

Suspect Narrowing: Low. Simply because the only possible culprit is one of the ten people on the island.

Crime Reconstruction: High. The first 6-7 murders can be recreated fairly easily, but problems arise in the last 3-4 murders. There is pretty much no logical explanation as to the exact order and method that the culprit used to finish off the last few people, outside of the trick that UN Owen used. >!Wargrave faking his death with Armstrong's help!<

Misdirection: Mid-high. The culprit is not directly in the list of suspects listed at the end of the book by the detectives, but if they expanded their view a bit more then its quite possible to consider the true culprit as a viable suspect. It is still not easy though because of the fake death order that was used.

Investigation Interference: Nonexistent. Due to the nature of the crime, it is impossible for UN Owen to alter any details of the crime during the course of the investigation. >!Because Wargrave is dead, and so is the only accomplice that he used!<

Danger: Nonexistent. Same reason as investigation interference.

Culprit Resources: Mid-high. UN Owen had pretty much all the time in the world to plan this crime, and to scout out the victims. We don't necessarily know how much money they have, but it's quite probable it isn't a small amount. We know they have enough resources to buy a literal island with a large mansion on it.

Detective resources and police cooperation are not applicable here. The book only covers the events of the crime and there is no real detective present. We are given the perspective of the police department at the very end, but that's about it. Given the setting of the story (1939) the detective would only have the forensics technology of that period, and any other form of technology, which could also raise the difficulty.

Overall: Mid-high. Could definitely argued to be higher in difficulty, but it is definitely achievable to solve for someone with good enough reasoning. The book does discuss a possible way to arrive at the correct conclusion. >!Also the motive lines up with the profession of Wargrave, so I could see that being beneficial to solving it as well.!< Admittedly, I only read the book once and didn't analyze it in super fine detail, so if I got anything wrong please feel free to correct.
