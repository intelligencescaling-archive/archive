The scholarly reputation of Professor James Moriarty within mathematical physics is established by his authorship of The Dynamics of an Asteroid, first mentioned in Sir Arthur Conan Doyle’s 1915 novel The Valley of Fear. Within the narrative canon, Sherlock Holmes describes this book as ascending to such rarefied heights of pure mathematics that no critic in the scientific press was capable of evaluating or criticizing its formulations. This masterwork serves to establish Moriarty’s intellectual equivalence to Holmes, supplementing his earlier academic milestone: a treatise on the binomial theorem composed at the age of twenty-one, which gained a "European vogue" and secured him a mathematics chair at a provincial university with an annual salary of £700. Dark rumors finally led to his resignation and he moved to London to coach an army coach. Moriarty’s mathematical genius remained unchallenged, eventually finding expression as the organizing intellect of London’s underworld.

1) Mathematical Framework of Celestial Mechanics and Perturbation Theory : To reconstruct the mathematical content of The Dynamics of an Asteroid, one must integrate the classical mechanics of the late Victorian era. As proposed by physicist Alejandro Jenkins, the singular use of "asteroid" in the title strongly suggests a theoretical focus on the "dynamics of a particle". In 19th-century scientific terminology, a "particle" (or "material point") referred to a solid body of fixed mass whose state can be fully characterized by one position and one velocity vector, rendering its physical dimensions, shape, and internal rotation irrelevant. Standard Cambridge textbooks of the era, such as those by Tait and Steele (1856) or Edward John Routh (1898), used this formulation to simplify mathematical derivations. By treating a minor planet as an idealized particle subject only to the gravitational attraction of the Sun and minor planetary perturbations, Moriarty's work aligned itself with pure mathematics rather than empirical, observational astronomy.
