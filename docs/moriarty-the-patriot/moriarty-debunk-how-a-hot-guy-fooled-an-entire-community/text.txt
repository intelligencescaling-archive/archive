# Scandal in the British Empire

The ballroom scheme relies on the noble accepting the role of the "Prince of Crime" and subsequently killing Albert after being provoked. But why should we assume that the noble would necessarily react this way? He could simply have left Albert alive. The entire scheme depends on predicting an individual's response to a situation that doesn't guarantee the desired [outcome.You](http://outcome.You) can't build a scheme around such a weak prediction and then expect people to call it flawless foresight.

But the worst part comes when Moriarty starts discussing the history of the Reign of Terror in France to establish an analogy between his scheme and Robespierre, effectively pushing the "Moriarty = Robespierre" argument.

This is precisely why we need to prioritize demonstrated feats over narrative statements.

At one point, Moriarty is presented as an expert on history with a supposedly near-perfect understanding of it. Yet his historical interpretation here is questionable. The Reign of Terror didn't magically unite nobles and commoners. It ended with Thermidor, followed by Napoleon's rise, and the idea that Robespierre was an English agent was an invented accusation. So much for that nigh-perfect historical understanding.

# Merchant of London

This is where the manga loses any remaining sense of a meaningful approach to intellectual schemes. Assuming it had one beforehand, of course.

Moriarty uses a pound-of-flesh contract to trap a noble, except there's a rather substantial problem: the contract's enforceability under English law is highly questionable. Baxter's steakhouse habits don't establish what a contract means. Contracts are interpreted objectively, not according to whatever private assumptions one of the parties happens to have.

Then there's the issue of the child plaintiff. A court wouldn't simply accept a child acting independently in the manner Moriarty's scheme requires. Even the document produced by Moriarty's own scalers acknowledges that orphans had no legal standing to pull this off. And somehow, Moriarty also decides to sue before the loan has even defaulted. Wonderful.

The confession obtained by threatening to cut someone's neck in a courtroom is equally ridiculous, to put it mildly. And the whole business involving the manor supposedly belonging to a friend raises another set of problems.

# Final Problem.

# By far, the worst plan. But I myself have lost a lot of braincells after seeing how dumb the characters were in the previous arcs, so let me allow one of my friends how bad the aim of Moriarty’s entire scheme in the story is :

Let's tackle the core misunderstanding the author, and as a consequence, WJM, seem to have, class division is NOT because nobles and peasants simply lack a "common ground" between themselves. They assume the divide was driven by petty things like prejudice and bad attitude. The divide exists because of exploitive economic incentives, and the oppressive system London had is what created such terrible nobles. There are no good nobles, because the system would filter them out. Just like today, there are no ethical billionaires, or even hundred millionaires.

WJM's bum ass thought that by eliminating bad nobles and "uniting" both the peasants and upper class, an era of class unity/change could come about. This is honestly as stupid, and as naive as light thinking that killing criminals would prevent crime absolutely. WJM did not properly identify why the divide existed, did not properly address or dismantle the root cause of why corruption was so prevalent, did not eliminate the financial incentives that brings bad people to power, etc.

By framing the issue around individual morality rather than systemic failure, his plan inadvertently sanitizes the rest of the British aristocracy. Once the "evil" lords and the "Lord of Crime" are gone, the remaining nobles get to pretend the problem has been solved, without giving up a single penny of their wealth or facing any repercussions.

Unity only came about because the author is stupid and molded the plot to WJM's scheme. Just like how he did with noahtic and JTR.

National unity works for wars, not class structures. History shows us that a external/common threat can temporarily unite a country (like an invading army ig), but that unity is fleeting. As soon as the immediate crisis passes, society snaps right back to its baseline material conflicts. (Example, Britian after WWII)
