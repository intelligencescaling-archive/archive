I was wrong. This guy probably had the biggest aura loss in SCD. Both in an outsmarting sense and as a character.

Jonathan Krantz from Prison Break.

I said before that Red John probably had the biggest "Aura loss". Some people agreed with me, others said it was other characters, but now I honestly believe it was this man.

Throughout the early seasons this man is slowly built up as the big bad antagonist of the series. He managed to build The Company, an extremely influential secret society that has the U.S. president under it's thumb, orchestrates worldwide events such as wars and is beyond feasible reach of the law. He's shown to be extremely mysterious and cautious, refusing to even speak out loud on the off chance one of his associates might be recording the conversation, or ever having his name mentioned. He is only ever referred to as "The General", which implies he has some military background as well.

So this man seemingly singlehandedly established the most lethal and mysterious secret organisation in his world, has the political knowledge to manipulate events on a global scale, and was likely a military badass in his younger days. He sounds awesome, right? An impressive high tier! Truly capable of being Scolfield's newest and biggest enemy! Right...?

Then S4 happens.

His whole schtick about super secrecy is completely thrown out the window. Pretty much every character knows who he is, even relatively low level agents, and he openly uses his voice all the time and meets with his people in person, and his associates know about his familial relations as well. This wouldn't be too bad by itself in fairness, but you'd think he'd have some solid tactical prowess and leadership skills, right...?

No.

This man gets outsmarted time and time again and again and again by Michael. I know that Michael Scolfield is considered a super high tier by many, but the way it's done isn't even clever. For instance: The Company's precious treasure (Scylla) is being accessed and he doesn't know by who or how... so he goes down to look in person. Just for context, Scylla requires several keys to access and Krantz has the only one that hasn't been compromised. So he walks it right to where he knows the enemy is. He literally has an army of guards in the building with him and he decides to take like two down with him for some reason instead of just... y'know sending down about 20 with assault rifles to dispatch all the intruders and not bring the last key into direct access of the enemy. To make him sound even stupider, he then gloate to Michael about how he's directly in the Company's building and will neeeeeever escape.

...bro. Do you seriously think he doesn't have a plan for that? Krantz knows how smart Michael is. In S2 he deliberately sent him to a particular prison knowing full well that Michael was going to find a way out because his plan requires the prison break. That was back when he was cool and seemed to know what he was doing. But here he's just like "hurr ur dumb cuz u didn't think of a way out of my building I win!!" It's just embarrassing. This  is just one example by the way.

He actually spends the better part of S4 nearly getting killed by everyone and being outsmarted in the dumbest ways.

Putting outsmarting antifeats aside, he's just lame now anyway. His own associates conspire against him, he's shown to be into women a third his age, he cries when he's about to die, he gets sent to prison and gets manipulated and outsmarted by T-Bag of all people. In addition to all of this, it's also implied that he depends on Christinna for all the clever strategies and such, so he can't even get narrative implications.

These would all be perfectly acceptable character flaws and all, but it just feels like such a jarring contrast to the mysterious, scary mastermind he was seemingly built up to be. It's not done intentionally either like with Red John, who uses his mythology to appear awesome when he's actually pathetic, as the narrative seems to act like he's still a scary threat but the writing just takes away all his intimidation factor for me.

As such, I believe this fellow had the "most generational aura loss".
