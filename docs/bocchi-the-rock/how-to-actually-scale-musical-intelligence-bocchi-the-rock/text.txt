How to actually scale musical intelligence.

Musical intelligence is a very real thing, but most people ignore it because you can't do many cool things with it outside of learning songs by ear. But, what if we wanted to scale Bocchi vs Yui? I know how to.

 Most music anime talks about western rock and pop music. This music is what is known as melody dominant homophony. Melody is usually what is being sung or soloed. Homophony is accompaniment in the form of many notes played simultaneously called chords.

 I think the broad skills in melody dominant homophony are:

\- Ear Training (Tuning, Learning by ear, Improvising)

\- Chords (Knowledge of different kinds, knowing how they go together)

\- Melody (Phrasing, Scales)

\- Drums/Rhythm

\#Ear Training

 Ear training in real life is sorted into the following categories:

\- Tone Deaf (0)

\- Untrained (1)

\- Relative Pitch (2)

\- Pseudo Perfect Pitch (3)

\- Perfect Pitch (4)

 Tone deaf people have a hard time knowing if an interval is going up or down. A relative pitch person can tell you what chords a song uses, but not the key. Pseudo perfect pitch allows you to know what key a song is in, but only for certain instruments.

 Perfect pitch is rare, and allows you to tell what key any song is in.

\#Chords

 The most basic chords are major and minor. Major sounds bright and happy, and it works well with the major pentatonic scale. Minor sounds sad and dark, and it works well with the minor pentatonic scale.

 Intermediate chord-masters may be aware of variations such as suspended (sus) chords and extensions (7th, 9th, etc.).

 In music school, one of the last things you are expected to master is chromaticism. Chromaticism is playing out of key in a way that sounds good. This can be done by changing a normally major key to minor or vice versa, and you can even use chromaticism to prepare changing the key entirely.

\- Major/Minor Chords (1)

\- Chord Variations (2)

\- Chromaticism (3)

\#Melody

 Melody is less straightforward. It is more like a collection of skills than a straight path. So treat it like a diagnostic test where someone has a score gotten by meeting checkmarks:

\- Understanding of phrasing (call and response)

\- Understanding of modes

\- Understanding of improvisation

\- Understanding of scales

\#Drums

 Drumming isn't a skill everyone knows, but even the best guitarists know a bit. Snares often occur on beats 2 and 4, but kicks occur on 1 and 3. Knowing that allows a guitarist to stay in time. Consider this scale:

1. Knows nothing about drums.

2. Knows how to complement the drums.

3. Can play the drums okay.

4. Can play the drums well.

\#Bocchi Analysis

 Unlike Yui, Bocchi is never mentioned as having perfect pitch. Let's assume she has relative pitch. (2)

 Bocchi's songs use a lot of varied chords and sometimes chromaticism. She is intermediate-advanced in terms of chords. (3)

 Bocchi's solos clearly show phrasing and improvisation, and some scale knowledge. (3)

 Bocchi's drum knowledge is largely unknown, so we can assume she is at 2. (2)

 Let us add the numbers to get a final score of 10, with the beginner or average musician being most probably around 6.5.
