**I say right away that there will be no detailed retelling of the manga here, I will just refer to the chapters and briefly explain the essence of the situation. I will also not quote phrases and dialogues verbatim, my goal is to keep the main point, and you can check for yourself how accurately I did it by reading the chapters that I mentioned.**

I'll say a few words about the inspector himself. Tsukume is the most overrated character that is currently popular. Most people consider him to be at a high level, and on YouTube I've often seen people put him above Akiyama Shinichi, Tokuchi Toua, and Patrick Jane. As someone who has read the manga, I can safely say that Tsukume is overrated in all categories, with the possible exception of AC. The police in the manga are complete idiots, their incompetence is simply amazing.

Most of the inspector's reasoning is based on pure luck (not intuition, but luck). And some other arguments in general should not have worked, but I will talk about this further.

1. Chapter 9. Inspector Tsukume looks at the surveillance camera footage of Kusakabe Makoto (the main character of the manga) in the store. After that, he says:

*-It's raining outside, but this guy is dry. How is that possible?*

The problem is that the author of the manga just forgot about what he wrote in the plot. Let's go back a bit, namely to Chapter 7. We see that when Makoto went back in time, he found himself outside, where it was raining! Also on page 30, we see that when Makota entered the store, the time on the clock was 0:01. One minute has passed, which is certainly a very short period of time, but given the force of the rain, Makota should have gotten wet.

2. Chapter 12. Tsukume once again puts forward an absolutely baseless theory about Makota's guilt. Most importantly, other investigators point out the obvious logical holes in the theory (Makota had no motive; Makota had an alibi for the time of Morishita's murder; I have already explained why Tsukume's reasoning about the CCTV cameras is invalid; his wallet was found at the fireworks festival on the 15th), and he doesn't even attempt to refute them! He himself understands that his theory is baseless and lacks evidence, but in Chapter 17, he will complain about why his superiors do not accept his version!

3. Chapter 13. There are three unclear points at once. First, Tsukube and his assistant realize that the footprint of the envelope found at the crime scene matches the shape of the envelope from the city hospital. I'm telling you right away, I'm not from Japan and I don't know how it works there, but do Japanese hospitals use an envelope with a special unique shape?  I just really don't understand, can't an envelope used in hospitals and an envelope used for letters match in size? Why did the investigators decide that it was definitely an envelope from the hospital and they did not consider other versions?

The second point: Tsukume claims that Shiori is connected with these murders and Makota kills for her sake. How did he know that? Tell me, what was the basis of this conclusion? This would make sense if the money was missing from the dead, it would be possible to recreate some kind of logical chain like: Makota's sister is very ill, money is needed for her treatment, which Makota does not have, Makota decides to kill people and take money from them to help her sister. There's a lot to find fault with in this chain, but it makes at least some sense. But the problem is that the money wasn't lost from the dead, it's just a baseless guess that Tsukume won't justify in any way.
Even his partner in chapter 17 tells him that his whole version is just empty guesses. In the same 17th chapter, Tsukume says: *"When it came to his sister, he started acting aggressively. She is the key to the solution!"*\- again, on what grounds did he understand this?

And the third point: Tsukume says that Makota could kill a man just for his sister. You know, I need to say a few words about Makota himself. Surely there will be his defenders here who will say: "Makota committed murders only for the sake of his sister," to which I boldly reply that this is not the case. No matter how anyone tries to justify Makota (including the manga author himself), he is a sadist. He commits murder with a ferocity that no normal person is capable of. Makota is not just a guy who finds himself in difficult living conditions and has to commit murder to save his sister, he is exactly that kind of maniac who likes human suffering.

4. Chapter 18. On page 9, Tsukume says:

*"I was able to find a recording on the cameras of how Makota left the park, but I did not find a recording of how he entered there. How is this possible? After all, all exits are under video surveillance."*

Why didn't he even think about the fact that there might be a hole in the fence somewhere, through which Makota climbed into the park? Or why couldn't Makota climb over the fence? Is that why Tsukume is considering some completely delusional versions with teleportation and flight, but not considering completely truthful versions?

In summary, I want to say that Tsukuma is very much overrated. It is, at best, at the Canon L level, although I would put it at the Canon Light level. The first 20 chapters of all his conclusions are based on pure luck. Not on evidence, not on psychology, but on pure luck.
