Patrick Jane: An Apologia for the Fake Psychic

PREAMBLE TO A FUTURE DOC ABOUT PATRICK. IT IS TIME FOR JUSTICE.

Someone has to come out in defense of Patrick. An underestimated and reviled character, indecorously classified as "overrated" or "mid". I am not here to expound on great feats, nor to narrate the plot of a chapter focusing on the objective complexity of many of his strategies. No, not that. I come to deepen into a single concept regarding Patrick: his fluid intelligence.

I am going to defend his place within the S-tier using only his fluid intelligence, without the need to expose a dozen of his feats.

Patrick was raised in a chaotic environment, lacking in morals, without a mother (who abandoned him) and whose only father figure taught him that he must adapt and control the losers or be one of them; a man who only saw him as a tool. Taking this into account, plus the key fact that he never went to school or any similar institution, knowing that Patrick possesses encyclopedic knowledge in several areas (for example, he knows all of Shakespeare’s works verbatim and in order), masters memory techniques like the Method of Loci, knows an incredible amount of "tricks" (sleight of hand) and is abnormally competent in observation, logical reasoning, social and emotional intelligence, the only possible (and coherent) conclusion for a character like Patrick, is that his fluid intelligence is an anomaly.

1— "Why do you take for granted that he learned practically everything in his life by himself and how does that relate to his fluid intelligence?"

It is an obvious question, of course. I answer: how else would a child born in the heart of deceit and greed itself have time and freedom to learn all the works of Shakespeare while mastering mnemonic methods, psychology, and polishing his logical reasoning? The only logical answer is that his brain is an anomaly.

But let's suppose he didn't learn Shakespeare by heart and without help, just reading for pleasure, before his adulthood. Let's suppose that as a teenager he did not yet master "tricks" or mnemonics. Let's even suppose that his Memory Palace was still an experiment. Even with all that, being a teenager (basing myself on the chapter where, in 1986, he is approximately 16 years old), being seriously considered a psychic, earning a living with his act and structurally mastering cold reading (observation + reasoning + theory of mind) and logical reasoning (predominantly induction and abduction), requires that, consequently, Jane be an innate talent; a prodigy.

For a child forced to learn the arts of deception, who probably had a lot of unorganized free time, in a hostile and chaotic environment, without help from his father or mentors beyond being "the psychic boy", to be able to read people with supernatural ease since he has had consciousness (Jane's father makes it clear when he mentions that Jane "is no longer a child", ergo, he implicitly makes it clear that Patrick since childhood was already extremely intelligent) and possesses elite observation skills, emotional intelligence and logical reasoning... he must, no matter what, have not only massive fluid intelligence, but a colossal learning ability.

If we take into account that he probably studied in his spare time, observing everything around him, interacting with people at the fair and making logical connections, it is clear that everything starts from an intrinsic ability for induction and abduction. That is to say, everything later in his life, including learning all the works of Shakespeare, mastering mnemonics (especially the Method of Loci), sleight of hand and encyclopedic knowledge of various areas, was a product of his nature itself. It is not just "he trained his skills", his environment itself awakened those skills due to his own innate capacity and, a product of the noxious environment and his need to survive, he exploited his fluid intelligence to the maximum. If we add to that a dedicated and curious person: we have Jane.

2— Why do I think this is, in itself, a (narrative-logical) feat?

While it is a product of the use of logical reasoning and an analysis of Jane's character and his context, it is still a feat in itself. It could be composed as follows:

Jane is born in a hostile and chaotic environment where he can "die" at any moment > forced to adapt and due to his nature as a prodigy learns everything necessary to survive > all this with only observation, pure logical reasoning, memory and his capacity to learn > triumphs as a "psychic boy" and later as a renowned "real psychic" making himself a millionaire based on his pure intellect (and in the process mastering on his own, since he abandoned his father, various methods).

That would be the "novel situation" that requires his fluid intelligence. And it is a feat in itself. Let's take into account that, due to the essence of his upbringing and personality, Jane surely did not learn everything in a "classic" way (reading), but through pure observation and reasoning. That elevates the potential feat even more. We are talking about a character who reached adulthood with extraordinary knowledge based on observation and induction/abduction (primarily).

3— Plus: how does all this connect with his emotional intelligence, social intelligence and "theory of mind"?

Due to his complex situation, Jane had to polish his emotional and social capacities (not only to survive as a "psychic boy", but to survive as Patrick Jane). This could be, potentially, another narrative-logical feat:

Jane was raised with exaggerated pressure, having to be his father's only way out and the only potential way out for his future. He learned to lie, to predict behaviors and "read minds". All this while encapsulating his true emotions and ideas. For years he had to not only manage his father's emotions, adapt to him and adapt to the frantic pace of the fair; he also had to manage himself, be a kind of chameleon.
Interacting with dozens of people a year, learning what works and what doesn't, emulating emotions, acting, creating a gigantic "psychological database", a kind of list of archetypes, surviving... In that way he developed extraordinary social and emotional skills. Each individual is a new piece, a new riddle, a new form of interaction and a different stimulus. Jane was raised in the worst environment for a child, but in the best to develop all his skills as a mentalist. Again: by pure intrinsic ability.

4— Conclusion

If we take this analysis into account, Jane should not only be considered a serious character (by any thinking human being), but he relaxedley maintains his place in the High Tier... and I could probably debate that he would enter the S-Tier.
If we take into account the two narrative-logical feats that I proposed (vaguely, I could expand more), Jane would increase his level in:

FSIQ, Social Intelligence, EQ, AC, Reasoning, Thinking, Fluid Intelligence and Learning Ability.

That is what I believe, there are probably more categories but those are the main ones.
