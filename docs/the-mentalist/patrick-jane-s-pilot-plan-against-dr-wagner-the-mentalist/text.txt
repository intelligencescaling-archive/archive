Patrick Jane’s Pilot Plan

After the double murder of Allison Randolph and Tannen, the crime scene is made to look like another Red John killing. However, Jane quickly notices that the staging does not match Red John’s usual pattern. Normally, the victim is positioned so that the smiley face is seen before the body, creating a specific psychological effect. At this crime scene, Jane sees the body first and only then notices the smile. From that deviation, he infers that the killer is imitating Red John rather than being Red John himself.

Jane begins suspecting Dr. Wagner almost immediately. When Rigsby mentions Red John, Wagner asks who Red John is, but Jane can tell from his expression that he is pretending not to know. Later, inside Wagner’s office, Jane notices books on criminal psychiatry that contain chapters about Red John. This confirms that Wagner lied during their first meeting. Jane also notices a set of small sticks in the office, which he remembers and later uses during the confrontation.

At this point, Jane already has a strong hypothesis that Wagner committed the murders. Wagner was close to the victims as their family doctor, had the medical knowledge necessary to dismember or mutilate a body without difficulty, and could potentially obtain physical evidence connected to other members of the family. Jane still needs a way to prove Wagner’s guilt or force him to expose himself.

Jane casually asks Wagner whether Tannen kept a diary, mentioning that a diary had helped solve a previous case. Wagner answers that he does not think Tannen had one. The question is not random: Jane is already forming a trap based around a nonexistent diary. Before leaving, Jane also asks Wagner about sleeping pills. Wagner says that Jane would need to come in for a consultation first. This gives Jane a legitimate reason to return to Wagner’s office later.

That night, Wagner leaves a Red John-style threatening message on Jane’s hotel-room door. Jane hears someone near the door, opens it almost immediately and sees a person running away, but he cannot catch him. The message further confirms that the imitation killer is now reacting directly to Jane’s investigation and is trying to frighten or mislead him.

Jane then spends the night creating a fake diary supposedly belonging to Tannen. He fills it with believable notes, diagrams and mathematical material so that it looks like a genuine personal notebook rather than an obvious prop. He deliberately remains awake and returns to the CBI office in the morning with an energy drink, making his lack of sleep especially visible. The purpose is to ensure that Lisbon tells him to see Wagner about his insomnia, naturally placing Jane back inside Wagner’s office without making the visit look suspicious or forced.

During the consultation, Jane tells Wagner a fictional story about why he cannot sleep. The content of the story is less important than the opportunity it gives Jane to remain in the office, observe Wagner and prepare the next stage of the trap. Jane also steals Wagner’s access card from his pocket without Wagner noticing.

As Jane is leaving the building, he deliberately gives Wagner false information. He claims that Wagner previously told him Tannen had kept a diary and says that the police cannot find it among Tannen’s possessions. Wagner correctly responds that he had actually said the opposite, but Jane pretends to remember clearly that someone told him the diary existed. He then says he will send forensic officers to search Tannen’s office for it.

The implication is obvious to Wagner, if Tannen really kept a diary, it might contain information connecting Wagner to the murders. Because Wagner knows he killed Tannen, he cannot safely ignore the possibility. Jane correctly predicts that Wagner will go to Tannen’s office himself to find and remove the diary before the forensic team arrives.

Jane has already planted the fake diary there. After Wagner enters the office and begins searching, Jane uses the stolen access card to get inside. He pretends that he returned because he left his phone there and asks Wagner what he is doing. Wagner admits that he is looking for the diary. Jane then helps him locate it, making the discovery appear natural, when it was him who put the diary there.

Jane takes the diary and starts to leave, leaving his phone in the office. Wagner reminds him that he supposedly came back for his phone. Jane goes with Wagner to his office, and Wagner pulls a gun and orders him to hand over the diary. Jane gives ir.

When Wagner opens the notebook, he discovers that it is a trap. The pages are filled with repeated accusations such as “You know you did it” and commands like “Confess,” along with the other notes and mathematical material Jane included to make the diary initially look authentic. At that point, Wagner understands that Jane deliberately manipulated him into thinking that diary would’ve helped solve the case from the start and that it was him who planted it. Patrick also really did leave a phone to have a legitimate reason to come back.

Jane explains the reasoning that led him to Wagner. Wagner pretended not to know who Red John was despite owning professional books that contained chapters about him. As the Randolph family doctor, he could easily obtain a strand of Tag’s hair and plant it at the crime scene. His medical training also meant that he could cut up a human body without the hesitation or technical difficulty most people would experience. Combined with the incorrectly staged Red John signature and Wagner’s reaction to the fake diary, the evidence points directly to him.

Jane then calmly tells Wagner that he is under arrest, even though Wagner is still pointing a gun at him. Jane claims that he removed the bullets earlier and pats his pocket, producing a sound resembling loose ammunition. This makes Wagner look down at the weapon in confusion. The bluff gives Jane the fraction of a second he needs. He grabs the sticks he had noticed during his first visit and throws them into Wagner’s face. Jane immediately runs while crouching low, reducing the chance of being hit because Wagner is likely to fire at normal upper-body height. Wagner shoots and pursues him.

Rigsby then arrives at the building. Jane tells him that he is late and orders him to draw his weapon. Rigsby does so and arrests Wagner.

Jane most likely instructed Rigsby to arrive at Wagner’s office at a particular time so that he arrests Wagner because he pointed the gun at Jane. However, because Rigsby might be delayed, Jane also prepared several contingencies. One of them is something in his pocket that sounds like bullets to distract Wagner, the sticks in the office as an improvised weapon (idk if the sticks usage was prepared or was a tactic in at the moment), and his escape route. It’s not proven that Patrick already knew about the gun, but the objects in his pocket strongly suggest that he anticipated the possibility of Wagner becoming armed and prepared a backup plan in case Rigsby did not arrive on time.

Rigsby comes while Patrick’s running away and arrests Wagner.
