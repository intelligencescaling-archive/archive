Patrick Jane's plan for disinformation

Hello everyone! I think most people who've watched The Mentalist remember episode 7 of season 4. Let's be honest, it's one of the most well-directed episodes in the entire series (ironically directed by Simon Baker, the actor who plays Patrick Jane), and I definitely remember it. In fact, the only issue I had was with the ending. It took me a while to understand why Patrick didn't try to capture Red Jogo. But a couple of days ago, I came up with a theory, and I'm going to try to explain and justify it. To do this, we need to study episodes 7, 23, and 24 from season 4.

And so, the finale of episode 7 of season 4. Patrick is on the air, he, together with journalist James Panzer, tells about the serial killer from San Joaquin, about his psychological portrait, about how the investigation is going and when the criminal will be caught. Jane already knows who the maniac is - James Panzer himself. He commits his brutal murders, and then writes about them on his blog, creating the image of a "serial killer genius", a kind of Hannibal Lecter, an intellectual and aesthete who leaves no evidence and who has no sexual or mercenary motive, but only pure sadism. But there is a problem, all of Patrick's conclusions are based solely on his intuition and ability to see through people. He had no actual evidence of Panzer's guilt. I will note that in this episode he tried to lure James into a trap (I will not tell his strategy, watch the episode yourself if you want), but unfortunately it failed. And during the live broadcast, Jane comes up with the following idea: to kill James Panzer. Patrick himself cannot do this, at least because Red John, whom everyone believes to be dead, is at large and Patrick needs to continue investigating his case. Therefore, PJ decided to use the help of his sworn enemy and friend, Red John. He indirectly manipulates Panzer to insult Red John on live TV. As you might have guessed, journalist James Panzer was soon found dead with a bloody smiley face next to his body.

At the time, I was very confused by this moment. After all, why didn't Jane set a trap for Red John? But now my theory is coming into force. And so, Jane knows that Red John will want to kill Panzer, but where and when will he want to do it? Maybe RJ will come for him in a few hours after the broadcast or even in a few weeks? Would he try to catch him on the street or sneak into his apartment while he was sleeping? And what should Patrick do? Set up 24-hour surveillance on James? Question: who will give him permission to do this? I don't think Patrick's superiors will like this plan. In this case, you'll have to use Patrick's friends, but I'll remind you that they're police officers and they have a lot of other things to do and worry about, they couldn't watch James around the clock. Nevertheless, Panzer is really a bait to get John into a trap, but not into such an obvious one, but into a much more confusing one.

During the live broadcast, Red John not only sees how Panzer disrespects him, but also how Patrick, in fact, incites John to kill Panzer. We all know that RJ likes to tease Patrick, and now, Jane creates the illusion that he admits defeat in the case of the San Joaquin killer and asks John for help. After Jane and CBI fail to set a trap for Red John, RJ no longer wants to just play with Patrick, he wants to make him his follower, subjugate him, as he already did with Sheriff Hardy (episode 23 of season 1) and Rebeca. As Jane himself said, the key to a successful con is offering the mark the thing they most want. RJ wants Jane to know and acknowledge that RJ is better than him in every way. In my opinion, Jane's fake breakdown plan started with Panzer.

I will also mention episodes 11, 13, and 17. In these episodes, Jane simulates an upcoming conflict. John makes clear hints that he is alive, but Patrick skillfully convinces Darcy (the FBI agent who was handling the RJ case) that Red John is dead. Although Patrick conceals this fact, Darcy becomes increasingly convinced otherwise. She begins to suspect that Patrick has become John's follower, which she mentions to Wainwright (Patrick's boss), who also begins to pressure Jane. Again, Patrick does this to simulate a nervous and pressured environment and make the simulation of a nervous breakdown more believable.

The climax was reached in episode 23 of season 4. According to the timeline of the series, this was the day of Patrick's wife and daughter's deaths. John sends a young girl to Patrick, who delivers a message offering him to surrender. At the end of the episode, Patrick literally buries a murder suspect alive to force him to confess. After this, he insults Wainwright, who angrily dismisses Jane, but Patrick declares that he is quitting.

To summarize. Patrick created a plan to simulate a nervous breakdown, in order for Red John to believe it, he needed to create plausible circumstances for this: in episode 7 of season 4, as we have already found out, he faked his "defeat" when he "resorted to the help of RJ" so that he killed Panzer; after that, In episodes 11, 13 and 17, he imitates attempts to hide the fact that John is alive, which leads to conflict with Agent Darcy and the management.; Patrick also obviously spent all this time pretending to spend all his free time working on the Red John case on his own, which takes a lot of effort; and finally, in the finale of episode 23, Patrick pretends that he lost his temper and left the CBI.

In episode 24 of season 4, Patrick demonstrates amazing EM and acting skills. He has been faking a nervous breakdown and alcoholism for 6 months, returning to work as a fraudster, and at the same time does not communicate with any of his friends and colleagues (Lisbon said this twice in the same episode). That is, Jane created the perfect reason for a nervous breakdown and perfectly fabricated it in order to lure Red John out and trap him.
