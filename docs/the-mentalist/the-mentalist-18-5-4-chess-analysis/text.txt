Cambridge Springs, 1904; Notes by Siegbert Tarrasch and Georg Marko.
“Thanks to this defense, which I jokingly called
"orthodox", the Queen's Gambit has been especially
favored by Whites for the last few decades.
They always achieved an advantageous position very quickly,
and therefore it seemed unwise to them to neglect
the advantages that could be achieved with such conveniences.
For more than 30 years I have constantly pointed out
the unsatisfactoriness of this defense, which
always gives the opponent a stronger piece to play.”
- Siegbert Tarrasch | Source: page 30 |
Short information: Siegbert Tarrasch (1862-1934) was a prominent German chess master and physician, renowned for his contributions to chess theory and literature. He was one of the leading players in the late 19th century, winning several major tournaments and earning the title of one of the original grandmasters. Tarrasch is particularly noted for his writings, including "The Game of Chess," which popularized strategic concepts and influenced generations of players. Siegbert Tarrasch was never a world champion, despite being one of the strongest players of his time. He declined a title match in 1892 due to his medical career and later faced Emanuel Lasker for the championship in 1908 but was defeated. Tarrasch is often regarded as one of the greatest players never to hold the title, having won several major tournaments and contributed significantly to chess theory and literature.
