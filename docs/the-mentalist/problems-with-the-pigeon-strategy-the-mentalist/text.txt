Pigeon strategy...

Oh boy uh. Yeah I've read the doc it's probably PJ's best feat in strategy and shit and it boost his concealment (still a light victim in it).

But aside from that I have problems with it.

The first one is PJ's tactic to get RJ to accept the church. He at first throws out a location and Bertram rejects it so PJ then throws out a second location so that it looks like a compromise. What happened  if Bertram did the sensible thing of being cautious and deciding instead to reject it and try to create some sort of mutual agreement of where a location could go? I don't even think this is nitpicking, this mfer thought PJ was desperate hence why he exposed Bertram to the media so it's very likely that PJ could have set up some trap with the police in case he died and yet he trusts the second location PJ gives him? The same PJ who is known to use deception heavily? And RJ, the guy who was portrayed as careful with a lot of foresight, accepted it? You could say "But RJ was also desperate because he was on the run from the FBI", the Blake association has a ton of influence it's very likely in the absolute worst case scenario they can just have Bertram disappear in a safe house until the manhunt slowly loses steam.

The second one is the church itself it makes me wonder why why did RJ do this? PJ essentially asked Lisbon to give him her gun as a ploy. When PJ went to the church, a goon of RJ was waiting for him and he checked him and found the gun. I know that the point of it was that the gun was a misdirection as RJ expected that PJ would try something desperate but consider this: RJ is the most important person in the association, the goon he brought with him is clearly a bodyguard to protect him in case anything happens so we can infer RJ would choose somebody competent for this task. And yet the goon wasn't! He found the gun and didn't even think about doing a full body check which would expose the pigeon and therefore ruin PJ's strategy (Peak invincibility truly).

Oh and also if I understood from the Pigeon strategy doc, PJ essentially used his friends as a shielding to his strategy meaning when they pointed their guns at FBI agent he had anticipated it. Except that once PJ killed RJ, he fled to Mexico. PJ did this knowing that his friends would be in serious legal and professional troubles for essentially threatening federal agents, and he had no type of plan to help them. This doesn't weaken the strategy itself but it goes against PJ's character considering he isn't evil and does care about his friends so to think he just used them like pawn to kill RJ is just wild. I do know that PJ goes to extreme lengths when it concerns RJ but he never has gone to lengths such as directly doing something this extreme. The worst thing he did was when he set up that trap in season 3 to catch RJ but that's about it from what I can remember.
