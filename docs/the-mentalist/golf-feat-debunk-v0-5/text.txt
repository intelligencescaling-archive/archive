Jane rapidly learned golf mechanics, identified flaws, and devised effective training.

1.The analysis says that PJ learned to play golf at a good level in a short period of time thanks to Battaglia correcting his technique using the method Patrick gave him.The problem with this is that to correct a bad posture you don't necessarily have to have a deep or good knowledge of golf or any sport, it requires good observation of the rhythm of the shot and posture.

2.The advice that PJ gives to improve the Battaglia technique is not the most complicated to create/imagine, it is a moderately popular technique that in fact, is not only used by golfers but is also used in many areas to concentrate or improve timing.
(If you have seen the series “the mentalist” you will know that PJ is an expert in the analysis of human expressions in general, obviously he can recognize bad postures.)

Instinctive Club Selection

 “Jane instinctively selects the appropriate club (either a driver or a hybrid) for the given course, recognizing the need for longer shots.”

This argument tells us that PJ demonstrates a decent understanding of golf, acquired in a short period of time with minimal exposure to the game itself, and therefore chooses a golf club that he recognizes as more appropriate. I have a problem with this: PJ possibly chooses a golf club based on the following reasoning:

The holes are far apart, so I need a golf club big enough to make it easier to reach them, but not so "fat"/heavy that it's uncomfortable to hold.

(I am revising the doc and decided to write another way of saying the above.) Attributing the club selection to 'instinct' implies a subconscious understanding of golf physics. A simpler, more probable explanation is basic visual assessment and logic: the target is far, requiring a large club , but perhaps avoiding the absolute largest/most unwieldy one ('not the fat one'). This requires common sense, not specialized knowledge.
