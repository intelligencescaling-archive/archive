How does MP work in the real world?

Memory Palace is the common name for Method of Loci or MOL. It utilizes the spatial relationships between loci or locations and recovers memories. There are mainly 3 stages a memory goes through. Encoding, Storage, and Retrieval. This method primarily works by associating different memories or parts of memories with specific locations in an imaginary palace and storing them in long term memory. This allows the user to recollect that memory by following the specific path set during the encoding of said memory. (Source: The method of loci (MOL) is a mnemonic device that relies on spatial relationships between “loci” (e.g., locations on a familiar route or rooms in a familiar building) to arrange and recollect memorial content.) This means that you need to ONLY visit specific paths and locations for specific memories.

Another thing to note here is that MP as a technique is meant to make it easier to remember things. It should make your life easier and not harder. (Source: thus offering an efficient filing of the information and an easy path for the regular review necessary for long-term memory storage.). Meaning, you only need to follow the easiest path to your memory. No, detours are necessary.

Lastly, the memory recall works by walking through the designated route of each memory. Which means that for each memory there should be a very specific path set and “walked” every time to recall that memory. (Source: It involves imagining yourself placing pieces of information around a room and then visualizing yourself walking back through to pick the information up.) Now that we have established how it works, let’s move on to the next section.

Takeaways:
1. You don’t need the entire palace just to recall a single memory, you just need the specific location where it was stored.
2. You need to walk through that route, which means you are NOT getting a bird’s eye view so that you can visualize the entire palace.
3. If a memory is stored inside a room, you need to visualize the room itself vividly from the inside, which makes it impossible to visualize what’s outside of it. I.e the rest of the palace.
4. For example: Close your eyes and try to imagine yourself waking up in your closed bedroom. Now imagine yourself walking inside it and vividly visualize the interior. Do you visualize the exterior of the building you're in while not breaking your POV? If you even try to visualize the exterior of the building (which is unnecessary as mentioned in takeaway 1), you will compromise how vividly you visualize the interior of it simultaneously, not to mention it is impossible to do in 3D.

How does Blackjack work?

In Blackjack, a couple of players bet against the dealer. The dealer deals two face up cards to each player, one face up and one face down card to himself. The cards can either be dealt out of a “shoe” containing multiple decks, or just one deck. Then each player can decide to either hit or stay. Hit means the dealer will draw a card from the top of the deck and give it to the player. Each card has a value. Cards 2 to 10 have their value equal to their face value. Ace can either be a 1 or a 11 depending on what the player wants. And the Jacks, Kings, and Queens all have a value equal to 10. If any player’s or the dealer’s hand has a total value of more than 21. Then they automatically bust. If the player gets a total value of 21 from their initial 2 face up cards, then they automatically win 1.5 times what they bet and are done with that round. Once the dealer has gone around the table once, they flip up their face down card. If a player has a total value less than the dealer, they lose their bets. If a player has a total value more than the dealer but not more than 21, they win twice of what they bet. Finally in case the dealer busts, each player remaining gets twice of what they bet.

Difference between possibilities and probabilities.

This is a very common confusion among people and they often consider them one and the same. Let’s take an example of a coin toss. A coin toss can have 2 possibilities, a heads (H) and a tails (T). Possibilities therefore are all of the outcomes that can happen after an action. Probability on the other hand is the chance of a certain outcome happening. Which means the probability of heads is 50% or ½. The difference between the two might not seem that important now but let's consider two coin tosses. All possibilities of two coin tosses are as follows:
HH
HT
TH
TT
The letters above signify either Heads(H) or Tails(T). Now if someone needs to know the probability of getting 2 heads in a row, it is easy to say 25% or ¼ after seeing all the possibilities. But you don’t need to see all possibilities to know that. We know that HH can only be one possibility. And we can calculate the total number of possibilities by using the formula 2^n. Here n means the number of tosses. Since we have two coin tosses, we can have 4 total possibilities. So the probability of getting HH is 1 in 4. Let’s take it a step further, what is the probability of getting exactly 2 heads in 10 coin tosses? Now here comes the issue. The total number of possibilities in this case is 2^10. This is equal to 1024. We do NOT need to write down each possibility to know the probability of getting a certain set of outcomes. In this case, if we use the probability mass function of binomial distribution formula:

Where n is the total number of trials (10 in this case), x is the total number or success needed (2 heads in this case), and p is the probability of success (½ in this case). The bracket on the right side of the second “=” sign means “combination”. nCx = n! / (x! * (n-x)!). Which in this case comes out to be 10!/(2!*(10-2)!)=10*9/2=45. The same principle can be applied to a deck of cards. If you need a king from a randomly shuffled deck, you can calculate that it has a 1 in 13 chance of happening. You can further add the restrictions but you don’t need to go through all 52 possible outcomes to know that probability.

Part 4: Dream Feat Debunk

I disagree with the notion that dreams indicate anything about our VSI capabilities. It was recently brought to my attention that there is a condition called aphantasia that proves that even people with no ability to voluntarily visualize can have quite vivid dreams.

What is Aphantasia?

Aphantasia is when your brain doesn’t form or use mental images as part of your thinking or imagination. This clearly means that they are unable to utilize or do not have VSI abilities like people usually do. 2% to 4% of the population has this condition.

Can people with Aphantasia dream?

People with Aphantasia can have vivid dreams despite not being able to visualize voluntarily when they are awake. This is because dreams and visualization use different parts of the brain. Dreams utilize bottom up processes controlled by the brainstem. Visualization utilize top down processes controlled by the cortex.

This means that VSI abilities are completely separated from dreams on a biological level. Therefore you can have vivid dreams and yet not be able to voluntarily visualize things to solve complex problems and vice versa. Which is why having vivid dreams is not indicative of VSI.

What makes dreams vivid in the first place?

Most vivid dreams occur during the REM sleep stage. Sleep has four stages: N1, N2, N3, and REM. N1, N2, and N3 are also known as NREM sleep. REM stands for Rapid Eye Movement and NREM stands for Non Rapid Eye Movement. The dreams that are considered vivid are often because the person wakes up during REM sleep. This is controlled by factors such as sleep deprivation, mental health issues, drugs and medications etc.

It is quite easy to debunk the reasons that the doc has given. The doc states that just because Jane can recall most of his dreams that means it was a VSI feat. That is not true as it was drug induced and it would more likely be a general memory recall feat. It also states that those who can recall most of their dreams are known to have good VSI and intellect. This is correlation and not causation. We do not know the exact relation between VSI and dream recall. The articles here, here, and here, also attribute vivid dreams to a number of causes such as stress and mental illnesses.
