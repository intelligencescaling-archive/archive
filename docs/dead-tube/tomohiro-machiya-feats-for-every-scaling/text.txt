Context – What Happened:
Rascal (a mysterious murderer) declares that they must correctly identify him (out of all the participants in that Dead Tube event) within 3 minutes, or he will kill the captured child.
* Machiya begins concentrating (shown across multiple panels).

* Machiya: "Give me silence, I'm in the process of editing."

Feat Summary:
Machiya recalls an entire day’s events with photographic detail, as though replaying a film in his mind, including even minor details (as explicitly shown in the panels and his reasoning).
Simultaneously, he solves a complex case on the spot. Both in a really short period of time. The manga doesn't go out of its way to show us the time, so we will have to find it ourselves given the information we know of.

Speed
There are some explanations as to when this feat began, but the one that is most backed up by evidence is this one.

“Third-rate thriller film” interpretation: considers the starting time of the feat when Mai Mashiro gave Machiya the idea, that he later used within his reasoning, that this was a “third-rate thriller film”. This is supported by two facts:
1- until Mai said that, Machiya held the usual apathetic face he had. Just after that moment, he made a strong expression of illumination, which is also supported by a little spark near his head.
2- he used this idea of the “third-rate thriller film” in his later reasoning
3- it was the first thing he pointed out in his reasoning.

Now, given that we now know *when* the feat started, we need to use something in order for us to quantificate the time spent.
We have two assets that will let us conclude the time:
1- the time spent since Rascal gave the order and asked for the reasoning (3 minutes)
2- the dialogues (meaning the number of words spoken, the “!!” / “?” / “...” marks etc.).

I calculated everything since the 3 minutes started began, until the Rascal finished it:
Words - 795 in total; 742 “K” (outside the time of the feat); 53 “W” (while the feat was performed)
Balloons - 123 in total; 108 K; 15 W
“!” marks - 6 in total; 1 K; 5 W
“!?” marks - 7 in total; 6 K; 1 W
“?” marks - 11 in total; 10 K; 1 W
“...” sign - 51 in total; 44 K; 7 W.
Considering the very obvious fact that each of these signs would mean a difference pace of speaking (which we cannot really know) let's assume that:
1 Ballon = + 1 word of time
1 “!” mark = -2 words of time
1 “!?” mark = -2 words of time
1 “?” mark = + 2 words of time
1“...” sign = + 2 words of time.

Meaning that the total K “words of time” would be 202 + 742 = 944 words of time, and the total W “words of time” would be 19 + 53 = 72 words of time.

Meaning that, within the 3 minutes, we have a total number of 1016 words of time.
Meaning that 180 seconds  = 1016 words of time, meaning that one word “weighs” 0.17716553543 seconds.
Since the dialogues that took place *while* Machiya was performing the feat, had 72 words of time, that means it took 12.75590551 seconds for Machiya to perform the feat.

FILM
Mai Mashiro says “Looks like Machiya is editing all that he witnessed today in his mind. Turning it into a film.” and “...so how is it…feel like you've crafted a good film?”.
All of this while Machiya is shown with a background of photographic detail of moments that happened during the whole day.
Mai Mashiro is also someone who is shown multiple times reading Machiya perfectly, so she is a very reliable source canonically as well.
Not only this but very obviously films are kinda the whole theme of Dead Tube, so it makes a lot of sense narratively as well.

Now: films (in most cases) are built at 24 frames per second. Meaning that Machiya reconstructed everything he witnessed that day, at 24 frames per second.

Now: we do not have clear knowledge on how much (until that point) the day lasted. We definitely know that they Machiya and the others were awake since the night, and considering all the events that happened (for the people who read the story), it would be very plausible to estimate a minimum of 5 up to a maximum of 15 hours (until the moment Rascal asked to reveal his identity). Especially considering that at the final moments, they were able to see very clearly (unlike the start of the night, where dark was prominent).

Meaning that in the lowest approximation, we would have: 24 (FPS) * 5 (hours) * 60 (minutes per each our) * 60 (seconds per minute) photos = 432.000 photos per second. Mind you: photos with perfect photographic memory AND while reconstructing the scene in his mind (angles, positions etc.).

If we distribute the time of processing equally (which most likely isn't the case, but this way I'm nerfing the feat the most), it would be 0.000002314814815 seconds of speed required to process 1 image.
