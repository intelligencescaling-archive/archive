Alice (Lewis Carroll Novels) feat list

**“Curiouser and curiouser!”**

\[General\] She conceived and constructed the totality of Wonderland within her own mind, incorporating mathematical frameworks, inverted systems of logic, paradoxes, riddles, locations, characters, and logical principles.

This feat showcases \[VSI (Visual Spatial Index) WMI (Working Memory Index) RM (Resource Management) CT (Creative Thinking) ILGT (Illogical Thinking) ABT (Abstract Thinking) LGT (Logical Thinking) SYT (Systematic Thinking) COT (Conceptual Thinking) CREI (Creative Intelligence) SPI (Spatial Intelligence) LMI (Logical Mathematical Intelligence) and Unpredictability\].

\[General\] She entered this self-created dream world and endured its pervasive illogicality with a logical mind, remaining aware of the dissonance between her former self and the transformed identity she assumed within Wonderland. Rather than succumbing to its irrationality, she persisted learning to accept and exploit the illogicality to her advantage, maintaining her resolve until she finally escaped—after ascending to the role of Queen.

This feat showcases \[FI (Fluid Intelligence) SA (Self Awareness) ST (Stress Tolerance) MF (Mental Fortitude) IN (Intrapersonal Intelligence) RT (Reflective Thinking) LA (Learning Ability) Determination and Perseverance\].

\[General\] She solved the Sixty-Four is the Same as Sixty-Five paradox by identifying the flaw in the Queen’s visual proof. After interrupting a conversation between the Queen and King, she was dragged by the Queen to two chessboards—one an eight by eight square and the other a five by thirteen rectangle—and warned that failure to solve the paradox would result in her beheading. Examining the first board, she noted that two of the cards covering it were trapezoids, with non-diagonal sides measuring five, five, and three units, while the other two were long triangles with non-diagonal sides measuring eight by three. When the cards were transferred to the second board, she began to discern the subtle flaw. Observing the trapezoid and triangle “soldiers,” she noticed that their edges seemed to form a continuous line from corner to corner. If that were true, the slopes of their diagonal sides should be identical. Calculating the slopes using the rise-over-run formula, she discovered the discrepancy: the trapezoid’s diagonal rose two over five, giving a slope of two-fifths, while the triangle’s diagonal rose three over eight, giving a slope of three-eighths. Recognizing the subtle mismatch, she used her shrinking potion to inspect the arrangement more closely and confirmed a minuscule gap between the trapezoids and triangles, forming a small parallelogram that accounted for the “missing” square. She then applied Fibonacci number properties: the squares of these numbers differ by exactly one from the product of the adjacent Fibonacci numbers—eight squared is one less than five times thirteen, and five squared is one more than three times eight. Using the ratio of successive Fibonacci numbers and their convergence toward the golden ratio, she deduced that the two arrangements were not identical. Thus, sixty-four is not, in fact, the same as sixty-five. She did this by remembering her past geometry class lessons. And she did all of this in her head in seconds.

This feat showcases \[PSI (Processing Speed Index) CPI (Cognitive Proficiency Index) NCPS (Novel Complex Problem Solving) WMI (Working Memory Index) LMI (Logical Mathematical Intelligence) CT (Creative Thinking) ST (Stress Tolerance) MF (Mental Fortitude) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) LGT (Logical Thinking) LR (Logical Reasoning) ABT (Abstract Thinking) CFI (Cognitive Flexibility Index) CI (Crystallised Intelligence) ST (Strategic Thinking) SE (Strategy Efficiency) SC (Strategy Complexity) SPI (Spatial Intelligence) SOA (Social Awareness) PR (Pattern Recognition) LOTM (Long Term Memory) and AK (Academic Knowledge)\].

\[Rabbit Hole + Pool of Tears\] She noticed how a white rabbit should not possess a vest or a wristwatch. As she contemplated her fall down the rabbit hole, she reasoned that she might emerge in New Zealand or Australia, judging by the distance she had fallen and the knowledge she gained from a past geography class. Upon landing, she attempted every door the rabbit could have passed through, in case any remained unlocked. She then observed a small curtain that had not been present before; behind it lay a garden accessible only through shrinking. From the very first minute inside, she recognised the world’s illogical nature. Finding the DRINK ME bottle, she ensured it was not poisoned by examining the label and performing a taste test. Upon drinking, she noted the exact flavors—cherry tart, pineapple, roast turkey, and buttered toast—and waited until the shrinking process was complete before approaching the garden also understood how she felt during the process. She then saw that the curtain was now locked, and the key rested on the table, attempting futilely to climb back toward it. Though she cried in frustration, she immediately recognised that her tears would not resolve the situation and stopped. Her attention then turned to the EAT ME cake. She reasoned that it might induce either growth or shrinkage, based on her prior experience with the bottle. From this, she concluded that either outcome would provide access to the garden: if she became tall, she could retrieve the key, and if she became small, she could slip beneath the door.

This feat showcases \[AR (Abductive Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) IR (Inductive Reasoning) PR (Pattern Recognition) SSO (Single Sensory Observation) PS (Problem Solving) EM (Emotional Management) LA (Learning Ability) ER (Emotional Realisation) IN (Intrapersonal Intelligence) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) SE (Strategy Efficiency) SPI (Spatial Intelligence) SOA (Social Awareness) ST (Strategic Thinking) AK (Academic Knowledge) and LR (Logical Reasoning)\].
