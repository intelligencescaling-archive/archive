Analysis of August Dupins mind reading feat in The Murders At The Rue Morgue

I decided to do an analysis of August Dupin throughout his first novel titled The Murder At The Rue Morgue. I'll be splitting it across a couple posts. This one will be focusing on him accurately reading the mind of the narrator (the Watson like character of this series).

I'll be mainly focusing on the feats themselves so only slight context surrounding the feat will be given. I suggest to read the actual novel, it's only 26 pages so it won't take long. Here is a link to read the novel:[The Murders At The Rue Mourge ](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qCcebsC-01b8D7jsUrYuFU1vvlPxEwNA/view?usp=drivesdk)

The reading of unspoken thoughts

**Context**

Both Dupin and the narrator first met at a bookstore then met a couple more times and began to talk with each other and began to bond over their shared intellectual curiosity. This led Dupin to decide to live with the narrator. Some days passed as they lived together and one day they went on a light night walk. It starts with both the Narrator and Dupin walking down the street together at night. After walking in silence for a while Dupin suddenly says: You're right. He is a very little fellow, that's true, and he would be more successful if he acted in lighter, less serious plays. The narrator then agrees with what Dupin said and didn't think much of it as Dupin agreed with what he thought but then the narrator realized that's the strange part, he only thought about that. He then expressed his shock at this miraculous feat and demanded Dupin to explain how he could have possibly known.

**Breakdown Analysis of the Feat**

**EVENT 1: Fruit seller collision**

The chain that led him to track the narrator's thoughts first starts with Dupin recounting back 15 minutes ago when the narrator gets into a collision with a fruit seller. Dupin observes the collision and tracks the physical impact, the narrator stumbling and stepping on uneven cobblestone, perceives the pain in his foot and immediately understands that this event will produce a mental response. Before any association can be followed Dupin must establish where the chain begins. The Fruit seller serves as that anchor point. Without establishing this anchor point the entire reconstruction would be unlinked without a tether point. This also goes to show his strong observation and EP to be able to recognize the initiating event from a 15 minute old encounter.

**EVENT 2: The narrators angry words**

Dupin then goes to state that the narrator mumbled a few angry words to himself. Dupin doesn't hear the exact words that he said but Instead reads the emotions behind them. He picks up on the frustration and irritation and understands that the pain of the collision registered consciously and that the narrator's mind has been marked by the event. This anger also tells Dupin something else, that the narrator's attention has been caught by the cobblestones. An event that passes without emotional reaction leaves little mental trace while an event that provokes anger lodges itself in the mind. Dupin uses the emotional intensity of the response to measure how deeply the stones have embedded themselves in the narrator's mind.

**Event 3: The narrators continued downwards gaze**

"Dupin then goes on to tell the narrator that he kept looking down at the cobblestones on the street, so I knew you were still looking at the sones". Dupin then uses some semiotic reasoning (reasoning through body language, which is also used in cold reading). The downward gaze is the first purely physical sign that Dupin reads without any verbal behavior accompanying it. Eyes directed at the ground when there is no longer any practical reason to watch ones footing is an indicator that the mind is not guiding the eyes, the mind is elsewhere and the eyes have drifted to where it's focused at. Dupin uses the narrator's eyes as a reliable index of where his attention is directed at. Dupin throughout this is not just noting where his gaze is, he's tracking his mental state overtime. The anger has passed, and the collision is already behind them by this point but the downward gaze continues. This tells Dupin that the cobblestones have not yet left his mind.

**Event 4: The newly cut street stones**

As Dupin still goes about describing his process he says "Then we came to a small street where they are putting down street stones which they have cut in a new and very special way." Dupin identifies a feature of the environment (newly cut stones using a distinctive technique) as an object that will interfere with the narrator's already active mental preoccupation. The narrator is already thinking about stones and these particular stones will add technical content to that preoccupation. Keep in mind that Dupin recognized this environmental stimulus before observing its effect. He knows that these stones carry a technical name and before the narrator even reacts Dupin is already predicting what that reaction would be.

**Event 5: The narrators brightened face and moving lips**

Dupin then goes on to say "Your face became brighter and I saw your lips move." (Referring to the narrator's reaction to these stones). Two distinct physical signs happen and are read together. The first is the brightened face that signals recognition, interest, or intellectual engagement. Whichever one it is what's important is that his mind has made a connection. The second is the moving lips that signal that a specific word has surfaced internally and is being subvocally pronounced. Both separated wouldn't mean too much but both together show that the narrator has recognized something meaningful and recognition has taken verbal form. From the lip movement alone Dupin concludes that the narrator is mouthing the word stereotomy. This required contextual reasoning. Dupin knows the word was in yesterday's newspaper, knows it is the technical name for the cutting method used on these particular stones, knows that it is an unusual enough word to capture attention and prompt subvocal repetition. The identification of the word stereotomy was only possible because narrator and Dupin read the same newspaper yesterday. Without that shared knowledge the connection between the lip movements in the stones would be unreadable.

**Event 6: The reasoning bridge from Stereotomy to** Epicurus

Dupin then goes on to say "I thought that the word stereotomy must make you think of that old Greek writer named Epicurus." Now Dupin starts using associative reasoning/logic. Dupin now predicts where Narrators mind will take him next. He reasons from how ideas connect. Stereotomy involves cutting matter precise pieces, this neighbors the philosophical aspect of atoms, Epicurus is the classic thinker most associated with atomic history. They had recently discussed these ideas together. These associative thoughts aren't linked randomly. They follow the internal logic of how these concepts relate to each other in an educated mind (the narrator) who has recently encountered them. Dupin didn't just think about stones and atoms randomly, he ran a simulation of what this particular person with this particular recent reading, would associate with this particular word. And this part was predictive and Dupin continued to observe the narrator to see if he was correct because if he was then the narrator's thoughts would then go to Epicurus, atoms, then the cosmos.

**Event 7: Skyward glance**

Dupin then goes on to say "I felt sure that you would look up to the sky. You did look up." The narrator's upward glance serves as confirmation that Dupin has been tracking his thoughts so far and has even predicted where they would go. The Logical chain goes thinking about Epicurus, then thinking about atoms and their cosmic arrangement, thinking about cosmic arrangement directs attention towards the sky, directed attention towards the sky causes him to look up. Dupin mapped out the route from an abstract philosophical concept towards specific observable behavior with enough precision to predict it before it even happened. The mind moved from idea to impulse movement and Dupin managed to track every layer.

**Event 8: Observing the stars Orion**

Dupin goes on to say "I too looked up, and saw that the group of stars we call Orion is very bright and clear tonight. I knew you would notice this." When the narrator looked up during their walk Dupin also looked to the sky to see exactly what the narrator would see. And what they see is Orion which is bright and clear. Orion being visible wasn't something that Dupin could have predicted but the chain's validity doesn't depend on it as it only required that the narrator would look up and whatever was prominent in the sky would attract his attention. Dupin knows that Orion would attract his attention because the name Orion is culturally and intellectually familiar to both of them, a bright prominent constellation naturally draws attention and naming it, the name Orion once activated in the narrator's mind will serve the bride to the newspaper article.

**Event 9: Reasoning Orion to Chantilly**

Dupin then goes to say "Only yesterday, in the newspaper, there was an article about the actor Chantilly, and the writer had used some words taken from a book we both had read. These words were about Orion." Now Dupin is using cross contextual association reasoning. It's one of the most complex associative bridges in the entire reasoning chain as Dupin must predict that the narrator will see Orion, think of the word Orion, connect that word to its recent appearance in another context (the newspaper article), recognize that article was about Chantilly, and then move from Orion to Chantilly. This requires the narrator's mind to traverse two wildly different contexts (astronomical and theatrical), using the single verbal bride that Orion has appeared in both of them. Dupin was able to map this cross context pathway with confidence as he knows both contexts are active in the narrator's mind. Dupin is essentially reasoning about how words are stored and retrieved. Specifically that a word encountered recently in one context will, when reactivated by a new encounter, carry associations from that prior context with it.

**Event 10: The narrators smile**

Dupin then says, "I saw you smile, remembering that article and the hard words in it." The smile wasn't a display of amusement but a small brief expression that Dupin caught and interpreted. He read it specifically as the smile of someone who found something slightly amusing (the unfriendly option of the article written of the Chantilly article).  This requires not just noticing the smile but also correctly interpreting the emotions behind it. Which is a recollection mixed with amusement at another's expense. The smile also serves as calibration on the narrator's mental state as it tells Dupin that the narrator is not thinking of Chantilly abstractly but that he is emotionally engaged in the memory, finding it amusing. This tells Dupin that the memory is visible and active in the narrator's mind.

**Event 11: The narrator standing**

Dupin then goes on to say, "Then I saw you stand straighter, as tall as you could make yourself. I was sure you were thinking of Chantilly's size, and especially his height." Once again Dupin uses Semiotic reasoning. The narrator goes on to unconsciously adjust his posture while thinking of Chantilly (who is notably short). While thinking about a man who is notably short the body performs a contrast (measuring itself against the subject of thought). The narrator didn't consciously do this but his body did it involuntarily due to what was on his mind. Dupin's interpretation depends on recognizing that the body's behavior was contrastive and not something like imitation. The narrator stands tall because he is thinking of someone small, the physical gesture expresses comparison. This required higher semiotic reasoning then the previous one involving the narrator's downward gaze (imo). Now the chain is complete. Every step of the predicted chain has now been confirmed by observable behavior. The complete sequence runs from fruit-seller to cobblestones to stereotomy to Epicurus to skyward glance to Orion to newspaper article to Chantilly's size and each transition has been verified by a specific physical sign at its moment of occurrence.

**Event 12: Dupin responds to Narrators unspoken thought**

Now we're back to where we started with Dupin saying "He is a very little fellow, that's true, and he would be more successful if he acted in lighter, less serious plays." Dupin's words serve as a casual continuation of a  conversation that has been going on for 15 minutes. He speaks naturally as from his perspective he's been receiving continuous communication. Which is crazy as this shows that throughout the entire silent 15 minute silent walk Dupin was doing all of this in real time.
