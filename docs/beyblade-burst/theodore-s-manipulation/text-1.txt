Theodore's Manipulation of Free De La Hoya (And, by extension, Shu and Joshua)

This is arguably one of the most important parts of everything that's going to happen afterward, especially when it comes to understanding the Requiem Project and the preparations Theodore made to bring it into fruition.

It all begins with Theodore managing to get Free De La Hoya onto the Raging Bulls.

Now, this wasn't simply a matter of wanting another exceptionally strong Blader on his team.

There were several underlying reasons why Theodore specifically wanted Free, and his recruitment would eventually become a crucial part of the larger plan.

I'll explain those reasons as we get further into this, but first, we need to establish exactly how Theodore managed to get Free to join his team in the first place.

This is something that is established fairly early on in the series, particularly during the first few episodes.

When Theodore initially becomes interested in having Free join the Raging Bulls, he is shown making multiple attempts to negotiate with Kris to get him onto the team.

![img](53xotuw8ykzh1 "\"Such a shame...\"")

However, regardless of how much he attempts to sweeten the deal or what incentives he offers, Kris simply refuses to budge.

She isn't interested in allowing Free to leave BC Sol, and Theodore's attempts to negotiate with her ultimately get him nowhere.

Now, naturally, this presents a problem for Theodore.

He wants Free on his team, but he isn't going to accomplish that by continuously negotiating with someone who has already made it abundantly clear that she has no intention of accepting his offers.

If he continues approaching the situation in the same manner, he's simply going to keep getting rejected.

So, rather than continuing to pursue the same method, Theodore decides to change his approach entirely.

Because instead of trying to convince Kris to let Free go, Theodore decides to make Free want to leave BC Sol himself.

![img](i2uuk5ofykzh1)

During Episode 8, Theodore takes advantage of an opportunity presented to him: the opening of the WBBA European League, specifically the match between BC Sol and Top Vant.

This event provides him with the perfect opportunity to approach Free directly.

However, there's something important to understand about his intentions during this initial interaction.

Theodore wasn't immediately trying to recruit Free.

At least, not in the conventional sense.

He understood that simply introducing himself as the owner of the Raging Bulls and offering Free a place on his team wouldn't accomplish much.

![img](gvbpz18iykzh1)

In fact, we can see this almost immediately.

When Theodore introduces himself as the owner of the Raging Bulls, Free simply begins walking away.

He's not particularly interested in entertaining the conversation, nor does he seem to have any intention of listening to an offer from Theodore.

So, Theodore changes the subject.

![img](mrimsxxkykzh1)

Rather than bringing up recruitment, he begins talking about BC Sol and, more specifically, how heavily the team relies on Free.

This immediately catches Free's attention.

He stops walking and takes a moment to listen to what Theodore has to say.

He wasn't interested in getting Free's attention through an ordinary recruitment offer.

He wanted to introduce an idea that would actually make Free stop and think about his current situation.

Once he has his attention, Theodore begins explaining his perspective.

He points out that BC Sol would be an exceptionally weak team without Free.

He isn't necessarily insulting the team or suggesting that its members are inherently incapable of Blading.

Rather, he's pointing out a fundamental problem with how the team operates.

BC Sol has become so dependent on Free that its overall effectiveness is heavily tied to his individual performance.

In fact, Theodore goes even further by pointing out that BC Sol has essentially become useless despite previously being a good team.

And then he introduces an even more interesting possibility:

Perhaps Free himself is part of the reason why BC Sol has become so dependent and, consequently, so ineffective.

After all, if one individual is consistently responsible for carrying an entire team, what happens when that individual isn't there?

What happens when they lose?

What happens when they encounter an opponent that Free himself cannot defeat?

The team is left without the necessary strength to compensate for his absence.

And that's precisely the problem Theodore is bringing to Free's attention.

Eventually, Free walks away from the conversation.

But the fact that he initially walked away doesn't mean Theodore failed.

![img](er3ujugsykzh1)

Quite the opposite.

Theodore wasn't expecting Free to immediately agree with him or suddenly abandon BC Sol after a single conversation.

He simply wanted to introduce an idea that would remain in the back of Free's mind.

And, considering what happens afterward, this approach proves itself to be particularly effective against Free.

Now, you might be wondering why Theodore specifically chose this particular argument.

Why bring up BC Sol's dependence on Free?

Why suggest that Free's own strength could be contributing to the team's weaknesses?

The answer is actually rather simple.

Free genuinely loves BC Sol.

This isn't something that's merely implied through his actions, either.

![img](m37qlgf2zkzh1)

It's something that is directly established through multiple characters throughout the series, including Free himself.

On several occasions, members of BC Sol demonstrate just how much they rely on him.

And one particularly telling example comes from Kris herself, who makes a statement along the lines of:

"We won't lose, we have Free."

Now, while this might initially sound like an ordinary expression of confidence in one of their strongest members, there's another implication behind it.

The team's confidence is being placed almost entirely in Free.

It doesn't necessarily matter how the other members perform or whether they lose their individual matches.

As long as Free is there, they believe they can win.

This creates an unhealthy level of dependence, where the team's overall confidence and ability to succeed are tied to one individual.

And this is something that Theodore recognizes.

He understands that Free's relationship with BC Sol is precisely what makes his argument so effective.

Free cares about the team, and because he cares about it, he would naturally be concerned if he began to realize that his presence was preventing the other members from developing independently.

![img](qjah8447zkzh1)

In fact, Free himself eventually acknowledges that perhaps he loved BC Sol too much.

And that statement is particularly important when considering Theodore's approach.

He isn't trying to convince Free that BC Sol is a terrible team or that its members are worthless.

He's making Free question whether his own presence has created a situation in which the team cannot properly stand on its own.

Essentially, Theodore is taking something that Free genuinely values and presenting it to him from an entirely different perspective.

Instead of viewing his strength as something that benefits BC Sol, Free is being encouraged to consider whether it has inadvertently become something that holds the team back.

However, there's another aspect of Theodore's approach that makes this considerably more effective.

And that's the timing.

Simply telling Free that BC Sol is overly dependent on him wouldn't necessarily be enough to convince him to leave.

After all, someone can make an argument about a situation without necessarily providing the other person with a reason to believe it.

Theodore needed something that would allow Free to experience the problem firsthand.

And that's precisely why he chose to approach him immediately before the match.

By introducing this idea right before BC Sol's battle against Top Vant, Theodore ensures that Free has very little time to simply dismiss what he's been told.

Instead, the conversation becomes something that can naturally resurface throughout the match.

![img](1moi0t7gzkzh1)

Whenever Free is the one expected to perform, whenever the team struggles, and whenever the outcome depends on his individual strength, he has an opportunity to reconsider Theodore's words.

And this isn't limited to the match itself.

The thought can continue resurfacing whenever BC Sol experiences a loss or finds itself in a difficult situation.

![img](9jebtsrjzkzh1)

Because every time the team struggles without Free carrying them, it provides another opportunity for Theodore's argument to feel increasingly convincing.

Theodore isn't just making Free question the team's dependence on him.

He's encouraging Free to test whether that dependence is actually necessary.

Or, alternatively, to prove Theodore wrong.

And this is where we get one of the clearest demonstrations of how effective his approach was.

During the match, BC Sol eventually reaches the final battle.

The score is 2–2.

Both of BC Sol's victories have come through Free, while Valt and Rantaro have both lost their respective matches.

Under these circumstances, Free should naturally be the one to participate in the final battle.

![img](rl6kj3vmzkzh1)

After all, Kris doesn't want to lose, and Free has already demonstrated that he's the one capable of consistently securing victories for the team.

Yet, despite this, something rather interesting happens.

Free sends Valt into the final match instead of participating himself.

This is particularly significant when considering the circumstances surrounding the decision.

Free is the individual who has been carrying the team throughout the match.

He's the one who has secured their victories, and he's the person BC Sol would naturally rely on when facing a situation where losing is a possibility.

However, he allows Valt to take his place.

And this directly connects to the idea Theodore had introduced to him beforehand.

Free now has an opportunity to see whether the team can succeed without him being the one to finish everything.

Whether this decision was motivated by a desire to test Valt, to test BC Sol's independence, or to challenge Theodore's argument, the circumstances make it particularly relevant to the conversation they had earlier.

And although Valt ultimately wins the battle, there's another detail worth considering.

Free was still the person who carried the match.

Valt was the one who secured the final victory, but the two earlier victories that placed BC Sol in a position to win were both obtained by Free.

So, even though the team managed to finish the match without him participating in the final battle, the underlying problem Theodore brought up hadn't necessarily disappeared.

Free had allowed someone else to take the final match, but he was still the individual responsible for putting the team in that position.

In other words, Valt's victory demonstrates that another member of BC Sol is capable of stepping up and winning an important battle.

However, it doesn't completely invalidate Theodore's argument about the team's dependence on Free.

And this is exactly the sort of situation Theodore wanted to create.

![img](8o2r3dewzkzh1)

He had planted the thought beforehand, and the events of the match gave Free an opportunity to examine it for himself.

Now, with the match concluded, Theodore finally has the opportunity to make his actual recruitment attempt.

And this is where his earlier actions begin to come together.

Rather than approaching Free with an ordinary offer from the beginning, Theodore waited until he had introduced an idea that could fundamentally change how Free viewed his position within BC Sol.

He allowed Free to consider the team's dependence on him.

He gave him an opportunity to see another member step into an important position.

And, most importantly, he waited until Free had experienced all of this before making his next move.

Essentially, Theodore strikes while the iron is still hot.

![img](x4l9s3l00lzh1)

He takes advantage of the situation and approaches Free again, this time bringing up the possibility of joining the Raging Bulls.

And now, unlike their previous interaction, Free has considerably more reason to entertain the offer.

The circumstances have changed.

Before, Free had little reason to leave BC Sol. He was attached to the team, valued his position there, and had no particular incentive to accept Theodore's recruitment attempts.

But now, he has been given something to think about.

He has begun considering the possibility that BC Sol might be too dependent on him, and that perhaps the team needs to develop beyond simply relying on his strength.

And Valt's performance provides another important factor.

Valt has demonstrated that he can step up and secure a victory when given the opportunity.

Even if Free was responsible for the majority of BC Sol's success throughout the match, Valt's victory shows that the team isn't necessarily incapable of functioning without Free taking the final battle.

This gives Free another reason to consider leaving.

He now has someone who can potentially help carry the team forward.

And that's important because Free doesn't necessarily have to view leaving BC Sol as abandoning a team that has no chance of succeeding without him.

Valt has demonstrated that the team has someone capable of taking responsibility.

So, Theodore's approach accomplishes precisely what he wanted.

He doesn't have to convince Kris to release Free through negotiations anymore.

Instead, he has created circumstances in which Free himself has a reason to consider leaving.

And with Free now joining the Raging Bulls, Theodore has successfully acquired the first piece of something considerably bigger.

Because Free was never only just another strong Blader that Theodore wanted on his roster.

There were two additional reasons why he specifically needed him.

And these are arguably the most important parts of the entire recruitment.

Now that Theodore has successfully gotten Free onto the Raging Bulls, he has access to opportunities that simply weren't available to him beforehand.

In particular, there are two individuals whose involvement is important to understanding what he was ultimately trying to accomplish.

Those individuals are Joshua Burns and Shu Kurenai.

And Free's presence on the team is directly connected to both of them.

First: Using Free to Recruit Joshua Burns.

![img](5i3oubqg0lzh1)

Theodore had already attempted to recruit Joshua before, but Joshua declined his offer.

So, naturally, this presented another problem.

Theodore wanted Joshua on his team, but his previous attempts to convince him hadn't worked.

However, Free's involvement changes the situation considerably.

You see, Joshua greatly admires Free.

![img](6shsclul0lzh1)

And Theodore understands that this admiration can be used to his advantage.

By getting Free onto the Raging Bulls, Theodore creates an opportunity to approach Joshua again under entirely different circumstances.

![img](6pdz5omr0lzh1)

Instead of simply presenting Joshua with another recruitment offer, he can now offer him the opportunity to join a team and be the leader of a team that already has someone he admires.

It changes the incentive behind Joshua's decision.

Previously, Joshua had no particular reason to accept Theodore's offer.

But now, with Free being part of the team, Joshua has something that could genuinely motivate him to join.

Essentially, Theodore is using Joshua's admiration for Free as a means of influencing his decision.

And rather than having to force Joshua into joining or continuously negotiate with him, Theodore can create circumstances in which Joshua would willingly accept the offer himself.

![img](jr7qvfpv0lzh1)

![img](no60pcpv0lzh1)

This is exactly what he wanted.

Second: Using Free to Break Down Shu.

![img](i14yzb5z0lzh1)

Theodore didn't just want Free for his strength or for the opportunities he provided in recruiting Joshua.

He also wanted someone who could thoroughly defeat Shu in battle.

And Free was particularly suited for this purpose.

Throughout their encounters, Free is shown to be capable of defeating Shu repeatedly and demonstrating a level of superiority that Shu struggles to overcome.

The important part here is that Theodore has access to someone who can consistently challenge Shu and expose the limitations of his current abilities.

![img](9m5e2as11lzh1)

By having Free battle Shu, Theodore can put him in situations where his existing strength isn't enough.

He wants to break Shu down.

Not simply defeat him in a few battles, but push him into a position where his existing identity, confidence, and understanding of his own strength are challenged.

Free's overwhelming ability provides the perfect means of doing this.

Every time Shu loses, he's given another reason to question his current capabilities.

Every time he finds himself unable to overcome Free, he's confronted with the gap between where he currently stands and where he wants to be.

And, considering Shu's personality and his desire to become stronger, these repeated defeats become harder and harder to bare.

![img](ttq67z051lzh1)

Theodore can use that frustration and dissatisfaction to push Shu toward seeking greater strength.

Ultimately, this is part of what prepares Shu for his involvement with the Snake Pit.

Theodore doesn't simply want Shu to become stronger through ordinary training.

He wants to bring him to a point where he becomes willing to abandon his previous methods and pursue strength through a completely different approach.

And Free is one of the key individuals Theodore uses to bring about that change.

Now, with all of these preparations having taken place, we can finally get to the outcome Theodore was working toward.

He has successfully recruited Free onto the Raging Bulls.

Through Free, he has created an opportunity to bring Joshua onto the team by exploiting his admiration for him.

And, more importantly, he has acquired someone capable of repeatedly defeating Shu and putting him under the pressure necessary to push him toward a different path.

All of these things contribute to the larger plan.

Because Theodore's objective wasn't simply to assemble a team of powerful Bladers.

He was arranging the circumstances surrounding several individuals to accomplish something much bigger.

And Shu is that objective.

Through the repeated battles and the pressure placed upon him, Shu eventually reaches a point where he becomes increasingly desperate to obtain greater strength.

This ultimately leads him toward the Snake Pit, where he abandons his previous identity and adopts the persona of Red Eye.

And that's exactly what Theodore wanted to happen.

He has successfully pushed Shu toward a situation where he believes that abandoning his former self is necessary to become stronger.

With Shu now operating as Red Eye, Theodore has accomplished another major part of his preparations.

And this leads directly into his plan for Shu's Disappearance.
