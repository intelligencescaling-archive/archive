Shu's Disappearance (Theodore's Manipulation of Valt, Daigo, Rantaro, Wakiya, and Cuza)

I'm going to explain the plan Theodore had in mind when he orchestrated Shu's disappearance.

Because, as we'll see, this wasn't simply a matter of making Shu disappear from everyone's sight.

There was considerably more going on behind the scenes.

The way Theodore arranged everything shows that he had several objectives he wanted to accomplish through this one incident.

First, I want to establish something important: Nobody, including the police, had any actual knowledge of where Shu was.

![img](5bh8ul2jzjzh1 "\"Shu's Missing!\"")

This is outright implied when Wakiya contacts Valt and Rantaro to inform them that Shu has gone missing.

If anyone had known where Shu was, there would have been no reason for Wakiya to gather these particular individuals and begin searching for him in the first place.

This is important because it establishes that the information they eventually discover throughout their search isn't something they stumbled upon by coincidence.

Rather, the information and circumstances surrounding their investigation were deliberately arranged by Theodore.

In other words, Theodore was essentially controlling the direction of their investigation from behind the scenes.

He was actively manipulating the circumstances surrounding their search to ensure that they would eventually arrive there.

And this brings us to the first part of his plan.

Theodore deliberately baited Wakiya into bringing three specific individuals along with him:

- Valt Aoi
- Rantaro Kiyama
- Daigo Kurogami

However, there was actually another person who would end up joining them along the way, although none of them were aware of this beforehand.

That person was Cuza Ackermann.

And this is particularly interesting because, once again, Theodore had timed everything perfectly.

![img](p2f2fumnzjzh1 "\"I'm training on my travels!\"")

Cuza was going to be in Mexico around the same time as the others, allowing Theodore to incorporate him into the situation without having to go through any particularly complicated arrangements.

Theodore wanted Wakiya, Valt, Rantaro, and Daigo there for several natural reasons, which I'll explain later.

Cuza was included for largely the same reasons, although he also served another specific purpose that would become relevant during their journey.

But before getting into that, let's look at how Theodore actually directed them toward the Snake Pit.

Theodore took advantage of an already existing circumstance: the Raging Bulls were scheduled to participate in a team battle in Mexico.

This gave him an opportunity to manipulate the situation without having to create an entirely artificial reason for everyone to travel there.

![img](fhyhrdmrzjzh1 "\"There's been a rumor...\"")

On top of that, there were already rumors circulating about the Snake Pit being located somewhere in Mexico.

And this is where Wakiya's involvement becomes particularly important.

![img](hgocevmvzjzh1 "\"There's a 300% chance that Shu's in Mexico!\"")

Wakiya naturally had access to his family's connections and resources, which he could use to investigate Shu's disappearance.

By utilizing those connections, he was able to uncover information regarding the Snake Pit and the rumors surrounding its location.

![img](15h2rgnyzjzh1 "\"Someone who wants to challenge themselves might want to go there\"")

Naturally, this would lead them to reach a fairly reasonable conclusion:

If Shu was anywhere, there was a good chance that he was at the Snake Pit.

Theodore had already accounted for the possibility of them reaching this conclusion.

He was taking advantage of the circumstances surrounding their investigation, allowing them to believe that they were independently uncovering clues and following leads.

But the direction they were heading in was exactly where he wanted them to go.

This becomes even more apparent once they actually arrive in the area surrounding the Snake Pit.

![img](hjmb5jm30kzh1)

While they were flying over the jungle where the Snake Pit was located, Theodore allowed them to take a photograph of it.

Now, this is an important detail because later on Theodore is shown to possess the ability to either relocate the Snake Pit entirely or conceal it from sight afterward.

Meaning that allowing them to photograph the location wasn't necessarily an oversight or a mistake on his part.

He could afford to let them know where it was because he had already accounted for what would happen once they arrived.

Furthermore, the location itself presented another obstacle.

![img](lv9xx6b80kzh1 "\"We can't land there.\"")

The aircraft they were travelling in couldn't simply land wherever they wanted.

The jungle surrounding the Snake Pit wasn't an appropriate landing location, meaning they were forced to use an ordinary landing spot near the entrance of the jungle.

This is another circumstance that naturally directs their movements.

They couldn't just fly directly to the Snake Pit, nor could they approach it however they pleased.

They had to land at a designated location and make their way through the jungle on foot.

And, as we'll see, Theodore had accounted for that as well.

![img](6hlgdhod0kzh1 "\"Woahhh...\"")

As they made their way through the jungle, they eventually came across a zip-line.

There was just enough equipment for all five of them.

Theodore—once again—had already accounted for the exact number of people who would be making their way toward the Snake Pit.

He wasn't simply providing a convenient method of transportation for whoever happened to arrive.

The equipment was seemingly arranged with their particular group in mind.

Furthermore, the zip-line effectively established the only direction they could take to continue their journey.

And while crossing it wouldn't present much of a problem for Valt, Rantaro, Daigo, or Cuza, there was one person for whom this would be considerably more difficult.

Wakiya.

![img](hu2ui9gg0kzh1 "\"I don't even wanna touch the rope, why would I wanna BE the rope?!? 😭\"")

The reason being that Wakiya has a fear of heights.

Even if one of the others attempted to cross back over the zip-line to help him, Wakiya would naturally become even more hesitant.

Given his fear, having someone approach him while he was already struggling with the height would likely cause him to retreat further.

So, how exactly was Theodore accounting for this particular obstacle?

Well, this is where Cuza's involvement matters, Cuza is a performer, and one of his abilities is tightrope walking.

Because of his experience as a performer, he possesses the balance and coordination necessary to walk across the rope itself.

And that's exactly what he does.

Rather than attempting to convince Wakiya to cross normally or having someone else approach him in a way that would make him even more uncomfortable, Cuza is able to walk across the rope itself.

![img](me53zsul0kzh1 "\"STOP BOUNCING UP THERE!!!\"")

This catches Wakiya off guard and gives him the opportunity to push him forward, getting him across the obstacle.

Once Wakiya has made it to the other side, Cuza can simply tightrope-walk his way back across.

And with that, the problem has been resolved.

This is another reason why Cuza's presence is so interesting.

His particular skill set provides a solution to an obstacle that would otherwise have prevented the group from progressing smoothly.

Theodore's arrangements weren't simply about getting five people to the Snake Pit. They also accounted for the individual circumstances of the people involved.

With everyone successfully across the zip-line, they could finally make their way to the Snake Pit.

![img](h4by0c9q0kzh1)

Once they finally reached the Snake Pit, they were greeted by Red Eye.

Or, more accurately, Shu.

He was already stationed outside, waiting for their arrival.

This is another fairly direct indication that Theodore was expecting them.

After all, consider the circumstances.

They had travelled all the way through the jungle, overcome the obstacles along the way, and eventually arrived at the entrance to the Snake Pit.

And there was Shu, already positioned outside, ready to confront them.

Theodore had arranged for Red Eye to be there because he wanted this particular interaction to happen.

![img](wbtpko6y0kzh1 "\"Shu Kurenai is dead!\"")

As Red Eye confronted them, he began pushing the narrative that Shu was dead.

He rejected his former identity, claimed that the person they knew was no longer there, and treated them as though they were nothing more than obstacles standing in the way of his pursuit of strength.

He also claimed that Shu wasn't worthy of Spryzen, which was why he had taken possession of it.

And, to reinforce this image, he proceeded to battle Valt and defeat him.

![img](ylgh2h0d1kzh1 "\"Get out.\"")

After doing so, Red Eye told them to leave.

Now, at first glance, this might seem like Theodore's intention was simply to have Red Eye defeat them and drive them away from the Snake Pit.

However, that's not actually what he wanted.

And we can see this through what happens immediately afterward.

Despite Red Eye having already returned inside, Theodore deliberately kept the entrance open for them.

He didn't close the doors and prevent them from entering.

Instead, he allowed the entrance to remain accessible long after the battle had concluded.

![img](8tneqez41kzh1 "\"So you've finally arrived...\"")

Eventually, he addressed them himself, saying:

"So you've finally arrived, young men?"

Not only does it now directly establish that Theodore was expecting their arrival, but it also reinforces the fact that their journey to the Snake Pit had been anticipated and planned from the beginning.

He wasn't surprised to see them, and he was waiting for them.

And despite Red Eye having just defeated Valt and instructed everyone to leave, Theodore was perfectly willing to let them enter.

This tells us that Red Eye's confrontation wasn't intended to end their investigation.

Theodore wanted them to witness Red Eye's strength, experience the difference between their current abilities and the standards of the Snake Pit, and ultimately become involved in what was happening inside.

So, with that established, we can finally get into the actual purposes behind Theodore orchestrating this entire situation.

Now, what exactly was Theodore hoping to accomplish by arranging all of this?

Well, there are five different purposes that can be identified from the events surrounding Shu's disappearance.

1. Gathering data on World-Class Bladers and their Beys.

![img](iij9h68t1kzh1 "\"Truly unique samples...\"")

The first purpose is fairly straightforward.

By deliberately allowing Valt, Rantaro, Daigo, Wakiya, and Cuza to enter the Snake Pit, Theodore creates an opportunity to observe several World-Class Bladers and their respective Beys.

These aren't just ordinary Bladers.

They possess considerable experience, developed techniques, and Beys that have been tested against high-level competition.

![img](b3vecor22kzh1 "\"Of course we're watching.\"")

Having individuals of this caliber enter the Snake Pit gives Theodore access to valuable information.

He can observe how they battle, how their Beys perform, what techniques they utilize, and how they respond to different opponents.

Essentially, their presence provides him with an opportunity to collect data on Bladers who operate outside the usual standards of the Snake Pit.

2. Testing World-Class Bladers against Snake Pit Bladers.

![img](7m3eae382kzh1 "\"Shown them.\"")

The second purpose is to see how World-Class Bladers perform against the Bladers trained within the Snake Pit.

By having these individuals participate in battles against one another, Theodore can directly examine how the abilities of World-Class Bladers compare to those of his own organization.

This allows him to evaluate their strengths, weaknesses, techniques, and overall capabilities.

Rather than relying exclusively on information obtained from outside sources, he can observe these battles firsthand.

3. Testing Snake Pit Bladers against World-Class Bladers.

This is essentially the opposite side of the previous objective.

While the second purpose focuses on evaluating the World-Class Bladers, the third focuses on evaluating the Snake Pit Bladers themselves.

By exposing his Bladers to opponents with different levels of experience and different battle styles, Theodore can see how effectively they perform against outside competition.

He can examine whether their training has prepared them for opponents of this caliber, how well they adapt to unfamiliar techniques, and whether they possess the strength necessary to compete beyond the environment in which they were trained.

In other words, It's also about determining how strong his own Bladers have become.

4. Determining how far Red Eye is willing to go for strength.

![img](5v6is1pp2kzh1)

Now, this is arguably one of the more important purposes behind the entire situation.

Theodore wanted an opportunity to genuinely observe Red Eye and determine just how far Shu was willing to go in his pursuit of becoming stronger.

Remember, Shu had essentially abandoned his previous identity and adopted the persona of Red Eye.

He rejected his former relationships, distanced himself from the people who once mattered to him, and began pursuing strength through the methods taught by the Snake Pit.

However, simply adopting a new identity doesn't necessarily prove that someone has completely abandoned their past.

And that's something Theodore would want to investigate.

By bringing Valt and the others into the Snake Pit, he creates an opportunity for Red Eye to confront people who are directly connected to his former life.

They're individuals who knew Shu before he became Red Eye.

Their presence provides Theodore with a way to observe how Red Eye reacts when confronted with reminders of his previous identity.

Does he hesitate?

Does he show any attachment to the people he once considered friends?

Or has his desire for strength genuinely become more important to him than everything he previously valued?

Theodore wants to see how far Shu is willing to go and whether he has truly abandoned the person he used to be.

5. Preparing Boa to become Red Eye's future pursuer.

![img](i6pzzqa53kzh1 "\"Admiration is a tool.\"")

And finally, we have the fifth purpose, which is particularly interesting because it involves Boa.

Boa is a Snake Pit Blader who idolizes Red Eye.

And Theodore takes advantage of this admiration by giving Boa the opportunity to watch Red Eye battle and demonstrate his strength.

This isn't simply about allowing Boa to admire a powerful Blader.

Theodore wants him to study Red Eye.

By observing Red Eye's battles, Boa can learn from his techniques, his abilities, and the way he approaches Blading.

At the same time, watching Red Eye defeat opponents of considerable strength reinforces Boa's admiration for him.

Theodore is essentially encouraging Boa to become even more invested in Red Eye's abilities.

And there's a particular reason why this matters.

![img](fvprhb6j4kzh1 "\"Chase after Red Eye.\"")

Theodore is preparing for the possibility that Red Eye will eventually leave the Snake Pit in search of stronger opponents.

If that happens, Theodore wants someone who can continue keeping an eye on him.

And Boa is the perfect candidate for that role.

By encouraging Boa to idolize Red Eye and study his battles, Theodore is effectively preparing him to follow after Red Eye when the opportunity presents itself.

So, rather than simply allowing Boa to admire Red Eye, Theodore is cultivating that admiration for a particular purpose.

He wants Boa to eventually chase after him.

5. The Disappearance of the Snake Pit

And there's one final detail I'd like to bring up, because it ties back to something I mentioned earlier about Theodore deliberately allowing the group to discover the location of the Snake Pit.

After everyone had been defeated by Red Eye and subsequently thrown out of the Snake Pit, Cuza made an interesting observation.

The pyramid was simply gone.

Now, once again, it shows that Theodore had deliberately allowed them to discover the location in the first place.

Remember, Cuza didn't immediately make this observation the moment they were thrown out.

His statement implies that he had spent a considerable amount of time searching around the jungle before reaching the conclusion that the pyramid had disappeared.

And this is especially notable when you consider that Cuza also has a bird.

Meaning that he has access to an additional means of searching the surrounding area, one that can cover considerably more distance than he could on his own.

Despite this, the pyramid was nowhere to be found.

Which brings us back to Theodore's ability to either relocate the Snake Pit or conceal it from sight.

The fact that the pyramid had completely disappeared after their encounter suggests that the location they had previously discovered was no longer accessible in the same way.

And this makes the earlier events even more interesting.

Theodore allowed them to photograph the jungle, discover the supposed location of the Snake Pit, and make their way through the obstacles leading toward it.

He even allowed them to enter the organization and interact with its Bladers.

Yet, once everything he wanted to accomplish had been achieved, the Snake Pit was no longer there for them to find.

This means that the location itself was never something Theodore needed to keep hidden from them permanently.

He only needed to make it accessible for long enough to accomplish his objectives.

Theodore wasn't merely using Shu's disappearance as an opportunity to bring a few Bladers to Mexico.

1. He was using the entire situation as a controlled operation.

2. One in which he could manipulate their investigation.

3. Determine who would arrive.

4. Account for the obstacles they would encounter.

5. And arrange the encounters that would take place once they reached the Snake Pit.

All five individuals had a reason to be there, and their presence served different purposes within Theodore's plans.

From collecting data on World-Class Bladers, testing the capabilities of Snake Pit members, examining Shu's commitment to his new identity, and preparing Boa to eventually follow Red Eye, Theodore managed to accomplish several objectives through a single orchestrated event.

And once those objectives had been fulfilled, he could simply make the Snake Pit disappear again.
