The Requiem Project (Theodore's Manipulation of Shu Kurenai)

Now I'm going to explain what Theodore actually had in mind throughout this phase, including the exact reason why Red Eye eventually left the Snake Pit in the first place.

At first glance, Red Eye's departure might seem like something that simply happened because he had no reason to remain there anymore.

However, when looking at the circumstances surrounding his departure and what Theodore does afterward, it becomes much more apparent that this was another situation Theodore had already accounted for.

In fact, Red Eye leaving the Snake Pit was necessary for Theodore's plans to progress.

To start with, let's establish exactly what caused Red Eye to leave.

![img](brnmupmuz10i1)

While Red Eye was away from the Snake Pit, Ren Wu Sun and Xander Shakadera arrived at the facility.

And they didn't simply defeat one or two Snake Pit Bladers.

They proceeded to completely defeat the Snake Pit Bladers before leaving.

This is extremely important because of how Red Eye would interpret what happened afterward.

From his perspective, the Snake Pit was no longer providing him with what he wanted.

He had already reached a point where he was dissatisfied with the strength available to him there, and now two powerful Bladers had entered the Snake Pit and defeated its members.

So, naturally, Red Eye would come to the conclusion that there was nothing left for him to gain from remaining there.

He had no reason to continue staying in a place that could no longer provide him with the opponents he needed.

![img](a5zgh9zyz10i1)

Because of this, he informed Boa to tell Theodore that he was going to leave the Snake Pit and pursue his own plans.

And this is where something important needs to be established.

Theodore wasn't there when Red Eye made this decision.

At the time, Theodore was occupied with his responsibilities as the owner of the Raging Bulls.

However, that doesn't mean Red Eye's departure was outside of his plans.

Quite the opposite.

He actually wanted Red Eye to reach the conclusion that he had outgrown the Snake Pit on his own.

The important part is that Theodore didn't have to directly tell Red Eye to leave.

Red Eye himself reached that conclusion.

And that makes the decision much more effective because, from Red Eye's perspective, he's acting according to his own desires.

But Theodore had already accounted for what would happen once Red Eye left.

Now, this raises an obvious question.

How exactly were Ren Wu Sun and Xander Shakadera able to find the Snake Pit in the first place?

![img](5bt3b454020i1)

After all, we've already established that in my last analysis that Theodore has the ability to move the Snake Pit and make it disappear from sight.

We've already seen him allow people to discover its location and then subsequently remove it from the area.

So why would he allow Ren and Xander to find it?

The answer is essentially the same reason he allowed Wakiya, Valt, Rantaro, Daigo, and Cuza to find it earlier.

He wanted them to find it.

The Snake Pit's location being discovered wasn't necessarily a security failure.

It was another opportunity Theodore could exploit.

![img](3gku2sbe020i1)

And, in this particular case, the person who benefits the most from Red Eye's departure is Boa.

Theodore needed someone capable of keeping track of Red Eye once Red Eye began traveling around the world in search of stronger opponents.

And Boa was the perfect candidate.

Boa already idolized Red Eye.

He already viewed him as an incredibly powerful Blader and had a strong desire to learn from him.

Theodore could therefore take that admiration and redirect it toward a specific purpose.

Once Red Eye left, Theodore could use Boa as someone who could pursue him, monitor his movements, and keep track of him whenever Theodore himself was unable to do so.

And this is something that is directly reinforced by what happens a few episodes later.

After Red Eye leaves the Snake Pit, Theodore has Boa defeat all of the remaining Snake Pit Bladers.

And he doesn't simply have Boa defeat them with his existing Bey.

He gives him a new Bey in the process.

This is significant because Theodore isn't merely replacing Red Eye with Boa.

He's actively developing Boa.

He is taking the person who already admires Red Eye and turning that admiration into motivation.

Theodore is effectively telling Boa, through his actions, that he has the potential to surpass the person he currently idolizes.

He begins feeding Boa the idea that he, rather than Red Eye, can become the strongest Blader in the world.

And this creates a very interesting contradiction within Boa's mindset.

On one hand, he continues to admire Red Eye.

On the other hand, Theodore is encouraging him to believe that he can eventually surpass Red Eye.

That admiration doesn't disappear.

Because now Boa isn't simply looking at Red Eye as someone he wants to imitate.

He's looking at him as someone he wants to eventually catch up to and surpass.

This becomes extremely important later.

Theodore has essentially created a person who can follow Red Eye, monitor him, and continue developing himself while chasing after the same goal.

So Red Eye's departure accomplishes two things simultaneously.

It gives Red Eye freedom to pursue the next stage of Theodore's plan, while also giving Theodore an opportunity to build Boa into a useful asset.

Now let's return to Red Eye himself.

Once he's free from the Snake Pit, Theodore wants him to pursue his own plans.

And what exactly are those plans?

He wants Red Eye to hunt down and battle the strongest Bladers around the world.

But there is a very specific reason why Theodore wants Red Eye doing this.

Because eventually, Red Eye needs to attract the attention of someone from his past.

A certain Blader.

A Blader who Shu desperately wants to defeat.

That person is Lui Shirosagi, the Number 2 Blader in the world.

Theodore understands that if Red Eye continues hunting the strongest Bladers around the world, eventually his path will cross with Lui.

It's practically inevitable.

If Shu wants to challenge the strongest Bladers, and Lui is one of the strongest Bladers in the world, then eventually the two of them are going to meet.

However, there is a problem.

Red Eye isn't strong enough yet.

Theodore knows that.

![img](ituvipho020i1 "\"You won't be able to beat me.\"")

At this point, Red Eye is still nowhere near Free's level.

Which matters because Lui and Free are relatively comparable in strength during this period.

If Free represents a level of strength that Red Eye still hasn't reached, and Lui is operating around that same general level, then Red Eye isn't ready for the confrontation he ultimately wants.

He needs to be pushed further.

And Theodore has a very specific way of accomplishing that.

He needs Red Eye to lose.

It might seem strange at first.

Why would Theodore intentionally allow the Blader he has spent so much time developing to lose?

Why wouldn't he simply allow Red Eye to continue winning until he eventually became strong enough to defeat Lui?

Because the loss itself is necessary.

Theodore needs Red Eye at his absolute lowest point.

And there are four specific reasons for this.

* Reason #1: Making Red Eye Question Why He Lost

First, Theodore needs Red Eye to question himself.

Red Eye has become obsessed with strength.

His entire identity has been built around becoming stronger than the person he used to be.

![img](x56zs0wa120i1 "\"You are Red Eye.\"")

He has discarded Shu in pursuit of that goal.

So if he reaches the point where he believes he has finally become the strongest version of himself, only to suddenly lose, it creates a psychological problem.

![img](jssa22jk120i1 "\"Why did I lose?...\"")

He has to ask himself:

\- Why did I lose?

\- What was missing?

\- What did he do wrong?

\- Why wasn't his strength enough?

Theodore needs these questions to remain in his mind because they prepare Red Eye for what comes next.

The loss isn't supposed to simply make him physically stronger.

It's supposed to make him psychologically vulnerable.

And that vulnerability becomes extremely important when Theodore eventually intervenes after the loss.

* Reason #2: Making Boa Question Red Eye's Strength

The second reason involves Boa.

Theodore has spent all this time building Boa's admiration for Red Eye.

He's made Red Eye into the person Boa looks up to.

He's encouraged Boa to study him.

He's encouraged him to believe that Red Eye represents the highest level of Blading.

So what happens when Boa sees that Red Eye isn't actually unbeatable?

It creates a conflict.

Boa can no longer blindly believe that Red Eye is the strongest.

And Theodore can exploit that.

![img](kxxqvppu120i1 "\"You disappointed me!\"")

Boa can lash out at Red Eye.

He can tell him that he disappointed him.

He can question why the person he admired so much was defeated.

And that criticism is important because it adds another layer of pressure onto Shu.

It's no longer just Shu himself wondering why he lost.

Now someone who idolized him is questioning him as well.

Theodore can use Boa's disappointment to reinforce the doubts that have already begun forming inside Red Eye's mind.

* Reason #3: Convincing Shu to Abandon Legend Spryzen

The third reason is connected to Shu's Bey.

Theodore needs Shu to abandon Legend Spryzen.

He needs him to believe that the Bey itself has become useless.

The logic Theodore is pushing is extremely simple:

A Bey that has lost has failed.

And if Legend Spryzen has failed to accomplish what Red Eye wanted, then why should he continue using it?

This isn't merely about replacing a Bey with a stronger one.

Theodore wants Shu to associate his previous Bey with his previous identity.

Legend Spryzen represents the Blader Shu used to be.

It represents his past.

It represents the version of himself that Theodore wants him to discard.

So getting Shu to abandon Legend Spryzen isn't simply getting him to use a new Bey.

It's another step toward making him abandon everything associated with his former self.

The Bey has failed.

Therefore, it must be discarded.

And if the Bey representing Shu's former identity is discarded, then Shu himself can be pushed toward abandoning that identity as well.

* Reason #4: Bringing Red Eye to His Absolute Lowest Point

And finally, Theodore needs Red Eye at his absolute lowest.

This is perhaps the most important reason of all.

Red Eye wouldn't simply abandon Legend Spryzen while he was still confident.

He wouldn't completely submit himself to Theodore's philosophy while he still believed his own judgment was superior.

Theodore needs to break down that confidence.

He needs Red Eye to reach a point where he is willing to accept Theodore's words as law.

![img](jsz98hha220i1 "\"That's why you lost.\"")

And the perfect way to accomplish this is to make Red Eye believe that his own decision-making is what caused him to lose.

Red Eye had been pursuing strength on his own.

He had been making his own decisions.

He had ignored Theodore's guidance.

And then he lost.

Therefore, Theodore can frame the situation in a very specific way:

\- You lost because you didn't listen to me.

This gives Theodore authority over Shu's future decisions.

If Shu accepts that logic, then the solution becomes obvious from his perspective.

If his own judgment caused him to fail, then he needs to stop trusting himself.

He needs to trust Theodore instead.

And that is exactly where Theodore wants him.

He doesn't merely want Shu to obey him.

He wants Shu to reach a psychological state where obeying Him feels like the only logical path toward becoming stronger.

And now, with all four of these objectives accomplished, Theodore can finally begin the next stage.

![img](a734xsny220i1 "\"Let the Requiem Project begin!\"")

The Requiem Project.

At this point, Shu has been broken down psychologically.

He has lost.

He is questioning himself.

Boa has questioned him.

Legend Spryzen has failed him.

And Theodore has positioned himself as the person who possesses the answers Shu needs.

Now Theodore can place Shu inside a Stasis Pod of some sort.

The pod forces Shu into a state of sleep while subjecting him to a series of tasks designed specifically around the data Theodore wants to obtain and the psychological changes he wants to force upon Shu.

Essentially, every single task is designed around something Theodore wants Shu to overcome or discard.

![img](df4wz5rt220i1)

The tasks include:

* Defeating Luinor and Lui.
* Defeating Free and Fafnir.
* Speaking and conversing with Spryzen, which exists as an entire spirit.
* Discarding Valt from his mind.
* And finally, once again defeating and slicing through the Beys previously mentioned, allowing Shu to completely discard what they represent.

The process is forcing Shu to repeatedly confront the very things Theodore wants him to overcome.

Lui represents the person Shu has been chasing.

Free represents the level of strength Shu hasn't yet reached.

Spryzen represents the relationship between Shu and his Bey.

Valt represents an important part of Shu's past.

And the final battles serve as the culmination of the entire process, forcing him to overcome and discard everything that Theodore believes is holding him back.

And the most important part is that Theodore doesn't simply stop the process when Shu begins showing signs of physical or mental strain.

![img](sc4sgqx8320i1)

The process continues for days.

Even when Shu's vitals begin spiking.

Even when the machines begin losing readings.

Even when his condition becomes increasingly unstable.

Theodore orders the process to continue.

And the reason is simple.

![img](h3xr7nhc320i1)

He knows that Shu is capable of enduring it.

More specifically, he understands that Shu's stubbornness is exactly what makes him suitable for the project.

Someone else might have broken down completely.

Someone else might have refused to continue.

Someone else might not have been capable of enduring the sheer amount of physical and psychological stress being placed upon them.

But Shu is different.

His stubbornness is one of his defining characteristics.

He refuses to give up.

He will continue pushing himself even when his body is telling him to stop.

And Theodore knows this.

So rather than viewing Shu's stubbornness as an obstacle, Theodore turns it into an asset.

The same determination that allowed Shu to continue pursuing strength becomes the very thing that allows Theodore to push him through the Requiem Project.

And eventually, after all of this data has been collected, Theodore finally has what he needs.

The battles, the opponents, the techniques, the beys, etc.

Everything has been recorded.

![img](igse613j320i1)

And Theodore uses the data generated through this process to create Shu's new Bey:

Spryzen Requiem.

It is the culmination of the data collected throughout the entire process.

The Bey is created using the information obtained from the specific Beys Shu was forced to confront during the project.

Theodore essentially takes the information gathered from these battles and uses it to create a Bey designed around the abilities he has observed.

And this is where the entire process begins to come together.

Theodore deliberately broke Shu down.

He forced him to confront stronger opponents.

Thus, he collected information from those encounters.

He used Shu's performance and the data generated by those battles.

![img](cxm6i7lp320i1)

And then he used all of that information to create the weapon that would become Spryzen Requiem using Shu himself.

But Theodore isn't finished yet.

Creating Spryzen Requiem isn't enough.

He needs to know whether it actually works.

So he takes Shu in front of a machine containing the data of the world's top Bladers.

![img](30mu2m8v320i1)

This includes individuals such as:

* Lui Shirosagi
* Free De La Hoya
* Xander Shakadera
* And other elite Bladers.

The machine is capable of taking the information stored within its database and producing holographic representations of these Bladers and their Beys.

![img](l4scggky320i1)

But these aren't simply ordinary visual holograms.

They are capable of physically interacting with an actual Bey.

This means Theodore can use the machine to recreate battles against the data of some of the strongest Bladers in the world.

And, naturally, this provides him with the perfect environment to test Spryzen Requiem.

He can put Shu and his new Bey against simulated versions of the world's strongest opponents without needing those actual Bladers to be physically present.

![img](wgwi028c420i1)

This allows Theodore to determine exactly how Spryzen Requiem performs against the abilities it was designed around.

\- Theodore has gone from manipulating Shu's circumstances, to breaking down his identity, to forcing him through an artificial training process, to gathering data from his battles, to using that data to construct a new Bey, and finally to testing that Bey against simulations of the strongest Bladers in the world.

\- Shu has been stripped down, rebuilt, and given a Bey constructed from the very data gathered through his own suffering.

\- Theodore can now determine whether the result of that process—Spryzen Requiem—is capable of doing what he created it to do.

With all of the needed information out of the way, we reach the final—most important—detail of the entire Requiem Project.

Theodore didn't actually expect Spryzen and Shu to become one.

At least, that wasn't something he had explicitly anticipated beforehand.

However, when it happened, his reaction is extremely telling.

![img](5s8udxh8620i1)

He basically says:

* "This is more than I expected... No, this is exactly what I wanted!"

That statement completely changes the Requiem Project.

Because even if Theodore didn't initially know that Shu and Spryzen would literally become one, the moment that possibility manifested, he immediately recognized it as the ideal outcome.

And this means that the Requiem Project wasn't simply about creating a stronger Bey.

It was also about creating the possibility of unifying the Blader and the Bey.

Theodore wanted Shu to reach a point where the distinction between himself and Spryzen could potentially disappear.

The Bey wasn't merely something Shu controlled.

It could become an extension of Shu himself, and Shu could, in turn, become an extension of Spryzen.

This is why Theodore's reaction is so important.

He doesn't say that the result is an accident or that something went wrong.

![img](trpjwcge620i1)

Instead, he immediately recognizes the result as something desirable.

That implies that the possibility of Shu and Spryzen becoming one was either:

\- A. An intended potential outcome of the project

or

\- B. At the very least, a result that Theodore had been hoping the project could produce.

It was about completely reconstructing Shu—physically, psychologically, and even through his connection with his Bey—until he could become something fundamentally different from the person he had been before.

\- Theodore first pushed Shu away from his old identity.

\- He allowed him to become Red Eye.

\- He allowed him to leave the Snake Pit and hunt the strongest Bladers.

\- He directed him toward Lui.

\- He needed him to lose.

\- He needed that defeat to destroy his confidence.

\- He needed Boa's disappointment to reinforce it.

\- He needed Shu to discard Legend Spryzen.

\- He needed him to abandon his past.

\- He needed him to reach his absolute lowest point.

\- And only then could he place him inside the Stasis Pod and begin the Requiem Project.

\- The project then took everything Shu had experienced and converted it into data.

\- That data was used to create Spryzen Requiem.

\- And finally, Theodore tested Requiem against the strongest Bladers in the world.

The result was something even greater than a powerful Bey.

\- Shu and Spryzen became one.

And Theodore's reaction makes it clear that this wasn't a problem for him.

It was the culmination of everything he had been working toward.

With Shu reconstructed, Spryzen Requiem completed, and the data of the world's strongest Bladers incorporated into the process, Theodore had finally created the weapon he wanted.

The Requiem Project was almost complete, and now he could go for a real test run—that being The God Blader's Cup.
