Theodore's Manipulation of Boa and Norman (God Bladers Cup)

The person who benefits the most from Red Eye's departure is Boa.

Theodore needed someone capable of keeping track of Red Eye once Red Eye began traveling around the world in search of stronger opponents.

The problem was that Theodore couldn't personally follow Red Eye everywhere.

He had his own responsibilities as the owner of the Raging Bulls, meaning he couldn't simply abandon everything to monitor Shu's movements.

If Red Eye was going to travel from place to place, challenging increasingly powerful Bladers, Theodore needed someone else who could follow him and keep an eye on what he was doing.

![img](o8hukmlo6n1i1 "\"Follow him.\"")

And Boa was the perfect candidate.

Boa already idolized Red Eye.

He already viewed him as an incredibly powerful Blader and had a strong desire to learn from him.

Red Eye was essentially Boa's ideal of what a Blader should be.

Theodore could therefore take that admiration and redirect it toward a very specific purpose.

Once Red Eye left the Snake Pit, Boa could pursue him.

He could monitor where Red Eye went, observe the opponents he fought, and keep track of his development whenever Theodore himself was unable to do so.

It is directly reinforced by what happens a few episodes later.

![img](8ztelft17n1i1 "\"Congratulations on making it this far.\"")

After Red Eye leaves the Snake Pit, Theodore has Boa defeat all of the remaining Snake Pit Bladers.

And Theodore doesn't simply have Boa defeat them using the Bey he already possesses.

He gives Boa a new Bey in the process.

He's actively developing Boa into a better blader.

Theodore is taking someone who already possesses an intense admiration for Red Eye and turning that admiration into motivation.

![img](t4m2va877n1i1 "\"You will be the Ultimate Blader!\"")

He's effectively telling Boa through his actions that Red Eye isn't necessarily the end goal.

Boa can surpass him.

This creates a very interesting contradiction within Boa's mindset.

On one hand, Boa continues to admire Red Eye.

He still looks at him as someone incredibly powerful and someone worth learning from.

On the other hand, Theodore is encouraging Boa to believe that he himself can eventually become the strongest Blader in the world.

And that means Boa's admiration for Red Eye doesn't disappear.

Boa is no longer simply looking at Red Eye as someone he wants to imitate.

He's now looking at him as someone he eventually wants to catch up to and surpass.

Theodore is therefore turning Boa's admiration into ambition.

Red Eye becomes the standard Boa measures himself against.

The stronger Red Eye becomes, the greater the challenge becomes for Boa.

And the farther ahead Red Eye gets, the more Boa has to improve if he wants to surpass him.

This creates a useful motivation for Theodore.

Boa now has an independent reason to continue pursuing Red Eye.

Theodore doesn't have to constantly order him to follow Shu.

Boa wants to do it.

He wants to see how strong Red Eye has become.

He wants to learn from him.

And, eventually, he wants to prove that he can surpass him.

So Theodore has essentially created a person who can simultaneously:

- Follow Red Eye.
- Monitor Red Eye.
- Study Red Eye.
- Improve himself by chasing Red Eye.
- And eventually challenge Red Eye.

This is exactly what Theodore needs.

Red Eye's departure therefore accomplishes two things simultaneously.

It gives Red Eye the freedom to pursue the next stage of Theodore's larger plan, while also giving Theodore an opportunity to develop Boa into a useful asset.

And Theodore doesn't stop there.

Because he also begins preparing another person for a future encounter with Red Eye.

Theodore has spent all this time building Boa's admiration for Red Eye.

He's made Red Eye into the person Boa looks up to.

He's encouraged Boa to study him.

He's given Boa the means to become stronger.

And he's encouraged Boa to believe that he can eventually surpass the person he currently idolizes.

But Theodore also begins encouraging Norman to take an interest in Red Eye.

![img](nq3fdypz7n1i1 "\"The God Bladers Cup!\"")

And this becomes particularly important when we reach the God Bladers Cup.

At first, it might seem strange that Theodore would encourage another Snake Pit Blader to become interested in Red Eye.

After all, if Theodore already has Boa, why does he need Norman?

The answer becomes clear when we consider what Theodore ultimately needs.

He needs strong opponents.

Not just people who can fight Red Eye.

He needs opponents capable of pushing Red Eye and, eventually, testing Spryzen Requiem.

And Norman provides another piece of that puzzle.

![img](uwxzv1h8bn1i1 "\"Norman, do you have what it takes to crush Red Eye?\"")

Theodore has spent so much time building Red Eye up as the strongest figure within the Snake Pit.

Boa idolizes him.

The others recognize his strength.

Red Eye becomes the standard by which the Snake Pit Bladers measure themselves.

But eventually, Theodore can turn that admiration into doubt.

If Boa is led to believe that Red Eye is the strongest, and then circumstances arise that demonstrate that Red Eye isn't actually invincible, Boa naturally has reason to question what Theodore has told him. (Red Eye's lost to Lui)

He can begin wondering whether Red Eye was genuinely the strongest Blader...

![img](asprp1yn7n1i1 "\"Am I just a tool to you?\"")

Or whether Theodore was simply using him as a tool.

Boa doesn't necessarily have to stop caring about Red Eye.

Instead, his admiration can transform into frustration.

He can begin thinking:

"If Red Eye isn't the strongest, then why was I told to follow him?"

And eventually:

"Was I just another tool?"

That doubt becomes useful for Theodore.

![img](j7g625ts7n1i1 "Gaslight of doom and despair 😭")

Because Theodore doesn't need Boa to blindly obey him forever.

He needs Boa to remain motivated enough to continue pursuing Red Eye.

And if Boa believes that Red Eye has failed to live up to the image Theodore created, that can actually make him even more determined to challenge him.

Norman's position is slightly different however.

Norman would naturally understand that Theodore is using the Snake Pit Bladers for his own purposes.

![img](w972s2sh8n1i1 "\"Not Interested.\"")

He's not necessarily someone who would blindly accept Theodore's schemes or allow himself to be emotionally manipulated in the same way Boa might be.

But Norman doesn't necessarily care.

He doesn't need to oppose Theodore's plans simply because he knows that Theodore is manipulating him.

Norman has his own objective.

![img](la11z35l8n1i1 "\"I'll show you I'm the strongest in the Snake Pit.\"")

He wants to prove that he is the strongest Blader in the Snake Pit.

And Red Eye represents an obstacle to that goal.

So even if Norman understands that Theodore is using him, he can still willingly participate.

![img](l9aclabr8n1i1 "\"I'm only interested in Red Eye.\"")

His reasoning becomes incredibly simple:

"I don't care what Theodore wants. I want to defeat Red Eye."

And this is exactly what Theodore wants.

Because Norman can believe that he's acting independently.

He can believe that he's entering the competition because of his own desire to prove himself.

He can believe that he's refusing to become Theodore's tool.

But from Theodore's perspective, that doesn't matter.

Because whether Norman enters willingly or unwillingly, whether he accepts Theodore's manipulation or sees through it, the result is the same.

Norman is still going to fight Red Eye.

And that's all Theodore needs.

Boa and Norman don't necessarily have to believe that they're working for Theodore.

In fact, Theodore benefits from them believing the opposite.

![img](gxoj4gq09n1i1 "\"I've already surpassed you!\"")

Boa can believe that he's pursuing Red Eye because he admires him and wants to surpass him/already has.

Norman can believe that he's pursuing Red Eye because he wants to prove himself as the strongest Blader in the Snake Pit.

Neither of these motivations has to originate from Theodore's direct commands.

Theodore has already created the circumstances necessary for those motivations to naturally lead them toward the outcome he wants.

Theodore doesn't need to control every decision they make.

He only needs to control the circumstances surrounding those decisions.

If Boa wants to pursue Red Eye because he admires him, Theodore benefits.

If Norman wants to defeat Red Eye because he wants to prove himself, Theodore benefits.

If both of them believe they're acting for themselves, Theodore still benefits.

Because ultimately, both of their motivations lead them to exactly the same destination.

Red Eye.

![img](vuu13i7j9n1i1 "\"God Bladers Cup.\"")

Once we reach the God Bladers Cup, Theodore has already established the motivations that will cause the both of these Bladers to participate.

Boa has his admiration for Red Eye.

Norman has his desire to prove himself.

Both of them have their own personal reasons for wanting to face Red Eye.

But Theodore has another reason for wanting them there.

He needs opponents capable of testing Spryzen Requiem.

The tournament, in which he created, provides him with the perfect environment.

Instead of having to personally arrange individual battles between Red Eye and various powerful opponents, Theodore can place them all within the same competition.

![img](ffz3jlrpbn1i1 "\"He's pulling the strings to match the brackets to his liking.\"")

Now he has a controlled environment in which Red Eye can battle multiple high-level Bladers.

And every battle provides more information.

Every opponent reveals something.

Every battle tests another aspect of Spryzen Requiem.

Every victory or defeat gives Theodore additional information about what the Bey is capable of.

And this is where everything surrounding Boa and Norman finally comes together.

They may believe they're entering the tournament because they want to defeat Red Eye.

But Theodore has already accounted for that desire.

Their motivations become another part of his plan.

![img](yhpajeggan1i1 "\"The Snake Pit was just a place for him to create the circumstances to create and perfect Spryzen Requiem using this tournament.\"")

Theodore is shown to have anticipated and foresaw the God Bladers Cup in advance.

The tournament wasn't something he suddenly decided to take advantage of after it began.

He had already been preparing for the circumstances surrounding it.

That means everything we've discussed up until this point can—and is—preparation for this eventual stage.

Free was recruited.

Joshua was brought into the Raging Bulls.

Shu was pushed toward becoming Red Eye.

Red Eye was allowed to leave the Snake Pit.

Boa was developed and given a reason to pursue Red Eye.

Norman was given a reason to want to challenge Red Eye.

And eventually, all of these pieces converge within the God Bladers Cup.

They're interconnected stages of the same plan.

The tournament gives Theodore exactly what he needs:

A large collection of powerful Bladers.

A reason for them to fight one another.

A reason for Boa and Norman to confront Red Eye.

![img](sqpn51bsan1i1 "\"Perfecting Spryzen Requiem.\"")

And, most importantly, an environment in which he can test the result of the Requiem Project and perfect Spryzen Requiem.
