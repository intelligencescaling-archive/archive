Theodore's Manipulation of Free De La Hoya (God Bladers Cup)

The God Bladers' Cup brings together 12 elite Bladers from across the world to determine who can truly be crowned the “true champion.”

![img](k486tzmmo23i1 "\"Theodore's Handpicked Chosen 12.\"")

On the surface, this is simply the ultimate competitive tournament—a gathering of the strongest Bladers, all competing against one another under increasingly intense conditions.

However, from Theodore’s perspective, the tournament serves a much more specific purpose.

![img](hcu88uxuo23i1 "\"It's disgusting how Norman and Boa managed to enter without any official records in the game.\"")

Theodore is the one pulling the strings behind the scenes, meaning the tournament’s lineup, competitors, and matchups are a part of the environment he has deliberately constructed.

He isn't simply waiting to see what happens when the world's strongest Bladers compete.

He is creating a controlled stress environment in which the absolute ceiling of Beyblade power can be pushed to its limits.

And that is precisely what Spriggan Requiem was created for.

Theodore’s objective has never been simply to produce another powerful Bey.

![img](deepqzz2p23i1 "\"This tournament was all for the sake of perfecting Spryzen Requiem.\"")

He wants to perfect Spriggan Requiem by exposing it to the strongest possible opponents and gathering data from their battles.

![img](1nzs7y4dp23i1 "\"It isn't about how strong you are; it's about how much you want to win over your opponent.\"")

The God Bladers' Cup therefore provides the ideal testing ground: place elite Bladers into an environment where victory becomes increasingly difficult, force them to push beyond their normal limits, and then use the resulting battles to determine how far Spriggan Requiem can go.

This is important in the case of Free De La Hoya.

![img](vav5ksgpp23i1 "\"I'd die if it meant I had a chance to become stronger.\"")

Free had previously developed beyond the ruthless, win-at-all-costs mentality that characterized his older self.

![img](yg860rv3q23i1 "\"He's still the current free.\"")

He had matured considerably, becoming more connected to his teammates and no longer relying purely on an aggressive mindset to achieve victory.

However, that growth did not erase the mindset entirely. Under sufficiently extreme circumstances, the older version of Free could still resurface.

That is exactly what happens when Free reaches the highest levels of the tournament.

![img](p0kidpxbq23i1 "\"Reverting to his old self.\"")

As the competition becomes increasingly intense and Free is pushed toward the top, he ultimately chooses to revert to his old self in order to reach the level necessary to win.

What makes this significant is that his mentality regresses without his experience regressing.

His mind is still that of the current, more mature Free, but he temporarily adopts the ruthless and unrestricted approach of his former self.

In other words, Free combines the experience and development of his present self with the aggressive mentality that he had previously abandoned.

![img](orir0pgpq23i1 "\"It's for fun.\"")

That allows him to push himself far beyond the limits he would normally impose upon himself.

However, that regression comes with an obvious cost.

Free's older fighting mentality is considerably more physically demanding because it prioritizes victory over restraint.

By deliberately pushing himself beyond his normal limits and refusing to fight conservatively, Free places an enormous amount of strain on his body.

The longer he maintains this state, the greater the risk of overexertion, exhaustion, and ultimately injury becomes.

This is where the structure of the God Bladers' Cup becomes especially important.

![img](7yqunq00r23i1)

Free is not simply facing one difficult opponent and choosing to push himself. He is participating in a tournament stacked with elite contenders, where every successive battle can force him to expend more energy and reveal more of his capabilities.

The competition progressively narrows toward the strongest Bladers, meaning that the closer Free gets to the top, the less room there is for him to fight conservatively.

Eventually, the tournament creates exactly the kind of situation where the strongest competitors are forced to max out against one another.

![img](o0xmcnr7t23i1 "\"I'm the strongest Blader.\"")

![img](6afrkkr7t23i1 "\"I won't lose to anyone.\"")

Free's regression, therefore, is not some completely unpredictable emotional breakdown.

Once the conditions Theodore has constructed are taken into account, it becomes a foreseeable reaction to extreme competitive pressure.

Free has already demonstrated that his older mentality exists within him, and the right amount of pressure can bring it back out.

The important distinction is that Theodore does not need to directly command Free to regress.

He only needs to create circumstances intense enough that Free chooses to do it himself.

And that is precisely why Free's eventual physical deterioration matters to Theodore's overall plan.

![img](u203geu9r23i1 "\"Finally, my dream can be realized.\"")

Theodore's primary objective is Spriggan Requiem and the realization of his own ambitions.

The other Bladers are ultimately valuable to him because of what they can contribute toward that objective.

Their personal well-being and longevity are secondary to the data, development, and results he can obtain from them.

This becomes even more apparent when considering Red Eye.

![img](x6qc1ubor23i1 "\"Show me what you can do...Red Eye, or rather, Spriggan.\"")

Theodore's endgame is centered around Red Eye/Shu becoming the ultimate Blader and proving Spriggan Requiem's superiority.

Theodore's plan does not require Free to remain healthy and capable throughout the entire tournament.

In fact, if Free's limits are pushed to the point where he suffers severe physical consequences, that can actually benefit Theodore's objective.

The stronger Free is at his peak, the more valuable he is as a test for Spriggan Requiem, or he's given everything he's got and didn't make it to the end.

But once Theodore has extracted everything he needs from Free, preserving Free's longevity is no longer the priority.

This is why Free's injury is a foreseeable consequence of the environment Theodore deliberately created, rather than simply an unfortunate accident.

When elite Bladers are placed into a high-stakes tournament against one another, extreme physical strain is an obvious possibility.

When one of those Bladers possesses a known tendency to revert to a more aggressive, physically demanding fighting style when pushed to the brink, the risk becomes even more predictable.

Theodore created the pressure cooker.

![img](gzw7031js23i1 "\"Hand-picked the 12 strongest Bladers for this very tournament.\"")

He assembled the conditions in which the strongest Bladers would be forced to fight at their absolute limits, knowing that Free's desire to remain at the top could eventually push him back toward his former mentality.

Once Free reverted to that mentality, the increased physical strain and risk of injury were natural consequences.

Thus, It is an acceptable byproduct of the plan.

Theodore does not need every Blader to emerge from the tournament unharmed.

He needs them to push themselves far enough to provide the strongest possible test for Spriggan Requiem.

And because his ultimate objective centers on Red Eye winning, Free's health ultimately becomes expendable in comparison to the success of the Requiem Project.

The God Bladers' Cup therefore functions as much more than a competition for the title of “true champion.”

![img](azzjew50s23i1 "\"He's become stronger than I anticipated.\"")

It becomes the final pressure chamber for Theodore's experiment: gather the strongest Bladers, force them into increasingly extreme confrontations, push them beyond their ordinary limits, and use the resulting battles to determine just how far Spriggan Requiem can go.

Free's regression and subsequent physical strain fit directly into that framework.

Theodore did not only take advantage of the tournament's pressure.

He constructed an environment where that pressure was inevitable.

Knowing that the strongest Bladers would eventually be forced to reveal their absolute limits.

And accepting the physical consequences that came with it.
