First, I shall be explaining a few things regarding X-Gaster's ability to create things, similarly to what I did in my previous post revolving around his Emotional Intelligence.

Understanding the limitations and requirements of this ability is essential because X-Gaster's creation ability isn't simply a matter of producing anything he wants through sheer will.

Rather, there are established conditions surrounding what he can create, and those conditions tell us a great deal about the amount of information he is capable of acquiring, retaining, processing, visualizing, and applying.

X-Gaster's ability to create things is heavily reliant on his existing knowledge and understanding of whatever he intends to create.

His ability isn't one that allows him to produce absolutely anything he can imagine without any limitations.

Instead, his creations depend on the information available to him.

In other words, before X-Gaster can properly create something, he needs to possess some degree of knowledge regarding what that thing actually is.

He needs to know what he is attempting to reproduce, and he needs to understand the relevant characteristics of what he is creating.

And, importantly, he needs to be able to visualize what that creation is supposed to be.

This limitation is demonstrated very early on in X-Tale.

![img](f0ca2rt2v11i1)

![img](itt45ot2v11i1)

For example, when Ink tells X-Gaster to "Hold the phone," X-Gaster responds by indicating that he doesn't even know what a phone is.

It establishes a limitation regarding his creation ability.

X-Gaster cannot simply create something that he has absolutely no knowledge of or understanding of in the first place.

If he doesn't know what something is, then he lacks the necessary information required to reproduce it.

![img](bazvt58rw11i1)

His ability to create things is fundamentally dependent upon what he knows and what he can mentally conceptualize.

If X-Gaster lacks the necessary knowledge to understand what something is, he cannot reliably recreate it, let alone reproduce it accurately.

![img](yv1r8971021i1)

![img](ojbxn971021i1)

Establishing that his creations are not completely independent of his own cognition.

His ability doesn't function as though he possesses an unlimited database containing every possible object, structure, concept, or phenomenon.

Rather, the quality and nature of his creations are dependent upon the information that he has previously acquired.

Whatever X-Gaster creates must therefore be informed, to some degree, by his own knowledge, perception, understanding, memory, and visualization.

With that established, we can now move on to the actual scale of what X-Gaster is capable of creating.

![img](oxx76rtev11i1)

X-Gaster isn't creating the universe entirely from scratch with absolutely no reference point for what it should be like.

Instead, he had already been exposed to various individuals and Alternate Universes through Ink.

![img](lhuo8avjv11i1)

Ink effectively gives X-Gaster access to information regarding other worlds, allowing X-Gaster to observe different universes and the things contained within them.

This means that X-Gaster has a reference point.

He is taking information that he has gathered through observation and using that information as the foundation for his subsequent creations.

But now we have to ask:

How accurately could X-Gaster recreate the universe based solely on the information he had gathered through observation?

To answer this, we first need to consider another important aspect of X-Gaster's characterization.

![img](jayck64xv11i1)

Following his overwriting of the first timeline, we learn more about his personality, particularly his perfectionistic tendencies.

X-Gaster is a perfectionist.

He possesses a strong desire to ensure that things turn out exactly as he intends them to, rather than simply accepting an outcome that deviates from his ideal vision.

Establishing that X-Gaster isn't someone who simply creates things without caring about their accuracy or quality.

![img](sxymdw16z11i1)

His perfectionism demonstrates that he places considerable importance on ensuring that the things he creates conform to the vision he has in mind.

He isn't satisfied with simply producing something that is "close enough."

His goal is perfection.

![img](ruhwm95h021i1)

Naturally, this same principle should apply to his creations.

After all, if X-Gaster's perfectionism were completely irrelevant whenever he used his creation abilities, it would undermine the significance of this particular personality trait.

It would also create a inconsistency within his characterization.

His desire for perfection would suddenly cease to matter whenever he was creating something, despite creation being one of the most significant applications of his abilities throughout X-Tale.

And if his perfectionism didn't influence the way he approached his creations, then a fundamental aspect of his personality would lose much of its relevance within the story.

![img](u5mlcafgw11i1)

Considering that the events of X-Tale are heavily connected to X-Gaster's desire to create his own ideal outcome and perfect story.

Therefore, it is reasonable to apply his perfectionistic tendencies to the way he approaches the creation of everything around him.

![img](hnqtpus7w11i1)

When X-Gaster creates the universe, he isn't attempting to reproduce a vague approximation of its physical appearance.

He would instead have to reproduce the universe according to the information he had gathered from observing other universes.

This means accounting for the various details, structures, characteristics, and relationships that make those universes what they are.

![img](z3ppfnnbx11i1)

X-Gaster's only established reference point for these universes comes from what he observed when Ink showed him the Doodle Sphere and the various Alternate Universes contained within it.

X-Gaster would have to rely heavily on the information he gathered through observation.

He isn't being handed a complete set of instructions explaining every individual component of the universe.

![img](n9fo8g7yy11i1)

Instead, he observes these universes and then uses the information he obtained from those observations to construct his own reality.

This requires him to take the information he has perceived and apply it toward an extremely large-scale creation.

Argos, him recreating an entire universe.

That means the information involved is exponentially greater than what would be necessary to reproduce an isolated object.

He would need to account for the various components that make up the universe while simultaneously maintaining a coherent representation of the whole.

Furthermore, after overwriting the original timeline, X-Gaster recreates the timeline again with the intention of making it more perfect.

This means that the feat isn't about creating a single physical environment.

![img](xai828upx11i1)

It also involves his ability to conceptualize and manipulate the structure of the timeline itself.

He has to understand what he is creating well enough to reproduce it, while simultaneously maintaining the overarching structure necessary for his intended reality.

And thus: it brings us back to the original limitation of his ability.

He cannot create what he does not know.

The fact that X-Gaster is capable of creating these things means that he possesses the necessary information and understanding required to conceptualize them in the first place.

The more complex the thing he creates becomes, the greater the amount of information he must possess regarding that thing in order to reproduce it accurately.
