The 10th Doctor's Six Words Feat analysis

This is one of my favorite feats the Doctor pulled off in either show, and probably one of his best excluding live action material, and since it's really short I figured I'd put out a simple analysis to show what he's capable of.

The feat happened in the pilot episode of season 2 from the revival show, "The Christmas Invasion."

For context, the newly elected Prime Minister of Britain is Harriet Jones, a friend of the Doctor whom he initially greatly respect. This changes when, after the Doctor defeats some alien invaders and sends them packing, Harriet decides to use a secret superweapon created from alien tech to destroy the alien ship, in order to avoid them potentially returning or telling other aliens about Earth.

This angers the Doctor, who sees it as unnecessary murder, and he notes that he should've stopped her. Harriet threatens him back by asking if she should consider him ti be an alien threat as well, and at that, he warns her not to challenge him, as he could "bring \[her\] government down with a single word". Harriet doesn't buy this of course, thinking that it's impossible, but after a couple of seconds, he corrects himself by saying that he could do it with six.

With this being such a specific answer, and Harriet knowing what the Doctor is capable of, part of her starts believing it, and this shows when she gets increasingly defensive as she insists the Doctor wouldn't be capable of it and he continues stating "six."

At this point, the Doctor turns to a man who works for Harriet, and who was likely distracted (though he was looking in their direction, his behavior later seems to suggest he didn't fully grasp the situation, and I believe the plan wouldn't have worked if he had been following the exchange), removes his earplug and utters in a casual tone...

"Don't you think she looks tired?"

This is what gets it started. It's basically a self-fulfilling prophecy, as Harriet is now stressed about the Doctor's threat, which is well-seeded in her mind. So she immediately asks the man what the Doctor told him, and he, who likely wasn't paying attention and didn't initially place importance on the random comment, says it was nothing, but internally notes how distressed she seems about this.

We catch up with this situation later, as Harriet is being questioned on live TV about rumors on her mental health and unfitness for her role, with that kind of predatory insistence typical of journalists (no hate to journalists, it's just how it is), while she tries to change subjects and insist she's fine in a way that lets her stress through.

What's implied to have happened is that the man who had internally noted the six words told someone else, probably reinforcing the notion due to Harriet's change in behavior, starting an avalanche effect that results in quick-spreading rumors and feeds any opposing politicians or narrative on top of that.

Meanwhile, Harriet is still aware of the Doctor's threat and sees the increasing adversities as part of the Doctor's plan and his threatening promise, amplifying her nervousness significantly and continuing to feed into the rumors with her increasingly stressed behavior.

This eventually leads to Harriet leaving her role as Prime Minister, which the Doctor achieved with nothing but six words, successfully predicting the psychological, emotional, and social processes regarding:

\- Harriet herself, her initial paranoia, her way of dealing with the stress and adversities, and her eventual inability to deal with the situation.

\- The random guy, what impression the scene he witnessed would leave him, and how he would ponder on the six words the Doctor uttered.

\- All of Britain pretty much: the general people, how rumors tend to form, spread, and escalate, how journalists hop on trends, cash on controversy, attack celebrities, how political opponents would take advantage of the situation to undermine Harriet as a head figure, and probably even more.

All of this, mind you, being formulated in a couple of seconds at most.

This is the end of the analysis, thanks for reading!
