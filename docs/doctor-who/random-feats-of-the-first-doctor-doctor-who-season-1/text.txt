Random feats of The First Doctor (Doctor Who; Season 1; 1963-1964)

The First Doctor is easy to overestimate if you haven't seen any of his Episodes, and easy to Underestimate if you only see the first handful. The First Doctor wasn't even supposed to be the main character of the show at first, but as time went on and the BBC figured out what they wanted to do, he solidified himself as the main character and became much more of a happy go lucky genius rather than the sour prick he starts out as at the beginning.

An Unearthly Child- This is an interesting concept in general. It can be very boring if you're looking through the wrong lens, but this is a story where The Doctor, after mocking 20th century humans for not being anywhere near his species' level of intelligence, finds himself completely helpless when he finds himself in cavemen times where intelligence means nothing to anyone. That being said, he Does manage to become a detective part way through and prove to the other cavemen that Kal is the murderer and not Za by showing whoose: 'Knife' (Rock) has blood on it. It's not exactly the most amazing thing ever, but the show does a good job at showing the impressive was of being able to get the cavemen to comprehend that level of detective work. (The Doctor is so savage in these early Episodes, that he's even about to straight up murder a wounded caveman with a rock for holding his group back, before Ian stops him, with The Doctor quickly coming up with the excuse that he was: "about to try and get him to... To Draw our way back to the TARDIS)

The Daleks- The Doctor manipulates his group into exploring the city of Skaro by making it appear that the fluid link to the TARDIS is broken. (I'm reality, it's perfectly fine, but he makes it Register as having no mercury)

The Edge of Destruction- While everyone, including The Doctor, is basically in a drunken hallucinating frenzy, The Doctor is the only one to put together even some semblance of coherence, which proves to be enough to realize there's a problem with the fast return button that was about to cause their imminent deaths if The Doctor didn't figure out what to do.

Marco Polo- The Doctor creates a fake key to trick Marco Polo into thinking he's been locked out of the TARDIS. In fact, The Doctor and co. almost manage to escape his grasp entirely halfway through the story, but they're forced to go back on their plan when they realize Susan was kidnapped when trying to say goodbye to Ping Cho. (Don't give The Doctor too much credit here, as while he manages to put up a good chess game against Kublai Khan, he fails to win when it really matters.)

The Keys of Marinus- In a society where people are guilty until proven innocent, The Doctor basically becomes a real life detective and manages to deduce the crooks' plan for stealing the final key, and manages to get Ian off of death row.

The Aztects- The Doctor shows a random guy a plant that he can use to scratch someone and cause them to grow weak. (He realizes too late that this random guy is planning to use it on Ian)
The Doctor also makes Coco in this Episode and accidently gets engaged. Making Coco isn't that impressive, but doing it in an ancient society is.

The Sensorites- The Doctor deduces that the Sensorites' biology causes their pupils to expand in the light instead of contract, meaning bright light would severely hurt them.
The Doctor also straight up cures a disease far worse than the common cold in this Story. (He would later go on to Literally cure the common cold in Season 3).
There's also a mention at the beginning of this Story that back in the day, when he visited Henry VII, he got thrown a person's nose at him, before he threw it back at Henry VII, which pissed him off enough to order that he and Susan be taken to the tower for imprisonment, however that was all part of his plan, as the TARDIS was inside the tower, allowing them to escape.

The Reign of Terror- This Story never fails to put me to sleep, but The Doctor manages to give Akiyama a run for his money as he puts on a disguise and passes as a high status person while walking around France in the middle of the French Revolution. He's eventually caught, but it doesn't really matter, as they manage to escape back to the TARDIS in the end.
