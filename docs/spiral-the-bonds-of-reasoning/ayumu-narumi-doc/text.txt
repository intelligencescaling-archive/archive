Brief Synopsis:
The story begins with the disappearance of Kiyotaka Narumi, an absolute genius who leaves his younger brother, Ayumu, trapped in a labyrinth of crimes and conspiracies. Ayumu must not only solve the cases presented to him but also do so under the suffocating pressure of the Blade Children: young people blessed with superhuman talents yet cursed by a genetic anomaly that condemns them to self-destruction.

Ayumu lives convinced that he is merely a “cheap copy” of his brother. His crippling lack of self-esteem is his greatest enemy; he believes that any success he achieves is only because he is following in Kiyotaka's footsteps.

Alongside Hiyono Yuizaki and his sister-in-law, Madoka Narumi, he will attempt to unravel the mystery of his missing brother and the dark destiny that binds them all.

The trick used by Mizue is called “Pulse Stop”, a method commonly used by magicians and mentalists: By placing a solid object in the vertex of the armpit and firmly pressing the arm against the torso, direct mechanical pressure is exerted on the artery that passes through that area (the brachial artery). In standard Japanese, Kanji is used for words with meaning and Hiragana for grammatical connection.

死の静止館: Shi No Seijyukan/Seijiyukan

• 死 (Shi – Kanji for death)

• の (no – Hiragana, possessive particle)

• 静止 (Seijyu/Seiji – Kanji for stillness/immobility)

• 館 (Kan – Kanji for mansion)

The approximate translation is "The Yew Mansion”.

In a modern magazine or newspaper, property names are often written in Katakana to highlight the proper name, or with the Kanji of the tree.

• アララギ館 (Written in Katakana: A-ra-ra-gi-kan)

• 蘭館 (Written in Kanji: Araragi-kan)

Although “Araragi” is an old reading, in botanical texts or catalogs it is often represented in Katakana:

• アララギ (A-ra-ra-gi)

Next, an explanation of the only significant feat in this volume—and it is not Ayumu’s, but Eyes’.

Context: Ryouko tells Eyes that they are going to play a game. However, since she hates mind games, it will be one she enjoys: a reflex challenge.

There are three balls, and eight meters separate them. He must hit her with them, while she will obviously try to dodge. He can throw the balls however he wants—one at a time or all at once—but the balls cannot touch the ground before hitting her; it must be a direct hit.

She also says she chose that game deliberately, since he could damage his pianist fingers. If he truly wants her on his side, he must prove he can overcome that disadvantage. Ryouko claims she has never lost; no one has ever hit her—not even a famous talented pitcher with “fastballs”.

Eyes says that is because they did not understand the essence of the game. It is not merely a test of reflexes; it is more like chess, because it requires ingenuity and judgment.

Ryouko thinks:

The balls are rubber and light; not even a professional pitcher could throw them at very high speed. Moreover, they are eight meters apart, so she can easily dodge them.

The game is not about throwing hard, but about creating an opportunity to hit the opponent with one ball, using the other two as traps or feints, based on the opponent’s movements and the logical way they will try to dodge. Then you corner them and throw the last ball when they can no longer move freely.

It is a duel of ingenuity, judgment, and physical mastery.

The person dodging also has it difficult, since they must read the thrower’s movements, even predict where he will throw, and quickly adapt to the opponent’s strategy in order to dodge the last—and truly dangerous—ball.

This is where Eyes’ scheme begins:

He tells her that he understands the way to win: essentially, he must make her lose her balance with two balls and then land the hit with the last one.

But he will not do that. Instead, he tells her that he will win using only one ball—the first one.

It should be noted that Ryouko believes this is a bluff, that he is using his aura of unshakable calm to deceive her. She thinks she can understand his strategy. In her view, it is impossible to land a hit with a single throw considering the theory of the game, yet he calmly asserted that he would win that way.

Ryōko concludes that Eyes wants her to focus excessively on that first ball, concentrating all her attention on it.

He will throw the ball straight upward.

She will follow its trajectory and lower her guard.

Immediately, Eyes will throw the other two balls with extreme speed, using a wrist-and-elbow motion, like throwing knives.

Since he is a prodigious pianist, she assumes he is ambidextrous, which would allow him to execute this trick.

Also, the first ball, still in the air, has not touched the ground, so when it falls back into his hand he could use it and throw it as well if the other two fail.

Therefore, technically, Eyes would be winning by using the first ball.

Ryouko believes she understands Eyes’ strategy. She thinks that, despite the game heavily favoring her, Eyes has created something very dangerous. She will not be distracted by his bluff; her concentration will remain entirely on him.

Finally, Eyes throws the first ball upward. Then he tells her that, just as he predicted, she would not be distracted. Ryōko asserts that she understood his strategy perfectly. But Eyes exclaims that she made too many assumptions, and because of that, she lost. “Checkmate”, he says—and the ball that was in the air strikes Ryouko’s body.

He did not throw the ball in a straight line, but instead created a parabolic trajectory.

Knowing that Ryōko would assume he was bluffing and conclude that she should not be distracted by the first ball, Eyes ensured that the curving ball would hit her, relying on her blind confidence.

Not only because she believes the pianist is too delicate to defeat her on her own ground, but also because she did not assume that he had training behind him. Kanon taught him knife throwing and advised him that the best way to hit a moving target is to force it to become a fixed one.

Eyes also knew that she would not accept his request and would instead challenge him to a game. Since she does not want to help him, the game must give her the advantage while still giving Eyes a small chance. Because the game has an underlying logic, she assumed Eyes would follow it—while creating some kind of deception.

He used mind games to force her to become a fixed target, giving her the necessary clues so that she would reach a logical—but incorrect—conclusion. He also used his own image of effortless “perfection” to make her believe that his strategy will be simple, because someone like him “wouldn't make an effort”.

Ryouko. Her talent is physical.

Eyes will have to confront his past.

Ayumu’s Plan:

First, he understands the logic behind Kanon’s brief “freezes.” In the middle of the fight with the Blade Children, he took no damage at all, but the only occasions on which he was hit involved Madoka (a kick to the stomach) and Ayumu himself: Kanon pointed a gun at him, sensed the danger, but froze for a second when he saw him (enough time for Asazuki to push him away).

He understands that it is a matter of pride and a desire to hold on to his own will—a rebellion against fate. He chooses not to harm those who are uninvolved with his objective.

With this in mind, he creates his plan, which is to incapacitate him with a dart. The steps are as follows:

Send Rio and the others to look for Hoyino.

She must dress up as a cat.

Ayumu plays a piece of piano music that connects Eyes to Kanon. Kanon goes to the location, clearing the area for Rio and the others.

Ayumu has a brief conversation with Kanon in which he directly tells him that he knows about his “problem.”

He immediately carries out Phase 1 of his plan: he throws the grenades and, together with Madoka, charges at him to shoot him. Ayumu trusts that Kanon will understand what he is trying to do. You could say that his objective is simply to keep Kanon focused on what is happening in that moment and buy enough time for Eyes to arrive.

Kanon manages to resolve the situation, but Eyes appears. This is also just a distraction so that Hoyino can reach the designated location. Kanon apparently defeats Eyes.

Ayumu knows that, having defeated Eyes, Kanon will use Madoka to force Ayumu to kill him. Ayumu had already taken this into account, and knowing that Kanon is practically desperate to be freed from his guilt, he expects nothing but his own death.

Ayumu aims the gun at Madoka’s shoulder and fires, injuring Kanon and automatically unlocking his instantaneous reflexes. Ayumu does this so that Kanon will unconsciously attack him. Kanon forces himself not to.

Then Ayumu calls out to Eyes. Eyes gets up and moves toward one of the tranquilizer guns. Once again, Ayumu had taken into consideration that Kanon would not kill Eyes, but would only knock him unconscious with one of Ayumu’s tranquilizer guns. However, those guns contained darts without any tranquilizer. This, too, was merely a distraction.

Finally, Hoyino appears and points a gun at Kanon from behind. He immediately turns around and is about to shoot her, but recognizes her and freezes: she is dressed as a cat (an animal Kanon adores). This is the final blow, because he does not want to kill her, and that extra second gives Eyes the opening he needs to shoot Kanon. Kanon finally falls defeated.

This is somewhat a vague analysis, I know, but to make it easier to grasp: everything that happens during this confrontation was Ayumu’s plan.
