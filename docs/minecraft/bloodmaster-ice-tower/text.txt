Preparing for the destination

The planet on which Roscow is preparing to travel to is Iceika. It is not really explained where this fictional planet is, but we can extrapolate roughly based on its description and eventual sighting.

Iceika is a planet slightly farther from the Sun than Earth is, as it’s overall colder. (Naturally, we have to keep in mind that this isn’t our Solar System, but BloodMaster’s Minecraft Solar System, but just so we can get the general idea of what it is.) However, in spite of it being colder, it is still similar to Earth enough that it possesses forests, rivers, oceans, just generally normal ecosystems with some exotic animals present.

We also know that Iceika’s soil is very thin, as most of its minerals and ores are closer to the surface. This is due to the biological activity of this planet being slower.

Iceika’s placement, therefore, would approximately be somewhere around 1.1 to 1.25 AU, roughly at least.

Kepler’s third law of planetary motions states that the ratio of the square of an object’s orbital period with the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit is the same for all objects orbiting the same primary.

We can roughly figure out Iceika’s orbital period because of this. Because Iceika is located within this universe’ Solar System, therefore sharing our Sun, we have everything we need to figure out that duration.

Let’s say that ‘a’ is 1.15 AU. The root of 1,520875 is 1,233, which is then the amount of Earth years Iceika’s orbital rotation takes. Or roughly 450 days.

The puzzle

Given how you only have 3 questions to ask, it entirely depends on which entity you get by random luck. Given how Roscow was on Earth primarily, it’d make sense to cover Earth first before moving on to other planets.

With his first question when he asked whether the entity would answer with ‘Frek’, only to follow that question with the identical one, except asking whether it’d answer with ‘Tajt’, he eliminated the possibility that he’s interacting with either the truthful or the lying entity.
* If he asked the truthful entity the first question, and ‘Frek’ meant ‘yes’, the entity would’ve confirmed the correct statement and said ‘Frek’.
* If he asked the truthful entity the first question, and ‘Tajt’ meant ‘yes’, the entity logically couldn’t answer, as if it answered ‘Frek’, it’d be deconfirming the statement, but by doing it, it would make the statement correct, meaning that the entity would’ve been lying.
* If he asked the lying entity the first question, and ‘Frek’ meant ‘yes’, the entity also couldn’t answer because if it said ‘Tajt’, it’d be saying no, meaning that the statement was incorrect, but if it’s supposed to be lying, it can’t answer anything without telling the truth.
* If he asked the lying entity the first question, and ‘Tajt’, meant ‘yes’, the entity would’ve simply answered with ‘Tajt’, as it’d be lying.
* If he asked the truthful entity the second question, and ‘Frek’ meant ‘yes’, the entity would be met with a paradox once again, where it couldn’t answer without lying.
* If he asked the truthful entity the second question, and ‘Tajt’ meant ‘yes’, the entity would’ve simply said ‘Tajt’.
* If he asked the lying entity the second question, and ‘Frek’ meant ‘yes’, the entity would’ve simply lied and said ‘Frek’.
* If he asked the lying entity the second question, and ‘Tajt’ meant ‘yes’, it would remain silent due to the impossibility of avoiding the truth there.
