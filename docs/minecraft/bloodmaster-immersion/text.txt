The economy system explanation

Every citizen has a personalized “ID” of sorts that is carried around with them, which stores said energy. These credits come from the energy that you provide to the town, not physical money. Buying something means transferring stored energy to the seller.

But there is a catch:

Sold energy will proportionally go with the percentage of the designated provider of said energy which omitted that power, given how you can’t sell physical energy. Meaning that if you sell energy that you earned by building/creating something, then the buyer gets a proportional percentage ownership over that thing, meaning that it’s not just trading credits, and instead the credits are tied to the source of the energy, and selling them can transfer ownership rights in the source.

This means that a very useful machine that generates a lot of credit without outright spending proportional energy makes you an equivalent multi-millionaire, simply due to the value of your product.

Given this link between the energy market and property rights, it explains the city’s unusual architecture, with the majority of actually meaningful and impressive structures being left near the pool of resources (hills, swamps, forests…), while most people live in a more desperate situation, given how, even if you create something meaningful, can end catastrophically if you end up buying things too hastily, which means there’s still a societal hierarchy.

The economic exploitation

This makes the system very exploitable, but only if you have the means to back it up.

If someone with heaps of influence (in this case, energy credits) simply buys more energy from poorer townsfolk, just to allow them to get by day-to-day, you’re essentially taking their ownership of whatever small-scale thing they’ve built, and over time, the said influential person could amass ownership over many town assets without directly managing them, becoming a landlord of the productive infrastructure.

Future actions of Roscow will show us that he fully understood this exploitation tactic from the get-go it was explained to him.

Another tactic Roscow considered was an attempt to create a debt spiral by lending credits to people in need, and then demand repayment with interest in credits, and if they can’t pay, take ownership stakes in their energy sources.

Design quirk

In the midst of designing elevators, and testing some other things in the flat field that aren’t relevant for the doc to mention, he at one point tests some other tech that was initially made by the mayor, which utilizes, in the classic Minecraft fashion, a redstone comparator.

The way it normally works in game is that it compares two signals. Signal A, which is entered through the backside of the comparator, whereas B signal goes from either side, and the output will only be received once signal A omits a stronger signal than B. However, the outlet can be turned to subtraction mode, at which point, the A signal will be subtracted by the B signal, and the value of it is how much energy it will generate.

Nothing truly interesting happens there, until, while experimenting, notices a flaw in the mayor's system. While it’s activated in the subtraction mode and used for this purpose, the power it generates will be omitted in individual ticks, hence powering up the outlet the same way a repeater would.

However, once he turned the subtraction mode off and used it that way, the outlet omitted instantly, or rather in a singular discrete tick. Once he already set up this second line, but then turned the subtraction mode off, it still didn’t omit ticks and instead omitted instantly, despite two lines being practically identical.

Meaning that a subtraction mode ramped over ticks, but the comparison mode sent the signal as fast as it could, which was somehow then prescribed onto the subtraction mode once the signal was already omitted through said circuit.

Blod reasons that in subtraction mode, the system internally used a per-tick update loop to adjust the output toward A and B gradually due to a flawed implementation where it was attempted to prevent rapid oscillation, whereas with the comparator, the output value is binary, therefore either 1 or 0, which is computed instantly.

Once a comparison mode was turned into a subtraction mode during the signal circling itself, it bypassed the delayed logic, meaning that this error most likely happened due to a different internal mechanism for two modes, most likely reusing an asset delay for subtraction.

Given this error, Roscow can infer that the mayor's other tech has a similar internal problem, where a similar error would be overlooked. Now, in the scene itself, he doesn’t say this, but later scenes will prove it.
