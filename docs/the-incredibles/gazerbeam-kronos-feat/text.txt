GAZERBEAM “KRONOS” FEAT

During the Superhero Relocation Program, Gazerbeam publicly spoke out against the ban on supers, even at the cost of his public image and personal safety. He believed that repressing extraordinary abilities created a dangerous vacuum, one that would eventually be filled by those with malicious intent. This outspoken activism inevitably placed him on government watchlists and may have provided indirect data to third parties like Syndrome’s organization.

While his cause was noble, Gazerbeam’s advocacy for the repeal of the ban made him more visible to those running Operation Kronos. By the time Syndrome’s recruitment tactics began, he was already cataloged as a potential target. This heightened risk wasn’t lost on Gazerbeam; instead of going underground completely, he chose to live in semi-secrecy, maintaining a public face while masking his travel patterns and locations.

[PSI, Planning, Observation]

Syndrome’s Omnidroid program operated by selecting increasingly powerful Supers to “train” each model iteration. When Gazerbeam was contacted under the guise of a private mission, he suspected an ulterior motive. His internal alarm was not based on fear, but on pattern recognition, he had already heard whispers of Supers disappearing after accepting lucrative offers for “secret contracts.”

Accepting the invitation anyway, Gazerbeam reasoned that gathering firsthand intel on the mysterious island operations could help others avoid the same fate. This decision involved high-risk. He carried small recording devices hidden in his visor lining, intending to store environmental and acoustic data for later decryption.

[Inductive Reasoning, Planning, Observation, Info Gathering]

Upon arrival at Nomanisan Island, Gazerbeam immediately began surveying the terrain. He memorized the layout of the landing zone, noting the approximate distance between security towers, patrol drone flight patterns, and the sound profile of automated systems. His powers, which could generate precise high-energy laser beams from his eyes, were used sparingly to avoid alerting the Omnidroid’s sensors.

When escorted to the initial “test field,” he made mental notes of the Omnidroid’s gait, response time, and visible armor joints. Rather than trying to overpower it immediately, he engaged in controlled evasions, forcing the AI to reveal its adaptive combat learning routines. This was his first confirmation that the Omnidroid was deliberately escalating, designed for eventual execution.

[Perception, Analysis, Counteraction]

Days turned into a tense standoff between man and machine. By rotating between defensive maneuvers and targeted strikes, Gazerbeam survived long enough to map partial sections of the island in his mind. He discovered a hidden geothermal vent network,  natural tunnels that led into parts of the island not directly connected to the “official” combat arena.

Realizing that no escape would be possible without leaving something for future targets, Gazerbeam began thinking about information survivability. Even if he died, the knowledge of Syndrome’s operations had to persist in a way that another super could find and understand. This led him to consider multiple encoding methods, weighing which would survive environmental degradation the longest.

[Metacognition, Planning, Observation]

The turning point came when he overheard two technicians discussing “Project Kronos” during a scheduled Omnidroid maintenance cycle. He concluded that Kronos was the codename for this extermination project.

Choosing the word Kronos as the central clue was deliberate. In Greek mythology, Kronos devours his own children to prevent being overthrown, a metaphor perfectly matching Syndrome’s intent to destroy all Supers before they could rival him. This not only made the clue memorable, but also symbolically relevant to anyone familiar with mythological references.

[Associative Thinking, Inductive Reasoning]

Gazerbeam had been tracking geothermal activity readings during his forced marches. One vent had partially collapsed into a cavern shielded from aerial drones by overhanging basalt formations. It was here that he decided to make his final stand.

Carving “KRONOS” into the wall was not a casual act. He calculated the angle and depth of each stroke, using his eye lasers at reduced output to avoid over-penetrating the volcanic rock. This ensured the inscription would remain legible for decades, even under extreme humidity or thermal shifts. The location itself was chosen so that any desperate super seeking shelter might naturally stumble across it.

[PSI, FRI, Divergent Thinking, Planning]

At this stage, Gazerbeam was physically deteriorating. Multiple encounters with the Omnidroid had left him with deep lacerations and internal injuries. He used his final hours to arrange his body near the inscription, a grim but intentional marker to lend weight to the message. A corpse would make the warning far more likely to be taken seriously.

[Metacognition, Counteraction]

Gazerbeam’s actions created a chain of causality that ultimately led to Syndrome’s defeat. When Mr. Incredible later arrived on the island, he encountered the carved word exactly as intended. Without the clue, accessing Syndrome’s computer systems and exposing the Omnidroid’s programming cycle would have been exponentially harder.

In effect, Gazerbeam had planted a dead man’s switch in the narrative. The Omnidroid might adapt to physical attacks, but it could not adapt to the infiltration of knowledge. Even in death, he had found the one attack vector the machine could never counter which was human ingenuity.

[Abductive Reasoning, Analysis, Observation]

He maximized every resource: environmental cover, symbolic encoding, controlled visibility to enemies, and predictive planning for unknown allies. He deliberately varied his attack methods to prevent the AI from reaching a predictive threshold, essentially slowing its learning curve. This gave him the time necessary to find the cave and encode the warning.

[Counteraction, Perception, Planning]

He alone bridged the gap between knowing the threat existed and enabling another hero to dismantle it. combining separate strands of information into a decisive, actionable point.
In the years following Syndrome’s defeat, declassified relocation files revealed just how close Operation Kronos came to wiping out the remaining Supers. Without Gazerbeam’s carved word in the cave, history could have ended differently.
