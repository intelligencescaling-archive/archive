BRIAN MOSER
THE ICE TRUCK KILLER
(WIP) Feats Document (WIP)
This document aims to explain the trajectory of Brian Moser, an antagonist from Dexter, as well as his actions throughout the series. It will cover his appearances in Dexter: Original Sin, Dexter, and Dexter: Resurrection, analyzing them episode by episode. Although Dexter: Resurrection and several episodes of Dexter throughout the seasons do not feature any direct actions by Brian Moser, it is possible to make inferences based on references and mentions about the character. Regarding the Dexter Novels, it is not guaranteed, but it is possible that a proper explanation of his actions will be provided in the future. For now, this document will focus exclusively on the TV series.
Brian Moser is the main antagonist of the first season of Dexter and also the secondary antagonist, after Aaron Spencer, in the first season of Dexter: Original Sin. He also appears in the second and sixth seasons of Dexter, as well as from the first season onward in Dexter: Resurrection. Although he appears as a ghost in those situations, he has quotes that can serve his narrative, stemming from his constant influence on Dexter’s mind even after death, having essentially left a mark on him years after his own passing and, of course, other characters' statements about him. He does not appear in Dexter: New Blood and Dexter: Early Cuts.
He is the eldest son of Joe Driscoll and Laura Moser, being the biological older brother of Dexter Morgan. During the Estrada Case he is seen around Laura Moser’s House and witnesses the developing relationship between his mother and Harry Morgan.
As a result of a terrible tragedy that would be known as the Shipping Yard Massacre, Brian would eventually grow up to become the N.H.I. Serial Killer and the Secondary Antagonist of Dexter: Original Sin. Later in his life he would also go on to become the Ice Truck Killer and the Main Antagonist of Season One in the original series.

Dexter: Original Sin

Dexter: Original Sin is a spin-off of Dexter that takes place before the original series, making it the first story chronologically in the entire franchise. It follows a young Dexter Morgan who has just graduated from high school and has to deal with family situations involving his sister Debra Morgan, his father Harry Morgan, and his growing urge to kill. At the same time, he starts working as a blood-spatter analyst at Miami Metro Police Department in Miami (duh), joining the Homicide Division. The series also explores Harry Morgan’s past and his involvement with Laura Moser, the biological mother of Dexter and Brian Moser, as he tries to bring down the drug cartel led by Hector Estrada. This gives more background to the events that shaped both Dexter’s and Brian’s lives. In this part of the document involving Dexter: Original Sin, Brian Moser’s past will be briefly explained in chronological order episode by episode, so it will be easier to understand the context of the entire situation and how it connects to the present version of Brian. In the second part, all of Brian Moser’s actions in the present timeline of Dexter: Original Sin will also be presented chronologically.

Part 1 - Dexter: Original Sin
“The most valuable information is the one you’ve lived”

In Episode 1 – And in the Beginning, Harry is portrayed as a sergeant for Miami Metro Police Department who is successful in his investigations. This episode shows the first scene involving Harry’s past. We learn about the existence of Harrison “Harry” Morgan Jr, the first son of the Morgan family, whom Harry had with his wife, Doris Morgan. In the scene, Harry is watching a football game with his son. After a while, his son wanders off to the pool area without Harry noticing. Harry continues watching the game for some time before realizing his son is missing. When he figures out that the boy might have gone to the pool, he rushes outside, only to find his son drowned. He jumps into the pool and pulls him out, but despite his efforts, he is unable to save him, and the boy dies.

In Episode 2 - Kid in a Candy Store, set in January of 1973, we are introduced to the Estrada Case, in which Miami Metro recognizes that Miami is being overrun by cocaine trafficking. Their mission is to take down the cartel by capturing or eliminating Hector Estrada, the suspected leader. The department concludes that they need an informant capable of infiltrating Estrada’s organization to gather inside information and eventually help carry out a coordinated strike. Harry is assigned by Aaron Spencer to find a reliable informant who can make direct contact with Estrada. Alongside his partner, Bobby Watt, Harry becomes one of the key figures in the Estrada Case, with both men appointed as the lead. With that, through a strategy devised by Harry and Bobby, the two manage to capture Laura Moser and Joseph “Joe” Driscoll, a couple involved with drugs who might have some kind of connection to Estrada’s organization, or who could potentially assist in the infiltration, even if unknowingly. Bobby tries to talk to Joe and use him to reach Estrada, but Joe firmly refuses. As a result, Harry explores a new possibility with Laura, aiming to use her as an informant to bring down Estrada. After some convincing, he manages to get Spencer’s approval to proceed with this plan. Meanwhile, it is shown that Harry and Doris have been trying to have another child for two years, which implies that their first son’s death occurred in 1971. The episode also reveals that Laura had two children with Joe: Brian Moser (7 Years Old) and Dexter Moser (3 Years Old).

In Episode 3 - Miami Vice, Laura begins her infiltration work, gradually climbing the ranks within the organization in an effort to get closer to Hector Estrada. This draws the attention of Alfredo who, as Harry had anticipated, becomes curious and starts snooping around Laura’s success, something she can use to get even closer to Estrada. With her growing influence, Laura manages to arrange a meeting with higher-ranking members of the organization. Taking advantage of this opportunity, Harry equips her with a wiretap to gather more evidence against Estrada’s cartel for court proceedings. Due to her remarkable success, Harry grows increasingly close to Laura, and their relationship becomes more personal and intimate. He even begins to genuinely worry about her safety, despite being a married man to Doris.

In Episode 4 - Fender Bender, Harry, who is usually confident and composed, begins to feel uncertain about the operation due to his growing concern for Laura’s safety. Because Laura’s babysitter is unavailable, Harry offers to take care of her sons while she continues the infiltration mission with Bobby to get closer to Estrada’s organization. While looking after Dexter and Brian, Harry notices Brian cutting off the tails of several small lizards without showing any emotion, remaining calm throughout, something that surprised Harry. Shortly after, Harry realizes Dexter has disappeared, and when he finds him, Dexter is sitting sadly, holding a dead lizard. Dexter explains that he tried to save it. After burying the lizard and the severed tails, Brian, who stays completely calm during the situation, laughs at it, amused even by the sight of the dead creatures. Harry, unsettled by this behavior, gives Brian a small scolding. After returning home from the investigation with Bobby, Laura finds Harry asleep with Dexter. After a brief conversation between Laura and Harry, they have sex.

Despite being dehydrated, starving, traumatised, and sitting in near-darkness, Brian was still able to perceive, in the seconds that Harry first arrived and looked at them, that Harry did not see him as a normal boy. As the years went on, he held onto those memories of his family, his brother and his mother, guarding them perfectly without forgetting a single detail until the age of twenty-five (and clearly even after that, due to his later plans that even involved his biological father). On one occasion, the medication he required was being pawned off in a drug deal by Raul Martinez, his only attachment in the facility, because Brian believed Raul genuinely cared for him. When Brian discovered this, he confronted Raul in the day room; Raul denied any involvement and told Brian he was acting “crazy.” This sent Brian into a rage, and he attacked Raul until other orderlies eventually restrained him and escorted him back to his room.
Brian, with his 25 years, was able to recount the story of what happened when he was seven years old and his conflict with Raul Martinez perfectly, describing his traumatic experiences to his therapist, Dr Petri, without losing his composure at any moment for the first time, as noticed by the therapist himself. Brian spent almost eighteen years in the institution, driven by the motivation that if he got better, he would be able to see Dexter again. He nurtured that motivation and persistence, using them to grow and try to improve, something we would later see reflected in his actions both in Dexter: Original Sin and in Dexter itself. After his therapist told him that he would not be allowed to see Dexter and intended to send him back to the institution, Brian judged that he had no use for him because he was not going to help him see Dexter again. Then, he redirected all the motivation he had toward seeing Dexter into attacking him, catching him off guard. Fueled by that mix of motivation and rage, Brian killed his therapist, then calmly walked out into public, showing no reaction whatsoever. He takes Petrie’s car keys and drives off to Miami. By killing Dr. Petri, Brian was able to avoid being sent back to the Harbor Light Mental Institute, finally being free to put into action what he had wanted since he was seven years old: Reunite with his Little Brother.
From that point on, Brian Moser began to analyze his own life with surgical precision, going far beyond mere reflection. He examined everything he could recall, both within himself and within the institution that had shaped him. He built detailed psychological profiles of the people around him and developed meticulous plans and strategies to kill them, not out of impulse, but as acts of deliberate revenge, punishing those who had failed to help him reunite with Dexter. At the same time, he shifted his focus toward his brother, already analysing from far away the kind of protection Harry Morgan would have imposed on Dexter. Brian constructed mental scenarios and contingencies, rehearsing approaches and subtle tests that would allow him to observe Dexter from a distance while keeping himself beyond Harry’s reach. He understood that to reach Dexter, he would first need to comprehend every layer of his brother’s life: his habits, relationships, values, and vulnerabilities, and that Harry would eventually have to be removed from the equation. However, as long as Harry was still alive, Brian knew he had to remain absolutely meticulous. Thus began Brian’s silent preparation: a gradual, inevitable advance driven by a rational conviction that his reunion with Dexter had to happen, and that the Morgan family itself, Harry and Debra, would have to be dismantled for it to occur. Soon after, he discovered that Dexter joined Miami Metro Homicide, using that knowledge to expand and refine his schemes. He would adapt perfectly to life in society after having adapted to the institute, and in the future, his adaptive capacity would prove useful in getting closer and closer to Dexter (more about that in Part 2).

Part 2 - Dexter: Original Sin
“The best tests don’t need questions, only reactions”

Before beginning the analysis of Young Brian Moser’s actions episode by episode, it is necessary to break down his overall strategy and planning: what he set in motion right after killing Dr. Petrie and driving to Miami.
First, Brian needed to find Dexter and determine where he lived, what his social relationships looked like, and how Harry Morgan and Debra Morgan were doing, as well as discovering that Doris Morgan had passed away. Only after mapping Dexter’s environment could he start turning his long-term intentions into concrete actions.
His overarching strategy will be called the Reunion Through Retribution Strategy (RTR Strategy), essentially showing that Brian moves closer to Dexter through vengeance against those who wronged him, using each act of retribution both as punishment and as a calculated step toward their reunion.
The first thing Brian does when revisiting his past in his mind is to profile everyone he intends to kill once he arrives in Miami. The mental list Brian creates is as follows:
   * Raul Martinez: The hospital worker who broke his trust so deeply that Brian ended up attacking him.
   * Blake Kersten: The only son of the Kersten family, the first family Brian was sent to after being removed from the Morgans, who deliberately locked him inside a closet and left him trapped there.
   * Felix Woodard: The eldest son of the Woodard family, the second family Brian was sent to after being removed from the Kerstens, who mistreated him until Brian stabbed his hand, which led to Brian being removed from the Woodards as well.
   * Barbara Plimpton: The social worker who arranged Brian’s adoption by the Morgans, his removal from the Morgans, his placement with the Kerstens and subsequent removal, his placement with the Woodards and subsequent removal, and finally his placement in the Harbor Light Mental Institute.
When he arrives in Miami, before making any move, Brian first needs to assess one crucial thing: Dexter Morgan’s life.
By observing and stalking Dexter from a distance, it can be inferred that Brian reaches the following conclusions about what currently defines Dexter’s world:
   * Doris Morgan, Dexter’s adoptive mother and Debra’s biological mother, has passed away, meaning the Morgan family is now structurally weaker and emotionally more dependent on Harry.
   * He sees that Harry Morgan still works at Miami Metro and raises both Dexter and Debra alone, reinforcing Harry’s role as the primary protective barrier between Brian and Dexter.
   * Dexter and Debra are both in high school, and Dexter is close to graduating.
   * Dexter is deeply interested in science, especially human anatomy, blood, and the mechanics of the human body, essentially leaning toward biology and anything related to how the body works and breaks.
   * Bobby Watt, one of the detectives involved with Harry in the Estrada case, still works at Miami Metro.
   * Aaron Spencer, who was the main commander of the Estrada Case operation, still worked at Miami Metro.
In addition, Brian would need to dig deeper and understand that Harry Morgan, Bobby Watt and Aaron Spencer were now part of Miami Metro Homicide. Since the case involving Hector Estrada was more focused on drug trafficking, it is likely that they originally belonged to Miami Metro Vice and later transferred to Miami Metro Homicide.
With this assessed, Brian needed to confirm whether Dexter remembered his past or not, not just his biological mother, but Brian himself. He couldn’t do this directly because of Harry, so it is likely that some time passed after this initial observation phase. It can be inferred that, upon seeing Dexter join Miami Metro, Brian saw the perfect opportunity to merge his two core objectives into a single, unified path: analyze and reunite with Dexter while taking revenge on the people who had wronged him. This would become the core of his Reunion Through Retribution Strategy (RTR Strategy).
At the crime scenes, Brian would stage complex, technically demanding setups to test Dexter’s abilities. In some of them, he would deliberately embed subtle clues tied to their shared past. If Dexter still remembered, he would react in a way that Brian, watching and stalking him, could detect and interpret, using each reaction to refine both his understanding of Dexter and his next move. Additionally, Brian needed to remain completely hidden so that Bobby, Aaron, and especially Harry, would not identify and recognize him under any circumstances.
Before we finish outlining the core of his strategy, we need to define something simple. Brian’s initial intention is to test Dexter’s abilities, but he still needs to determine whether Dexter actually remembers him. If Dexter does not remember, then it becomes necessary to embed clues into the crime scenes. If he does remember, Brian would only need to evaluate his capabilities.
The scenario that plays out is that Brian realizes Dexter does not remember him after he gets the confirmation. As a result, he merges two ideas into one: testing Dexter’s abilities and trying to make him remember their past. However, in Dexter: Original Sin, the main focus is on his acts of revenge and on testing Dexter, even though we can still identify some of the clues Brian leaves behind for him. Brian needs to remain in the shadows because of Harry’s existence, something he has already identified as a direct barrier to his advances. The clue aspect is explored in much greater depth later in the main Dexter series itself, where the emotional and symbolic weight of those hints becomes more explicit.
With the core of Brian’s strategy now clearly established, we can finally move on to what he actually does in the episodes of Dexter: Original Sin. In other words, seeing all of this put into practice.

The first murder Brian would commit to draw Dexter out was that of Raul Martinez in Episode 2 - Kid in a Candy Store, the hospital worker who had broken his trust so deeply that Brian ended up attacking him. Brian had three objectives with this first killing:
   * Take revenge on Raul Martinez.
   * Blend into the crowd to see whether Dexter Morgan would be assigned to analyze the crime scene, acting like a normal civilian.
   * Follow Dexter Morgan after he finishes his analysis and confirm whether he remembered him or not.
To kill Raul Martinez, Brian tracked and followed him in the early hours of the dawn and shot him point-blank in the forehead with a small-caliber handgun after coming up behind him and catching him off guard. Raul Martinez was a drug user and had already been released from the Harbor Light Mental Institute (this detail about his drug use would be important for the nickname Brian would receive: the N.H.I. Serial Killer, which will be explained shortly).
While watching Dexter, Brian was able to see his abilities as a blood-spatter analyst and, coincidentally, witness the first crime scene Dexter would analyze. In other words, Brian orchestrated the first crime scene Dexter ever analyzed.
After Dexter finished his analysis, he went to a restaurant near the beach with Bobby Watt, Vince Masuka, and Angel Batista to have lunch before heading back to work.
At the restaurant, after Bobby, Masuka, and Batista went to order at the counter, Brian had the perfect chance to come closer and confirm whether Dexter remembered him or not, and that’s what he did.
Judging correctly and making a quick decision while Bobby, Masuka, and Batista were busy, Brian walked up and asked Dexter if one of the chairs was taken. Dexter replied that he was saving seats for his friends, pointed to Bobby, Batista, and Masuka, and apologized. Saying it was no problem, Brian walked away. Brian, who had wanted to see Dexter again directly since he was seven years old, was able to show absolutely no reaction that might raise suspicion, maintaining full emotional control, while pretending to be a normal person.
This might sound like a vague exchange (AND IT IS) but what Brian was able to conclude from it is insane.
Conventionally, a normal person who hasn’t seen someone for 17–18 years wouldn’t remember them, especially if they were 3 years old at the time and the other person was 7 (in this case, Brian was 7 then and Dexter was 3). Enduring narrative memories (of specific events) begin to develop and become easier to store from around age 3; even so, there’s no guarantee every human will remember that period much less 17–18 years later.
Brian, however, broke with convention. From that extremely generic, vague conversation alone, he was able to infer that Dexter didn’t remember him. He went further and concluded that Harry was responsible by keeping those memories hidden.
In other words, Brian went beyond the conventional, concluding from an absolutely vague and generic interaction that Dexter didn’t remember him, and that Harry was one of the people responsible for keeping the memories of what had happened in his early childhood “erased” in his mind. After 8 seconds of conversation, he reached AT LEAST the conclusion that Dexter DID NOT remember him. From Brian’s perspective in episode 10, we can see the look on his face that signals he’d arrived at that conclusion.
It’s possible to infer that Brian concluded that Harry had kept Dexter’s memories of his past from developing, likely based on the following line of reasoning:
   * In general, it’s common that from around age 3 we start remembering things.
   * The more emotions and feelings involved, the easier it becomes to remember those things.
   * Dexter Morgan was 3 years old at the time when he last saw Brian.
   * Dexter Morgan went through a traumatic experience with Brian known as the Shipping Yard Massacre, where his mother, Laura Moser, was brutally murdered by the cartel.
   * It’s normal to think that Dexter would have lost his memories precisely because of the trauma, and that his brain made him occasionally forget it.
   * However, Brian was able to go further and understand that Harry was largely to blame for keeping Dexter’s memories suppressed (you can arrive at this conclusion from what Brian says in episode 12 of Dexter about having understood since age 7 that Harry didn’t see him as a normal boy. Harry would probably make Dexter forget him) via him knowing that Harry and Laura were having an affair, which makes it plausible to think that Harry would not like to talk about the past because Laura died because of his mistakes. Of course, a father would probably not tell his own son about something like that as well.

It’s certainly possible to confirm with 100% certainty that he at least understood that Dexter didn’t remember him based on that vague, generic exchange, and after that conversation he realized that Harry was the one behind it all. The conversation scene between Brian and Dexter lasts about 8 seconds, starting from Brian’s first line asking if anyone was sitting there.
Before we can understand why Brian wanted to do what he did, we need to break down how he reached the conclusion that Dexter no longer remembered him. Not the reasoning itself, where he eventually deduced that Harry was behind it, but the method. In Dexter’s Season 1, Brian tells Dexter that he could see in Harry’s eyes that Harry never viewed him as a normal child, but rather as a “fucked up kid.” The first and last time Brian ever saw Harry as a child was when he was seven years old, right before leaving the Morgan household. Given this, it is entirely possible that Brian used a form of emotional perception on Dexter, perhaps analyzing his eyes (the same way he did with Harry), tone of voice, or behavioral microtells. Regardless of the specific mechanism, some level of psychological reading occurred. The fact that Brian could determine Dexter’s mental state based solely on an 8-second, generic, vague interaction is nothing short of extraordinary. What we are trying to say is that Dexter was speaking normally about something vague, and yet Brian was still able to reach a conclusion. We considered the possibility that this scene might start with pure emotional perception on Brian’s part, and then he would reason through everything. In addition, Brian correctly deduces Harry’s shame regarding Dexter’s past. With a massive number of patients under Dr. Petrie’s care, and knowing Petrie was a therapist whose clientele would naturally be documented, Brian made sure to sell the stolen car without ever giving his name, ensuring there was no transactional record that could identify him. In other words, Harry was a statistical anomaly, not a failure of foresight.

She had no access to Brian’s sealed juvenile records, nor to any of the information at Miami Metro that Harry, with Camilla Figg’s help, had meticulously buried. To LaGuerta, the name would mean absolutely nothing, just another former patient among many.

He pre-emptively eliminated the single most obvious forensic link to himself and correctly predicted law enforcement’s investigative trajectory. He engineered the situation so that only the one man in Miami with the power to expose him, Harry Morgan (probably Bobby and Spencer too), could ever pose a threat. And even that scenario required an extraordinary coincidence.
Furthermore, if Brian had kept the car and the investigation had led detectives to cross-reference Petrie’s patient list, discovering that a former patient of the murdered therapist was in possession of his vehicle would have immediately escalated suspicion and severely compromised Brian’s position. Selling the car anonymously prevented precisely that chain of events from ever occurring.
Since the car belonged to Dr. Petrie, the police would either suspect Brian of being the killer or, at the very least, treat him as a major person of interest the moment they discovered he was one of Petrie’s former patients. Keeping the vehicle would have directly funneled the investigation toward him.

Investigating the car would lead them to Tampa -> Investigating Tampa would lead them back to Petrie’s files -> Investigating Petrie’s files would lead to a list of nearly 300 patients.

The Silent Years
“The most dangerous force is the one shaped with patience”

In this section of the document, we will examine what Brian Moser likely did during the interval between Season 1 of Dexter: Original Sin and Season 1 of Dexter.
Since Dexter: Original Sin was canceled, we do not have exact confirmation of everything Brian did during this period. However, we can infer several things based on information from both Dexter and Dexter: Original Sin.
In this part of the document, in addition to discussing Brian’s actions during this gap, we will also explore a theory we will call Silent Removal Theory (SRT), which proposes that Brian Moser was the one who killed Harry Morgan and Juan Pablo Aliso, perfectly concealing this from Dexter Morgan, Debra Morgan, and, of course, everyone else. The year is 1991. Brian Moser steps back, allowing Harry Morgan to “win” in Dexter: Original Sin, agreeing to let him keep Dexter Morgan. There is a fifteen–year gap between 1991 and 2006, which allows us to infer many things Brian may have done after the end of Dexter: Original Sin.
First, Brian almost certainly killed more people during this period. It is highly likely that he targeted additional individuals connected to the Harbour Light Mental Institute (it is very improbable that Raul Martinez was the only person he despised there), as well as random victims, both to practise and, given how much he enjoyed killing, simply to satisfy his own compulsion. Considering how much pleasure he derived from murder, it is reasonable to assume Brian maintained complete composure while killing these people. In Dexter: Original Sin, his murders were driven by revenge and intense emotional motivation. This naturally leads to the question of Rudy Cooper, the identity Brian eventually assumed in 1998.
There are several plausible explanations for how Brian selected and replaced Rudy Cooper. Despite being known as a plumber, nothing prevents Rudy from having a medical past: pre-med coursework, incomplete medical training, certification related to prosthetics, or prior enrollment in a health program. The Sorbonne detail is striking, but entirely plausible. Large universities often have alumni who later abandon their field. It is theoretically possible that Brian invented the Sorbonne detail purely to impress Debra Morgan. But this would be an extremely risky lie: the moment Miami Metro looked into his academic history, any inconsistency would destroy his cover instantly. For this reason, the most probable scenario is that Brian intentionally hunted for someone who truly had a medical-related background and had indeed attended a university like the Sorbonne. He would then have killed Rudy Cooper, assumed his identity, and used it to enter the medical system legitimately.
Throughout these years, Brian also monitored Harry Morgan from afar. Eventually, he would learn of Harry’s death, some months after Dexter: Original Sin. This was a decisive moment for Brian. Harry had always been the final barrier between him and Dexter. With Harry gone, Brian no longer had a guardian watching over Dexter or obstructing his approach. Her position in Miami Metro Vice offered a convenient but temporary avenue, useful, but not quite close enough to Dexter’s daily environment in Homicide. Brian understood this immediately. It is likely that, even during this whole time, he was already imagining how he could eventually maneuver Debra into Homicide, placing her directly beside Dexter and creating the perfect bridge between them (this will be explored in the Season 1 section).
Brian kept track of Dexter himself. At some point during these fifteen years, he correctly deduced that Dexter was a serial killer, and that he followed a moral code restricting him to killers, understanding that Dexter liked to kill. Brian would have recognized the influence of Harry Morgan immediately. He could not know whether Harry taught Dexter the physical act of killing.

Silent Removal Theory (SRT)
“The deadliest truths are the ones left unwritten”

The Silent Removal Theory (SRT) proposes that Brian Moser was the one who killed Harry Morgan approximately one year after the events of Dexter: Original Sin, rather than Harry dying from an intentional overdose on his heart medication caused by stress, effectively taking his own life as the official narrative suggests.
The theory also asserts that Brian concealed this perfectly from Dexter Morgan, Debra Morgan, and everyone else, turning Harry’s death into the ideal invisible move. Since no one suspected anything, this hidden act would later serve as a significant advantage, allowing Brian to eventually position himself closer to Dexter when the time came.
The SRT also plans to address another claim: that Brian Moser was responsible for the death of Juan Pablo Aliso, one of the men involved in the murder of Laura Moser. Just as Dexter personally dealt with Santos Jimenez in Season 2 and Hector Estrada in Season 7, Brian would have eliminated Aliso even before, completing his own share of vengeance while ensuring that all loose ends of the past were closed. Furthermore, the theory fills the apparent “mistakes” or inconsistencies in Brian’s behavior during Dexter: Original Sin. It reframes events that initially seem like oversights, such as Brian not killing Harry when he had the chance, or failing to anticipate that Harry would investigate the Paul Petrie case, as deliberate choices rather than flaws. Under the SRT, Brian intentionally held back from killing Harry because he needed more time to perfect his method, and prepare to remove someone as high-profile and respected as the most famous man in Miami Metro. Likewise, his seeming inability to predict Harry’s involvement in the Petrie investigation becomes reinterpreted as part of a broader manipulation strategy, one in which Brian wanted Harry to follow the trail exactly as he did.
In addition, by the end of Dexter: Original Sin, Harry had actively prevented Brian from getting close to Dexter, and combined with Brian’s longstanding hatred toward Harry, this would have given Brian more than enough motivation to eliminate him. Removing Harry becomes both revenge and the strategic destruction of the only barrier preventing Brian from reaching Dexter.
This part of the document will explain this theory in detail, establishing a new designation for Brian, the SRT Brian Moser, similar to SET Takuya Yagami from Classroom of the Elite and MT Nate River (Near) from Death Note.
Due to the cancellation of Dexter: Original Sin, there’s a chance this theory could be confirmed in Dexter: Resurrection, but it’s really pretty unlikely to happen. If it does happen, you can be sure that this interpretation might completely change and move into the The Silent Years section of this doc (this SRT section would be deleted) since it would become canon rather than just a theory, and there’s even a chance it might end up being erased if a debunk happens in Dexter: Resurrection.

The theory we initially intended to create was based solely on the idea that Brian Moser had murdered Harry Morgan and concealed his death, as well as having killed Juan Pablo Aliso. However, we decided to go further and expand the theory by connecting it to the events of Dexter: Original Sin. Rather than simply assuming that Brian viewed Harry as a problem to be removed, we began to explore the possibility that everything was part of a much older and far more elaborate plan. This theory, known to you as the Silent Removal Theory (SRT), attempts to resolve the inconsistencies in Brian’s behavior during Original Sin, proposing that he manipulated Harry from the very beginning, anticipating and influencing each of his decisions.
When Brian kills Dr. Petrie, he tears out the appointment pages to remove his name from the investigation. This forces the police to sift through nearly 300 patient files stored in four separate boxes, and even if they found Brian’s name, there would be nowhere near enough evidence to accuse him. The problem is that Harry, contrary to Brian’s expectations, personally investigates the case. As a result, the one person capable of suspecting Brian goes there, sees the files, and connects Brian to Raul Martinez. Although many patients could have had some kind of link to Raul, Harry fixates on Brian, more due to unfortunate coincidence than any flaw in Brian’s actions.
The Silent Removal Theory, however, proposes that Brian actually wanted Harry to investigate Petrie. He sold Petrie’s stolen car in Miami, the city where Harry worked, fully aware of the implications, predicting that Harry would investigate the case. By doing so, Harry would inevitably be drawn into the Petrie case and follow the trail to Tampa. Brian tore out the appointment sheets not to hide, but to appear as though he were hiding, effectively pushing Harry directly toward him.
Brian’s short-term reasoning in this scenario likely begins with information he picked up during his time at the Harbour Light Mental Institute or the 4 years being Petrie’s patient. Through Dr. Petrie or other staff members from the institute, he would have learned that Harry Morgan had been transferred from Miami Metro Vice to Miami Metro Homicide. That single detail is enough to give Brian a crucial insight: Harry is now in a position where a murder like Petrie’s could realistically land on his desk.
Starting from that premise, Brian’s logic branches out. If Dr. Petrie disappears or turns up dead, the case will not stay confined to Tampa forever. Petrie’s car showing up in Miami links both cities. Brian uses this to his advantage by deliberately selling Petrie’s stolen car in Miami, fully aware that once the vehicle is identified, the investigation will inevitably cross over into Harry’s jurisdiction. The presence of the car in Miami creates a natural investigative bridge between Tampa’s Police Department and Miami Metro Homicide.
From Brian’s perspective, this accomplishes several things at once:
      1. It subtly guides Harry toward the case
If Tampa identifies the car and traces it back to Petrie, they will treat it as a homicide or at least a suspicious disappearance. Given that the car surfaced in Miami, and that someone anonymously sold it, coordination with Miami Metro becomes almost obligatory. As the homicide detective in that department, Harry is perfectly positioned to get involved.

      2. It uses institutional procedure against Harry
Brian doesn’t need to “force” Harry to investigate anything. He lets standard police protocol do the work. The car is found in Miami -> Tampa discovers Petrie is dead weeks before -> interdepartmental cooperation is triggered -> Harry has every professional reason to look into Petrie and his connections.

      3. It turns Harry’s new position into a predictable vector
Knowing Harry is now in Homicide, Brian can reasonably predict that any cross-jurisdictional homicide case with ties to Miami might pass through him. Selling the car in Miami was smart.
In this framing, Brian’s reasoning is about directing where the suspicion will flow and who will be pulled into Petrie’s case. That would design Harry Morgan to discover his existence.
Now, with Harry Morgan (and of course LaGuerta) working together with the Tampa Police Department, the investigation would begin to unfold. Before reviewing the patient files, both Harry and the Tampa detectives would discover that Petrie’s killer had removed the appointment list from his office, something both departments would interpret as an attempt to hide the perpetrator’s identity. This forced the police to dig through nearly 300 patient files, and Harry began analyzing them one by one.
Brian, however, had orchestrated the situation on a massive scale, creating that investigative environment and predicting that Harry, once faced with those files, would eventually spot a folder with the name Brian Moser and take it for himself. But how would Brian know that Harry would immediately focus on his file and treat it as a personal matter? In the final episode of Dexter: Original Sin, Brian essentially reveals to Harry that he understood Harry’s shame regarding the past, specifically that this shame was one of the reasons Harry tried to prevent Dexter from remembering him. Brian had already deduced that Harry buried his existence deep within the system. This means that, in Tampa, a city where Harry had no influence over record manipulation, Brian’s file would remain untouched. So the moment Harry saw the name “Brian Moser,” he would almost certainly isolate it and take it personally.
Upon seeing the file, Harry would connect Brian to Raul Martinez, the orderly from the institution where Brian spent fourteen years, and where he had a documented history of violent incidents. Although several of Petrie’s patients could theoretically have had some connection to Raul simply by being former residents of the same institution, Brian understood that Harry would fixate specifically on him, and only him, and would pursue the investigation accordingly.
With this in mind, Brian stages the murder of Barbara Plimpton, the social worker who removed him from the Morgan family, as a calculated operation with three objectives:
         1. Avenge himself on Barbara.
         2. Use a chainsaw as the murder weapon, the same type used in Laura Moser’s death, hoping it would trigger memories in Dexter (even though Dexter never arrives at the scene, as he is busy pursuing Aaron Spencer, and Brian still does not know that Dexter is a serial killer).
         3. Draw Harry into a carefully orchestrated confrontation, giving Brian the opportunity to meet him face-to-face and manipulate him.
The only unforeseen variable was Dexter’s absence and Harry’s confronting him earlier than Brian had wanted.
Even so, the confrontation Brian wanted still occurs, and Brian needs to adapt. He adapts to Harry’s sudden advances, defeats him, and then disappears, pretending he was leaving for good. This ties directly into his question to Harry: “Did the chainsaw give me away?” He was merely pretending to be surprised, reinforcing the illusion that he had never intended for Harry to find him.
The fact that Brian is able to do this, to spare Harry for the sake of his long-term objective, despite hating him since he was seven years old and wanting to kill him for almost his entire life, is extraordinary.
Some time later, around September 8th, 1991 (the reasoning behind this specific date will be explained later), now far more experienced and professional, Brian would kill Harry Morgan, catching him completely off guard and covering up the murder so flawlessly that neither Dexter nor the rest of the Morgan family would ever suspect a thing. This theory is further reinforced by the fact that Harry was the only true obstacle standing between Brian and Dexter.

This was not hesitation, nor a mistake, it aligns perfectly with the idea that Brian wanted to eliminate Harry in a clean, invisible, and irreversible way. Harry Morgan was not an ordinary target. During this time, of course, Brian continued studying the Morgan family as a whole, with particular focus on Dexter Morgan.

Instead of Brian simply trying to avoid being discovered by Harry, the Silent Removal Theory reframes his actions as part of a broader and more elegant long-term structure, one in which Brian strategically uses Harry rather than avoiding him. It basically buffs him in some parts, and nerfs him in others when it comes to strategy.
The second thing you need to understand is how the date of Harry Morgan’s death was determined. Here is a brief summary, based on canonical evidence:
When Debra is investigating Evelyn Vogel’s files, she finds Harry’s last recorded session dated September 5th 1991, detailing the events surrounding Harry discovering the dismembered body of Juan Ryness, a scene first shown in Season Two. Ryness was the man who had murdered Harry’s former partner, Davey Sanchez. While Davey never appears in Dexter: Original Sin (Harry’s partner there is Bobby Watt), it is possible that Davey was Harry’s partner during his earlier years in Homicide in the 1980s. He then told Vogel he could not live with what he had seen, ending the session.
Harry took his own life three days after that session.
Therefore, if the last appointment was on September 5th, 1991, and Harry killed himself three days later, the canonical date of Harry Morgan’s death is September 8th, 1991.
Under the Silent Removal Theory, this date marks not Harry’s suicide, but the day Brian killed him, having waited for the perfect moment to strike. Given the uncertain internal chronology of Dexter: Original Sin (we know the story takes place in 1991 but not the exact month), Brian certainly waited some months after the events of Original Sin for the surrounding chaos to settle. He would have waited for:
         1. The media frenzy surrounding Aaron Spencer to die down (since the public believed he fled after being accused of murder, when in truth Dexter killed him),
         2. Bobby Watt’s retirement, which was likely triggered or accelerated by the gunshot wound he sustained and over twenty years of service.
Only after these two major variables stabilized would Brian have moved in to eliminate Harry with precision.
Finally, the theory does not undermine what was established between Harry and Vogel in Season 8. Harry saying he couldn’t live with himself after seeing that does not necessarily mean he would have taken his own life. Words spoken in emotional distress do not equal a confirmed suicide. Under the Silent Removal Theory, Brian Moser likely entered Harry’s home at a moment when no one would notice and killed him, perfectly staging the scene as a suicide. He concealed the truth not only from Dexter Morgan, Debra Morgan, and Miami Metro, but even from Evelyn Vogel herself. In this interpretation, Harry’s death remains consistent with the emotional turmoil described in Season 8, while re-contextualizing it as the culmination of Brian’s objective rather than Harry’s own final act.
To conclude the Silent Removal Theory, we turn to the idea that Brian Moser may have been responsible for the death of Juan Pablo Aliso. By killing Juan himself, Brian could later use this as leverage to psychologically influence Dexter. If Dexter ever discovered the truth, Brian could present it as proof that he was taking care of the men who destroyed their family, subtly implying that Dexter should finish what remained unfinished. In other words, Brian may have envisioned persuading Dexter to kill Santos Jimenez and Hector Estrada, using both emotional appeal (the assassination of their mother) and practical reasoning (basically saying that he already took one).

In this section of the document, we will explain Brian Moser’s actions throughout Season 1 of Dexter, expanding upon all 12 episodes. From this point on, Brian Moser, who had already been living under the identity of Rudy Cooper for eight years, works at a hospital specialising in human prosthetics, and the murders he commits during the season lead the media to profile him as the Ice Truck Killer. His primary objective is to bring Dexter Morgan over to his side, and the actions he takes toward that goal will be explained.

Before we begin analyzing Brian Moser’s actions episode by episode, it is necessary to first outline his overarching plan for the season, clarify his primary objectives, and revisit key elements previously discussed in the The Silent Years section.
During this 15-year gap, Brian would have closely observed Dexter and his surroundings, like certainly Debra, and possibly even the Miami Metro Homicide Department itself, while simultaneously refining and practicing his own killings. It is highly likely that Brian continued killing throughout these years in a way that never drew attention to himself or placed him in situations that could expose him.
At some point, Brian discovered that Dexter was a serial killer, someone who shared the same intrinsic urge to kill. The crucial distinction, however, was that Dexter exclusively targeted other murderers, particularly those who had evaded justice due to systemic failures in the legal system. From this realization, Brian began to deduce the existence of a structured moral framework behind Dexter’s actions, eventually forming multiple inferences about what would later be understood as the Code of Harry.
The reasoning process Brian could logically construct is as follows:
         * Dexter Morgan kills repeatedly, to the extent that he qualifies as a serial killer.
         * Dexter Morgan derives satisfaction from killing.
         * However, Dexter only kills other murderers, specifically those who have escaped legal consequences due to failures of the justice system.
         * The fact that Dexter prioritizes killers who evade the law indicates deliberate research and selection, rather than coincidence or impulsivity.
         * This selective victimology strongly suggests an underlying pattern, resembling a codified rule set. Many serial killers operate under consistent patterns or modus operandi. For example, Brazil’s Maníaco do Parque, who repeatedly lured women into a park before assaulting and killing them.
         * Harry Morgan actively suppressed Dexter’s traumatic memories and concealed aspects of his past, implying a deep sense of guilt, fear, or shame, alongside an extreme desire to protect his son at any cost.
         * Brian himself developed homicidal urges as a direct result of trauma, making it reasonable for him to infer that Dexter experienced similar impulses. If Harry loved Dexter deeply, and even went as far as preventing Brian from approaching him in the past, then Harry could have eventually discovered Dexter’s violent urges.

From this, Brian could logically conclude that a code was created to regulate Dexter’s behavior, and that this code was either designed or heavily influenced by Harry Morgan.
The foundation for these deductions is reinforced in the final episode of Dexter, when Brian explicitly references the code while speaking to Dexter and directly asks whether Harry taught him how to kill. This moment confirms that Brian had already reasoned through the existence of such a system, the only uncertainty remaining was whether Harry explicitly trained Dexter how to kill or merely shaped the framework indirectly.
With these factors in mind, it is also possible to infer additional conclusions that Brian would have drawn regarding Debra Morgan, based on years of careful observation of her routine, behavior, and relationship with Dexter:
            * Debra Morgan works in the Miami Metro Vice Department.
            * Debra Morgan displays a clear and persistent desire to transfer to Miami Metro Homicide.
            * Debra Morgan has had multiple romantic relationships over time, suggesting emotional vulnerability and openness to interpersonal attachment.
            * Debra Morgan is extremely close to Dexter Morgan, maintaining a deep and consistent bond of trust.
            * Debra Morgan frequently communicates with Dexter, even during working hours, discussing both professional and personal matters, which further reinforces their level of intimacy.
These conclusions would be critical to Brian’s long-term planning. They would inform both his direct manipulation of Debra and his decision to use her as a strategic bridge to integrate himself into Dexter’s social circle. Indirectly, this also connects to his involvement with the Ice Truck Killer investigation in the future.
Additionally, there is a frequently overlooked aspect of Brian’s scheme: the possibility that he deliberately influenced Debra’s entry into the Homicide division as part of his broader plan. By positioning her within that specific department, Brian would not only bring her professionally closer to Dexter but also place her as an active participant within an investigation he himself would be controlling from behind the scenes, since Debra would not be able to know that much about the Ice Truck Killer while being in Miami Metro Vice. These observations and deductions would also lead Brian to define a clear objective within his plan: the elimination of Debra Morgan. She represented Dexter’s last significant emotional connection to the Morgan family, as both Doris Morgan and Harry Morgan were already deceased. Removing Debra would effectively isolate Dexter from any remaining familial bonds, thereby facilitating Brian’s emotional manipulation and his attempt to draw Dexter fully to his side.
Brian had also designed and constructed a refrigerated, temperature-controlled room within his own apartment, a space carefully prepared for the execution of his killings and for the deliberate staging of highly peculiar crime scenes. It allowed Brian to preserve bodies and reproduce a specific aesthetic pattern directly tied to his identity as a killer. At the same time, the creation of this room fulfilled a communicative function: to impress Dexter (something he predicted it would happen) through a highly personalized and intellectually provocative style of murder, while simultaneously prompting Miami Metro Homicide to recognize a consistent pattern and classify the crimes as the work of a serial killer.
With all these elements established, it becomes possible to clearly define Brian Moser’s overarching plan in Season 1 of Dexter. This plan can be referred to as the Family Reunion Planning (FMR Planning), as its central objective is to reunite the Moser family, specifically, to reconnect with Dexter, while simultaneously removing the influence of the Morgan family from his life, particularly Debra Morgan, and dismantling the idealized image Dexter holds of Harry Morgan. To Brian, Dexter is not truly a “Morgan”, but a Moser who was artificially shaped by Harry. As such, a crucial component of his plan is to free Dexter from this imposed identity, both emotionally and ideologically.
The primary goals of this plan are as follows:
            * To impress Dexter Morgan through a highly specific and symbolic style of murder, designed to function as a direct message to him, while simultaneously leading Miami Metro Homicide to identify a pattern and classify the crimes as the work of a serial killer.
            * To make Dexter Morgan recognize the existence of an intellectual and psychological “game”, thereby drawing him into active participation and engagement with Brian.
            * To systematically trigger the recovery of Dexter Morgan’s memories, memories erased by psychological trauma and reinforced by Harry’s intervention, so that Dexter would ultimately recall his shared past with Brian Moser (the Memory Recall Manipulation).
            * To remain consistently one step ahead of Miami Metro Homicide, avoiding capture by exploiting institutional flaws and anticipating law enforcement movements.
            * To orchestrate Debra Morgan’s transfer from the Vice Division to the Homicide Division of the Miami Metro Police Department, strategically placing her at the center of the unfolding events.
            * To approach Debra Morgan under the false identity of Rudy Cooper, presenting himself as a charismatic and trustworthy man, initiating a romantic relationship with her, and using that access to gain privileged insight into the Ice Truck Killer investigation and enter Dexter’s “inner circle”.
            * To gradually undermine, in Dexter Morgan’s eyes, the perception of Harry Morgan as a morally irreproachable figure, exposing his flaws and secrets.
            * To push Dexter Morgan into killing Debra Morgan as a symbolic act of definitive separation from the Morgan family, should Dexter refuse, Brian is prepared to kill her himself and then attempt to reclaim Dexter both emotionally and ideologically.
            * To lead Dexter Morgan to abandon the Code of Harry, freeing himself from its moral constraints, and ultimately join Brian as his partner, rebuilding their “family” on Brian’s terms.
With the core of Brian’s Family Reunion Planning (FMR Planning), fully established, we can now proceed to examine his actions on the series.

From this, it becomes possible to conclude that Dexter Morgan would take the case, allowing Brian to accurately predict this outcome. This, in turn, would cause Dexter to fall directly into Brian’s trap, which he would manage with other tactics to make Dexter join the psychological game with him.

Common Misconceptions

This section of the document will focus on addressing common misconceptions regarding Brian Moser and his actions, as well as preventing potential misunderstandings that may arise.

“Brian Moser does not appear at all the crime scenes in Dexter: Original Sin. Therefore, his alleged sensory feats and others are invalid.”
This is a question that has already been addressed earlier in the document, but it is worth reinforcing. Brian’s absence from the crime scenes at the beginning does not mean he was not there. His primary goal in Dexter: Original Sin is to observe Dexter’s actions and gradually lead him to recover repressed memories of his past, including memories of Brian himself, since Dexter does not remember him. The idea that Brian was not observing Dexter directly contradicts the show’s narrative, Brian’s other actions, and even statements made by Harry. Additionally, the series provides subtle hints of Brian’s presence early on, while deliberately avoiding overt reveals in order to preserve the mystery and the gradual narrative buildup. And, most importantly, Harry also investigates Brian and confirms that he is present at the crime scenes. This serves as the definitive piece of evidence, one that many people may overlook.

“In Dexter: Original Sin, Brian Moser successfully manipulated the staff of the Harbour Light Mental Institute by pretending to be cured and fit to live in society, despite his mental health issues and need to kill.”
This is a far more complex issue. The only thing Brian truly concealed was his need to kill, something he deliberately suppressed, but not necessarily evidence of a carefully orchestrated manipulation to leave the institute. We believe Brian was genuinely trying to improve, as shown in his conversation with Dr. Petrie, where it is revealed that he previously struggled to talk about his past but later managed to recount it clearly and coherently. His desire for recovery was directly tied to his wish to see Dexter. However, after being advised by Petrie not to pursue this and facing the real possibility of being sent back to the institute, Brian acted impulsively and murdered Petrie to prevent that outcome. It is important to note that Petrie was in contact with the institute and was aware of Brian’s psychological issues, except for his homicidal urges. As such, the institute knew a lot about Brian’s issues, except the ‘need to kill’ part, chose to release him, and kept him under therapeutic supervision. Therefore, claiming that Brian manipulated the institute is an oversimplification, even though there is some logic behind that interpretation.

“Considering the Silent Removal Theory (SRT) as canonical is the correct approach, given that it follows strong and coherent logic.”
It is true that the Silent Removal Theory (SRT) has strong logical foundations, although some aspects of it are more plausible than others. A clear example is the idea that Brian directly manipulated Harry on the balcony, which is far more believable than the notion of an overly elaborate scheme designed to lure and manipulate him. In short, as the name itself implies, it is a theory. Using it for scaling purposes is not incorrect, just as other widely used interpretations are accepted, such as Takuya Yagami’s Self-Expulsion Theory or Near’s Matsuda’s Theory. Despite their internal logic, these theories also lack direct confirmation within the narrative or from the author. In the case of Dexter, it is quite likely that some aspect of the Silent Removal Theory (SRT) would have been confirmed in a second season of Dexter: Original Sin, but unfortunately, the series was canceled. That said, it is not entirely fair to treat it as strictly canonical or to directly attribute it to the character as an established fact. Since it remains a theory, although a well-founded one, it should be used with caution and not imposed as an absolute truth within the narrative or as a definitive trait of the character.

“Brian Moser’s Family Reunion Planning (FMR Planning) in Season 1 is completely undone once Dexter Morgan refuses to kill Debra Morgan.”
No. From the very beginning, Brian intended for Debra to die, and his primary goal was to make Dexter the one who carried it out. However, he already had a contingency plan: to act on his own if anything went wrong, whether Dexter was involved or not. Ultimately, Brian would have killed Debra himself. This is shown when Dexter hesitates to kill her and Brian then grabs the knife and attempts to do it himself, only to be stopped by Dexter. Shortly afterward, upon emotionally realizing that Debra would want to stay at Dexter’s apartment, Brian goes there and tries to kill her again, but ends up falling into Dexter’s trap. While it can be argued that Brian acted impulsively upon realizing Dexter did not want to kill Debra, and that this proves he lacked a contingency, this argument does not dismantle the core idea. Brian’s overarching focus throughout the season was to make Dexter kill Debra as well, thereby severing his ties to the Morgan family completely. When Dexter refuses, Brian temporarily loses his composure, because it was his primary goal: Dexter needs to kill Debra, it makes sense that Brian would lose emotional control because of that. Even so, Brian would never give up on trying to convince Dexter. The idea that he would abandon this goal contradicts the character’s established narrative. Regardless of whether he killed Debra himself, either in the initial scene with Dexter or later at the apartment, Brian would have continued attempting to manipulate and persuade Dexter by whatever means necessary.

“Brian Moser is generally impulsive, meaning that when considering the character as a whole (Dexter: Original Sin + Dexter), this trait reduces his effectiveness in countering scenarios against other characters.”
Claiming that Brian is generally impulsive is a common mistake. Many people argue that Brian is overrated due to his impulsivity in Dexter: Original Sin, or that he completely lost control and destroyed his entire plan in Dexter when Dexter refuses to kill Debra, an interpretation that is incorrect, as already explained in the response to the previous misconception. In Dexter: Original Sin, it is true that Brian acts far more impulsively in his killings, particularly in the murder of Dr. Petrie and in his confrontation with Raul at the institute. However, aside from the situation involving Raul, the Petrie incident already presents a more mature Brian. Despite killing Petrie impulsively, Brian adapts remarkably well by successfully manipulating the crime scene. He is only caught due to sheer bad luck, being profiled by the only person in the world capable of doing so at that time: Harry. In Dexter, however, Brian is a completely different man. It is not reasonable to label him as impulsive based solely on Dexter: Original Sin, a series set approximately 15 years before the first season of Dexter. Over the course of his life, Brian clearly corrected and refined these traits. Even so, when Brian does act impulsively after realizing that Dexter refuses to kill Debra, he still demonstrates exceptional adaptability. He fights Dexter and defeats him, escapes the police flawlessly, and later understands Debra emotionally, enough to predict that she would want to go to Dexter’s apartment. From there, Brian enters the apartment undetected by the police with the intent to kill her, a tactic that only fails because Dexter anticipates Brian’s emotional reasoning and leaves Debra at the hospital against her will. More importantly, Brian is consistently portrayed as extremely methodical and meticulous in his planning, which is a direct indicator of strong impulse control. An impulsive individual acts primarily on emotion, without good foresight or preparation, while Brian operates through complex chains of reasoning, prolonged observation, emotional profiling and other things. If Brian were truly impulsive, he would not exhibit the level of patience and precision that define his behavior throughout the series. When comparing Brian’s few anti-feats in impulse control to the overwhelming number of feats that demonstrate restraint, patience, and strategic thinking, the disparity is enormous. Thus, two main conclusions can be drawn. First, Brian was indeed more impulsive in Original Sin, yet he still displayed strong adaptability and, by the end of the series, had become a more mature character, most notably by choosing not to kill the person he hated the most, Harry. Second, in Dexter, Brian is a distinctly different character, he is more controlled and disciplined. Even when acting impulsively after Dexter refuses to kill Debra, he adapts extremely well, and he demonstrates additional notable feats during Season 1, such as maintaining an intimate relationship with the woman he had wanted dead for years, among others.
