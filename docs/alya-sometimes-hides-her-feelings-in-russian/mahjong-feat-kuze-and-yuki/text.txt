To understand the details and importance of the feat you should understand how the game works. But worry not here's a small explanation made by me who learnt Mahjong just for this feat:

Mahjong basic understanding -
4 of every tile
3 suits all 1-9

Suit 1: Sticks
Suit 2: Dots
Suit 3: Chinese Numbers

Other tiles: no need to memories just see if identical

Played: Shuffled face down in 17 stacks

Every player starts with 13 aside from the first turn having 14

Pick first tile of the 17 stacks and after doing so you can discard any tile you have

2 identical tiles = Pair
2 types of sets: Triple (3 identical)
3 in a row (same suits, sequenced: X, X+1, X+2)

Set only valid with 3 tiles
Winning Hands: 1 of the pair, and 4 of sets (can be either a triple or 3 in a row or mixed)
You collect the tiles until you'll be keeping parts of the winning hand, having 13 tiles.
And then when you get the 14th you won't have to discard it, IF it'll complete the winning hand.
Additional: When it's not your turn -
You can take the discarded tiles in 2 ways (Pong + Chow)

You can only take a discarded tile after saying it outloud and before the next players turn for Pong.

And only for the player on your left (one turn before you as the game goes clockwise) you can take a discarded tile after saying Chow.
Say Pong when someone discarded a tile that would complete a triple for you. Say Chow when someone discarded a tile that would complete three of the same suit in numerical sequence. After doing so you turn the tiles face up for everyone to see. You'll also need to discard a tile after doing the meddling.
But if you complete a triple, three of the same suit in sequence or pair without meddling you don't have to expose it.
If you'll get a Quadruple through either Kong or Picking (and decide to use it after picking) then you'll need to win with 15 tiles but if you do it's higher reward.
You can also win with 7 doubles.
How Mahjong tiles look:

You pick tiles from the middle, and one by one, you can't choose any but only the one that's counted as the first and all the players do the same (Unless you use the dice, then it's chosen by the dice number. Which is what was used here)
And because of this you can't actually cheat unless you can control the dice AND also know your tiles, your opponents tiles and the tiles that are waiting to be drawn (Once you understand the basics of Mahjong it should be pretty obvious), as you don't just draw whichever tile you want and desire.

Explanations:

Red Dora refers to special dora tiles that are marked in red, providing extra points when they form part of a winning hand. These are - The 5 of Dots, The 5 of Sticks, The 5 of Chinese Numbers.
Kuitan refers to winning with an open hand (calling tiles) while completing the "Tanyao" yaku, which means the hand consists of only simple tiles (numbers 2 to 8) without honors (winds or dragons) or terminal (1 and 9) tiles.
Backdoor in Mahjong refers to a strategy where a player keeps options open for multiple possible winning tiles.
Agariyame allows the dealer to end the game immediately after winning a hand if they are ahead in points.
Double Ron occurs when two players simultaneously declare "ron" (a win by taking a discarded tile) on the same discard. In this situation, the discarding player is responsible for paying both winning players.
Triple Ron occurs when three players simultaneously declare "ron" on the same discarded tile. This situation is rare, and in many rulesets, it results in a "chombo" (a penalty or hand reset) since the discarder cannot pay all three winners.
Double Yakuman occurs when a player achieves two yakuman (limit hands) in a single winning hand. [Yakuman = Thirteen Orphans, Four Concealed Triplets, Big Three Dragons, Little Four Winds, Big Four Winds, All Honors, All Green, All Terminal, Four Quads, Nine Gates]
Triple Yakuman is even rarer than a Double Yakuman, involving three yakuman in one hand.
