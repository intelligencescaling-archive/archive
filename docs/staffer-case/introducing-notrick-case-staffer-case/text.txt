# Introduction & Background

https://i.redd.it/ojsm9y2z9a2i1.png

In 1960s London, there is an important but often overlooked building that houses the Pheno-Mana Bureau. The government agents inside this building are responsible for managing and investigating situations that relate to supernatural abilities, whether that be regarding Staff (inanimate object) or Staffers (a living person). In the event either was used to commit a crime, its up to the Mana-Affairs Division, a task force comprised exclusively of Staffers, to investigate. As crimes related to Pheno-Mana are extremely difficult to solve in general, being a Staffer is a requirement. To give some examples of people in the division, you have [Bryan](https://ibb.co/pBzBMGSc), a man with superhuman perception who can spot traces of human interaction (fingerprints, shoe markings) and clearly estimate exactly how long ago they where made from just a glance at their changing colour, or [Redfins](https://ibb.co/NgW2sQYy), a woman able to feel the memories of any object she touches, such as the prior fullness of a now empty glass of water.

The newest recruit to the Mana-Affairs Division is 21 year old Notrick Case, a young man from New Mexico who was scouted for the role straight from the top brass. Outside of being the assistant to a local hometown private detective agency, he has zero experience in proper law enforcement, has no apparent connections to the UK and, well, he knows basically nothing about Pheno-Mana, which isn't great when investigating it is your new job. To top it off, unlike everyone else in the division, he isn't a Staffer, he doesn't have any supernatural powers, he shouldn't even be allowed in this division.

So why is he here? Other than the fact he has a license to drive an excavator, there's not a whole lot to note about Notrick. Yet.

# Accomplishments, Feats and Strengths (Case 1)

https://i.redd.it/qee5xu9u992i1.png

After forgetting the concept of what room numbers are (no, [seriously](https://ibb.co/8DChCty7)), and after barely convincing everyone to not fire him on the spot, Notrick gets started with his first case, the murder of the leader of a circus troupe, killed via blunt force trauma to the head while he was asleep at his desk. Despite having not known anything about Staffers until today, he makes use of his team's abilities, relying on Bryan's powers to estimate all footprints in and out of the room, setting a timeline. He uses another member of the team not yet talked about, Thena, who can detect abnormal heart rates within range, to interrogate the suspects and catch any potential lies, leading the team to reveal the less than pleasant relationship between some suspects and the victim. He met the team an hour ago, didn't even know about the existence of Staffers, and he's already playing everyone to their strengths while being careful to reveal none of this to the suspects. He almost slips up at one point, but spins enough nonsense to hide Bryan's ability under more mundane explanations, so the suspects don't know their footprints have all been dated. Talk about adaptability, and a good display of field skills.

Regardless, the estimated time of death doesn't match up directly with any pair of footprints, not inside the room at least. Notrick spins a couple of theories of possible murder from outside the room, primarily from a woman who can [levitate objects](https://ibb.co/zVmgCWfh) and a woman who can [freeze water](https://ibb.co/mnKtKbp), but neither have the range or vision into the room to do it from outside. However, he realises that the victim has the ability to [propel objects incredibly hard](https://ibb.co/svQbHqpY), and owing to the lack of culprits entering the room, deduces the victim had killed himself by smashing his own head into the surroundings with his own powers. This, makes sense?

Except, no, something feels wrong, it makes sense, but Notrick's intuition is yelling at him. He throws his prior conclusions out, engages in some "Hexa-logic" (random bullshit go) and starts testing seemingly unrelated pieces of evidence against each other looking for a connection. After a few minutes of making everyone concerned about his mental state, he realises the trick behind one of the prior culprit's levitation powers thanks to a height consistency with affected items and prior circus stunts, and after further testing, discovers her real power, the ability to make transparent, heavy cubes. He deduces that she must have been standing on them and using them to walk through the air as part of her 'levitation' tricks and dropped one on the victim's head.

Most cases in Staffer Case, thanks to the supernatural aspect >!and a couple post-crime manipulations here and there from Redfins!<, cases in Staffer Case have multiple valid solutions. Canonically, Notrick arrives at the truth every time. I'd like to remind you that he had just discovered magic is real an hour ago, and he still managed to solve his first case by correctly deducing someone was lying about how their Staffer skill works works. He uh, certainly learns quick on the job.

# Mistletoe (Case 4)

https://i.redd.it/jhqjyfh0b92i1.png

Okay, the big one. Notrick's greatest accomplishments are in Case 4, which makes sense, its the most complicated case. I'm going to be skipping over a ton of detail because, like I said, its really complicated and I can only really cover the main brushstrokes. If you're interested in this game and don't want case 4 spoiled, this is your cue to stop reading.

Anyway, in society, Staffer skills and abilities are graded by their danger level. The highest known by the public is Estienne, or tank rating. The highest rating known used by the Bureau is Oppenheimer, or nuke rating. They're exactly what their name sake implies, an Oppenheimer has the potential to rival a nuclear bomb in the impact their powers could cause. These individuals are kept hidden underground under maximum security in a dedicated containment facility, with a lot of contingencies in place on what to do if reality starts breaking to contain the prisoners. In that facility, a prisoner has died, and Notrick has to find out exactly how it happened under 24/7 surveillance within the most secure facility in the world.

Problems begin immediately with the [victim](https://ibb.co/pYJCH6g), who has the power of "[Sevarance Protection](https://ibb.co/pYJCH6g)", a power that forms a barrier between her and anything that could potentially harm her, so basically, Gojo's infinity. This has continued after her death, preventing autopsy and forcing the staff to move the room just to get her out of the facility. The other Oppenheimers include a [man ](https://ibb.co/7N2R9fZk)that can [time travel](https://ibb.co/cS96QBjv), a [woman ](https://ibb.co/VPXNM8B)who [mind controls](https://ibb.co/chgQt3tr) anyone that hears her voice or sees her writing and a [boy ](https://ibb.co/K1kxx59)that can [create an object out of nothing](https://ibb.co/Q3wgtv7F) and rewrite reality as to convince everyone that the object had always been there (an effect he's not immune to, not even he knows what he's made with his power).

So, with no obvious leads, Notrick speaks with the time traveller. Except, the time traveller already knows him, and he's gone through this before. He claims that Notrick had already solved this "murder" in the future that he came from, but also claims that he won't spoil the answers, because he's a bro like that. He next interviews the boy, and Notrick quickly realises he's lying about, pretty much everything, because he's an excitable kid trying to show off to a detective by making cool stuff up. Most importantly is that he lied about using his power, but nobody, not even him, knows what he used it to create, only that it was used. The mind control woman is last, and it goes about as badly as you'd expect, but Notrick had a great display of foresight by sending Thena in alone and with no pencil so only she would be impacted by the Staffer ability. Thena is detained and the facility is put on lockdown, though interrogating his own partner gives Notrick valuable insight on the perception of mind controlled individuals.

After quite a bit of investigative work, Notrick decides to ask the head guard about a certain lounge in the facility that all Oppenheimers have access to so long as they remain alone. She cannot answer who made it, and he presses further. Her responses are familiar to how Thena was acting, and Notrick realises that the guard must have already been mind controlled. Using this knowledge, a bunch of phony evidence is tossed out, and further interrogation reveals that she'd been tasked to crush painkillers into the meals of the victim, potentially causing an overdose. Notrick reasons that the lounge might have been made by the boy, as a mind controlled guard wouldn't be able to make such sweeping changes to the facility without being detained herself (Again, I'm cutting out a lot of detail here, there's a bunch more going on, this is just the important brushstrokes).

Therefore, the killer was the woman with mind control, right? Its not exactly safe to get her side of the story, given her powers, but regardless, it seems to work?

Notrick isn't satisfied. He visits the time traveller again and speaks to him. The time traveller's answer matches up with his. He arrived at the same conclusion as he did in another timeline, this has to be the truth, so why isn't he satisfied? The boy used his powers, but was it really for the lounge? He looks into the victim again, there's nothing about her past, she recently arrived, there's nothing to go on. Notrick realises that nothing is what's been bugging him, and though that, he notices just how wrong everything has gone.

>!What the boy created was the victim herself. He had created a seemingly immortal individual that he wanted to kill to create an impossible murder, and the chain of events leading up to that point, warping reality to his will. While the cause of death was correct in the other timeline, it was not correct in the present, as the time traveller had warned the victim about the potential to overdose on her own food. This, in turn, caused her body to see any food as a threat, so even eating wouldn't sustain her, and she would die of starvation. Therefor, in the first timeline which the time traveller saw, the mind control woman murdered the victim via overdose, while in the second, which this Notrick experienced, the time traveller had inadvertently gotten her killed, and it was all masterminded by the boy, who could not recall any of his planning as not even he's immune to the retroactive memory warping of his powers.!<

>!Notrick figured all of this out despite having shoddy, unreliable evidence produced by mind controlled individuals actively manipulated into screwing him over, finding a mastermind who didn't even know they where the mastermind and solving a murder committed in two separate timelines at once. He has no Staffer powers, yet in the entirety of the Bureau, there is nobody more skilled at untangling the twisted logic of Pheno-Mana cases than he is.!<

# Conclusion

https://i.redd.it/9k6to84i8a2i1.png

Disregarding case 4, I'd say Notrick is comfortably in the top bounds of low tier, having some great FSIQ, Reasoning and Thinking feats that carry but tending to struggle on the social front. The other members of his team mostly take over for interrogations, since he struggles to reach people emotionally unless there's specific evidence he can bring up that lets him. Including case 4, I would probably put him somewhere in mid tier, probably closer to the lower end than the higher end. Narratively his scheming should also be pretty decent from post epilogue content, but we don't get to see much of it. Foresight is pretty good too, but not stellar. His Intuition can be absolutely insane at times, but he needs to have worked through all the facts and come to a conclusion before he starts to feel like he's missing something important, otherwise its nothing special.

Overall tier: Lower mid tier

Strongest categories: Thinking, Reasoning, FSIQ

Weakest categories: SQ, Deception, Observation (Bryan hard carries this category a little too hard for him)
