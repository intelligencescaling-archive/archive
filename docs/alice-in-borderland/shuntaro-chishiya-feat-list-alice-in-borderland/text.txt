Shuntaro Chishiya feat list

Shuntaro Chishiya Intelligence feats:

**“I don't care if you're the King of Intelligence, you are still dumber than me**”

Who he is: [https://aliceinborderland.fandom.com/wiki/Shuntaro\_Chishiya](https://aliceinborderland.fandom.com/wiki/Shuntaro_Chishiya)

During the Five of Spades, he was able to spot the Tagger almost immediately, He then quickly deduced from the Tagger’s unusual shooting pattern that they were actually strategically shooting intentionally trying to steer players away from the safe zone on the fourth floor by shooting from a higher position, using the bullets to guard the correct door.

This feat showcases \[AR (Abductive Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) ST (Strategic Thinking) TE (Trap Evasion) PS (Problem Solving) PER (Perpetual Reasoning) AT (Analytical Thinking) LT (Linear Thinking) Observation and Deduction\].

Before the Ten Hearts game he noticed Aguni's subtle glance toward a specific deer painting when the black envelope was being discussed, deducing it was the location of the hidden cards specifically that Aguni and his militant faction were hiding all the collected game cards at the Beach, within a safe behind the deer painting in the Hatter's suite. Not only this but he deduced from a subtle moment of confusion on Aguni's face that the envelope was empty and the actual code was the wax seal BOSS which he translated to numerical text to figure out the code was 5022.

This feat showcases \[EU (Emotional Understanding) AR (Abductive Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) VCI (Verbal Comprehension Index) ST (Strategic Thinking) PS (Problem Solving) PER (Perpetual Reasoning) AT (Analytical Thinking) LT (Linear Thinking) Observation and Deduction\].

He now needed a plan to get those cards, and after observing Usagi and Arisu’s kind nature, he decided they would be perfect for it. He approached them and convinced them to help him steal the cards by appealing to Arisu’s desire for a better life and Usagi’s loyalty to Arisu. He then instructed them to go to the Hatter's former suite, telling Arisu to try opening a visible—but unknowingly fake—safe with an unknowingly false code. Knowing the alarm would go off and that Arisu and Usagi would quickly be captured by the militants, he used their capture as a distraction. While Niragi, the Beach’s militant faction, and everyone else focused on the commotion, he slipped away and stole the real cards.

This feat showcases \[DIM (Direct Manipulation) IDM (Indirect Manipulation) LM (Logical Manipulation) PM (Psychological Manipulation) MM (Mass Manipulation) PP (Planning Practicality) SE (Strategy Efficiency) LT (Linear Thinking) LGT (Logical Thinking) ST (Strategic Thinking) PS (Problem Solving) and NCPS (Novel Complex Problem Solving)\].

During the King of Diamonds, he noticed that Daimon was using the same strategy as him in the final rounds. He knew she needed a number above 50 but not too close to 90 for the average to work in her favor if the others chose low numbers. After eliminating the unrandom numbers within her range, only a few possibilities remained—primarily 62 and 74. Based on his read of her personality and her tendency to avoid anything that seemed too logically obvious, he deduced she would choose 62. Since she was acting random on purpose, he was able to eliminate all non-random options, and his conclusion turned out to be correct.

This feat showcases \[AR (Abductive Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) ST (Strategic Thinking) TE (Trap Evasion) PS (Problem Solving) PER (Perpetual Reasoning) AT (Analytical Thinking) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) EU (Emotional Understanding) WMI (Working Memory Index) Observation and Deduction\].

During the King of Diamonds, after determining that abstract concepts like Nash equilibrium, recursive selection, and cognitive limits could be disregarded due to the rational gameplay—and that perfect rationality leads to perfect predictability—he decided to choose illogical, random numbers to influence the average outcome. This approach would allow him to deceive people into believing he was using intuition, disrupt the game flow, and target the King of Diamonds.

This feat showcases \[PM (Psychological Manipulation) IDM (Indirect Manipulation) MM (Mass Manipulation) PP (Planning Practicality) SE (Strategy Efficiency) LT (Linear Thinking) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) LGT (Logical Thinking) ST (Strategic Thinking) PS (Problem Solving) Knowledge Unpredictability and Deception\].

During the King of Diamonds, he exploited Kuzuryu's sense of justice and his inherent belief in the equality of life by setting a trap. He intentionally revealed his number as 100 in the final round, forcing Kuzuryu into a moral dilemma and making him decide whose life was more valuable. Bound by his morality, Kuzuryu pressed both 0 and 1 simultaneously to avoid judging life, which ultimately gave Chishiya the win, since Kuzuryu pressed 0 slightly faster.

This feat showcases \[PM (Psychological Manipulation) DIM (Direct Manipulation) IDM (Indirect Manipulation) IM (Individual Manipulation) PP (Planning Practicality) SE (Strategy Efficiency) EU (Emotional Understanding) LT (Linear Thinking) TS (Trap Setting) PS (Problem Solving)\].

During the Six of Diamonds it was his first time playing blackjack. He quickly picks up the rules just by quickly observing other players and devises a mathematical card-counting method, clicking and shifting his chips like an abacus to calculate odds, helping his stack grow to twenty-one chips. He observes that his neighbor is wearing a reflective ring, reasoning that it is being used to cheat, and telling the man to take it off. He becomes the next dealer. A card mechanic now threatens him, but he remains calm even though he is close to busting and a high card could destroy him. He then extends his hand; with a flourish, he flips the deck so the spent high cards are on top and wins the round. After observing the card mechanic has one or two cards up his sleeve, he challenges the card mechanic to a bet, with the bet being if the man was hiding one or two cards up his sleeve. He invites the woman to weigh in, and she responds vaguely. Then he takes a card and crumples it in front of everyone, clearly cheating in plain sight. This forces the woman to act as a judge and either shoot him or take the gamble he was right and shoot the presumably cheating card mechanic, which is what he was hoping she would do by exploiting her curiosity about whether he was right. She shoots the man, and two cards fall out from his sleeves, proving he was right. The woman, thinking she was going to die, says her goodbyes, but he instead uses each 28 chips they have left to flip both their chips and stand, causing them to both win.

This feat showcases \[LA (Learning Ability) AT (Analytical Thinking) LR (Logical Reasoning) ST (Strategic Thinking) EM (Emotional Management) ST (Stress Tolerance) PM (Psychological Manipulation) IM (Individual Manipulation) IDM (Indirect Manipulation) LT (Linear Thinking) TS (Trap Setting) PS (Problem Solving) PP (Planning Practicality) PER (Perpetual Reasoning) Observation Adaptability and SE (Strategy Efficiency)\].
