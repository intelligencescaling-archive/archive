The Source of the Fear: The "Impossible Truth"
Ike scared Nayuta by revealing a specific piece of intelligence during the interrogation that should have been impossible for him to know.
In the middle of the pressure, Ike yelled out: "[OG Traitor's Name] is dead! The Black Star is dying too!".
This terrified Nayuta for two specific reasons:
                        1. Exclusive Knowledge: Only Nayuta and the Kamishinkai know the OG Traitor is dead (because they killed/orchestrated it). To the rest of the world, including the Kagenkai and the Black Star, the OG Traitor is simply missing.
                        2. The "Cripple" Anomaly: Nayuta views Ike as an outsider—a "crippled journalist" with no status or deep connections. For Ike to possess a secret restricted to the highest level of the Kamishinkai implies that Ike has an incomprehensible source of information or deduction.

How This Caused the Silence
When Ike subsequently asked, "Who do you think the traitor is?", Nayuta was paralyzed by the implication of Ike's previous revelation.
Nayuta's thought process, driven by fear, locked him into silence:
                           * He cannot name Higa: This creates a paradox (why torture your own spy?).
                           * He cannot name a scapegoat (e.g., Akabane): This is the crucial fear. Because Ike just proved he knows "impossible" secrets (the OG Traitor's death), Nayuta terrifies himself with the possibility that Ike already knows Higa is the traitor.
                           * If Ike already knows it is Higa, and Nayuta points the finger at Akabane, Ike will immediately see it as a desperate lie to protect Higa.
                           * This would confirm that Nayuta is complicit with Higa, ruining the entire operation.
The Result: Nayuta realized that any spoken answer was a trap. If he lied, the "all-knowing" Ike would catch him. If he told the truth, he would expose his spy. Thus, fear of Ike's "impossible insight" forced him into silence, which he hoped would be interpreted as ignorance, though Ike later deduces it was recognition of the trap.
