Character Profile: Jane Marple
Miss Marple (BBC, 1984–1992) — Joan Hickson
Case analyzed: "The Body in the Library" (Chapters 1–3)

Methodological Note
This profile follows the methodology established in the base document: on-screen verification before accepting a feat as canon; separation between what is stated and what is mechanically demonstrated; and explicit notation when a feat lacks a reconstructible information chain.
Labels used: [CONFIRMED] = confirmed with a reconstructible mechanism shown on screen. [UNREALISTIC] = correct result but without a verifiable information source ("unrealistic feat" — usable in comparisons/debates, always cited with this caveat). [WITH RESERVATION] = reasonable interpretation by the narrator, not confirmed by a direct textual quotation.
Unlike other fictional characters in the base document, Marple's failure pattern toward the unrealistic is not extended sensory perception (she does not see/hear farther than humanly possible), but knowledge without a source: she reaches correct and specific conclusions without any narrative channel shown on screen explaining how she obtained that information. This pattern occurs at least 5 times throughout the case, elevating it from an "isolated writing error" to a recurring character trait.

Complete Feat Catalogue
Full Scale IQ
Feat 1 — Photographic memory under adverse conditions
Tier: High [CONFIRMED]
While examining the corpse in the Bantry library, under very poor lighting, Jane captures and retains enough visual detail for later use (bitten nails, type of dress). The scene reinforces this visually with a "mental capture" effect. It combines observation under unfavorable sensory conditions with eidetic retention of detail.
Feat 5 — Social/routine memory
Tier: Low [CONFIRMED]
She effortlessly remembers the usual time Mrs. Bantry wakes up. A minor feat, but indicative of the general method: she consistently retains trivial details about her social circle.
Emotional Intelligence (EQ)
Feat 6 — EP — Isolation of the correct stimulus (Josie Turner)
Tier: Medium-High [CONFIRMED]
Jane identifies that Josie becomes specifically uncomfortable when Mark and Adelaide Jefferson are mentioned (raised eyebrow, change in tone) — not when Jefferson himself is mentioned, nor at the death of her cousin, which genuinely affects her with grief. She correctly isolates the real stimulus among several possibilities.
Feat 9 — EP + Sustained social prediction (Colonel Bantry)
Tier: Medium-High [CONFIRMED]
Jane predicts that Arthur Bantry, despite not showing it, will withdraw into himself because of the distress caused by the case — and that this could "kill him" if it is not solved. The prediction is explicitly confirmed on screen: the village ostracizes him, the shopkeeper distances himself, and he rejects offered help. It requires deep knowledge of the subject's baseline personality to detect deviation in someone who actively suppresses his state.
Feat 12 — Intrapersonal emotional realization
Tier: Medium [CONFIRMED]
On the beach, Jane realizes in real time that she has begun to move past 8 years of mourning, an insight triggered by Dolly's companionship. Genuine emotional self-awareness, shared without filtering or repression.
Feat 16 — Josie Turner's hidden anger without behavioral signal
Tier: No closed tier [UNREALISTIC]
Jane concludes that Josie was angry about Ruby's death even though no behavioral signal in the original scene points to that emotion — not even indirectly. The only observable crack in that scene (discomfort) corresponds to a different subject (Mark/Adelaide). There is no reconstructible mechanism. It is recorded as a recurring pattern pending additional confirmation, not as a closed feat with its own tier.
Feat 18 — Strategic emotional preparation of Dolly
Tier: Low-Medium [CONFIRMED]
Jane verbally explains to Dolly the catalogue of probable social reactions to an unsolved case, anticipating and softening a future emotional blow at a controlled moment — deliberate protective management, not merely social diagnosis.
Social Intelligence
Feat 13 — Confirmed Concealment / Acting Skill (the license plate)
Tier: Medium [CONFIRMED]
Jane pretends not to know where to report a newspaper clue, deliberately using the façade of a "confused old lady" in front of Officer Slack to indirectly establish the connection between the burned car's license plate and Ruby's car — without exposing herself to being dismissed as the source. The clue works: the police act on it.
Feat 15 — Discerning Personality through local analogy (Mark Gaskell)
Tier: Medium-High [CONFIRMED]
She compares Mark with Mr. Cargill from her village to work out that his marriage was motivated by financial interest, without being fooled by his attractive appearance and "silly talker" façade. She recognizes the tactic in others because she herself has mastered the same tactic (see Feat 13).
Feat 19 — Profiling Mark Gaskell while appearing inattentive
Tier: Medium-High [CONFIRMED]
During her first interaction with Mark and Adelaide, Jane appears absorbed in knitting. Even so, she produces one of the most precise character readings of the chapter. It combines multitasking, psychoanalysis, and passive concealment.
Feat 20 — Observation of an undeclared romantic dynamic (Star/Adelaide)
Tier: Medium [CONFIRMED]
From eye direction alone, she detects an undeclared romantic/economic interest between Raymond Star and Adelaide before Dolly consciously notices it.
Feat 25 — Blake and his wife's secret marriage
Tier: High (result) / Unrealistic (mechanism) [UNREALISTIC]
Jane confronts Blake's wife, categorically stating that they are secretly married while maintaining a façade of being single — the result is 100% confirmed on screen. However, there is no scene showing Jane witnessing or receiving this information through any verifiable channel; the couple's only known discussion was witnessed solely by the commissioner, with no indication that he told Jane.
Adversity Capacity
Feat 4 — Stress tolerance regarding the corpse
Tier: Medium [CONFIRMED]
Jane examines Ruby's body without flinching, despite her advanced age (and therefore genuinely greater physical vulnerability to acute shock).
Feat 14 — Sustained Cognitive Discipline / Perseverance
Tier: Medium-High [CONFIRMED]
Across the three chapters, Jane keeps the problem actively "open" regardless of the social context (beach, tea, trivial conversations), without apparent fatigue, and solves it faster than the official investigation with equal or lesser access to evidence.
Reasoning / Thinking
Feat 2 — Profiling Ruby from minimal physical traits
Tier: Medium [CONFIRMED]
Without seeing the corpse's face, Jane connects bitten nails and flashy clothing with a specific social profile, arriving at an identity hypothesis faster than the officials, who needed formal identification.
Feat 11 — Ruling out a passionate/chaotic crime
Tier: Medium-High [CONFIRMED]
From indirect data (movement of the body, change of dress, absence of signs of struggle), she concludes with complete confidence that there was careful premeditation and that "something went wrong" — later confirmed by the discovery that barbiturates had been administered before the strangulation.
Feat 21 — Escalation prediction (third attack) + Ruby-Pamela connection
Tier: High [CONFIRMED]
Before police confirmation, Jane connects both cases and predicts, without direct evidence, that the perpetrator will attempt a third murder — extrapolation from the criminal's internal logic rather than reaction to a new clue.
Feat 22 — The fingernails: the corpse's false identity
Tier: High — Central feat of the case [CONFIRMED]
Jane connects her own observation from Chapter 1 (the corpse's bitten nails) with a new and trivial piece of information (a nail clipping that a child kept by chance, showing that Ruby kept her nails short and even on the night of her disappearance). The resulting inconsistency leads to the boldest conclusion of the case: the corpse in the library was never Ruby King. The police had the same physical evidence (clippings in the wastebasket) and did not interpret it. Actively verified before drawing conclusions.
Feat 23 — Prediction about the schoolgirl's friend
Tier: Medium [CONFIRMED]
Before the police confirm it through direct investigation, she anticipates that Pamela Reeves — a "normal" schoolgirl — would have a trusted friend, leading to new interviews and the key clue (Florence Small).
Feat 27 — The wrong dress
Tier: High [CONFIRMED]
She observes that Ruby changed into a less flattering dress before her disappearance — inconsistent with the hypothesis of a romantic date, for which one would expect her best dress. Discerning Inconsistencies with fully reconstructible logic.
Feat 28 — Interrogation of Florence Small
Tier: Medium-High
She identifies Florence's internal conflict (she knows something but struggles to say it) through body-language reading, and exerts effective social pressure without an explicit threat (Aura/Charisma). The additional detail that she already suspected a connection with "the cinema" before Florence's confirmation is recorded separately, without a clear source.
Strategy, Planning and Information Control
Feat 24 — Early resolution + strategic information management
Tier: Medium-High [CONFIRMED]
Jane explicitly tells Dolly that she already knows the killer's identity, but decides not to reveal it, explaining that Dolly would talk too much and ruin the only opportunity to obtain conclusive proof. She accurately models the likely behavior of a third party (her own friend) to decide what to share and when.
Feat 30 — Allowing an unjust arrest to proceed as a tactical move
Tier: Medium-High [WITH RESERVATION]
Reasoned interpretation (not confirmed by a direct quotation): Jane allows the police to arrest Blake, knowing he is innocent, in order to create a false sense of security for the real culprit and avoid alerting her before the final operation.
Feat 31 — Documentary verification at the London civil registry
Tier: High [CONFIRMED]
When hard proof is required, Jane uses the correct method: she travels personally and obtains official documentation of the secret marriage between Mark Gaskell and Josie Turner. This contrasts with the "unrealistic" feats — it demonstrates that she knows when a hunch is sufficient and when verifiable evidence is necessary.
Feat 32 — Final capture operation
Tier: Medium-High (shared credit)
Coordination of a nighttime trap (a substitute butler, armed officers, a window deliberately left open) to catch Josie Turner in the act. The script does not clarify whether the specific tactical design belongs exclusively to Jane or was developed jointly with the commissioner; credit is therefore recorded as shared.
Major Unrealistic Feats (Recurring Pattern)
Feat 17 — Psychological profile of Conway Jefferson without direct contact
Tier: Medium-High [UNREALISTIC]
Jane constructs a specific and non-obvious psychological explanation of the Jefferson–in-laws–Ruby dynamic (shared grief rather than genuine affection; a feeling of abandonment) without ever having spoken to Jefferson. She surprises the professional detective, who has direct access to the subject. She may have had some indirect context from Sir Henry, but this is not confirmed on screen.
Feat 26 — Complete reconstruction of Basil Blake's role
Tier: No closed tier — the highest unrealistic feat in the catalogue [UNREALISTIC]
Starting only from a carpet fiber (which could not even have been identified as such without laboratory analysis), Jane reconstructs: that the carpet specifically belonged to Blake; the exact psychological sequence that night (panic, drunkenness, indecision); that he transported the body as a "joke" against Arthur; and that he would attempt to destroy the evidence (burn the carpet, wash the car). Blake confirms every detail verbatim in his final confession. Result: 100% correct. Mechanism: not shown or reconstructible through any visible narrative channel.
Feat 29 — Hugo McLean's alibi without an identifiable source
Tier: No closed tier [UNREALISTIC]
Jane casually mentions that Hugo was in Devon/Deauville when the crime occurred and that he did not take long to travel after being summoned — information without any preceding scene supporting it.
The identity of the person in the photograph the butler found in Ruby's bag (revealed to be Basil Blake) is another instance of the same pattern: not even the butler, the only possible source, knew whose photograph it was. This is added to the list as the fifth instance of the pattern, reinforcing its status as a recurring character trait rather than an isolated writing error.
Escalation Conclusion
Jane Marple's confirmed core comfortably places her at the high end of the realistic human spectrum established by the document — comparable in methodological rigor to real-world field investigators such as John E. Douglas and Joe Navarro, but with an unusually broad range and speed of synthesis for a single reasoning method (the "Marple Method": mapping patterns from St. Mary Mead onto any social context).
Her actual ceiling, however, is defined by the recurring pattern of "knowledge without a verifiable source" — at least 5 documented instances in a single case (Feats 16, 17, 26, 29, and the identification of the photograph). This pattern, explicitly cited as an unrealistic feat, functionally places her at the level of an omniscient detective within the framework of each case — without qualifying for a Supra Tier, since there is no violation of physical laws or sensory perception beyond human limits: the knowledge she displays remains within what a human could theoretically have inferred with perfect information — information that, nevertheless, is never shown to have been obtained.
