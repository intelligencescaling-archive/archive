How Inductive & Abductive get affected by the law of large numbers:
Let’s assume that there's a general rule among a set of observations.
The law of large numbers conveys the idea that the more set of individual observations you have in relation to a particular point (in this case a rule), the average of those individual observations each time gets closer and closer to the true value. It’s used in statistics and probability mostly but its way of affecting induction and abduction in simple words is that the more samples there are the closer to the true value you get. For example, you may flip a coin and get 7 times in a row heads, but if you were to flip that coin infinitely, each time it’d get closer to the statistically true value which is 50:50 i.e 1:1 ratio between heads and tails. This affects both abduction and induction because they are both probabilistic types of reasoning.
In essence, less observations for a particular conclusion/rule = more difficult to induce/abduct.
