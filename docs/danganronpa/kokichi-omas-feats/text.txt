Kokichi's ideology

Before delving into the detailed analysis, it is important to understand Kokichi's beliefs, motives and goals throughout the entire killing game to better comprehend his intentions and perhaps gain a fresh perspective on Kokichi's character.

Many people label Kokichi as a sociopath or even a psychopath, but I strongly disagree with this notion. Kokichi exists in a moral grey area; he is not inherently good or evil. His focus is on achieving results, even if it requires sacrificing someone or something along the way. This means he engages in morally questionable actions to save everyone from the killing game, such as sacrificing Gonta in Chapter 4, for instance. Kokichi intentionally presents himself in a negative light, appearing as if he does not take the game seriously, even though he is deeply passionate about putting an end to the cycle. His disdain for the killing game drives him to try and dismantle it from within.

His ultimate goal extends beyond ending the current killing game, he aims to eradicate future killing games as well. This is why he does not bother identifying the mastermind, recognizing that the current game is orchestrated by a larger entity with authority over the killing game. Even if he eliminated the mastermind, the cycle of killing games would persist. Kokichi wants to both win the current game and dismantle the entire system.
Throughout most of the killing game, Kokichi operates as a hidden force, subtly influencing events while guiding Shuichi to reach conclusions he had already deduced beforehand. This strategic approach allows him to remain unnoticed and deftly control the course of the killing game. By presenting himself as a mischievous troublemaker, he aims to win favour with the spectators and avoid detection by both the mastermind and the other participants. Through his manipulation of Shuichi, he effectively steers the game as he desires, maintaining a level of secrecy and authority throughout.

Kokichi's actions are predominantly indirect, he seldom states things explicitly but implies them instead. The true meaning behind his motives becomes clearer when we carefully examine each individual action and connect them to form a cohesive interpretation. While he leaves room for various interpretations, a predominant interpretation emerges, enabling us to analyse him despite his seemingly mysterious intentions.

In summary, we have established that Kokichi operates in moral grey areas and is willing to go to great lengths to achieve his goals. His preferred method involves indirect tactics, using Shuichi as a tool in his plans. His overarching plan is not just to stop the killing game, but to dismantle it from within, allowing it to decay gradually and subsequently, preventing its continuation.

1. To identify potential killers in the game: He could identify the killers because those more likely to give up on escape were deemed to have weaker willpower and are far more likely to resort to irrational actions such as murder.

2. To assess the threat level of other participants: Kokichi aimed to identify those who would discern the underlying eeriness behind his message. This way, he would learn about the astutely sharp individuals within the group. This strategic move allowed him to keep a close watch on everyone that would be able to spot this throughout the killing game before any dangerous actions had even unfolded in the first place, which would be beneficial in the long run especially for somebody like Kokichi, who gained advantage in the killing game especially through profiling people.

3. Tighten their bonds: Isolate Kaede and for the upcoming hours strip her out of her leading position to rebuild their friendships completely with an even stronger bond than ever before. (I will elaborate on this below)

Additionally, Kokichi rapidly undermined Kaedes leadership by swaying those more easily influenced against her in the heat of the moment, achieving his third goal. Later on, after they slept, and met each other at the diner, it was Kokichi who first spoke to Kaede again. By taking Kaede's side in the conflict, he steered the situation towards resolution, aiming for Kaede to offer apologies to diffuse tension and restore her leadership role, and therefore her trust along with it as well, despite there being people who initially raised clear objections against Kaede. This way, everything should go smoothly and no further arguments should stir up anymore and Kaede will be as strong as ever in her position as a leader of the group.

This strategic move aimed to strengthen the group's bonds by resolving conflict with a mutually agreeable solution. Bonds always tend to get stronger after every fixed argument, where a solution is presented that fits everybody, which is a pattern that will fortify a relationship without fail. Had a single person remained dissatisfied, his plan would have crumbled, underscoring Kokichi's astute understanding of personalities involved in the game. But this had never happened, showing that Kokichi had in fact psychoanalysed everybody during Chapter 1, because a chain reaction of emotions had to be calculated ahead of time to reach this outcome. His tactic aimed at fostering stronger bonds through a fabricated conflict - a tactic he confessed to later. He claimed the intention of his actions was to unite everyone, which would lead to better teamwork of everybody in the group. He acknowledged his deception, but asserted that many of his lies were rooted in good intentions, highlighting his underlying motives for actions, which were needed to distinguish his actions in other chapters.

So, what can we conclude from all of this?

Kokichi strategically waited for the right opportunity when everyone grew fatigued, and therefore disinterested in pursuing what seemed like an aimless scavenger hunt in order to escape the killing game. With keen observation of the situation and about half of the participants in the desired mental state, he initiated his plan. He shifted all of the blame onto Kaede, insinuating that she compelled them against their will into a seemingly meaningless activity, as repeated attempts yielded no exit whatsoever.

The real intention behind this was to manipulate the emotions of the others while discrediting Kaede's emotions, diverting everybody's attention, making them forget the purpose behind Kaede's actions and from the actual goal - escaping the school in which they were trapped in. Initially, it might appear that this was to sow discord among the participants and create internal chaos, but that's not entirely accurate. The primary aim was to evoke remorse in Kaede and this way compel her to apologise to everyone, and for the group to accept her apology afterwards.

Kokichi's understanding of each individual's psyche, including Kaede's, guided his actions. He anticipated that the group would forgive Kaede without bearing any deeper grudges against her anymore, despite her pushy actions. Recognizing Kaede's dominant role within the group and her resilient willpower, he saw the necessity for her to apologise to everybody in order to maintain her influence and therefore ensure the safety of everybody else. This approach aimed to fortify the bonds among all the participants in the killing game, making team building and cooperation significantly more attainable than before. Despite them being put in a situation where they are assumed to distrust everybody, their trust had grown exponentially thanks to Kokichi as early as Chapter 1.

Given the games are inherently designed for observation by spectators, it can be inferred that Kokichi was cognizant of the concept of “spectator” since the inception of the narrative throughout Chapter 1 ranging from the established rules to the nature of the killing game. Consequently. His non-cooperative demeanour exhibited throughout Chapter 1, such as his refusal to divulge his alibi during the investigation preceding the class trial, was a deliberate portrayal aimed at depicting him as a disruptor - a figure simultaneously appealing to a fraction of viewers, particularly from the spectators' standpoint.

This deliberate portrayal as a troublemaker was orchestrated to imbue him with a certain level of protection for survival within the overarching game's context. By cultivating a persona that garnered attachment from spectators, there existed a diminutive defence mechanism - creating a plausible deterrent against potential harm from a hypothetical orchestrator of the game. This strategy relied on the premise that the mastermind, if present, would be less inclined to eliminate him due to the established affinity of spectators towards the persona he meticulously sustained throughout the game.

2. Given his established reputation as an untrustworthy individual, he recognized that others would naturally distrust him, allowing him to act without the burden of others relying on him.

3. Understanding that the killing game was intended for spectators, he adopted the troublemaker role to generate conflicts and drama, captivating the audience and their interests. This heightened attention served as a form of protection, as it would create a vested interest among spectators to keep him alive. Importantly, he ensured that the drama he incited would not directly antagonise the mastermind, thereby avoiding becoming a prime target. He understood the psychology of the people that would be watching a killing game and made a selection based off the information available to him to act a certain way that would ensure his safety.
