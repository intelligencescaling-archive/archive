Why I think Kanade is overrated (she's a mid tier imo) (The text is going to sound AI because I basically used a voice message to put it all into AI then had AI remove the umms and stuff because I didn't want to type it all out)

Okay, so if you’re reading this, I want you to please try looking at the explanation I’m about to give from a realistic perspective. Imagine a real-life person going into the world of Super Danganronpa Another 2 with a twin. This is just a quick prelude before the actual explanation, but keep this in mind while reading.

If you and your twin were in the Danganronpa world, especially if you had a relationship like Kanade and Hibiki Otonokoji, it’s very reasonable to say that even an average human who wanted to kill would think about escaping with their twin. Even an average person in that situation would think, “If I want to kill, I want to escape together with my twin.”

But within the rules of the game, only one person can escape. So naturally someone might think: what would happen if we both killed someone at the same time? Then we would technically both be the blackened, and we could both escape.

Again, this is just a prelude. I just want you to keep this in mind.

Now, we see Kanade say during the trial that ever since she heard the rules, she’d been thinking about ways to pull off a murder where both her and Hibiki kill one person together. She literally mentions this. That also implies she basically had a whole week to plan out how she would kill with Hibiki and waited for the perfect opportunity.

After that, if you look at what actually happened, she and Hibiki killed at the same time and then created this whole complex murder plan to perfectly frame Iroha Nijiue. The entire point was that even if the class figured out that either Hibiki or Kanade was the killer, they wouldn’t figure out that both of them were responsible.

Now, there are three main aspects people use to rate Kanade highly, and in my opinion these are the three most important ones:

* The double murder idea — two people killing one person.
* The framing of Iroha.
* The overall strategy as a whole, including all the details like transporting the body and everything.

Now starting with the third point — the overall strategy — I want to tell an actual real-life example.

After I played the game, I told a friend of mine to play it as well. This guy is pretty smart. He’s not a genius or anything, but he’s definitely smart. When he got to Chapter 3, he did something he likes to do: after finishing the investigation, he doesn’t immediately go into the trial. He tries to deduce what happened in the case first.

Now, in this case, he already knew Kanade was involved because he had gotten spoiled, but he didn’t know anything else. And based purely on deduction — just normal reasoning, not detective-level thinking — he was able to figure out the entire murder plan and how she did it. He was even able to figure out that both Kanade and Hibiki were the killers.

The way he figured out that part specifically was because he had played other Danganronpa games and knew that Chapter 3 usually involves a double murder. So when this one looked like a single murder, it made him suspect that two people would actually be responsible, which led him to conclude that both Kanade and Hibiki were the killers.

Now, this isn’t a full debunk of the double murder idea or the framing, because he did know some things beforehand. It’s also not a perfect debunk of the double murder concept since he already expected something like that. But what this does show is that the overall strategy — and even the framing of someone else — realistically wasn’t that hard to see through.

The game and the narrative make it seem like it took a long time and that the deduction was extremely difficult, but honestly, it really wasn’t that hard to come to these conclusions.

So most of the strategy itself isn’t actually that complex to figure out.

Now let’s look at the two main things that supposedly make her impressive: the double murder and the framing.

In my opinion, the double murder idea is just a basic concept that almost any person could come up with. It’s simply the idea of two people killing one victim at the same time so both are responsible. There are many ways to do this — not just the very specific method Kanade used.

For example, two people could just put poison into a drink at the same time, give it to the victim, and that’s already two people killing simultaneously. There would be no clear single killer, so Monokuma would be forced to treat both as the murderers. So yes, it would be confusing for the class trial, but actually coming up with the idea itself isn’t some huge strategic feat.

Then regarding the final part — the framing of Iroha — yes, that part was good. But it’s not something that should put her in a super high tier. It was solid, but it’s not extremely complex or beyond what a normal person could plan, especially considering she had a full week of preparation time.

So overall, yes, she’s better than other Danganronpa characters, which is why I put her in mid-tier. But she’s definitely not high tier. There’s nothing in her strategy that’s extremely special. Most of what she did is something an average person could realistically come up with, especially with prep time.

That’s why I don’t think she should be rated that highly.
