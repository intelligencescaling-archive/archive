Starting the deduction
There’s a part in the video where the press machine suddenly stops, then suddenly slams down again. Why is that?
The answer is the “Force Stop” button on the press.
The camera only has three buttons: record, stop, and resume (after stopping). It doesn't have any editing features, so why did it suddenly stop and then continue to crush?
To make the press stop mid-action, you'd have to coordinate the stopping of the camera with the stopping of the press.
But the idea that the Safety System caused the machine to stop sounds… illogical. Think about it: then why would Kokichi throw the Electrobomb? When you throw an Electrobomb, it disables the Safety System completely. However, the press can still function normally, and even more powerfully once it’s started. In other words, the safety system and the press system are separate, and the press can still operate mechanically even without electricity, just without safety protection.
So, the only possible explanation is to use the Force Stop button. Not to mention, pressing "Force Stop" allows Kokichi to deliberately control the exact moment the machine stops. He just needed to press Force Stop a few seconds before stopping the video to create that specific effect.
There’s also one more detail that supports this theory: the camera’s position. It’s placed in a way that hides the victim’s face after the machine lowers, and it’s very close to the press; this is very suspicious. Like, why is the camera set up in such a “perfect” spot? They could’ve angled it directly at Kaito from a distance, if they really wanted to show Kaito clearly as the victim, and if Maki accidentally killed him, then just show it clearly.
But no. This was planned. The placement of the camera, close to the press and at the “perfect angle”, was intentional. Along with the theory of pressing Force Stop, then pressing stop video 1–2 seconds later, this lets Kokichi make the video appear unedited, while manipulating the timing using the buttons. This creates a short time window where everything in the video still looks normal, but allows him to switch things in reality.
→ Kokichi deliberately leads the group to suspect that “maybe the video was edited this way,” but he made the mistake on purpose so people would notice.
So, what was that time window used for?
→ To switch places, obviously.

Suddenly, Kaito stepped out of the Exisal, showing himself to everyone, driven by a strong desire to save them, and a fear that Monokuma might not believe Shuichi’s lie and execute everyone.
(Well, that’s how Kaito explained it anyway, to make it sound less like they were bluffing.)
So what really happened?
Kaito revealed himself and exposed the “Script”, confessing to being the culprit.
That became the key factor that allowed Monokuma to conclude Maki wasn’t the culprit.
Why?
Because Maki still had a 50/50 chance of being the culprit.
But once Kaito revealed the plot script, Maki was temporarily removed from the suspect list.
The idea of using the antidote bottle as a trade-off to carry out the plan might sound illogical; maybe Kaito only drank part of it.
But in any case, the explanation that Kaito died sounded more reasonable, and at the very least , it gave Monokuma some hope that the process was “fair.”
Besides… if someone confesses, it’d be kind of weird not to accept it, right?
Now, everyone was forced to vote.
Monokuma would be forced to make a judgment based on instinct, and instinct alone.
And the case would fall into one of five scenarios:
               1. Kokichi or Kaito gets voted, and Monokuma gives an explanation to justify why Kokichi/Kaito was the culprit, but that would fall apart if the one voted was already dead.
               2. If Maki gets voted… that’s probably the “safest” choice. Monokuma has the full authority to declare her guilty or not.
To satisfy the viewers, there's a high chance he would make Maki lose in order to keep the game going.
However, this is a less likely outcome because Shuichi would probably vote for Kokichi instead of Maki if forced to pick randomly, and he'd likely convince everyone to do the same.
               3. If people vote as a pair, splitting their votes between two suspects, Monokuma would have no basis to end the game or execute someone at random, because it’s still possible to identify the killer.
Still, even in that case, Monokuma would be forced to declare a verdict, a dangerous move for him, since he doesn’t actually know anything.
               4. If all three suspects are voted on, then Monokuma would definitely have to make a declaration.
In short, many of these outcomes would put Monokuma at a disadvantage, making this trial feel unfair.
Kokichi’s main goal? To create the sense of “unfairness” and expose the loopholes in the game, and more importantly, Monokuma’s weakness: his lack of fairness.
Unfairness here is Monokuma’s biased judgment toward the players.
First, he’s led around like a sheep by Shuichi; whatever Shuichi says, people do.
If Shuichi says “vote Kaito,” they vote Kaito.
If he says “vote Kokichi,” they do it.
Then Kaito walks out of the room, and Monokuma just declares Kaito guilty again.
All of this is based on “cases.” The chance of Maki not being punished is “high,” but it’s not 100%, which only proves Monokuma’s judgment is unjust.
Kokichi also demonstrates a way to manipulate Monokuma in the future, causing the program to pause or stall for a long time, unless Monokuma wants to be seen as “corrupt.”
So, is Monokuma really acting on his own, not being controlled by a Mastermind?
Well, here’s the thing: this is a risk Kokichi fully accepted, especially given the situation he was in.
To reiterate: Kokichi is in this program as a player, and what he needs to do is completely destroy the system, without revealing himself to the Mastermind until it’s too late (because in a Killing Game, being eliminated is always a threat, and murder is "allowed").
That’s why Kokichi gambled on the idea that he could make the game unfair.
Because unfairness is exactly what can destroy the essence of a “test” like this.
Of course, this isn’t even that big a risk.
As for the idea of there being multiple Monokumas, I’ve already mentioned that earlier.
The fact that Monokuma is being modified and controlled using other energy sources and surveillance equipment…
In a virtual world, countering these materials isn’t impossible.
Not to mention, they still have to maintain some level of realism in case they want to prevent a “second Kokichi” from showing up later.
Also, rest assured, even with modifications, the final judgment still comes from a human, or at least that’s what the audience will believe.
And no matter what, the audience will boycott the show after Kokichi’s revelation about its “unfairness.”
Perhaps the worst-case scenario would be creating a hyper-sensitive AI system that can detect death flawlessly.
But remember, the hardest thing for humans to regain is trust.
No one will be sure if it’ll leak again.
And this is a program, a program with this level of massive investment, that is most likely made for public entertainment (because if they went through all this effort and expense, they wouldn’t do it just for fun).
There must be profit involved.
And if people stop watching, where’s the profit?
If the profit dies, then it’s better to shut it down.
So why let everyone live instead of letting them all die?
Because killing them off isn’t necessary at all. It would only increase the risk and lower the chance of success.
For example, what if, hypothetically, they weren’t able to shut the program down immediately?
That would be a disaster.
Whereas if Shuichi and his friends are still alive, they’d not only carry the motivation inspired by Kaito and Kokichi’s efforts, but they also have the skills.
They could expose the true nature of the program on their own, maybe even track down the Mastermind, if one actually exists.
From there, the chances of ending the Killing Game once and for all would only increase.
