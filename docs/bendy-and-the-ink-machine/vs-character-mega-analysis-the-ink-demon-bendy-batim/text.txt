Background

(Redscreen (Me))

(DimEstion)
“I.. am the Ink Demon. This realm.. is MINE. You were BORN from it.. You BELONG to it..”

    In the dilapidated walls of a cycle repeated, a rotting animation studio left to catch dust as its inhabitants fight for their own survival, there is a beast that walks the halls. Searching for any poor souls to feast upon and slaughter. A beast that predates all the suffering, all the expansion. It starts and ends with him, and it doesn’t stop for the rest of eternity. The beast goes by the name of The Ink Demon, but he was not always like this. It all begins with the hatred, greed and envy of one Joey Drew.

Joseph “Joey” Drew was always a man with ambition. Long before his glory days, he was the son of a shoemaker, raised by his mother and living comfortably although being poor. An experience from his dad where he snuck in and learned that the “shoe elves” his mom said helped him make the shoes were actually just made up characters he did to make the hours pass by taught the boy that fantasies and make-belief made life more bearable. This might sound wholesome, but at 10 years old, he witnessed a man fall or jump off a building, possibly to his death, which made him “realize” that life was pretty much a movie. Where the people around him weren’t actually people, but “characters.” Basically, he thought reality and fiction were one and the same, which was.. very unhealthy.

Later on in life, he spent time in the First World War, where he would meet a friend for life by the name of Nathan Arch. Many years later down the line around 1922, he met a Miss Abby Lambert while working at Mr. Kirkpatrick’s Bookstore, and later, one Henry Stein as they worked together on the job. Later seeing Henry’s art at an art gallery hosted by Abby, Joey was inspired to try his hand at animated cartoons. He and Henry met at his apartment, and after throwing around adjustments to a design Henry sketched up, Bendy was born.

Thanks to an investment from Nathan Arch and Mrs. Richmond, Joey Drew Studios was born, Joey leading the helm with Henry as his business partner. Although, due to questionable work ethics on Joey’s end, abuse, and a want to spend more time with his wife Linda, Henry would leave the company, sparking Joey’s fury as it never ceased.

As the cartoons released and employees were hired, Bendy grew and grew in popularity. He gained an expansive side cast, like the gluttonous yet good hearted Boris the Wolf, the charming Alice Angel, and the nasty trio named The Butcher Gang. Toys, fame, even a theme park by the talented Bertrum Piedmont, it wasn’t long before Joey Drew had the world spinning on his thumb. But the ambition he always had, always sought towards didn’t cease. It grew and grew into something bigger than himself. A legacy of poor decisions that would become his legacy, a monument to choosing the wrong road. Joey wanted his cartoons to be lifelike, larger than life.

No. He wanted them to be real.

Partnering with the dodgy GENT Corporation, Joey got to work achieving just that. He and GENT built a machine with the capability of doing such a thing. A machine that could create a 3D print of a living character in its depths of metal and ink and print them out into our own reality. This was the Ink Machine, and Joey Drew’s very first test was that of his very first and most favourite creation, the little devil darling himself.

But it didn’t turn out right. His smile was a tad too crooked. His build a bit too malnourished and bony. His height a little too tall. He was a failure, a mistake and a monster due to his lack of soul. A mere imitation of Bendy, that while ultimately was harmless, merely wandering the halls curiously searching his new surroundings, was incredibly uncanny, and in Joey’s eyes, could scare off future investors. So what did he do?

He locked the creature in the depths of the Infirmary, never to be let out while he tweaked his tests. If a lack of soul was the reason that thing turned out so poorly, then all that was needed was well, a soul. Souls he owned plenty of. So Joey began to sacrifice his employees, turning them to ink monsters of all types, from warped ink humanoids to twisted versions of Alice, to mere bony Lost Ones as he sought the perfect living cartoon.

But down in the Infirmary, Bendy’s bitter anger was boiling. The spite set into him from Joey’s actions and the forceful internalization of the concept of him being a mistake and a monster by the people around him set in, causing the beast to snap. If Joey wanted a monster, he would get a monster. A living representation of his failure, his sins that would haunt him until his grave. This was no longer a mere failed Bendy clone. This was the Ink Demon.

Accidentally freed by a newbie employee named Buddy Lewek, the Ink Demon began to shed blood in the studio, taking the lives of many and severely injuring others. Drowning certain people in ink forced them to become ink creatures themselves, adding more members to the growing group of ink hybrids. Joey’s hatred for Henry however never stopped growing, and this spurred him on to do something. Using the Ink Machine’s properties sometime during all this, he created an entire parallel world of ours, one he used to capture and contain all of his creations, including the Ink Demon. This world was a looping purgatory called The Cycle, and Joey used the realm to torture an inky clone of Henry that retained his same memories, forcing him to stop the Ink Demon via a reel called The End over and over again as he also dealt with other threats, a looping story with the same mundane events repeating in it. So the Ink Demon fought Henry over and over again over hundreds of continuous cycles, the clone of Henry slowly wising up to the nature of his existence.

In the real world however, Joey Drew was long dead, spending the rest of his days in a cheap rat infested apartment. The Bendy franchise was bought off by Nathan Arch’s new company, Arch Gate Pictures, who wanted to honor Joey’s memory and regain Bendy’s popularity. People were hired to animate new Bendy cartoons, one of them being a mysterious girl named Audrey, an orphan with no memory of her parents, that unbeknownst to her, was a byproduct of the Ink Machine and the daughter of Joey Drew, half-human and half-ink.

However, Nathan Arch’s somewhat neglected son, Wilson Arch, who worked around the studio as a janitor, soon discovered the Ink Machine and the Cycle, and realized this was an easy way to live up to the legacy of his father. Entering the realm, he froze it in place. He created the Keepers and an assistant named Betty, capturing the Cyclebreakers, like Henry, Sammy Lawrence, the twisted Alice, and a few others from the old Cycle. And most notable of all, he and the Keepers managed to capture the Ink Demon, beginning expansive tests to see if killing the beast was possible.

Unfortunately, it was not. The demon was pretty near damn unkillable, showing very little emotional responses. It was a failure, but what actually ended up happening from the quarter-hourly physical and emotional torture inflicted upon the beast was forceful evolution and a weird trauma response where he reverted to a docile form more closely resembling his cartoon form. The Keepers kept in this form, Wilson claiming he “killed” the Ink Demon and taking control of the Cycle.

Soon, he brought in Audrey to the realm to use her as a soul for a creature to actually finish off the Ink Demon for real, but during her escapades, she encountered the docile Bendy and hurt him, re-triggering his newer, more advanced form. And just like that, the Ink Demon was free again, only being able to be triggered back into his older form by the GENT Suppression Towers around the studio. Gradually taking back control of HIS realm, it wasn’t long before he helped Audrey defeat Shipahoy Wilson.. Or moreso just stepped in while she was legless.

Cornering her, the Ink Demon used a combination of projection of his insecurities and persuasion to get Audrey to fuse with him, the daughter of Drew and the demon now one. Unfortunately for him, it was this choice, along with killing the living memory of Joey Drew that was in the studio, that allowed Audrey to forcefully take control of him, and with the help of some friends, play The End one more time, successfully restarting the Cycle. But this time, possibly due to how they were fused, it appears that the Ink Demon is now in the real world… In his cartoon friendly form, at the very least.

Perhaps maybe, separated from the cycle of agony he was tied to for all of his life, far away from the trauma and insecurities that still ate at him for all of his unnatural inky existence, the Ink Demon can finally find peace.

…

Yeah, nah.

At the end of it all, even if the darkness we were born from doesn’t define who we are, the Ink Demon has made the conscious choice to hunt and kill until the end of time. A tragic victim of his environment? Yes. Someone you should underestimate? Absolutely NOT.

For he is the Ink Demon, and this realm.. is HIS. And no matter how many cycles repeat its same mundane happenings on end, there will always be one Ink Demon to hunt and maim everyone down.
Experience/Skill

    Bendy is a physical powerhouse, repeatedly implied to be the strongest being in the Cycle by multiple people to the point he was being worshipped like a god by many. The sheer power he has allows him to destroy a lot of potential threats to his reign over his realm (or people he just wants to kill for funsies), from the Projectionist (shown above) who’s around his size, to even the massive Shipahoy Wilson. He’s so powerful and threatening that it’s stated by Cycle veteran Henry that the entire realm revolves around him, even if the Ink Demon is still bound to its rules. Which isn’t even incorrect as the act of killing the Ink Demon with The End reel is literally what resets a Cycle in the first place.

He’s no slouch in the intelligence department too. Now, is he some hyper intelligent mega genius? No, he’s still very bound by his animalistic tendencies and active enjoyment of inflicting pain on others, but he’s still capable of greater intelligence, like when he managed to convince Audrey to fuse with him by whittling her down using some projection and general persuasion. The Ink Demon seems to be an active shit talker, reveling in striking fear and poking at people and attacking when they’re vulnerable. He’s capable of coherent speech too, seeming to have moderately above average linguistics compared to most others at least.

Cycle Weapons

A variety of weapons can be found across the Cycle’s piss yellow walls to arm yourself. The Ink Demon has no reason to use these, but many of the Lost Ones he can command to his bidding are capable of scrounging around to get these.

It’s important to note that all of these items are of a varying rarity, and it’s very unfeasible to say that the Lost Ones can find or even access some of them. We’ll indicate this in the mini weapon descriptions. We’re just including everything for the sake of completion.
* Axe: Old reliable. Simple weapon that can be used to cut down boards or smack down ink creatures in a few hits. Seems like you can even block with it in the next game. These can seemingly be found across the entire studio.
* GENT Pipe: Pipes used by the GENT corporation. They can be upgraded with schematics and charged with batteries to open doors, or power up and kill in a few hits. Even on their own they can break apart loose brick sections of the studio to access new areas. Certain Lost Ones are actually seen using these, albeit the un-upgraded version.
* Pipe Wrench: In Chapter 3 of the first game, this was given to Henry by Alice to open special gear boxes around the studio. On their own though, they’re weaker than an axe, but swings faster and hits harder than a normal GENT pipe.
* Plunger: Weak and pathetic. Only would reasonably ever be used for the funny.
* Syringe: Used to collect extra-thick ink from Swollen Searchers as well as a weapon. Insert tetanus joke here. Once again only seen being granted to Henry by Alice.
* Empty Bacon Soup Can: Empty can of Bacon Soup that can be thrown as a projectile. Stockpile on the stuff and make sure you have solid aim!
* Scythe: The most powerful weapon in the OG BATIM, although it isn’t really accessible through normal means, but rather through a really long winded path you have to take throughout the entire massive game. Because of this, it’s unlikely it can be accessed at all. Still ridiculously overpowered though.
* Tommy Gun: You’ll mainly find Twisted Alice having this. A gun and another really overpowered weapon, can take down the Projectionist with a few shots. Lost Ones aren’t seen using it though.
* Ink Launcher: I cannot find information or clips of this thing for the life of me, but the page is there with a screenshot to prove I’m not crazy. I’d imagine it’s probably the most OP projectile weapon, shooting out massive blobs of ink. Questionable if they’d have this, considering it was in the confiscated weapons area. (Or now that I think about it it could just be an Easter egg)

Abilities
The Ink

“Ink never disappears. It's always there, like it was hiding, waiting to reveal itself. It's always there to remind you. It'll never go away. At first it's not so bad. You get used to seeing it on the inside of your index finger like that. Maybe it's even a friend. Or a mark of pride. That's how it lures you in. But it blossoms and burrows. And seeps and sinks. And drowns and drinks. It's alive. It's everywhere. It's inside me. It breathes for me. I can feel it sloshing about in my lungs. I can feel it in my brain. I am ink.”

    Ink is the driving power force of the entire Bendy franchise, the same way that souls/remnant is for FNaF or that Chaos Energy is for Sonic. (I couldn’t think of another good indie horror game power force so there you go.) Now I shouldn’t have to explain to you how normal ink works, right? Viscous, black, liquid that’s typically used on paper to write or create art. Pretty simple, but the ink in Bendy is different.

It’s important to note a few distinctions though. As shown in Dreams Come To Life, not all ink in the studio is this special ink. Only ink from the ink machine seems to have the special properties I’m about to talk about. Joey Drew Studios has an abundance of normal ink that they give to artists to draw with. The ink from the ink machine is specifically for creating a living cartoon and has the special properties I’m about to list. This isn’t really an issue considering pipes for the machine are everywhere in the studio and the ink has spread all over the place in the Cycle, but it should be mentioned and noted for the sake of context.

Anyways, dubbed as “Thick Ink” by the studio, and also described personally by Joey Drew in a letter to employees to be “high-quality ink,” this ink courses all life into the Cycle Bendy resides in. It is naturally formless, the studio taking advantage of this seemingly to use it to create many tools, like gears, pipes, and axes, both in the Cycle and in real life, at least according to the Employee Handbook yet again. However, this ink can also interact with souls, where the cool stuff actually happens.

If a person dies while submerged in ink, they are reincarnated as one of the many ink creatures seen in the studio, shown on many, many occasions. Buddy, Sammy, Wilson, Susie Campbell (implied), etcetera. Their soul becomes bonded to the ink as a collective mass, and if they were to die again in their inky body, they would be reborn/respawned due to the Cycle’s nature.

Certain sectors of the ink actually seem to be literally alive and sentient, as shown in the first novel where some of it got onto Buddy’s grandfather, crawled up him, and attempted to get into his body. It also seems to be heavily resistant. In BATDR, when the Lost Ones were electrocuted by Alice, the ink on their bodies did not visibly burn away, implying that the special ink from the machine is much more resistant and durable then normal ink.

The Ink Demon can create this ink himself, albeit not to a massive extent. His mere presence can flood rooms and coat walls with ink, these shadows capable of killing lesser creatures like Searchers and the Butcher Gang. The ink shadows also serve as a detection range or a way to track down his prey.
Ink Physiology

Bendy is a creature created from ink, and because of this, he shares quite a few noticeable differences from the average human physiology-wise. Minus uh.. his obviously inhuman appearance.

Unlike many other ink creatures that reside in the Cycle, Bendy does not actually have a soul, which does exempt him from the automatic respawning of the realm, notably. He is composed entirely of ink, a living blob of it, no organs or nothing inside of his body. Any ink that comes into contact with his body will be used in some form, most often his regeneration. Scarily enough, Bendy also seems to rapidly evolve, growing in size (from being slightly taller than Henry to being 11 feet tall in Audrey’s cycle) and strength according to Buddy. Henry stated that if Bendy kept on growing in strength, he’d soon break the very rules of the Cycle that he was currently bound by.

He doesn’t need to eat, drink, or breathe due to being ink (no duh). Along with this, as shown by Audrey, ink creatures can morph their body and bend it in odd ways, although Bendy is never seen doing this.
Regeneration

    Bendy has the capability to regenerate, as shown in the Bendy novels. Liquidy, and akin to a symbiote, he’s been shown healing minor incisions to entire lost limbs as long as he has a steady supply of ink to draw from. He’s even been able to regenerate after being melted/mixed into a massive puddle of ink.

Emotion Manipulation

    The nature of the ink brings out the darker side of the people who are around it. People who make contact with it have their emotions heightened, most particularly their more repressed emotions they choose to hold back. In Bendy: The Lost Ones, when Constance made contact with the ink, she became irrationally angry due to her repressed frustration with her homelife and the inadequacies around it. Essentially, they shed their hesitation and show off their “true selves.” Some victims of the ink have said they feel happier in this true state, although it’s unclear if it’s just more manipulation from the ink or genuine.

Enhanced Senses

    The Ink Demon’s senses are incredibly acute, according to many of his prey. He can hear the most miniscule of sounds from incredibly far distances, letting him hone in on his victim’s location quickly. This was showcased to be unlikely to be hyperbole in Chapter 3 of BATIM, where he was able to hear Henry break the Bendy cutouts and get to his location.
Telepathy

    Bendy’s evolving due to BATDR’s prolonged Cycle allowed him to gain the ability to speak.. Albeit not by flapping his lips. The Ink Demon communicates telepathically with other characters, whether it’s manipulating them or talking smack (yeah, he’s kind of a prick.) He can project his voice to as small as one person to as large as an entire room of people.

It’s also heavily implied that through this telepathy (or through the ink itself), the Ink Demon can quite literally read your mind and know everything about you, even things you may not know about yourself. Directly telling Audrey that “the ink speaks to me, it whispers your secrets.” He later seems to directly acknowledge Audrey’s existence as a half-human half-ink hybrid, calling her a mistake and a monster like him despite not being there when Joey revealed it to her, adding further credence to this idea.
Supernatural Presence

“The Ink Demon is coming. Hide.”

    Due to his sheer power over every other creature in the Cycle, the Ink Demon’s mere PRESENCE in the area has repeatedly caused a lot of oddities to surroundings and people around the Cycle. If the demon is near, lightbulbs will flicker, weird light flashes will occur,  and people can get odd visions (we’ll go more in depth later).

I’ve mentioned this before, but Bendy’s presence can flood rooms with ink to slow people down, create splotchy ink patterns around him and even cover the person’s sight with ink in the mobile port of BATIM. The closer he gets, the more ink that appears.

Bendy’s mere presence is also shown to affect people’s emotional and mental state. In the first novel, Dreams Come To Life, his presence put staff at heavy unease due to his unnatural, uncanny valley existence, even if he wasn’t hostile or aggressive. As time progressed this presence grew as it evolved from a sense of uneasiness to downright fear. If the Ink Demon is coming after you, your heartbeat starts to pound rapidly, you begin to hear random noises, particularly whispers, your vision becomes black and white, and even strong willed characters become mere cowards and hide for their safety.

Horror Visions

    As shown particularly in the fourth chapter of the first game, Colossal Wonders, Bendy has the capability of making you tweak off the perc.

Jokes aside, Bendy’s presence can cause hallucinations/visions, not just of general scary imagery, like a swinging chandelier or inky arms reaching out towards his victims out of walls, but of important events or events that will occur in the future.

Soul Consumption

“CONSUMED. Death is only the beginning.”

    Due to the inherent nature of ink creatures and their tie to the Cycle, they can never truly die permanently. Unfortunately for them however (or fortunately depending on how you view things), Bendy has a way to circumvent this immortality. In Dark Revival, Bendy can consume the souls of Lost Ones and Audrey, circumventing automatic regeneration entirely, and in Audrey/the player’s case, forcing them to restart from their last autosave.

Other ink creatures seem to also have this ability. The Widow King, the Lurker and Shipahoy Wilson, all Cycle “top tiers.”

Bendy can also consume people purposefully to fuse with them, both of their minds in his body. He did this with Audrey at the end of BATDR, but it left him vulnerable to Audrey taking control and restarting the Cycle at last.

Banishment

    A gift from the ink itself to Audrey Drew, Banish is an ability Audrey used during the events of Bendy and the Dark Revival to banish foes back to the dark puddles they crawled out of. Sneaking up behind the enemy and grabbing them with your special glowy hand activates this ability, banishing foes and replenishing a little health in the process.

While Bendy is never seen using this in BATIM or BATDR, it is an inherent ability of the ink itself, which Bendy seems to have a fine tuned control over. Along with that, with how he and Audrey fused at the end of BATDR, it can be assumed that Bendy has access to this ability now.

It’s unknown how this would work on a person outside of the Cycle or if it’d even work at all due to the ability’s nature.

Flow

    Granted to Audrey by a (GOATED) lost one named Porter and an inherent ability for all ink creatures in the Cycle, Flow allows the user to disappear into inky nothingness and reappear several feet in front of them. While the move has a cooldown of 10 seconds between uses, it’s still incredibly effective for accessing difficult-to-reach areas and stealthy approaches.

Bendy showcases an ability similar to Flow in one of his kill animations in Dark Revival, appearing and disappearing in front of Audrey to kill her.

Similarly, in the final fight with Henry in Chapter 5 of the first game, Beast Bendy is capable of phasing through multiple walls at high speeds without cooldown, implying that he could possibly be exempt from this. In the novels, he also disappeared the moment Buddy opened the Infirmary door before reappearing behind him.
Fast Travel

    The dark puddles have been repeatedly used as a nexus for travel. Bendy and several other Cycledwellers can sink into the ink, reappearing in another puddle in another area of the studio. Referred to as Fast Travel by childish Lost One Heidi when gifting it to Audrey, Bendy has been seen on screen replicating this plenty of times. It also seems to have a cooldown similar to Flow.

While the normal version of the ability in Dark Revival requires the use of linker pipes, Bendy is seemingly not limited by this. Any ink in the studio can be used as a way to traverse the massive realm  and hunt his prey. He’s even capable of hiding them and striking at the right time

The Cycle

"It's a cycle, Audrey. An ink-stained nightmare forever stuck on repeat. With just a pencil and a dream... and with a little help from the Gent Corporation, it came into being."

    A studio existing as a testament to choosing the wrong road, The Cycle is a massive realm coexisting with our own. In its piss-yellow walls, time is an abstract concept that flows in a circle, repeating itself and a set few events. All individuals have very little knowledge of the previous Cycle minus a select few Cyclebreakers. It is an alternate dimension that distorts the form of those who enter it, turning them into monotone creatures of ink, not too dissimilar from Bendy’s old cartoons back in his heyday.

The Cycle is reset by playing a reel called The End and getting the Ink Demon to watch it, which will disintegrate him and start it anew as the same few events begin again. Without the reel, the Cycle is unable to repeat.

The longer a Cycle goes on, the more powerful the Ink Demon seemingly becomes. The length of Audrey’s cycle (along with assumingly the torture inflicted upon the demon by the Keepers) caused the Ink Demon to forcefully evolve, becoming capable of speech and becoming so powerful that barely anything in the studio could harm him.

Each repetition slightly differs from the other. While the events still do play out and the people that died do come back to life, The studio seems to be able to rot and decay, as shown in Dark Revival where the foyer you start in BATIM Chapter 1 was decrepit and rotted.

Those who die while in the Cycle are reincarnated in a new inky form, seemingly in minutes as shown when Sammy Lawrence had a secret voiceline in Chapter 3 of BATIM, seemingly a very short while after his death in the previous chapter. They usually take on the forms they had in their previous lives, but they also have the chance to become utterly lost souls, trapped between the space of our world and the Cycle and unable to go to either.
Army

Searchers

    Searchers are a species of aggressive ink monsters, the most lowly of them all. Basically they’re the goomba or motobug of the Bendy franchise. Created from a bit of ink and a human soul, they like to attack in groups and are capable of travelling through puddles through the studio not too dissimilar from Bendy himself. Although they’re pretty measly, only taking one or two hits to actually kill.

There are multiple types of searchers scattered throughout the studio. Swollen searchers are bulkier searchers made from “extra thick ink.” There are searchers that don’t differ too far from the normal ones minus cool head gear, like the Miner Searcher. There’s also Searcher Minibosses, stronger and faster ones that take more hits to put down and come in groups of 12. They also don’t go away when Henry goes into a Little Miracle Station to hide, they just wait for him to come out. There’s finally the Searcher boss, a tophat donning searcher that’s the strongest, fastest, and most durable out of all the others. Bendy can summon them to help out, but shows no mercy to these creatures, often feasting on them and killing them for his own fun.
Lost Ones

    Lost Ones are also incredibly lowly army members, but they differ in Searchers in the fact that they’re more complex. Not only are they slightly more durable and actually closely resemble humans (for the most part), they’re also capable of complex thought. They’re capable of identity and humanity, being able to form friendships and speak to others. Some are friendly, some are insane and violent, but they all seem to carry weapons on them, of which we covered in the weapons section earlier. Part Two: Common Misconceptions + Clarifications

(BallBeater_1000)

    Bendy may be the goat of all time, but he’s not immune to multiple odd misconceptions about the lore of his series and his ability set. I’m going to run down as many common misconceptions (and believe me, there’s been a TON in this sub and in the versus community over the years) as I can and address them one by one. This will also serve as a section to clarify certain things I think need clarifying. Got all that? Let’s start.
Cartoon Bendy ≠ BATDR Bendy

    A common misconception I’ve seen once or twice but I feel like I should squash really quickly for my sake and yours. There’s been a lot of people equating the metafictional Cartoon Bendy as seen in the posters around the studios and the comics and the, y’know, cartoons, with the adorable Cartoon Bendy seen in BATDR. It is with a heavy heart that I regret to inform you that that is like.. The farthest from the truth.

I don’t really know how this misconception came into being. Maybe it’s the nickname of the BATDR Bendy commonly being Cartoon Bendy…? Anyways yeah, they’re not the same being at all.

The “Cartoon Bendy” as seen in BATDR is a byproduct of the torture done to the Ink Demon by the Keepers, a limiter form that’s docile and friendly, capable of showing a lot more emotions than usual. Notice how none of this has anything to do with the metafictional Cartoon Bendy in universe. Why? Because they aren’t the same characters. They both exist for completely different reasons. BATDR Bendy isn’t capable of Cartoon Bendy feats, or has access to his arsenal, or vice versa. The Bendy verse doesn’t operate on Captain Underpants logic of “If Bendy can do this in a comic, he can do it in real life too!”

In fact, the entire Bendy franchise draws a very VERY defined line between Cartoon Bendy and the Ink Demon. The Ink Demon is a failed recreation of Cartoon Bendy, something he is very insecure about. Cartoon Bendy is basically in a completely separate universe from the Cycle, and most of the full cartoons we know of (minus Tombstone Picnic, but that’s probably unrelated to the Ink Demon) are completely normal, no Ink Demon involved. They are essentially two different characters entirely, and should be treated as such in a versus sense.

So to make a long story short, no, they aren’t the same character, and BATDR Bendy isn’t capable of the same feats Metafictional Bendy is capable of. Sorry, chat.

Bendy Ink vs. Normal Ink

    This is kind of half-misconception, half-clarification, so bear with me here. So there’s been a lot of takes regarding the nature of the ink in Bendy, and over the years, there’s been many people equating the properties of normal ink and applying it with Bendy’s ink. This is.. Kind of a double edged sword, so I figure I should cover the two major ones in detail.
“The Ink can’t resist extreme temperatures!”

    I’ve seen arguments that the Ink cannot resist extreme temperatures, like severe heat or cold. Are they valid? Well.. Sort of…?

The reason for apprehension is a scene in BATDR, mainly during your first confrontation with Alice Angel and solving her little “game.” After you complete it correctly, the table of Lost Ones are electrocuted instead of you. When they get electrocuted, the ink on their bodies does not burn away or show any visible damage. Electricity can cause burning at high enough currents, so this implies that the ink can handle some form of higher temperature. However, electricity doesn’t naturally make things super hot or anything. We still have no idea how the ink reacts to severe cold or even higher heat, like fire. In the end, the nature of the ink in Bendy is too vague to gauge reactions like this.

“Ink creatures can be washed away!”

    This claim is sourced off a quote from Allison in Chapter 5 of BATIM, where she says that she and Tom couldn’t wade through an ink ocean that laid in front of them because they’d get washed away, leaving Henry to leave them behind and end the Ink Demon. The problem is that this idea blatantly misses so much important context in the game and outside of it, it’s kind of crazy it went on for this long.

First of all, this claim seems to originate from the fact that both Allison and Tom are both lesser ink creatures, but they directly mention Henry as “not like them.” We have no idea what they mean by this, as for all we know, they’re pretty much identical, not really having an actual soul and just being ink recreations of old employees, already making this incredibly vague and based off of random assumptions.

Most damningly, you know what was on the other side of the massive ink ocean? The Ink Demon’s lair. I.E, where he seemingly resides in his free time? You could argue he travels there through the walls, but there’s just as much proof that he does do that compared to him just.. Walking through the ink ocean. Which I’m referring to is the fact that there’s zero proof of either in any context.

The only time something comparable to this has ever happened to Bendy himself was in Dreams Come To Life, in which after being drowned in a massive ink puddle, he was mixed and incorporated into it. Pretty solid evidence that he’s vulnerable to this, right?

He regenerated.

Anyways, to make a long story short, this argument relies on only getting half of the context of a full scene and a bucket list of assumptions that could be easily held up to scrutiny with just a bit of thought. And in a certain matchup’s case, believing wholeheartedly that the train of logic you’re using applies to every liquid ever, which is very questionable and relies on even more assumptions without any real evidence.

So to make a long story short, The Ink in Bendy clearly does not have many of the properties seen in normal Ink, making it heavily questionable to assume they operate the same way or equating their properties. But at the same time, we don’t have enough info to fully gauge what the Ink can and can’t do. So if you have to do it, do it, albeit with heavy caution.

How Does Bendy Fight?

    I just wanted to include this section because I feel like a lot of people treat Bendy like some hax god when he really isn’t. I’ve stated it in the weaknesses section, but Bendy doesn’t really do combat strategies. He’s bound by animalistic tendencies and sadism, along with really enjoying playing with his food. This could be exploited by a more skilled fighter.

As for how he fights, period? Well, the Ink Demon, despite the versatility of the ink.. Doesn’t really ever use it offensively. Would he never use it? Probably not, I’ll go into it later, but his go-to combat strategy at all times is overpowering his foes through his sheer overwhelming strength. He can occasionally subdue them to make sure they don’t resist or run away, but he will always try to fight physically first. As you can guess, this doesn’t really fare well if, hypothetically, a foe was stronger than him. He’s very unlikely to go for his hax first.

I can only ever imagine him using his hax if he realizes that physically overpowering a foe is not an option. Only then I can really see him trying to use his ink more. And just for the record, he can’t really generate a ton of ink freely, his presence only creates a small amount.

Also he can’t teleport. I heard this once and I’m still bewildered about it. He’s never teleported.

It’s been improving but we’re still a long way from redemption. The Ink Demon, as heavily implied through the lore of the series and his convincing of Audrey to fuse with him in BATDR, has very deep underlying insecurities about himself, in particular not being the perfect recreation of Bendy. Basically what I’m trying to say is that the Ink Demon is a really cool character and it sucks that a lot of his matchups don’t really go into what makes his character so interesting.

Also I just wanted to note here that his cartoon persona in BATDR is sort of a split personality that both are probably aware of, as implied as how he walked away ashamedly after Audrey saw his alter ego and also how he gathered parts around the studio to make a structural recreation of the Ink Demon in a series of Bendy social media posts.

Novel Canonicity

    This is probably gonna be really complicated, so bear with me here and keep an open mind. The canonicity of the Bendy novels are sort of.. Unique. They’re apocryphal, which by definition is a story/tale of doubtful authenticity, but hailed as true. But what does this even mean in the context of the books themselves? And why did I include them? Good question. Let’s run down both sides of the argument.

Proof that the novels might not be canon
The main proof for the novels not being canon is mainly what Meatly and Mike (the two main creators/developers of Bendy) have said about them. theMeatly has stated in a recent tweet that the books are given the liberties to do whatever they want and that the games are what decide the canon. This is repeated by Mike D., clearly stating that the games are the main canon and the books aren’t. This could possibly mean that novels are only “canon” if the lore from them is backed up in the games, but it’s never been fully specified by either of the two. Well, this is pretty definitive, right? Quite literally the word of god prevails, right?

Well…
Proof that the novels could be a lil canon
Okay, this is where things get a little convoluted. The tweets I mentioned above are rather recent, 2023 and 2024 respectively, but.. It sort of contradicts earlier statements?

Adrienne Kress, the author of most of these Bendy books, back when DCTL was about to release/had recently released, has stated that Dreams Come To Life is canon and was approved by theMeatly, Mike, and Bookpast. Twice. This heavily implies some form of change in direction later on after most of the novels were released due to how recently the canonicity shifted, perhaps due to not wanting to bolted down by abiding by the timeline of the books. There’s also plenty of supporting evidence, mainly how none of the events in the novels really seem to contradict anything stated in the games heavily. But the smoking gun in my eyes is the existence of Buddy Boris, the Boris that accompanies you in Chapter 3 and the one you try to rescue in Chapter 4 of the first game.

As heavily implied (or downright shown, depending on your view) in Dreams Come To Life, the protagonist of that novel, Buddy Lewek, becomes the Boris we meet, roaming the halls of the studio and becoming acquainted as he loses his memory and becomes less like himself and more like Boris. This is nigh confirmed, hence how Henry nicknamed him “Buddy,” how there’s a human like visage in Boris’ safehouse that could be him recreating himself before he lost his memory, and through the ending of Boris and the Dark Survival. Considering all of these novels take place in a connected universe so far, if DCTL is canon, so should all of the others by default.

I’ve decided to include them in the end for these reasons. Even if you’re fully on the side of the word of god, there’s no direct proof or contradictions in their timelines and lore to fully say they aren’t canon. All they do is give us more to work with for the Ink Demon in terms of ability and lore, so there’s no harm in including them.

    So after reading all of this, you may be wondering, “Redscreen, how do I even make a good Ink Demon MU?” I’m glad you asked. VS. Mary (Ib)

(BallBeater_1000)

    Mary is a very underrated MU for the Ink Demon, yet at the same time, I think she gets the recognition she deserves in like.. Her own way? I guess she’s just “rated” or whatever.

Anyways, Mary excels at really interesting connections covering the fact that they’re both abandoned supernatural living artworks  in a different, supernatural realm from ours associated with a liquid used for art (ink and paint), along with miscellaneous connections that are decently thematic and go into their characters really well. As for the fight potential, I like how you can utilize both of their armies to the fullest, and the unique setup of having it be in the Crafting World (Mary’s realm) where Bendy essentially chases down Mary, contaminating the entirety of the realm with the ink it generates.

What I appreciate most about this MU though is that it has a lot of character potential for the Ink Demon. The two are both decently similar with each other and while the Ink Demon is far too arrogant and cocky to sympathize with Mary, I could very well see Mary being able to understand and sympathize with him. The ending could be pretty emotional if she does, I think.

The debate I also find really interesting. Although some could argue Mary usually wins more often than not due to better teamwork and battlefield control, Bendy having way better physicals and more combat experience is nothing to scoff at, along with the possibility of his beast form getting into the mix. Overall, good MU!
VS. Skitzo

(DimEstion)

    Skitzo seems to be one of Bendy’s most popular alts and a generally well-received MU, and for good reason. Now I’ll admit that a long long time ago I wasn’t super into this MU. I thought it was just the evil and messed up version of Cuphead that went nothing into either character and was very surface level. But let’s just say a certain friend of mine who’s name may or may not be above this block of text sold me on it hard to the point it’s one of my favorite alts for the demon.

While the connections and contrasts aren’t super deep or riveting, they excel at their simplicity, with some even covering Bendy’s character, particularly the one where Bendy could’ve turned out good if he was treated better while Skitzo had no shot of ever being a good person. And the fight potential is so hard. Skitzo is basically a one-man army, and the potential for brutality and violence galore is amazing. Of course the banter potential doesn't go too crazy (Skitzo barely even speaks) but treating him as a massive troll, messing around and breaking Bendy cutouts just to tick the Ink Demon off is fun. Kaiju fight with Skitzo’s larger form, suspenseful moments, potential for more Bendy projection, this MU is just a fun time and it’s easy to appreciate over a certain other MU because of that.

The debate is sort of fluctuating for me. I lean towards Skitzo high diff. His durability is nuts and I think that’d give him enough time to absorb Bendy or overwhelm him before he could outhax him with the Ink and get his soul. But the fact that it’s genuinely debatable is very good.

VS. The Blot

    Who IS this fella?? Alright, The Blot is a very, VERY underrated MU for Bendy. Nobody talks about him. He’s the unloved little brother. Why is he even here???? That’s a good question, reader. I think he’s Skitzo 2 due to the simplicity of their connections and core themes (goop monsters from cartoon/cartoon-based video games), but not in a bad way. They both do different things.

The Blot is way more army based. You could have the fight be him and his underlings trying to take over the Cycle and corrupt it, with Bendy and his army (and possibly the entire Cycle as a whole if you give him leeway) fighting back to protect it. It is very, VERY cool. You could have the fight escalate and build on itself into a kaiju fight, with Blot’s absolutely gigantic form dwarfing Bendy’s Beast form. You still even have potential for character development as the Ink Demon is facing a being not too dissimilar from himself, but WAY eviller.

As for the debate, not too sure. I can’t find anything in terms of Blot feats and I’m not sure how Paint and Thinner would affect Bendy’s ink. I feel like this MU’s just waiting to be used for like, a blog or something and revealed to be pretty debatable methinks.
VS. Goji

(DimEstion)

(BallBeater_1000)

    This MU is very controversial. You either like it or hate it. The side I’m on is very obvious. I think this MU is great (no, not because my friend made it, dummy) and I’m very bewildered about the hate for it because I think a lot of it hinges on either Bendy wank or just not watching either series for some reason.

Right off the bat you have a rock solid core theme and ton of shockingly thematic connections that go over the tragedy of both characters and how they became the way they are now, straight up tied with Mary for some of the best connections for a Bendy MU, period, followed by a list of very interesting contrasts that open up a lot of cool banter potential, like how Bendy’s insecurities about his form clash with how Goji prefers his new form over his old one, seeing it as an improvement. And then you get to the fight potential.

Right off the bat you have a very fun monster fight with a lot of cool environmental usage. Emphasizing Goji’s bulkiness by making him this physically imposing force to be reckoned with compared to Bendy (which is fair because I think he’s stronger than him in base due to the weight of the Godzilla suit) that smashes him through floors and walls. You can have Bendy use his more hax based abilities once he finds out that overpowering Goji isn’t gonna work out for him.

You can have an army fight with the other suits and Bendy’s usual minions as a neat background set piece, having Anguirus roll into a ball and flatten Lost Ones and whatnot as the battle escalates. You can have a Godzilla movie inspired kaiju fight with Beast Bendy and Goji in the Lost City due to his size manipulation where they shove trees down each other's throats and destroy the buildings around them, with a very dramatic climax where Goji uses his atomic blood vomit and whittles away Bendy’s ink until he only has a small stockpile left to use.

I already mentioned banter, but they’re both god complex monsters with a tragic side so you have a lot of mileage. Both of them talking smack to the other, Goji being his usual American-hating self, yadda yadda yadda. Please read Ballsy’s megapost, the MU is so cool and I do not understand the hate for it. VS. Springtrap

    Oh, now we’re getting good. Now, it’s no surprise that Springtrap is my absolute favorite option for the Ink Demon. Why? Glad you asked.

Right off the bat, while the connections between the 2 aren’t super crazy or anything, the contrasts are what make this MU. A tragic creation that rules over a looping purgatory vs. A hateable creator that got trapped in a looping purgatory. Afton is basically a living representation of Joey Drew in a way, and that instantly makes way for insane character potential for the Ink Demon as he’s forced to confront his past in full instead of skirting around it. This also allows the chance to show off Afton’s evilness in full, along with his fear of death and whatnot. These two MUs easily utilize their characters better than a lot of their other MUs, which I adore.

The fight potential is great, allowing for both a fun monster fight that allows their abilities and arsenal to clash in fun ways. You could frame it as a rapidly escalating game of cat and mouse, both of them taunting each other for the failures and their pasts with Bendy’s telepathy and Afton’s illusion discs and Phantom Animatronics. An army fight with Bendy’s minions and Afton’s Phantom and Twisted Animatronics, hell, even a Kaiju fight with Beast Bendy and the Afton Amalgamation, the potential is great.

The banter and story potential, as hinted at before, goes hard. You can have Afton’s boastful intelligence clash with the Ink Demon’s more animalistic sadism, and once again, I must reiterate, Bendy is literally fighting a physical representation of the man who made him the way he is. Imagine the character moments and development you could get from that. What if Bendy quite literally overcomes it and accepts himself for the way he is at the end? That would be amazing. Debate’s great too, really coming down to interpretation of how souls work in both universes. Just an amazing MU all around. There’s a reason why this is my favorite for both.
VS. Cuphead (What does it do wrong?)

    The year is now. I am explaining why I dislike Bendyhead for the third time publicly. Don’t worry, I won’t be long. I want you all to read my debunk/critique first before reading this section since I don’t want to reexplain myself for like, the zillionth time. I’ll just TLDR.

Basically, what Bendyhead does wrong is really piss poor unthematic connections that either range from being meta, odd comparisons, or the biggest stretches you’ll ever see just to make a forced legacy MU work. It also requires you to soft-composite Cartoon Bendy and Ink Demon, which as established, doesn’t work for his character and is arguably kind of bad in a versus sense due to how the two are constantly treated as two different characters entirely.

The fight potential barely works due to both really bad verse equalization and a very weak ability clash due to the different ways the two characters fight (Closed range physical force vs. Ranged keepaway) and even at its best, it’s boring and very uninspired with little substance beyond toon force shenanigans. The banter potential doesn’t fare much better. The cartoon Bendy is practically the same character as Cuphead, making banter very boring, and the Ink Demon would tonally clash hard with Cuphead, not really allowing you any comedic potential on Cuphead’s end because you have to take him seriously.

And the debate is just a boring Cuphead stomp. So yeah.

I’ve stated this before, but I think this MU is just Cuphead ft. Bendy. Cuphead is the protagonist. He gets the triumphant one up over Bendy. Hell, even the BRANDON YATES track is more Cuphead-heavy and barely uses any Bendy motifs. If that isn’t a funny coincidence I don’t know what is. So overall, this MU is a big stinky because it underutilizes Ink Demon to an absurd degree while not representing him properly.
So how do I make a good Bendy MU?
I thought I’d come to some rocket science conclusion or concurrent variable that all of the Bendy MUs I like have, but no. It really all comes down to just understanding his series and doing what you usually do to make good MUs. I hope this guide and this blog helps you make lots of good MUs for him because GOD does he need it.
