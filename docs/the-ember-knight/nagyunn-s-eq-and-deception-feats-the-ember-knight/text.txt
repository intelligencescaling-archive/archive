Nagyunn vs SCD character (EQ/Deception)

EQ

When he faces the Dokkaebi, whose ability amplifies emotions like hostility, fear, rage, excitement, and murderous intent, the power completely fails. The story states this directly: the ability requires hostility to activate, and Nagyunn has none. Even as an ordinary human standing before an overwhelmingly powerful being, he does not feel hatred, rage, or fear toward the Dokkaebi. His mind is focused on only one thing — finding a way out. With no emotional hostility to amplify, the Dokkaebi has nothing to work with. As the Dokkaebi himself realizes, multiplying zero by anything still results in zero. This doesn’t mean Nagyunn is emotionless. It means that in this exact situation, he maintains absolute control and never allows his emotions to turn into aggression or loss of composure, making him a natural hard counter to emotional amplification.

That level of control goes so far that even the Narin — a conceptual weapon that detects genuine will and intent — is fooled by him. Nagyunn alters his own intent completely, fabricating a false “will to swing” without applying any actual force. Even an artifact that transcends physical law and reads true intent cannot tell the difference. He isn’t just hiding his will — he’s rewriting it.

He can also manipulate emotional signals so perfectly that lies become indistinguishable from truth. Normally, people give themselves away through pulse changes, micro-expressions, and involuntary movements. Nagyunn can control those tells at will. He can cry convincingly, project pressure, and radiate emotion so realistically that others genuinely believe it. Because of this, people assumed he had grown stronger — not because his power increased, but because he made them feel like it had. The stronger and more sensitive the opponent, the more cautious they become around him. Even the gate guard, whose perception was partially blocked but still relied on emotional sensing, felt Nagyunn’s emotions as completely real. His fabricated emotional state was indistinguishable from the genuine thing.

His ability to read emotions is just as extreme. While normal people perceive emotions in broad, simplified categories, Nagyunn can distinguish them with surgical precision — wariness, worry, fear, anxiety, panic, excitement, tension, chaos, confusion, surprise, admiration, interest, anger. He doesn’t just know that someone is afraid. He knows exactly what kind of fear it is, where it comes from, and how close it is to turning into action.

More than that, he can perceive these emotional states from multiple people simultaneously. He doesn’t process emotions one target at a time — he reads overlapping emotional signals across an entire group at once, separating and tracking them in real time. This allows him to understand group dynamics instantly, anticipate collective reactions, and predict individual actions even in chaotic, high-pressure situations.

During his spar with Yulnia, he read her emotions so clearly that he knew exactly when she was about to attack. Even though he didn’t block every hit, he still controlled the entire flow of the fight. He stayed calm throughout, completely hiding what he was actually feeling. Everyone assumed he was holding back because he might accidentally kill her, when in reality, he was much weaker at the time.

He was also able to impersonate his twin brother flawlessly — not just in speech and behavior, but in emotional expression. No one noticed. He had to constantly adapt because he lacked the natural combat talent of his younger brother, who was known as a prodigy.

When Myusa was overwhelmed by fear, Nagyunn didn’t dismiss or deny it. He acknowledged it as real and reframed it as something meaningful. He told him that fear is what keeps everyone alive, and that courage isn’t the absence of fear, but moving forward despite it. In doing so, he shifted Myusa’s self-image from being a burden to being the most courageous one there, restoring his confidence and emotional strength.

Even when fighting Luce, after taking a blow to the arm so severe it felt like it would be torn off, Nagyunn showed no pain or panic. Instead, he taunted Luce. That reaction made Luce’s anger spike and his next move predictable, allowing Nagyunn to read him perfectly.

Deception for Nagyunn starts at the absolute highest level: perfect emotional control. Most liars are exposed by involuntary tells like micro movements, pulse changes, or subtle tension. Nagyunn controls those signals consciously. He can simulate emotions such as killing intent, pain, fear, or sincerity so precisely that even highly perceptive opponents register them as completely real. His fabricated emotions are indistinguishable from genuine ones. This goes so far that even Narin, a conceptual weapon designed to detect true will itself, is deceived. Nagyunn creates a false intent to strike without applying force, successfully fooling a weapon that exists specifically to sense genuine intent.

His deception is not limited to emotion, but extends into constructing full false realities and maintaining them under extreme pressure. When he discovers that one of his allies is a spy, he chooses a far riskier path and pretends to be that spy himself, deceiving both sides at once. He manipulates Naryun’s perception by using what Naryun can physically see, especially the sword in his hand, as proof of his fabricated identity. His lies are never direct. They are built from carefully mixed truth and falsehood, vague answers, and missing details that force the enemy to complete the story on their own. Because the enemy supplies the logic themselves, the deception becomes internally consistent and impossible to disprove. This manipulation allows Nagyunn to protect the entire group and maintain his cover for three full days until the knights arrive.

His acting ability allows him to assume completely different identities, adjusting posture, speech patterns, tone, and emotional expression to match the role perfectly. He disguised himself as Najin, his stronger twin brother, so convincingly that even people close to them never noticed. He posed as a spy of the Dragon’s Descendants to misdirect Naryun. He infiltrated the Eastern Continent by pretending to be Naryun’s own disciple. He even disguised himself as Waron to pass Zius’s test without raising suspicion. These disguises are not surface level. They are sustained, reactive, and emotionally accurate under scrutiny.

He is also an exceptional bluffer, capable of overwhelming opponents far stronger than himself through pure psychological pressure. Despite being physically weaker, his composure, confidence, body language, and tone create the illusion of absolute control and hidden power. He forced hesitation from the Pure White Elephant by threatening to burn down a village. He intimidated an entire group of knight apprentices into attacking him all at once by projecting overwhelming presence. He bluffed Waron by claiming he had a countermeasure ready at the exact moment she was about to kill him. He humiliated Kallan by pretending to accept her contempt, acting as though he was helpless without weapons, then calmly stating he could defeat her barehanded and proving it by reading and countering every move without injuring her.

These are his major feats, presented in a broad and concise way. You can ask me for more details if you want to explore any specific aspect more deeply.
