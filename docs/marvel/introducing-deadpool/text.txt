Introducing: Deadpool.

As requested. The idea of this post is to present some of the feats of Wade W. Wilson, aka Deadpool, the Merc with a Mouth. His feats in practically every category and subcategory have a clear methodology, but many of his feats **are still unrealistic** or impossible in real life, so I won't go into unnecessary depth.
Here I'll list some feats—not all of them, nor necessarily the best ones, but the easiest ones for introducing the character. Without further ado, I hope you enjoy the post.

**General Intelligence (GI):**

* Gs, Gwm, Gv: He mentally solved a puzzle of hundreds or thousands of pieces in a second, after glancing at the disorganized pieces (*Deadpool Vol.2 #41*).
* Gv, Gwm, Glr: He has a mind palace that appears to be an art gallery and is completely organized, allowing him to walk the halls to retrieve his memories of every lived experience through the artworks with memories associated with them. Inside, it has rooms that store his remembrances as eternally accessible and perfectly preserved recordings (*Despicable Deadpool #300*).
* Gf: He learned on the fly how an advanced war suit created by Weasel works, which requires a massive level of induction and abstract modeling of unknown complex systems (*Deadpool Vol.2 #23*).
* Gc, Gp: Wade belonged to the special forces, where he received training in covert operations, infiltration, and handling of all kinds of weapons. He is an expert in multiple martial arts and is fluent in English, Esperanto, German, Japanese, Russian, Spanish, Symkarian, and even American Sign Language. In addition, he possesses ridiculous knowledge of pop culture (*multiple comics*).

**Emotional Intelligence (EI):**

* EP: He smelled Blind Al and perceived her fake indignation, anger, and her attempt to throw Wade off with a piece of advice, discovering that she was so nervous because she had brought the mailman to try to help her escape her confinement (*Deadpool Vol.1 #12*).
* EF: When facing Crossbones, he decides to start using his anger as a literal enhancer to feel invincible and attack more brutally. Later, when he sees Ellie on the battlefield, he uses his paternal instinct to fight more effectively, "with a clarity he hadn't had in years" (*Deadpool Vol.8 #03*).
* EC: He managed to "observe" Hit-Monkey's hallucination about a man in a suit who served as his mentor, understanding and explaining not only the origin and evolution of the trauma, but also the monkey's state, as well as his desire for revenge, confusion, and existential helplessness (*Deadpool Vol.2 #61*).
* ER: He saved a girl named Danielle, who was about to jump from a building. Instead of trying to save her with his chaos, he takes her for a walk, entertains her with his missions, and at the end of the comic, admits that he isn't the right person to help her and takes her to a hospital. Wade acknowledges his limitations and tempers his need for control and his natural ego in an act of humility that promotes Danielle's growth (*Deadpool Vol.4 #20*).

**Manipulation:**

* Indirect, emotional, psychological: He induces a Skrull army general to accept his fake proposal to work for them. He achieves this by instilling fear in him—shooting down his ship, making him watch as he defeated his soldiers (including a Super-Skrull), and having him at his mercy, on the verge of assassinating him. Wade makes him believe the whole time that he is going to kill him, when in reality he was giving a demonstration of his ability to make his offer irresistible. Moreover, by having the general on his knees and not killing him, he makes them feel that they are vastly inferior to him and that they truly need him to win the war against Earth (*Deadpool Vol.2 #01*).
* Indirect, logical: He manipulates Norman Osborn through the Thunderbolts. Knowing that Osborn won't allow him to stay alive (since he holds an important secret), Deadpool seeks out Norman to collect his debt, causing him to send his Thunderbolts to hunt him down. After they fail multiple times, Osborn himself shows up at the moment they catch him, but Wade had provoked him into doing this in order to free himself and steal the gold card from him during a brief physical confrontation (*Thunderbolts Vol.1 #131*).

**Deception:**

* Acting skills, misdirection: He heads to Utopia Island to request joining the X-Men, but they reject him. In an attempt to prevent Wade from doing something crazy, Cyclops accepts Deadpool's demands and gives him an X-Men uniform that proves he's part of the team, but Wade tells Domino that he's going to kill a man who is giving mutants a bad image in order to help the team. Obviously this would produce the opposite effect, so the X-Men begin a desperate race to stop Wilson before he kills him, while he harasses the man all over California. The public believes the mutants are murderers, but it turns out this was planned by Deadpool, who in the end managed to get the X-Men to defeat him in front of the cameras and save the man, simultaneously declaring that Wade doesn't belong to the team. This cleans up their image and wins over the masses, just as he foresaw (*Deadpool Vol.2 #15–18*).
* Fabrication, misdirection, concealment: Logan asks Wade for help to defeat Romulus, an enemy who had orchestrated the former's life and knew him inside out, so he anticipated all his ideas. An enemy who, moreover, neither Logan nor his son could kill. Wilson's plan was to make Romulus believe that Logan had asked someone else for help to devise the plan, so that he (Romulus) couldn't predict it. Thus, Romulus embarked on a tireless search for the truth through a set of undecipherable clues left by Logan (following the scheme), until he blackmailed Ruby Thursday into revealing it to him. She tells him, so Romulus discovers what he already suspected, but Deadpool had planned Ruby's betrayal to give Romulus this information, since the real plan was another one. To finish deceiving him, Wade instructed Logan to ask Daken (his son, who hates him) for help and reveal the fake plan to him, knowing that Romulus would go to Daken to ask him whether the plan he heard from Ruby was true. Daken honestly tells him yes, since he believes the lie. Finally, Romulus falls into the traps of the real plan, in which Logan used a replica of the Muramasa sword to simulate Daken's death; and afterwards, as Wade planned, Logan kidnaps Romulus and leaves him trapped in Cloak's Dark Dimension, out of everyone's reach in an unknown place (*Wolverine Origins #40–48)*.

**Scheming:**

* Effectiveness, structure, integration, versatility: Deadpool wants a dose of a serum capable of killing him, but since he doesn't know who manufactured it, he devises a master plan so that other factions interested in the serum find it for him. He made X-Force believe that the serum nullified mutations and was in the hands of Wilson Fisk, the Kingpin, so that they would investigate on their own. He asked Daken to look for it to kill him, but he knew Daken wouldn't look for it for that reason, but rather to try to nullify Logan's mutation and kill him. Either way, he would find the serum and Deadpool would get to it. He also used Bob from Hydra to inform Kingpin and Tombstone about the serum, managing to make the former move heaven and earth to get it, since it would be a lethal asset in the hands of Shadowland, his empire. Since Kingpin was after the serum, the X-Men believed he was actually covering his tracks, just as Deadpool planned. He also invented a fake gang war to attract the attention of New York's superheroes, creating a smoke screen for X-Force's actions. For his part, Tombstone (who had ordered the serum to be created) orders more doses to be made when he discovers that Deadpool is still alive, and gives it to Bob to use on him. Daken also steals some doses to use them on Logan, and in the end, Wade created so much chaos that no matter what, someone would find the serum and put it within his reach, managing to get Bob to use it on him upon seeing his total lack of control, in an attempt to stop him. The end of the plan is the most twisted part: Deadpool knew that when the truth was discovered, it would enrage X-Force and many more people, and they would go after him to kill him, making his future death a huge spectacle, just as he wanted and believed he deserved for himself (*Deadpool Vol.2 #50–54*).

**Foresight:**

* Logical prediction: When he was hired to kill Logan, Wade devised an intricate plan in which he predicted every one of Logan's actions—such as which attacks he would use, where he would run, what he would do, etc.—as well as why. The timing precision is so ridiculous that at one point, Wilson sets a planned trap and predicts that they will meet in about 20 minutes, and exactly after that they see each other again. Deadpool textually declares that "he knows what to do to make him do what he did, and to deceive himself" (*Wolverine Origins #21–25*).
* Anticipation: Basically any of the plans already mentioned in previous categories always involves a level of anticipation of possibilities that is almost ridiculous.

The analysis of almost everything here is purely superficial, since his plans, deceptions, and manipulations are **infinitely more complex and extensive** (no counting everything I left out) ut I don't want to make this introduction longer than necessary. I hope you enjoyed the post and that Deadpool impressed you, since he really is an incredible character when it comes to outsmarting—he almost always faces extreme adversities in terms of both resources and opposition, but even so, he devises highly efficient strategies with enviable strength. Unfortunately, being a Marvel Comics character who isn't popular for his tactical prowess, these aspects often go unnoticed, but in my opinion he is easily above the duo from Death Note, the one from Moriarty the Patriot, the one from The Mentalist, and many others. That said, I consider this post concluded. Thanks for reading, until next time!
