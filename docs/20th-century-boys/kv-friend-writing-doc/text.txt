This is by no means a comprehensive doc; I will only cover the most important points and symbolisms. I doubt a truly comprehensive doc will be made any time soon though. Also please read the manga before reading this analysis.

I will list most of the symbolisms that Friend has. However, I only analyze symbolisms if they are good ones. A huge amount of people in the writing community seem to think that having references to biblical or classical figures somehow makes that character's complexity, depth, symbolism, or writing better. IT DOES NOT. Only when the symbolism makes sense, fits in with evidence from the text, parallels the character well, and most of the time even adds something to the character (like a new pov) does it increase the rating of that character, otherwise it is just a surface level symbolism. I have seen plenty of characters (like Johan) be plastered with 50 different characters (which are all either religious figures or from classical texts) and have that be the foundation for their complexity or depth. I don’t at all like it. This also goes for ideology and philosophy; just because a philosophy is sort of adjacent to the character doesn’t mean that the character symbolizes it. In my opinion this is by far the most grueling aspect about writing comparisons, along with the “ah, so you think you can scale the character? Please list to me their 4 layers of depth, and if it doesn’t perfectly align with my view then it’s automatically wrong” (granted I’ve been guilty of doing similar things, partially the reason I’m making this analysis).

This doc won’t be covering OG or PE friend, this analysis is ONLY on KV Friend. The other two deserve completely separate docs.  In my opinion, KV is far superior to the two others.

This doc might also focus on Kenji and some other aspects of the story because they’re either integral to Friend or I just wanted to include them to show how good 20th Century Boys is. (I’ll try to not include too many of those types of segments).

I wish I could do a full manga analysis, however I don’t have the time. This doc would be about ten times longer if I had to analyze everything relevant.

Quick Section on the Friend symbol:

Here is the most likely familiar Friend symbol. It is shown all throughout the manga and especially so during the end of the world arc.
First thing we observe is that it’s a mask. This can tell us about a hundred things about Friend, however we won’t reach that part for a while yet. For now it’s best described as a symbol, a symbol for something. Similar to how V from V for Vendetta wears a mask to be portrayed as a symbol, Friend does the same thing. He wants to be anonymous, he wants to establish a sense that everyone and anyone is Friend. It’s similar to how Jesus is very sparingly described in appearance in the Bible, or how Muhammad’s face has to be covered in art as well. This is to make a connection between the person and the figure; to make it so anyone can relate to them.
As said earlier, this could also be a sign that Friend is afraid of showing his face.
The second observation we notice about it is the fact that it’s black and white. Black is the colour used to draw the symbol and white fills the rest of the mask. The white is symbolism for heaven or virtue, the black is symbolic of evil. It is a metaphor for a false prophet/god. Satan in eden.
The outermost rim of the symbol is the print of an eye. This is supported by the pupil in the middle. The eye is full of eyelashes on all sides which look like thorns, possibly a nod to Katsumata’s protective nature regarding his past. This is done so on both the eyes (exterior and interior), showing Katsumata’s thoroughness which we’ll see in the rest of the series going forward.
The eye is painted everywhere in the series, rarely will you go a chapter without seeing it at least a few times. It is meant to show that Friend is always watching, every event was done because he allowed it to happen. Friend sees everything and everyone.
The last part that we notice is the giant hand pointing up, encompassing the pupil of the eye. The hand is pointing to heaven or god, symbolizing and solidifying Friend as a god/prophet and his committee as divine.
Put this all together and you get a terrifying message: Friend is god, looking down at you, seeing every action you take no matter how you hide it, conform to the regime. On the other hand, it is a safety for those who are in the regime, Friend watches every action; he will reward the righteous and dismember the impure.

Surface level analysis will tell you that this is just Friend furthering his plan on indoctrinating the cult members by spitting more drivel about some higher state electing new friends. However, the interesting part about this, and even possibly about the Friend cult altogether is whether Friend actually believes in this. It’s a question which I've always posed when researching about cults: does the leader actually believe in whatever they’re preaching or is it just an elaborate hoax to get more members?
In the case of Friend this question is incredibly incredibly complex. It’s also not a question that we can answer right now, so we will come back to it a bit later, but do keep it in the back of your head when you read this as it will be important later.
Back to the main topic. Friend references “the cosmos” as the entity which chooses friends and presumably saves them, giving them some kind of salvation. What is the cosmos? It’s most likely not Friend himself, or at least not directly so. Yet, it does seem strange that it references the word “friends”. Obviously Friend could just be saying that word to further increase that name’s theme (which I have discussed briefly but I promise that there will be a more in depth analysis on that as well). However, I do not think that the friend theme is the only thing the line is referencing. Cosmos can be interpreted as “god”; this cosmos elects “friends”; Friend is a prophet/god, similar in status to Jesus; this gives us a sort of dichotomy, whether Friend sees himself as a prophet or a god but evidence seems to suggest that he sees himself as more a prophet which is just related to god through that. From this we can deduce that Friend sees himself as an almost reincarnation of a greater being. Yet, he hopes to pass on this power as the line clearly states that there will be multiple friends. If you still don’t understand what I'm referencing then just take a look at rebirth plan or the Friend Era. Yes, I believe that this line is directly referencing the Friend Era (yes, we’re skipping around in continuity a bit). During the Friend Era there were multiple Friends who ruled different parts of the world, I believe that a few of those Friends are likely from the very cult circle that you just saw. First of all, this is insane levels of foreshadowing; Usogui and One Piece type foreshadowing. Second of all, we get a new look into Friend’s psyche, and this is where his characterization reaches new heights. For first readers this would actually come out sort of selfless in a way. I mean……Friend is just allowing people to take on a position similar to his with no restraints or curfews. Isn’t it actually pretty noble in a sense? In like a deranged psycho killer type of way?
Well…no, quite the opposite in all likeliness. We know that Friend is selfish, extremely so. He seeks constant praise and attention from all, he is the embodiment of self-centredness (partially the reason he started the cult in the first place). He certainly wouldn’t allow anyone to be equal to him. Two conclusions can be made from this fact. A) He will ascend the title of Friend in some way. B) He doesn’t consider multiple Friends to be that at all; all Friend’s are extensions of himself, if a stunt double does something then that basically means he did it, that is how delusional he has become. For option A, we also have to consider a VERY crucial fact. Friend is currently Fukubei, not Katsumata. Katsumata doesn’t care about Fukubei, actually he hates him quite a bit [for reasons that will be discussed later]. If Fukubei was seen as just another Friend among the crowd, Katsumata would be completely okay with that, in fact he’d like that very much. Katsumata does want attention but from his actions we can tell that he’d be perfectly content with just being labeled “Friend”, he doesn’t need a name, at least not yet.
This sentence also provides us with some insight on Friend’s ideology. Friend is a strong believer of fatalism, at least as far as the cult is concerned. He references the cosmos, which is most likely a reference to god, sure, but it also could be that Friend thinks of the cosmos as something which has already determined the events of the universe. This is where we get our first signs of Friend subscribing to compatibilism. Compatibilism is the belief that humans have free will, but reality is already determined. We don’t see the latter part be relevant or brought up until much much later, however we do see the notion of predetermined reality pretty early on: “the world will end soon”, “there will be no survivors”, etc etc. This will be further expanded on when we discuss the book of prophecy, both old and new. For now, let’s move on.

Friend’s masks:

Finally at the point where I can talk about them more freely. If you remember I did a little segment on the Friend mask at the beginning of this analysis (Friend Symbol), but that was more-so suited for the Friend “symbol”, I focused much less on the mask aspect of it.
Here’s the mask analysis:
First mask: The first mask is the typical Friend mask. I have already gone over its symbolic potency so I’ll instead focus on the one who wears it. The mask is worn by Katsumata and Fukubei. Both wear it as a protection method, literally hiding their eyes from the real world, thus the reason why only the two of them wear it. They believe in the religion they are fostering (at least Fukubei does), and both made “Friend” possible. They only start wearing the mask in their adult years; it’s meant to symbolize the start of the dreaded question they pose to Kenji.
Second mask: The second mask is the Hattori mask. Sadakiyo[?] and Fukubei wear it. Some very credible sources list Sadakiyo as a wearer of the mask, I am more skeptical. The foundation of this idea comes from Kenji’s memory. Back in the first volume when Kenji remembers Sadakiyo, he pictures him with a Hattori mask on, running alongside him (adjacent to him is more accurate). I don’t think this is Sadakiyo. Sadakiyo has never been seen with this mask. In fact, when Fukubei and Sadakiyo went to the hanging hills, Fukubei didn’t give Sadakiyo the Hattori mask. I think it’s more likely that the person Kenji remembered was Fukubei. The Hattori mask is from the hit anime series  “Ninja Hattori-Kun”. I have never watched it, so I’m basing some of this based on the synopsis of it. Ninja Hattori lives in a society of non-Ninjas, it often causes wacky mishaps to happen because he can’t understand the social norms. This is very similar to Fukubei, I’m sure I don’t need to explain why, just go to the PV section and see for yourself; he is a messed up person, not understanding limits and social dynamics. This is where the parallels stop and the satire begins. Hattori the ninja is an incredibly kind and caring person, Fukubei is the opposite of that. There’s not a single scene where Fukubei is nice to anyone, he hates everyone [even himself!]. The mask represents Fukubei’s close ties to childhood (and Katsumata by extension). Why does Fukubei call it his favourite mask? A) Hattori is speaking, of course the Hattori mask, the one which captures his essence, would be his favourite. B) It’s the mask which most resembles his childhood. C) The mask has the least negative experiences associated with it [Eg. Friend mask was created by Kenji (that’s what Fukubei thinks at least). Sadakiyo’s mask reminds Katsumata of the time he got beat up and of the Hanging Hill incident]. Take note of the fact that every time Fukubei shows up around Kenji, he is wearing the Hattori mask, not the Friend mask. It is to both mock him with the fact that he is Hattori and to remind Kenji of his childhood anime. He spreads the Friend symbol for similar reasons. It also apparently relates to a Japanese samurai who was the greatest at everything, relating to Fukubei’s desire to do the same thing.
Third mask: The third mask is the Sadakiyo mask. Its notable wearers include Sadakiyo and Katsumata. It’s supposed to be a mask of Ultraman (I think); that’s why the cover art is so symbolic for Sadakiyo. The Kenji faction does the Ultraman pose in the cover art for 20th Century Boys, so Sadakiyo wears an Ultraman mask to try and make friends with them. It is also the only mask of the three to have its eyes free (holes in the eye sockets of the mask), symbolizing how Sadakiyo is able to see the real world unlike Fukubei (yes, Fukubei did wear it but no notable moments occurred with it. In fact, when he did wear it, it was ironically taken off by Kenji.). So why do I think Katsumata wears it, isn’t Fukubei the one who wears it? No, I don’t think so. (Here), this is the scene where “Fukubei” borrows it from Sadakiyo. Fukubei’s character wouldn’t take off his mask, he never does unless forced to by Kenji. This has to be Katsumata. Katsumata takes off his mask because “it’s too hot”; his brain is too hot, it is overheating with information and questions. He sees himself in the mirror, he notices something strange, that he can see the real world, in an act of desperation and self gratification, he takes off the mask completely, exposing himself to the real world. But that doesn’t go to plan; once he takes it off he is again reminded that he isn’t ready for the real world, he is nobody. So what does he do? He goes back into Fukubei and remains dormant until a while longer, until the very end in fact: 1973.
This mask is the only one which is never used by Friend, outside of his earlier years. Mainly because Friend can’t see the real world again, his urges might act up again. It’s rare that Katsumata wears it, but when he does it's always for an important occasion. It also connects to Sadakiyo but we’ll discuss that later.
There are in total 3 masks. Katsumata by extent wears all of them. There are about a thousand different things you could use that for.

Fukubei hates being called “Child A” because it puts a number on him. It contains him and it normalizes him, he is just another child. The worst case scenario for a narcissist. If people will give his situation attention then at least let the people know it’s him. As Fukubei, Katsumata feels superhuman, he really can bend spoons. But people call him a fraudster and liar, Fukubei can’t handle that. Fukubei will destroy the entire world, he will take down everyone who called him a liar, they deserve it since they’re hypocrites. They so desperately wanted to take him down, so why not do the same to them? ‘Fukubei isn’t a fraud, but even if he was, doesn’t that make everyone else a fraud too?’, Katsumata thinks. ‘This isn’t just an attack on Fukubei, it’s an attack on me as well’. This isn’t just. ‘I will kill everyone and rule the world, making sure to make everyone as miserable as possible before killing them’. ‘Then, then the only person left will be me…’. ‘The only thing worse than lying to others is lying to myself’, ‘I’ll kill Fukubei then, to relieve him of his pain, and to reinstate me as the true Friend’. ‘After that…after the world and Fukubei are dead…there’s only one thing left to do.’. This is the first scenario, if the Holy Mother descent is not smooth, this will be the end. The first ending will be a doomsday.
Quick bit on the “superhuman” part: it’s actually a closely related concept to Nietszche’s Ubermensch/Superman. Friend’s whole persona tries to be an Ubermensch. He goes by his own morals as demonstrated by his “There is no evil or good/ In my universe.” quote. 21st Century Boys

(I will be using both the original and perfect edition version of 21st Century Boys. Mostly the original though. Viz translations are weird.)
Before we get into the chapter contents of 21st Century Boys, we have to analyze the title and volume.
21st Century Boys is simple but effective foreshadowing and overview of the events that will happen in this manga. 20th Century Boys is all about being stuck in the 20th century. Fukubei and Katsumata are stuck there all their lives. Kenji was stuck in the 20th century for a long time as well (only relieving himself of it after his 3 days rejuvenation in the mountains). You can extend this phrase to most people in the manga, especially Kanna and those who were friends with Kenji. The move to the 21st century suggests development and acceptance. A conclusion has to happen if this is the title. Old perspectives will be shattered and new ones will be sown.
The 上 character is used for the volume number. I’m not an expert on Japanese. I know a tiny bit of Chinese, based off that knowledge this is either referring to heaven/god (such as in 天上 or 上帝). It could also be the word for “start”. I think “god” is more symbolic but “start” is probably the author’s intent, or maybe it’s both.
Little warning before we start. 21st Century Boys utilizes Tomodachi Land’s virtual reality a ton. Every flashback utilizes the VR, they are not actual flashbacks. This is never confirmed but the way the manga is laid out makes it fairly apparent. The VR world, while being perfect in terms of visuals, doesn’t have to be perfect in terms of character moments. In the VR world, Katsumata and Fukubei are two separate people. The characters there, with some small exceptions, are programmed AI. However, even though they are AI, all of them were at one point made by Katsumata. Consider everyone as an extension of Katsumata and his wants/needs; the VR world is Katsumata.

Now, for the meaning behind all of this.

I’vealready explained the reason for the suicide jump so we’ll skip that part. Why does Katsumata change his mind about committing suicide when he listens to 20th Century Boys?
I don’t know. We don’t know. And I think that’s part of the magic of what makes this scene so great. Perhaps we will never know why he jumped down.
I’ve mentioned that 20th Century Boys is the song of “Invincible justice”, and while that is partially true, it doesn’t tell the whole story.
20th Century Boys is the song of justice for Kenji. Kenji cares about justice, you could even say it’s integral to who he is as a character. But Katsumata isn’t the same. Not just Katsumata, no one is the same.
The song is played numerous times for the world to hear, such as the time the robot was heading to destroy the secret base or the time Kanna had to get everyone inspired to go to the expo.
It doesn’t mean justice for everyone. It’s more akin to hope, but even that isn’t a complete thought.
Hope comes in many forms and shapes. 20th Century Boys is the catalyst for your own hope. For some people that hope might be that they’ll be able to eat steak, for some it might be a reassurance that justice is what they’re fighting for, and for others it’s a reminder that at the end of the tunnel, they will prevail.
Katsumata has his own purpose too, something worth fighting for. Katsumata has just buried that desire so deeply that now it’s nearly impossible to excavate. The only thing able to rekindle his passion is 20th Century Boys. I’m sure even he doesn’t know why it does. There’s a good chance he has no idea why he didn’t commit suicide then and there. It’s a feeling that can’t be manifested into words.
Katsumata, inspired by 20th Century Boys, gets enough courage to confront Kenji about it.
He says he doesn’t have any plans for tomorrow, and that he hasn’t had any for a while. It’s very depressing listening to this. Katsumata’s life has no meaning for him. He has nothing he wants to do. The hanging hill incident, the hanging of 71’, the spoon bending incident, the rumor spreading, etc etc; it had no meaning for him in the grand scheme of things, they were all small distractions from a much larger existential crisis.
He asks if Kenji can be his friend.
Kenji says sure, but a friend isn’t something that you make by asking. Once again relating to this theme of hope and the soul being more important than the brain (think about 20th Century Boys motif for example).
Katsumata walks away. I don’t think he’s necessarily upset or mad, just confused, perplexed.
Kenji stops him and explains his incorrect version of events. Katsumata probably isn’t too happy about Kenji getting the mystery wrong. This does put into question how conscious this Katsumata is. In my opinion Katsumata made his young version the most conscious out of all, so he could most resemble real life Katsumata. It’s not unlikely that this is Katsumata himself in the VR.
Katsumata leaves to think about everythings that's happened once more.
At this point, he knows that the anti-proton bomb has failed, but that was never his true goal. One of his goals has already been completed; both adult and child Kenji admitted their mistakes and apologized to Katsumata [and Sadakiyo]. This relates back to compatibility. Katsumata knows that the outcomes of destiny are certain, but the actions one takes to reach there aren’t. He tested Kenji through many trials; Kenji succeeded, even if he didn’t get everything correct. It’s not enough to completely change him, but it does help alleviate the pain. Kenji admitted he was the evil emperor. This along with the new hope that 20th Century Boys gives, leads perfectly into the next scene.
Katsumata comes up to Kenji and sings the lyrics that he knows all too well.
Kenji takes a liking to it and suggests the two become a duo.
Katsumata smiles.
They continue to sing Gutalala Sudalala together on the school rooftop.

Katsumata doesn’t know why he’s doing this. It just feels right, after all a friend isn’t made from verbal vows but from the soul. I don’t think katsumata has yet deciphered what a friend is and why people can make them so easily without remorse. But he doesn’t care. There’s a sense of hope that friendship gives Katsumata. It’s no longer the fake friendship that Katsumata has been building for the last 50 years, it’s real and genuine. Not spawning out of necessity or fear, but from an unknown yet genuine source [thus why I call it the soul]. It’s a truly beautiful ending. There’s no one conclusion or epiphany that Katsumata comes up with. He just goes with what he feels is right; what his soul tells him to do. And that’s the essence of what 20th Century Boys is about.
Your purpose and destiny is determined by you alone.
