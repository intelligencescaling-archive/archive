Yuuichi’s strategy begins when he gives the players in the sold room a contract to sign regarding a bet on who can guess what order the teams in the end game will be based on their money, and the “final order” will be when 2 or more of the people who take part in the bet confirm the scores. The only debunk falsely assumes each player in every team gets a vote, but this line from the raws debunks this idea:

当たらなかった 俺たちの予想は完璧にはけど惜しかったなぁ

“We weren't quite right, but man, that was close!”

俺たちの予想は -> (our prediction is)
  There are 24 possible outcomes for the end game scores based on pure randomness (4 teams have 24 different ways to be ordered), but naturally with each team would choose a bet of their team being in the top ranks, then Mishima’s and Kamishiro’s teams would have no chance of voting correctly due to Yuuichi’s later exploits, which leaves only Kaidou’s team, whom have a 1 on 6 chance to vote correctly (Kaidou’s team would vote on their own team being top 1, meaning out of 6 possibilities where their team is the top team, 1 is correct), however, due to Yuuichi’s initial blunder when he first gambled at the beginning of All-Bets, the players' perception of him dropped, which led Kaidou’s team to instead pick between the 2 outcomes where Yuuichi is last, and while at first they chose the correct one, as evident by one of them stating AFTER they have concluded their guess was right:

いやだが、この順位を予想した

“I don't like it, but I predicted this outcome”

Directly showcasing that while they were deciding what to vote, they had be torn between two guesses
Kaidou > Kamishiro > Mishima > Katagiri
And
Kaidou > Mishima > Kamishiro > Katagiri

With them ultimately deciding on the latter, despite one of them predicting the former.
Yuuichi’s blunder in the beginning of All-Bets couldn’t have been intentional, as it’s never confirmed nor does anything imply it, and it’s more logical to assume it was actually Yuuichi underestimating how bad he actually was at gambling, because if Yuuichi had done that on purpose, then he would’ve been purposely giving Kaidou’s team a 50% chance of ruining his strategy if they simply guessed.

Possible rebunks assume Yuuichi somehow cheated or simply saw Kaidou’s team's guess and went with the opposite of it, both of which are completely not implied by anything in the manga, and would directly contradict the fact that Kaidou’s team members did not recognize the order they confirmed as Yuuichi’s own, and while yes, Yuuichi is the creator of the bet, it doesn’t give hm privileges over other players, and even if we were to assume he could’ve seen their guess, he’d have no reason to reverse it, and moreover, for him to only put a guess after the other players had would raise too much suspicion on his actions; which again, was never even slightly mentioned by any of Kaidou’s men.

Yuuichi’s own prediction was:
Kaidou > Kamishiro > Mishima > Katagiri

  Second, we have the context, in both the FTL and the OTL, the scenario goes as follows:
- Yuuichi is begging people for help (acting)
- Kamishiro steps up to help Yuuichi
- Kamishiro sells the lady on his team
- Manabu ponders a bit before giving her price
- Kamishiro responds with confirmation in the lines of “Well… that much was expected (FTL)” or “I’m not surprised (OTL)”.

Implying in both cases he wasn’t sure her price would be enough, but he wouldn’t be surprised if it does, which fits exactly with Shinji’s previous statement, and also fits the current context as well, because Manabu wouldn’t ponder her price had he stated it and Kamishiro wouldn’t ask for her price either.

While it’s valid to say it’s possible Kamishiro asked for her price off-screen between his lost against Yuuichi and him pretending to help him here; it’s less valid than to say that Kamishiro was simply cocky as he trained her himself, and he wanted to look proud as he humiliated Yuuichi, but both interpretations are still pass-able and don’t affect much overall, even though the latter one is still more consistent with Kamishiro’s character and the overall context and narrative of All-Bets.

Third and last, we give the rebunk the benefit of the doubt and accept it, but with it arise new problems in the logical consistency of the characters

1. Inferring the lady's price from her assumed origin is impossible for multiple reasons.

A- Her price didn't stem from her lineage, influence, or background; instead, it came from an unkonwn variable of Kamishiro's supposed training of her.

Both TLs and the illustrations show Kamishiro is talking about something not-so-nice to say the least, but it's still too vague to infer fully; we still know it's not something related to blood relations to achievements.

B- This argument even completely ignores the cult's existence since it assumes that the old geezers either:

I- Knew anything about the lady in the first place, as cults tend to kill individuality and thrive for suppressing genuine connections and fostering superficial ones that are built on shared beliefs rather than deep personal connection.

II- Were willing to participate in such entertainment, as it'd contradict the idea of cults training discipline, especially with how old day are, thinking they'd engage in this actively, and choose a member of their own team to talk about loops back to point I.

III- Assuming point II is dismissed, an intuitive move for a couple would be to share stories of themselves or their blood relations, not a person they'd know nearly nothing about naturally

2. Assuming pre-existing knowledge on her price doesn’t make sense.
This does not even matter, as the existence of the knowledge doesn't suddenly make it possible to infer her price from unrelated information, but this is disregarded anyway due to:

A- In BOTH TLs, Kamishiro asks Manabu in a figure of speech form, you can hold on to the "again", but what stays true is that in both TLs, Manabu is confirming her price for the first time, as in both cases he isn't saying "as I told you" or implying her price was set to begin with.

B- Shinji who we see is the closest person to Kamishiro as evident by all his actions in the arc and who under your interpretation should've known the price of his own teammate had Kamishiro actually asked about it said their chances were slim; if your interpretation was true, why did Shinji expect their chances to be so low? he should've been confident that they could jump out of debt easily and pay for the leaving fee with such an expensive

3. The effects of the possible lie on the prediction.
Now we get to the most important point, the fact that, if Yuuichi was lying, then he didn't predict the canonical final order.

Because, Yuuichi is predicting Kamishiro > Mishima, having Kamishiro sell both his teammates guarantees this possibility being more likely, saying Yuuichi was lying and hence didn't care about Kamishiro jumping the ranks even with an inflated assumption about guessing the price of the lady would show his prediction was instead Mishima > Kamishiro, which isn't canon.

YUUICHI HAS TO BE TELLING THE TRUTH to be consistent with his Kamishiro > Mishima prediction.

With that all the interpretations regarding Yuuichi somehow getting information about the lady’s price proven impossible, it’s time to address the remainder of the miscalculation.

Yuuichi bet on Kaido > Kamishiro > Mishima > Katagiri in his contract with the sold players, this is a significant mistake on his behalf. Kamishiro already being in debt already makes it unlikely that he'd get above Mishima who can easily sell one or two of his girls (who generally go for high prices such as 100-200 million yen.)

Yuuichi's safest bet would've been Kaido > Mishima > Kamishiro > Katagiri.

WHAT COULD HAVE YUUICHI DONE?

Mishima's final result possibilities (as should have been calculated by Yuuichi): ¥100,000,000 to ¥200,000,000.

This calculation is derived from the observations that Yuuichi had on hand.

Mishima Girl #1: ¥150,000,000

Mishima Girl #2: ¥100,000,000

Kokorogi: ¥200,000,000

Average price of females based off Yuuichi's observations: ¥150,000,000.

200 million is a highball, and not a safe bet for Yuuichi. He can either go for the low-ball or mid-ball of 100 or 150 million yen respectively.

Kamishiro's final result possibilities (using both the low and high-balls): -¥41,000,000 to ¥59,000,000.

Both Mishima and Kamishiro are likely to sell a woman from their teams. Mishima can be predicted to only sell one person to make sure he isn't left with ¥0 as his final total in the game, while the same goes for Kamishiro so he can get out of debt and be wheeled around.

Both Kamishiro and Mishima can be expected to receive a similar amount of yen from the selling of their teammates, however, what makes Mishima > Kamishiro far more plausible in the final results are their starting points

Kamishiro goes into this with a debt of -¥141,000,000, while Mishima only has ¥0. This means that even if Kamishiro had the high-balled amount of 200 million from his selling, and Mishima only got 100 million, Mishima would still have ¥59,000,000 more than Kamishiro.

In other words, it was complete LUCK on Yuuichi's part for the girl that Kamishiro sold to net him ¥350,000,000. Even with this amount, Kamishiro only got 9 million yen more than Mishima, who got ¥200,000,000 from the girl he sold. This is dangerously close despite the absurd amount that Kamishiro received, and what makes it worse was that this was based on Yuuichi’s own actions, he could’ve saved himself the trouble here by betting on a lesser amount with Kamishiro earlier, which would’ve caused his current debt to be low enough for a logical estimate of his teammates prices.
