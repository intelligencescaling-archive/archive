Yuuichi's Counter-Gamble/EM Analysis

Yuuichi Katagiri possesses an extraordinary ability to switch between seemingly contradictory emotional orientations: **genuinely trusting others and refusing to trust anyone.** The distinction becomes clear through his comparison with Kei. Although Kei can scam others, his conscience prevents him from doing so, and growing closer to someone increases his guilt about deceiving them. Likewise, being betrayed by someone he loves hurts him more because of their emotional significance. Yuuichi, however, can betray even those closest to him without hesitation when pursuing his objectives. When betrayed himself, he can rapidly change his attitude and retaliate without remaining bound by his previous trust.

Kei describes this through the concept of a *Counter-Gamble*: just as a gambler bets on opposing outcomes to avoid losing regardless of the result, Yuuichi maintains both the heart to trust and the heart to trust no one. He can form genuine emotional attachments without allowing them to permanently constrain his decisions, switching to a ruthless orientation whenever necessary. The manga explicitly describes him as capable of making his emotions *"true or false at any time,"* suggesting control over his actual emotional experience rather than merely its outward expression.

This principle also underlies Shiba Taizen's ideal successor. Taizen describes people as having an angel and a demon within their hearts, constantly struggling against each other. According to Shinji, his ideal successor must understand others' pain, earn the trust of the weak, and nevertheless become either an angel or a demon when necessary. Taizen therefore sought someone capable of genuinely empathizing with vulnerable people while retaining the ability to exploit or betray them when their objectives demanded it. Yuuichi embodies this duality by retaining access to both orientations without allowing either to permanently restrict the other.

# What the Ability Requires

The Counter-Gamble implies an extreme degree of **emotional self-regulation and control over the influence of emotions on decision-making**. Unlike ordinary emotion regulation, which can modify emotional intensity and duration through processes such as cognitive reappraisal, Yuuichi's ability suggests that he can deliberately activate or disengage from genuine emotional states at will.

Crucially, he must separate emotional experience from the information associated with it. Disengaging from affection cannot erase his memories, understanding of the relationship, or knowledge of the other person's feelings; otherwise, he could neither continue understanding them nor restore his genuine attachment. This implies selective control over emotional responses while preserving the associated cognitive information. **By extension, such control could help him disengage from guilt, fear, anger, or distress when they interfere with his objectives.**

Finally, **subjective emotional control does not automatically establish complete physiological control**. Emotional experience, outward expression, and bodily responses can change independently. Controlling his feelings therefore does not necessarily mean controlling his heart rate, sweating, stress hormones, or other autonomic responses; that would require additional evidence.

Ultimately, Yuuichi's Counter-Gamble is not simply emotional suppression, deception, or an absence of empathy. It is the ability to experience genuine attachment while retaining the capacity to disengage from its influence, preserve his understanding of others, and act ruthlessly when necessary.
