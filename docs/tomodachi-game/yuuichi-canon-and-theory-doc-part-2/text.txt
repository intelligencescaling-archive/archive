Why Yuuichi can't be blamed for not being able to perceive through Kokorogi:
Before the events of Tomodachi Game, Yuuichi was not yet highly skilled in psychological profiling, even though he had some foundational knowledge. His experience and intuition were still developing, and he wasn’t adept at applying these skills in every situation, particularly when the circumstances didn’t explicitly demand it.

At the time, overwhelming evidence pointed towards Shiho being the traitor, making it incredibly difficult to suspect Kokorogi. This evidence came from multiple angles: some of it was reinforced by plot armor, while other parts stemmed from well-crafted tactics. Kokorogi’s approach, in particular, was especially deceptive. She employed a strategy involving a positive money debt—essentially framing her actions as ones that resulted in gains rather than losses—which was subtle and extraordinarily hard to detect.

Additionally, recognizing this "+ debt" tactic would have required Yuuichi to assume that the true traitor had full support from the Tomodachi Game administrators. Such an assumption would have seemed baseless at the time, as no explicit evidence suggested this level of collaboration. Even if Yuuichi had considered the possibility of Kokorogi employing a "+ debt" strategy, it would not have helped narrow down the suspect list to her. Instead, it would have expanded the suspect pool, as this theory could apply to anyone with potential administrative backing. Worse, defending Shiho based on this theory would have appeared irrational and counterproductive, undermining his credibility and weakening his position in the game.

There is also the fact that Yuuichi does not like doubting or profiling his friends unless the situation absolutely forces him to. This reluctance to turn his analytical abilities against those he cares about would have further prevented him from scrutinizing Kokorogi too closely. Compounding this is Kokorogi’s own admission of having conflicted emotions toward Yuuichi—simultaneously loving and hating him. Her emotional complexity likely added another layer of difficulty for Yuuichi in discerning her true intentions.

Moreover, Yuuichi’s reasoning during this period was grounded in logical deduction and evidence. Based on what he knew, there was simply no concrete reason to suspect Kokorogi. Accusing her of being the traitor would have required pure intuition and baseless speculation, which were neither reliable nor consistent with Yuuichi’s analytical approach. It made more sense for him to focus on Shiho, given the extensive circumstantial evidence against her. On top of that, Kokorogi’s tactic of masking her true nature through prior manipulations—such as framing herself as a victim through fabricated stories of bullying and even resorting to becoming a prostitute—added another layer of complexity to her deception. She carefully built a persona that seemed entirely genuine and innocent.

Adding to this was Kokorogi’s flawless acting. Throughout most of the manga, she maintained a façade so convincing that it wasn’t until the Murder Game arc, where she was forced into a corner, that her true personality and motivations were revealed. Until that pivotal moment, there was no way for Yuuichi—or anyone else—to uncover her true nature.

In conclusion:

1. At the time, there was no logical basis to suspect Kokorogi as the traitor. Any suspicion directed at her would have relied purely on intuition, which isn’t a dependable method of deduction. Yuuichi’s inability to see through Kokorogi stemmed from a combination of her manipulative tactics, his limited experience with psychological games, and the overwhelming evidence against Shiho, which naturally diverted his attention.

2. The "+ debt" strategy further complicates the situation. Identifying this tactic would require assuming that the true traitor had significant support from the Tomodachi Game administrators—a leap that lacked evidence and would have widened the suspect list rather than narrowing it to Kokorogi. Using this theory to defend Shiho would have appeared irrational and counterproductive.

3. Yuuichi’s reluctance to doubt or profile his friends also played a role. His unwillingness to scrutinize Kokorogi, combined with her conflicted emotional attachment to him, made it even harder for him to see her true intentions.

4. Yuuichi’s perception was not a reliable tool in this scenario. Kokorogi’s ability to manipulate those around her and maintain her perfect persona made her practically undetectable until circumstances forced her hand.
