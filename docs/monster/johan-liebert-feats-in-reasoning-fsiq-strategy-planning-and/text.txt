reasoning

Logically: When Johan enters the hospital after hearing Tenma's request, he creates a strategy that consists of poisoning candies. This way, the police would have two theories: either he was the Liebert murderer or he was a doctor with knowledge of chemistry. To avoid suspicion on Tenma, he calculates the hours of each shift so the police cannot be inclined to suspect Tenma.

Deductive reasoning: When Jan Suk approaches her, she seduces him by pretending to be his sister to gain his trust. When he tells her he has a recording of himself as a child, she decides to listen to part of his past to deduce where he keeps the tape, which she discovers by noticing a strong connection between his mother and him.

Adductive: When Johan befriends Jungers, he gains his trust through letters. Jungers shares communication with Johan. Johan infers that Jungers suffered abuse at the hands of his mother, deciding to investigate his past further. He manages to uncover a part of it and uses this to kill one of his adoptive mothers.

Adaptability: When Johan enters Kinderheim 511, he is put through physical and mental tests which he easily overcomes, leading to him being subjected to further tests (this time using memory erasure). However, Johan demonstrates a great adaptability to drugs and pain, which makes him be seen as a perfect monster or a high-ranking military officer.

For illogical reasoning: believing that Tenma was going to shoot him, he used the strategy of killing the entire village to make Tenma shoot him in that way (also using the child as a reference), believing that seeing so much violence would change Tenma's ideology

To do this, he calculates everyone's arrival times, ensuring there's a gap between Tenma and the supervisors. This way, after he plants the candies, flee the hospital.
