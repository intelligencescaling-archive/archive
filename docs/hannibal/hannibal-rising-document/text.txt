The novel begins with a narration about Hannibal Lecter. The narrator is talking about his memory palace. The narrator explains that Hannibal's memory palace is in the darkness at the center of his mind, and it has a latch that can be found by touch alone. The narrator also talks about Hannibal's early memories being static and incomplete. He describes it as, “Attic shards held together by a blank pastor." His other rooms hold sound and motion, and great snakes wrestle and heave in the dark and are lit in flashes. There are pleas and screams that fill some places on the grounds where Hanniabl cannot see. His Palace was constructed when Hannibal was a boy. When he was in confinement, he had no books, so he decided to expand and improve his memory palace. Hannibal's Memory Palace is so vast and big that it has over 300 spaces. It is described as rivaling the Topkapi Museum in Turkey. On the internet, the Topkapi Museum has over 300 spaces. This includes numerous halls, rooms, courtyards, and pavilions. In total, there are over 300 spaces. Hannibal's Memory Palace and its complex designs demonstrate his ability to think and perceive complex concepts and symbolism. He also uses observed facts and phenomena to draw conclusions and expand his memory palace.
