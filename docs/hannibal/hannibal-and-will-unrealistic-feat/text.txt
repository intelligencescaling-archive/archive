Now, trying to understand the feat, let’s see what Hannibal and Will had to understand in order to create this conversation: Hannibal and Will both knew exactly which place they were going to visualize, even down to the meter, since we see Hannibal looking around the environment when he is questioned. Hannibal also knew when Will would want to speak to him, but Will could do the same, and this was down to the minute, even the exact second. They predicted each other’s every word, the responses, the intonation, the choice of words, the emphasis, the rhythm, the structure, the purpose of the conversation, the speaking time | when they were going to speak down to the second… They also knew about the change of location, meaning they knew the exact location, again, down to the meter of Hannibal’s memory palace. Obviously, there are also the facial expressions, the movements of their bodies, the emotions they feel in the moment, their shared memories, their understanding of each other, and the fact that they had to adapt their modeling of one another knowing that the other’s mental state affects their modeling of their interlocutor… all of this, of course, with perfect accuracy in order to replicate an authentic conversation.

It’s also important to understand that they are imagining what the other would say, so they had logically anticipated the entire conversation, because if we take Will’s perspective, Hannibal is speaking to him but in truth it’s Will’s imagination knowing what Hannibal would say and the same for Hannibal. So, taking this into account, we can deduce that both characters can anticipate their conversation entirely in advance, they know what the other is going to say, they know what the other is going to ask and answer. Now we are going to see how this buffs the feat, but just before that, let’s try to explain how the system in which they operate resembles or is a chaotic system.

Sensitivity to initial conditions: If, for example, Hannibal is 0.1% more irritated or more tired than Will thinks, the entire prediction of tone, words, and imagery changes.

Interdependence of variables: Changing a single word changes the emotional reaction, which changes the facial expression, which changes the choice of the following words. There is therefore an exponential factor.

Impossibility of perfect calculation: The amount of information required is astronomical and would demand a mental model of the other at neuronal resolution.

The system contains too many things they predict with 100% accuracy, with in addition a huge exponential factor knowing that this is a conversation, and that the example of initial conditions also applies to Hannibal, who could think that Will is 0.1% sadder, which would change much about Will and even about Hannibal. But no both know perfectly, without the slightest apparent error, what the other feels despite the astronomical number of parameters that must be taken into account.

In a chaotic system, time acts as an exponential factor. We can see this through the weather: we can predict the weather in 3 days, but not in 1 month, because it would require INCREDIBLE precision in the initial conditions. The more time passes, the more actions there are, each action can lead to several different results, which in turn can do the same. We therefore see the exponential factor here, which is formalized in chaotic systems with the formula:

It must be understood that in a conversation, each action triggers other actions, which in turn trigger others, and so on, creating a multiplicative effect. Hannibal and Will must not only analyze the immediate reaction to a word or a gesture, but also all the alternatives that could have happened if one of them had acted differently. Each potential alternative creates a new possible trajectory, and each trajectory leads to further branches. This forms a tree of possibilities that grows exponentially, after a single exchange, there are already several paths, and after just a few exchanges, their number becomes astronomical.

For example, if each sentence or gesture offers 10 realistic alternatives, after 5 exchanges there are already 10⁵ potential scenarios. Hannibal and Will must analyze every realistic possibility and identify which is the most probable, in order to achieve the perfect conversation. This may seem unrealistic, because it is. Recreating a conversation with perfect accuracy is totally impossible for humanity, yet they do it.

This also does not even take into account microscopic variables such as a 0.1% change in tone, or a tiny variation in facial expression, which in turn alters the emotional reaction and therefore the choice of the following words. In a system so sensitive to initial conditions, these minute variations propagate at each step, amplified by the exponential effect, until they make any perfect prediction theoretically impossible. Yet, in the scene, Hannibal and Will seem to predict with absolute precision the emotional, verbal, and even spatial content of the conversation, which would be equivalent to solving, in real time, a chaotic system of a complexity unattainable for a human being. Emotional understanding
 On an emotional level, they do not merely read instantaneous emotions, they model the other’s complete internal state, anticipating not only their current feelings but also how those feelings will evolve throughout the conversation. They integrate microscopic signals (minute variations in mood, memory, mental imagery) that are normally imperceptible, and project them with perfect accuracy into the future, which implies an emotional simulation at a near neuronal resolution.
What does “near-neuronal” mean? A near neuronal resolution means that one would have to model the crucial internal state of a person, including neuronal states (activity in localized groups of neurons, firing patterns), synaptic weights and their dynamics, hormonal/neuro-modulatory levels (adrenaline, cortisol, dopamine) which modulate decision-making, working memory (what is currently in mind), and unconscious associations that tip the choice toward one word rather than another.
They are therefore at a level of emotional understanding far beyond what we can currently achieve, they know with 100% accuracy anything that touches the other’s emotions and can perfectly anticipate future emotions as well.
emotional perception
Hannibal and Will also possess an emotional perception that surpasses any human frame of reference, because in order to recreate an authentic conversation without direct sensory signals, they must already have memorized with absolute precision every micro-expression, every vocal inflection, every muscular and physiological variation the other might express in any situation. Obviously, they cannot memorize absolutely everything, as that would likely be an immense number, but they must have a memory vast enough to then infer, via emotional understanding, the other’s face in X situation
This perceptual memory is not a simple general impression, but an exhaustive mental database where each emotional state is encoded as a complete sensory pattern, including physiological correlations invisible to the naked eye, such as microscopic pupil dilation, minute facial tensions, or imperceptible variations in breathing rhythm
To build such a database, they must have accumulated over time a volume of perceptual data that no human could ever collect or store, and above all, they are able to reactivate it with perfect fidelity. Thus, even without perceiving in real time, they can mentally simulate the other’s exact emotional state

This level of precision implies that their raw emotional perception is already at an unrealistic level,  no bias, no noise, no loss of information,  just a complete and exploitable capture of the other’s emotional state, which then allows them to feed their unrealistic emotional understanding with data of a purity unattainable for us
Why can a human not reach this precision? FSIQ | sensory
First, the necessary information is inaccessible: to know the neuronal/synaptic state of another would require invasive measurements (very high-resolution scans, electrophysiology, etc.). Human senses (vision, hearing, smell) do not transmit the fine information required to reconstruct synaptic weights or the exact firing pattern. Even chemical signals enhanced smell) only provide partial clues, not the complete map.

There is also the size of the state space, which is enormous. To give an idea: the brain has about ~10¹¹ neurons and ~10¹³–10¹⁴ synapses. If you wanted to very roughly represent the state of the synapses with 16 bits each, you get about ~1.6×10¹⁵ bits ≈ 200 TB of raw information just for the synaptic weights. This is not only a numerical constraint, you also have to capture these bits from the other person, which is impossible through normal perception.

Now let’s move on to the reasoning necessary for this (we will also look again at FSIQ).

Hannibal and Will operate in a massively combinatorial probabilistic space, where every word, intonation, or silence opens a range of possible trajectories. At each step, they estimate not only the most probable trajectory at the moment, but also how this trajectory will be perceived and reinterpreted by the other, which modifies future probabilities. In practice, this is equivalent to exploring an exponential tree of possibilities, if at each turn of conversation there are N plausible responses, after T turns there are N^T possible trajectories. For N = 20 and T = 15, this reaches 20^15 ≈ 3.27×10^19 paths, a space so insane that no human can exhaustively traverse it. Hannibal and Will seem to reduce this space to a single trajectory perfectly predicted, which amounts to solving in real time an optimization problem over an exponential space with zero error. This is a reasoning feat superior to what the best modern AIs can do, because here the state space is not defined by fixed rules like in chess, but by human psychology, which is far more chaotic.

Reflective loop
We must also take into account that the system has a reflective loop. What is that? Well, I had already mentioned it a bit, it’s when A models B, but B models A in return. If both are very good, it becomes a phenomenon of nested simulation: A simulates what B will say, but B simulates what A thinks B will say, which forces A to simulate what B thinks A thinks B will say… and so on.

Mathematically, it’s like having a function f that takes as input the model of the other, but whose input is itself the output of the other function. Finding the perfect trajectory comes down to finding a fixed point of this recursive system , that is, a stable configuration where each person’s predictions align perfectly.

The problem is that the deeper the level of reflection (he thinks that I think that he thinks…), the more complexity explodes. In theory, an infinite depth is impossible to compute, but Hannibal and Will seem to navigate without loss through several layers of recursion, as if they had direct access to the perfect fixed point.

To understand how impressive this is, we will briefly look at studies on this type of reasoning, often called "strategic reasoning." In the linked studies, they say that level 2 or higher strategic reasoning is already a high level, and that’s only “I think that you think that I want to leave” or more. Another study shows that 34% of people stop at just being one step ahead, so "I think that you think..." We can clearly see that humans struggle with this type of reasoning, while Will and Hannibal can manipulate an unquantifiable number of layers, which must be very large. But let’s try to at least give a small estimate.

C1: My current state, what Will thinks/feels at the moment. C2: His model of Hannibal’s state, what Will believes Hannibal feels. C3: Hannibal’s model of Will’s state, what Will assumes Hannibal thinks about Will (reciprocal reflection). C4: Speech objective, what Will wants to achieve with his reply (inform, test, seduce, trap). C5: Anticipation of word strategy, which word Hannibal will choose according to his model of Will’s objective. C6: Predict pauses, emphases, silences (direct influence on interpretation). C7: Expected micro-facial expression, precise contraction of a facial muscle if a particular phrase is said. C8: Which specific memory the phrase will trigger in the other (image, precise recollection). C9: Emotional reaction to this memory, how the memory modifies the emotion (guilt, nostalgia, anger). C10: How the other will adapt their phrase upon seeing the emotional reaction. C11: If the other intentionally modifies their message to provoke a reaction in Will. C12: Perception the other has of manipulation, Will anticipates if Hannibal believes he is being manipulated and how he will react. C13: Choice of a precise word because the other attaches a particular connotation to it. C14: Consideration of N plausible words and why each would be chosen, simulating reactions for each. C15: Which place/position/object in the memory palace will be evoked and how the other will mentally position themselves there. C16: How the conversation in the next 10–30 seconds will redirect the other’s memory and emotion. C17: Instant recalculation based on observed micro-reactions (pause, blink, micro-breath). C18: Will simulates how Hannibal simulates his own simulation (refining the fixed point). C19: Prediction of the stable position where their models will converge (the ideal dialogue).
C20: Final choice of action | speech that maximizes the desired effect given all the levels above. Each layer is not just a repetition of "he thinks that": it concerns a different domain (emotion, memory, word, prosody, spatiality, strategy, metacognition).

To predict exactly a word, a pause, and a micro expression, you must first resolve all these dependencies, the word depends on the activated memory (C8), the connotation (C13), the strategy (C11), and how the other will interpret the connotation (C12), which itself depends on the other’s belief about your belief (C3, C18).

Two key points to understand why 15–20 is an underestimate:
For each order of belief layer, there are multiple dimensions (lexical, facial, hormonal, spatial). Even a single order layer can contain multiple decision sublayers. Moreover, conversation is not a single act anticipating the trajectory over multiple turns adds one layer per turn for each predictive dimension. We can compare this with the Traveling Salesman Problem, which is already one of the most well known problems in mathematics. The problem involves a traveler who crosses a country and must find the shortest route linking a set of cities without passing through the same place twice, and this problem quickly becomes impossible to solve exactly as the number of cities increases, to the point that even supercomputers must use approximations.

 Now imagine a dynamic version where the cities change position in real time, where distances vary every instant, where some cities appear and disappear while you calculate, and where you also have to guess their exact position from noisy data. For a normal human, this is totally unachievable, because our working memory, calculation speed, and probabilistic precision are far too limited to track a moving graph with such a degree of complexity.

Yet, in their conversations, Hannibal and Will solve in real time a mental problem equivalent to this dynamic version, except that each city represents a possible emotional state of the other, each distance is a probability of transition to another state, and the graph changes every microsecond because the other thinks and acts simultaneously. Not only do they find the optimal trajectory, but they do it with no visible approximation, without a computer, and integrating an astronomical number of microscopic variables, which clearly places them above all known human capacity.

Learning ability

In cognitive psychology, they exceed the most advanced researchers by reading and modeling the complete internal state of a person with perfect accuracy, where even modern science only provides a rough approximation. In linguistics, they surpass discourse analysis experts by instantly detecting every nuance of language, micro expressions, and vocal rhythm without error, then integrating these into a 100% reliable behavioral prediction. In behavioral chemistry, they go far beyond neuroscientists by inferring precise hormonal or neuromodulatory states (dopamine, cortisol, adrenaline) from subtle signals and anticipating their future influence on the other’s choices. In applied mathematics and probability, they outmatch the greatest statisticians by reducing an astronomical possibility space to the single correct trajectory, in real time, without ever making a mistake. In sociology and game theory, they surpass the brightest strategists by perfectly simulating complex social interactions several moves ahead while accounting for chaotic variables.

To maintain this level, their brain plasticity, their speed of integrating new information, and their ability to instantly combine this knowledge into a unified mental model are far beyond known human biological limits

In summary Hannibal and Will operate within a complex chaotic system where each interaction generates an exponential factor of variables and uncertainties. Their capacity for emotional understanding far exceeds what humans can achieve, integrating microscopic signals, nearly inaccessible neural states, and emotional predictions with perfect precision. They manipulate deep reflexive loops well beyond 20 layers, allowing them to model not only their own thoughts but also those of the other at multiple nested levels. The volume of information to process is astronomical, requiring sophisticated probabilistic reasoning to sort and anticipate
