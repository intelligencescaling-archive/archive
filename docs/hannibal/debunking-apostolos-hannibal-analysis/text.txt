Debunking Apóstolos’ Hannibal analysis

A canonical and epistemological debunk of the wanks, omissions, and fallacies attributed to Hannibal Lecter.
Introduction
The purpose of this doc is to debunk the heavily inflated scaling Apóstolos attributes to Hannibal Lecter The Silence Of Lambs (SOTL). iHis analysis portrays Hannibal as possessing abilities that supposedly "stretch beyond human capabilities", relying on claims such as "epistemological impossibility", "supernatural Theory of Mind", and "literal telepathy" to justify placing him alongside characters like PM Hal and Baku.
This entire "Devil of Omniscience" narrative begins to collapse the moment you actually open the book. Apóstolos's scaling relies on four fundamentally flawed pillars: (1) the omission of Chapter 26, which directly undermines a substantial portion of his arguments; (2) the misattribution of deductions made by Clarice Starling to Hannibal; (3) basic English reading comprehension errors, such as confusing an add-a-beads necklace with a Catholic rosary; and (4) the literal interpretation of poetic metaphors.
Throughout this document, I will examine each of Apóstolos's arguments against the actual events and descriptions in the novel, provide the relevant context, and explain why his interpretations are canonically untenable. I will also distinguish Hannibal's genuine intellectual abilities from the extraordinary capabilities attributed to him, before presenting a more accurate assessment of his standing within the SCD.
1. Physiological Control and the "Near-Death Simulation" Hoax
What Apóstolos Argues
When analyzing Hannibal's attack on a nurse during an electrocardiogram and his subsequent escape from the courthouse in Memphis, Apóstolos establishes the following physiological predicate:
"Throughout all four novels, it was implied several times or directly stated that Hannibal has perfect control over all his physiological processes (heart rate, breathing, blood pressure, perspiration, etc.), which makes him completely unreadable. It was shown that Hannibal could easily bypass multiparametric psychophysiological data-acquisition systems such as lie detectors, polygraphs and ect".
"The persuasiveness of the replacement was enhanced by the simulation of a near-death state, which can be interpreted as a manifestation of Hannibal's extraordinary control over his physiological reactions. His successful simulation of a near-death state was achieved through his ability to exert physiological control, including slowing his heart rate to critical levels and suppressing external signs of activity".

Contextualizing the rest of the argument, Apóstolos uses the fact that Hannibal's heart rate did not exceed 85 bpm while mutilating the nurse to conclude that he voluntarily controls all of his autonomous biological processes, applying this same premise to claim that Hannibal deceived Sergeant Tate and the paramedics in Memphis by lowering his heart rate to "critical near-death levels" and suppressing any sign of external activity.
Primary Evidence and Context from the Work
The attack on the nurse (Chapter 2): While leading Clarice Starling to the maximum-security ward in Baltimore, Dr. Frederick Chilton explains why Hannibal only leaves his cell in full restraints and wearing a mouthpiece. He recounts that on July 8, 1976, after a year of model behavior, Hannibal complained of chest pain and was taken to the dispensary for an electrocardiogram. When his restraints were removed and the nurse bent over him, Hannibal broke her jaw to swallow her tongue while remaining hooked up to the monitors: "Lecter was hooked up to the monitors the entire time. He broke her jaw to get at her tongue. His pulse never got over eighty-five, even when he swallowed it".

The physical fight against the guards in Memphis (Chapter 36): During his escape on the fifth floor of the Memphis courthouse, Hannibal unlocks his handcuffs, cuffs Guard Boyle to the table leg, rams the iron door into Pembry, bites Pembry's face, blinds Boyle with Mace, beats Boyle to death with five blows of a riot baton, and knocks Pembry unconscious with a blow to the back of the head. Immediately after the physical exertion, the narrator records Hannibal's physiological reaction: "Dr. Lecter's pulse was elevated to more than one hundred by the exercise, but quickly slowed to normal".

The rescue by the police officers and paramedics (Chapters 37 and 38): After putting on Pembry's uniform, placing the flayed skin of the guard's face over his own, and firing three shots to draw the guards, Hannibal lies down on the fifth-floor floor. When Sergeant Tate arrives at the scene and kneels to check on the bloodied man, Hannibal reacts visibly and noisily: "Tate knelt and as he reached for the neck to feel, the awful thing on the floor groaned and blew a bloody bubble". Tate finds a heartbeat and noisy breathing ("there was a heartbeat, he found it, there was breathing. It was ragged and gurgling but it was breathing"), and young Patrolman Murray tries to hold the body that was groaning and thrashing on the floor ("trying to hold the groaning, jerking body"). When the ambulance attendants take over, they secure his clenched fists under his belt, insert an airway, and apply a nonstick surgical pressure bandage over the disfigured face. Inside the ambulance (Chapter 38), the paramedic checks Hannibal's vital signs and transmits the exact numbers over the radio: "He's comatose but the vital signs are good. He's got good pressure. One-thirty over ninety. Yeah, ninety. Pulse eighty-five. He's got severe facial cuts with elevated flaps, one eye enucleated. I've got pressure on the face and an airway in place".
The Debunk
I don't know if Apóstolos simply skipped Chapter 38 or if he doesn't know what basic vital signs mean in medicine, but his argument here self-destructs against the text itself:

Explicit physiology vs Biological fantasy: The canonical text gives us two objective facts: (1) Hannibal's pulse stayed at 85 bpm when attacking the nurse; (2) Hannibal's pulse rose to over 100 bpm due to the physical exercise of beating Boyle and Pembry, slowing back to normal shortly afterward. This proves that his cardiovascular system responds normally to the metabolic demand of physical exertion. The fact that it did not exceed 85 bpm in the nurse scene merely reflects an absence of autonomic arousal/fear in the face of violence (psychopathy/emotional composure), not a superpower to control his heartbeat at will.

The fabrication of the "near-death state": Apóstolos claims that Hannibal fooled the police and paramedics by "slowing his heart rate to critical levels and suppressing external signs of activity". Let's look at the actual chain of events in Chapters 37 and 38:

— Hannibal did not suppress external signs of activity → he groaned, blew bloody bubbles, and kept thrashing/jerking on the floor ("groaning, jerking body") specifically to get Tate's attention and be rushed out as an injured survivor.

— Hannibal did not slow his heart rate to critical near-death levels → the paramedic literally states over the radio that the vital signs are good, blood pressure is 130 over 90, and pulse is 85 bpm!
85 bpm and 130/90 blood pressure are literally Hannibal's normal resting vital signs (the exact same 85 bpm pulse from the nurse scene, LOL). He fooled the paramedics because he placed Pembry's flayed face over his own and had his face covered by a surgical pressure bandage in the middle of a SWAT operation, not because he "slowed his heart rate to critical levels".
2. The 102.32 km² Fallacy
What Apóstolos Argues
When analyzing the drawing of Florence hanging on Hannibal's cell wall in Chapter 3, Apóstolos writes:
"Starling notices the paintings on the walls and asks about them. Hannibal points to several paintings and names them: 'It's Florence. That's the Palazzo Vecchio and the Duomo, seen from the Belvedere'. Hannibal says that memory is the only thing that replaces the view from the window for him. All these building structures (including the interior) were included in his huge memory palace from which he was able to transfer them in detail onto a canvas of paper. Let's see how much visual information Hannibal visualized:"
Right below this claim, Apóstolos pastes entire Wikipedia pages detailing the complete internal architecture of the Palazzo Vecchio (the Hall of the Five Hundred at 52m long with 39 ceiling panels, the Hall of Lilies, the Hall of Maps with 53 painted maps, and a numbered list of 31 internal rooms across three floors) and of the Duomo (dimensions, 7 external doors, and 19 internal monuments, busts, and frescoes). At the end, he cites an excerpt from the literary sequel (Hannibal) about the same drawing and drops the spatial metric of the feat: "Florence Area: 102.32 km2 = 102. 320.000 m2".
Primary Evidence and Context from the Work
The cell dialogue (Chapter 3): Clarice notices the drawings on Hannibal's cell walls and asks about them:"Did you do the drawings on your walls, Doctor?" "Do you think I called in a decorator?" "The one over the sink is a European city?" "It's Florence. That's the Palazzo Vecchio and the Duomo, seen from the Belvedere". "Did you do it from memory, all the detail?" "Memory, Officer Starling, is what I have instead of a view".

The description of the drawing in the literary sequel (cited by Apóstolos himself): In the excerpt brought up by Apóstolos on page 12, a character contemplates the panoramic view of Florence from the Forte di Belvedere and recalls seeing a photograph of Hannibal's cell with that drawing in the background: "It was the same view he was seeing now, old Florence from the Belvedere, the best view there is. But not in color. No, a pencil drawing, shaded with charcoal... Lecter had drawn Florence from memory and the drawing was hanging in his cell in the asylum".
The Debunk
This is a textbook example of a non sequitur fallacy used to inflate VSI and CPI with empty numbers:

External panoramic view ≠ Internal museum floor plans: Both in Chapter 3 and in the Hannibal quote provided by Apóstolos himself, the drawing depicts the external view of old Florence as seen from the Belvedere viewpoint ("old Florence from the Belvedere, the best view there is").

— When you look at the Palazzo Vecchio and the Duomo from the top of the Forte di Belvedere, you see external facades, towers, domes, and tiled roofs. You do not see through stone walls to view 31 enclosed interior rooms, 53 wall maps on the second floor, or 19 busts and altars inside the cathedral—and you certainly do not transfer the interior of locked rooms onto an outdoor landscape drawing on butcher paper!

— Even more absurd is throwing "Florence Area: 102.32 km2" at the end as if Hannibal had drawn all 102 square kilometers of the modern municipal jurisdiction of Florence, when the text explicitly states it was a drawing of the historic center view ("old Florence") focused on the Palazzo Vecchio and the Duomo. It is a great feat of artistic visual memory, but trying to scale it using Wikipedia lists of interior rooms and total municipal area is pure intellectual dishonesty.
3. Clarice Starling's Psychological Profile: The "Catholic Rosary" Myth and Severe Anachronisms
What Apóstolos Argues
When analyzing the first conversation between Hannibal and Clarice in Chapter 3, Apóstolos claims that Hannibal dissected Clarice's entire life and unconscious mind in 10 to 15 minutes of Cold Reading:
"Following a brief exchange with Agent Starling, Hannibal reaches the following conclusions concerning her image, revealing her true social origins, complexes, fears and ambitions via cold reading: ... Her emotional attachment to the shoes is associated with her father, and the fact that she wears them constantly emphasises their importance and the lack of resources to replace them".
"The surname 'Starling' meaning 'starling (the bird)' - together with regional associations with farming states such as Oklahoma or West Virginia, as well as the limited choice between college and the Women's Army Corps, it points to a provincial origin in a conservative environment. Financial constraints, stemming from the early loss of her father, were the reason she joined the military".
"Agent Starling's psychological profile is defined by deep trauma connected with her father's death, which manifests itself in a sense of helplessness and emotional vulnerability, metaphorically expressed through the image of slaughtered lambs. The suppression of Catholic symbolism, in particular, the slipping rosary with small beads, indicating a modest lifestyle and the secularisation of religious practice demonstrates a conscious distancing from painful associations with her family home".
"The synthesis of regional characteristics of West Virginia and information obtained from the dialogue makes it possible for Hannibal to narrow down the location of her childhood to a few specific farms in a single area, which simplifies her identification. ... All these conclusions were reached in just 10-15 minutes of interaction with Clarice (based on the length of their conversation in the text, it can't be any longer). Keep in mind that Hannibal clearly hadn't met her before and this was their first interaction".
Primary Evidence and Context from the Work
Clarice's actual resume (Chapters 1 and 2): In Chapter 1, Jack Crawford reads Clarice's file: she never served in the military or law enforcement ("no law enforcement background"); she graduated with honors from the University of Virginia (UVA) with a double major in psychology and criminology, worked two summers in a mental health center, and qualified as a Forensic Fellow in the FBI lab prior to the Academy at Quantico.

The actual dialogue of the 1st meeting (Chapter 3): After smelling Clarice's ID card and catching the scent of Evyan skin cream and L'Air du Temps perfume from her purse, Hannibal compliments her bag and notes that it is much better than her shoes. Later, when Clarice challenges him to point his perception at himself on the questionnaire, Hannibal delivers his cold reading:"You'd like to quantify me, Officer Starling. You're so ambitious, aren't you? Do you know what you look like to me, with your good bag and your cheap shoes? You look like a rube. You're a well-scrubbed, hustling rube with a little taste. Your eyes are like cheap birthstones—all surface shine when you stalk some little answer. And you're bright behind them, aren't you? Desperate not to be like your mother. Good nutrition has given you some length of bone, but you're not more than one generation out of the mines, Officer Starling. Is it the West Virginia Starlings or the Okie Starlings, Officer? It was a toss-up between college and the opportunities in the Women's Army Corps, wasn't it? Let me tell you something specific about yourself, Student Starling. Back in your room, you have a string of gold add-a-beads and you feel an ugly little thump when you look at how tacky they are now, isn't that so? All those tedious thank-yous, permitting all that sincere fumbling, getting all sticky once for every bead. Tedious. Tedious. Bo-o-o-o-r-i-ing. Being smart spoils a lot of things, doesn't it? And taste isn't kind. When you think about this conversation, you'll remember the dumb animal hurt in his face when you got rid of him".

The narrator's note on Hannibal's misses (Chapter 4): Right at the opening of Chapter 4, as Clarice leaves the interview, the narrator notes:"Clarice Starling was excited, depleted, running on her will. Some of the things Lecter had said about her were true, and some only clanged on the truth".

How Hannibal actually finds out about Clarice's father, the ranch, and the lambs (Chapters 22, 25, and 35):

— In Chapter 22 (3rd encounter), Hannibal demands personal information in exchange for help (Quid pro quo) and asks: "What's your worst memory of childhood?". It is Clarice who answers: "The death of my father", recounting that he was a town marshal who was shot by burglars after short-shucking his pump shotgun against the door of his pickup.

— In Chapter 25 (4th encounter), Hannibal asks what happened after her father's death. Clarice tells him that her mother worked as a motel maid for two years and then sent her, at age 10, to live with relatives on a sheep and horse ranch in Montana, from which she ran away after seven months with a blind mare destined for slaughter, ending up at The Lutheran Home in Bozeman.

— In Chapter 35 (5th encounter, in Memphis), Hannibal tries to guess what made Clarice run away from the ranch that morning and guesses wrong twice in a row:"Did your foster father in Montana fuck you, Clarice?" "No". "Did he try?" "No". "What made you run away with the horse?" Only after Hannibal asks what woke her up that morning does Clarice reveal the slaughter of the lambs for the first time: "I woke up and heard the lambs screaming".
The Debunk
Apóstolos's analysis of this scene is a parade of reading comprehension errors and chronological distortions:

The "Catholic Rosary" blunder (Add-a-beads): Apóstolos claims that Hannibal deduced "the suppression of Catholic symbolism, in particular, the slipping rosary with small beads". This shows that Apóstolos has no clue what "a string of gold add-a-beads" means in American English! Add-a-beads is not a Catholic rosary; it was a secular 7mm gold bead necklace (made by Imperial Gold) worn by American teenage girls in the 70s/80s, onto which a girl added a new gold bead for every gift from a boyfriend—which is the exact reason why Hannibal talks about the "tedious thank-yous, permitting all that sincere fumbling, getting all sticky once for every bead" before she dumped her hometown boyfriend! Furthermore, Clarice wasn't even Catholic: she was raised in The Lutheran Home in Bozeman. Confusing a high-school make-out necklace with a Catholic rosary says everything you need to know about the level of "textual comprehension" in this analysis.

Clarice NEVER joined the military: Apóstolos states as an established fact that financial constraints from her father's death "were the reason she joined the military". In the novel, Clarice never joined the armed forces (she went to college at UVA); Hannibal merely threw out a rhetorical jab asking if it was a toss-up between college and the Women's Army Corps—and, as the narrator explicitly states at the start of Chapter 4, several things Hannibal said about her merely "clanged on the truth" ("some only clanged on the truth", meaning they were far from 100% accurate).

Fabrication about the shoes and her father: Not a single line in the book links Clarice's cheap shoes emotionally to her father; they simply show that she spent her savings on one good handbag to look respectable, while her shoes hadn't caught up yet.

Blatant conflation of meetings (Ch 3 vs Ch 22, 25, and 35): Apóstolos swears that Hannibal deduced her father's death, the trauma of the slaughtered lambs, and narrowed down her childhood to "a few specific farms in West Virginia" within the first 10 to 15 minutes of Chapter 3. In reality:

— The sheep and horse ranch was in Montana, not West Virginia;

— Hannibal knew absolutely nothing about her father's death or the lambs during the 1st meeting;

— Clarice verbally told him about her father's death in the 3rd meeting (ch 22), about the Montana ranch in the 4th meeting (ch 25), and about the screaming lambs in the 5th meeting (ch 35)—at which point Hannibal still guessed wrong twice in a row, thinking her foster father had molested her before she told him about the lambs!

4. "Emotional Synesthesia" and the Sammie Case: Self-Contradiction and False EP:

What Apóstolos Argues

When analyzing the Chapter 22 scene with the patient Sammie, Apóstolos tries to manufacture an "epistemologically impossible" (LMAOOO) feat of EP and synesthesia:

"Hannibal concludes that Chilton has misdiagnosed and Sammie's true diagnosis is schizophrenia with catatonic manifestations. This conclusion is based on two key implication: 1) First, an analysis of Sammie's behavior pattern: Hannibal notices that the content of the poem he wrote: 'I WANT TO GO TO JESUS' is transformed into a meaningful sequence: 'go to Jesus' to 'go with Christ', that is, a linear progression. ... He sees a causal connection between his behavior and goals, which also indicates the presence of structured thinking. The ability to cope with rhyme and express emotions (crying, an emotional reaction when reading a passage from a poem, which Hannibal deliberately provoked) also testifies to this..."

"In our case, Sammie showed no signs of catatonia, which means the following: in order to identify the signs of catatonia in Sammie, Hannibal had to associate catatonia with the smell of goat. In essence, for Hannibal, catatonia becomes a tangible object on which actions can be reproduced. He somehow manages to recreate the structure of catatonia (which is intangible by nature) in a tangible form, namely in the personification of goat sweat. ... In other words, Hannibal faces an epistemic impossibility: to create a tangible form from the nature of an intangible syndrome."

"In essence, ts is an example of an epistemologically impossible phenomenon. Hannibal EPs Sammie, a patient at the Baltimore Psychiatric Hospital suffering from schizophrenia with signs of catatonia. ... The materialisation of catatonia in the form of the goatish smell of sweat requires the reconstruction of hidden neurodynamic processes, ranging from the quantitative assessment of symptoms based on the Bush-Francis Catatonia Rating Scale to the integration of sensory, cognitive and affective components. In doing so, Hannibal overcomes an epistemological barrier whereby classical methods of empathic perception (analysis of facial expressions or prosodic characteristics of speech) are blocked as a result of catatonic stupor. Catatonia masks emotional expressions by 80-90 per cent, which makes empathic perception equivalent to 'reading' internal neural networks in the complete absence of external behavioural signals."

Primary Evidence and Context from the Work

Let's look at what actually happens in Chapter 22 when Clarice walks past Sammie's cell and talks to Hannibal:

Sammie's visible stupor: As Clarice passes the cell, Sammie is sitting motionless on the floor, his vacant stare fixed on the television and a thread of drool hanging from his mouth to his shoulder: "A man sat on the cell floor in a litter of shredded construction paper. His face was vacant. The television was reflected in his eyes and a shiny thread of spit connected the corner of his mouth and his shoulder".

The motor stimulus and emotional outburst: Hannibal says Chilton diagnosed Sammie as a "hebephrenic schizoid and irretrievably lost". He passes Sammie's poem to Clarice; when she reads it normally, Sammie does not react. Hannibal then crouches, claps time, and chants the verse fervently; instantly, Sammie leaps against the bars shouting the lines in explosive echolalia with the veins bulging in his neck ("Sammy up and mashing his face into the bars, livid and straining, the cords standing out in his neck"), has to grab his own jaw with his hand to stop its involuntary movement ("Sammie grabbed the lower part of his face and halted its movement"), and at Hannibal's command immediately subsides back to the floor in the exact same motionless stuporous posture, now visibly crying ("three streaks of silver on his face now, spit and tears").

Who actually verbalizes the clinical deduction and EP: Hannibal challenges Clarice to diagnose Sammie: "Now. See if you can apply yourself to his problem and perhaps I'll apply myself to yours. Quid pro quo". Look at who says what in the dialogue: Starling had to bear down hard. "The verse changes from 'go to Jesus' to 'go with Christ,'" she said. "That's a reasoned sequence: going to, arriving at, going with". "Yes. It's a linear progression. I'm particularly pleased that he knows 'Jesa' and 'Criez' are the same. That's progress...", "He sees a causal relationship between his behavior and his aims, that's structured thinking", Starling said. "So is the management of a rhyme. He's not blunted—he's crying. You believe he's a catatonic schizoid?" "Yes. Can you smell his sweat? That peculiar goatish odor is trans-3-methyl-2 hexenoic acid. Remember it, it's the smell of schizophrenia". "And you believe he's treatable?" "Particularly now, when he's coming out of a stuporous phase. How his cheeks shine!"

The Debunk

Apóstolos's attempt to turn this scene into "supernatural EP reading neural networks via goat sweat" fails on four elementary levels:

Comical self-contradiction regarding catatonia symptoms: On page 22 (and again on page 87), Apóstolos categorically states that "Sammie showed no signs of catatonia". Yet just two pages later (pp. 24–25), in order to inflate the difficulty of EP, he claims that classical facial reading was blocked in Sammie "as a result of catatonic stupor" and the absence of expressions due to complete motor immobility! Make up your mind, buddy: either Sammie showed no signs of catatonia, or he was in a catatonic stupor blocking facial expressions! In the book, Sammie displays literally every classic motor sign of catatonia (motionless stupor, fixed stare, sialorrhea/drooling, initial mutism, followed by sudden psychomotor excitement with echolalia, jaw dyskinesia, and return to stupor)—so much so that Hannibal himself states verbatim: "Particularly now, when he's coming out of a stuporous phase"!

— Sammie's emotions were perceived visually and audibly, not via smell: Apóstolos claims that "Hannibal EPs Sammie" in the "complete absence of external behavioural signals" because catatonia masks 80–90% of emotional expressions. Did Apóstolos even read the page? After Hannibal chants the poem, Sammie screams at the top of his lungs with his neck veins bulging and then sits down with tears literally streaming down his cheeks ("three streaks of silver on his face now, spit and tears" / "How his cheeks shine!")! Perceiving that a man is emotional and not affectively blunted because he just screamed and has visible tears rolling down his face is basic visual and auditory Emotional Perception (EP), not "decoding latent emotions through invisible neurophysiology"!

— Clarice made the Emotional Perception and the diagnosis before any mention of smell: Apóstolos takes Clarice's line ("He sees a causal relationship between his behavior and his aims, that's structured thinking... He's not blunted—he's crying") and writes it on page 21 as if it were Hannibal's internal thought. In the book, it is Clarice who perceives his tears, analyzes these elements, and asks if Sammie is a catatonic schizoid before Hannibal ever mentions his sweat! If a trainee without "supernatural synesthesia" performed that exact EP and clinical diagnosis using only the poem and Sammie's visible tears, where is the "epistemological impossibility"? Lol.

Smell of schizophrenia ≠ Materialization of catatonia: Hannibal explicitly states that trans-3-methyl-2-hexenoic acid is "the smell of schizophrenia" (the odor of general schizophrenia, per the real 1969 paper cited by Apóstolos himself), and not the specific smell of catatonia. Chilton already knew Sammie was schizophrenic (he diagnosed him as a hebephrenic schizoid); correcting the hebephrenic subtype to the catatonic subtype came 100% from observing his stuporous phase, the structured thinking in his poem, and the visible tears on his face.

5. The "Butterfly Inference" Hoax, the Imago, and the Omission of Chap
ter 26

What Apóstolos Argues
We now arrive at the core of Apóstolos's doc. He claims that Hannibal deduced Buffalo Bill's entire psychological profile (butterfly metamorphosis, sewing a skin suit out of real women, false transsexualism, the imago concept, and rejection at surgical clinics) from a single marker (the insect in the West Virginia victim's throat), without having read the case files and without ever having met Jame Gumb:
"During an investigation in West Virginia, the body of a girl with an insect in her mouth is found. As Clarice notes, this fact was not reported in the media. Hannibal assumes that it is a butterfly, which is later proven to be correct. During a conversation with Clarice, he helps to determine the psychoportrait of a serial killer known as Buffalo Bill, and, based on the butterfly symbolism left at the crime scene, he establishes his location, coming to the real name of Buffalo Bill: Hannibal notices that Buffalo Bill symbolizes himself with a butterfly, which represents metamorphosis and transformation. The symbolism of the cocoon implies an intermediate state during which the caterpillar turns into a butterfly, which Bill interprets as a desire to transform into a woman. He makes himself a girl's costume - from real girls".
"What is especially impressive about this analysis is the fact that Hannibal was able to come to such profound conclusions without having access to the Buffalo Bill case material. The main complexity of the feat stems from one seemingly insignificant but conceptually game-changing detail mentioned in the novel by Hannibal himself (At the moment when he makes a butterfly inference): ... 'That's all I want to say, I think, until I've read the case. You will leave it with me.'"
"Hannibal had only Raspail's information at his disposal; provided only one or two clear and significant marker (Jame Gumb's obsession with butterflies and his expression in murders), which is insufficient on its own".
"Why do I say 'not yet fully formed' if he had already killed? Well, before he became the full-fledged serial killer known as Buffalo Bill, Jame Gumb' murders were senseless and purposeless. It was Frederic Bimmel (officially recognised by the FBI as BB's first victim) who was murdered for the purpose of obtaining skin for a suit. It was this murder that constituted the full manifestation of his unconscious patterns, which until that moment had been hidden and inactive. ... Bear in mind that Hannibal discovered the imago before its full manifestation, before Frederic Bimmel's murder, whilst it was still in an inactive state".
Based on this premise of "epistemological deficit", Apóstolos even sets up a Bayes' Theorem equation (P(H|E)) on page 35 to argue that associating a butterfly (E) with the desire for sex reassignment (H) from a single data point has "almost zero statistical mass" and goes beyond human cognition.
Primary Evidence and Context from the Work (The Chapter 26 Apóstolos Hid)
Why is this entire thesis a fraud? Because Apóstolos completely omitted Chapter 26 of the book, where the narrator enters Hannibal's mind and reveals exactly what he knew since 1975. Let's reconstruct the canonical sequence across Chapters 3, 18, 19, 22, 25, and 26:

What Hannibal already knew about the current crimes (Chapters 3 and 22): In Chapter 3, Hannibal already knew from the papers and from Clarice that Buffalo Bill had killed five women and flayed them all ("All flayed?" — "Partially, yes" / "because he skins his humps"). In Chapter 22, Clarice tells him that the sixth victim in West Virginia was heavy, wide-hipped, flat-chested, shot in the chest, and had an insect inserted in her throat.

Jack Crawford's analysis of Hannibal's prior knowledge (Chapters 18 and 19): When Johns Hopkins examiners find a moth cocoon in the throat of Klaus's head (killed in 1975 and stored in Raspail's car), Crawford immediately realizes that Klaus's killer in 1975 is the same Buffalo Bill operating now, and that Hannibal already knows concretely who he is. However, Crawford warns Clarice in Chapter 19: Hannibal will never admit that he is merely "snitching" on someone he knew about through Raspail, because his ego requires pretending he figured it all out through pure theoretical brilliance:"He offered to help, Starling. He didn't offer to snitch Snitching wouldn't give him enough of a chance to show off. ... So Dr. Lecter has to think we're coming to him strictly for theory and insight". When Clarice asks how Hannibal can steer them to Buffalo Bill while pretending to use only "theory and insight" without showing he has special prior knowledge of the man, Crawford replies: "I don't know, Starling. He's had a long time to think about it. He's waited through six victims".

Hannibal's internal memory in Chapter 26 (The Smoking Gun): In Chapter 25, Hannibal puts on the exact performance predicted by Crawford: he talks about the chrysalis and the imago, says "Billy" is sewing a girl suit out of real girls, describes him as a sizable white male under 35 with a history of childhood violence and juvenile internment, suggests checking rejected applicants at the three sex-reassignment clinics, and says: "That's all I want to say, I think, until I've read the case. You will leave it with me". Right at the opening of Chapter 26, while strapped upright during the cleaning of his cell, Hannibal recalls Benjamin Raspail's final therapy session (in March 1975), revealing where all of that information actually came from:"Dr. Lecter was musing on how he would give Jame Gumb to Clarice Starling, and it was useful to remember Raspail. Here was the fat flutist on the last day of his life, lying on Lecter's therapy couch, telling him about Jame Gumb: ... 'I'd told him I simply couldn't put up with his behavior, and Klaus had just come into my life, of course. Jame is not really gay, you know, it's just something he picked up in jail. He's not anything, really, just a sort of total lack that he wants to fill, and so angry. You always felt the room was a little emptier when he came in. I mean he killed his grandparents when he was twelve, you'd think a person that volatile would have some presence, wouldn't you? ...
