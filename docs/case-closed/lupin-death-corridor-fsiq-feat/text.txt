in the lupin iii 2019 movie, lupin’s crew comes across the  death corridor, an enormous structure with a circular chamber. there are 2x2 holes— 2 circular & 2 lung shaped holes — within a patch that constantly move along the walls, floor, and ceiling & emit lasers upon detecting movement
there are 5,500,000 lasers within the death corridor

each laser in the corridor has two possible binary states: off & on
each laser’s state is independent of the others, so the total number of possible combinations of laser states can calculated as  2ⁿ, where n  is the number of lasers
for 5,500,000 lasers, this becomes 2⁵⁵⁰⁰⁰⁰⁰ possible configurations of on/off states for the lasers across the entire corridor

argument for validity:

essentially, this feat can be identified as a cognitive feat— indicated by:
* lupin closing his eyes, taking time to process and think

* after processing, he becomes confident that he can safely transverse the corridor compared to what how he was feeling before about the corridor

* after processing, he performed the feat without needing to look at lasers whereas he initially had to look and consciously dodge the lasers before processing

* throughout the series, lupin when it comes to bypassing security has been shown consistently to think about possible outcomes. here and here are the fewest of examples. therefore it’s likely lupin in character must have thought of all possible outcomes before traversing the death corridor
this feat is then narrowed down further to lupin processing the configurations as lupin couldn't:
* pattern recog
* simply memorise the laser layout
* solely visualise a way through the corridor

these will be addressed in the contention section below

some might argue that:

1. lupin didn’t need to process 2⁵⁵⁰⁰⁰⁰⁰ configurations of the death corridor’s lasers. instead, he could have only memorised the positions of 1,100,000 lasers per second
this argument is inconsistent with the dynamic of the death corridor
processing out lasers would suffice only if the laser beams are constantly fixed to specific positions and sadly, the lasers aren't constantly fixed to specific position, shown by
* the laser layout is constantly shifting (clip 1)

* lasers adapting to lupin's traversal, meaning the laser can reconfigure on his position, trajectory, and speed, creating new hazards (clip 1 & 2)

therefore memorising the laser layout wouldn't suffice and would result in lupin unexpectedly being fried

processing possible laser configurations would suffice only if the laser beams can move around, or shift in response to lupin’s movements or the system’s design and thats shown to be the case
therefore, lupin couldn't have only mapped out a snapshot of the contemporary positions and instead look at the possible configurations to determine the safest route through the adapting corridor
2. lupin could have relied on pattern recognition or shortcuts instead of solely processing

this argument is inconsistent with the context of the scene. after lupin throws jigen’s hat to test the lasers, lupin closes his eyes, removing any external visual information
lupin closing his eyes signals a shift from external observation to internal processing implies more that lupin is mentally simulating the entire laser maze to work out the safest path as opposed to using his vision to track the holes for a weakpoint
this argument also falls victim to the counterargument shown in 1.

3. lupin could have solely visualised a way through the corridor instead of processing configurations

that alternative explanation gets outweighed by many arguments:

1. visualisation alone relies on a fixed sequence of movements, meaning lupin’s success would depend on the environment remaining unchanged but the lasers still shift positions, meaning his pre-planned route would immediately become invalid

2. lupin got distracted by his grandfather’s cane and got caught by a laser but immediately corrected his movement without hesitation and without looking at the lasers. if lupin had only visualised a fixed path, the distraction would have completely thrown off his execution, forcing him to stop and reassess or possibly get fried

3. lupin’s statement “i can do this” strongly suggests he had reached a level of certainty beyond mere visualization

visualization alone wouldn’t guarantee success. picturing a path, still doesn't account for unexpected changes in the laser patterns that could disrupt his route

instead, his confidence indicates he had fully comprehended the system rather than half heartedly visualise the layout

this confidence wasn’t based on assumption and was based on a deep, systematic grasp of how the corridor functioned
4. most of the total configurations lupin processed are probably easy to rule out (for instance, one configuration could have the first laser on while all others off)

this is a valid concern however there are 2 counterarguments to this:
1. SIMPLE CONFIGURATIONS CAN STILL BE APART OF COMPLEX CONFIGURATIONS
this argument assumes that most configurations lupin processed are simple and can be ruled out. however, this overlooks the fact that even seemingly “simple” configurations can still be part of a larger, non-simple sequence that lupin must navigate
for example, a simple setup with only one laser activated may quickly transition into a much more complex arrangement of laser, requiring lupin to anticipate how the pattern evolves
