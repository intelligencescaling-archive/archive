Richtofen Ultimi Feats

Richtofen's Psychological Torture and the Use of Teddy Bears

During his time within the Nazi regime and his work with Group 935, Richtofen did not limit himself to conventional scientific experimentation. From early on, he developed a clear inclination toward psychological torture as a method of obtaining information and domination. Unlike other officers or scientists in his environment, his interest was not solely in physical pain, but in the prior mental destruction of the victim.

Although the records do not detail with precision the exact methods he employed, there are clear indications that Richtofen deeply understood the impact of fear, discomfort, and anticipation on the human mind. For him, torture did not begin with the act itself, but much earlier: in the emotional preparation of the victim.

This is where the teddy bears come into play.

Richtofen used to appear accompanied by these childish objects during interrogations and executions. Apparently harmless, their presence was deeply disturbing in a context of violence, confinement, and despair. This contrast was not accidental. The use of the teddy bears fulfilled a precise and calculated psychological function.

First, they served to break the victim's emotional logic. An object associated with childhood, safety, and innocence, deliberately placed in an environment of terror, generated extreme dissonance. The victim could not properly process what was happening, which facilitated confusion, anxiety, and loss of emotional control. In that state, mental resistance decreased considerably, making it easier to extract information.

Second, the teddy bears functioned as an instrument of intimidation prior to death. Richtofen did not seek quick or neutral executions. He wanted the victim's final mental state to be absolute fear, devoid of any consolation. The teddy bear, as a symbol of childish protection, transformed into a silent mockery: a total denial of any possibility of a "peaceful" or dignified death.

This behavior fits perfectly with Richtofen's psychology. He did not only want practical results; he wanted to exert total control over his victims' emotional experience. For him, fear was not a byproduct of the process, but part of the experiment.

Moreover, this early stage reveals something important: Richtofen already understood that true power was not in physical force, but in the mind. Psychological torture, the symbolic use of teddy bears, and anticipatory emotional humiliation clearly foreshadow his later evolution, where mental manipulation, soul control, and psychological domination become the pillars of his grander plans.

This feat is not defined by explicit cruelty, but by psychological sophistication. Richtofen used symbols, discomfort, and anticipated fear as tools as effective as any physical method, demonstrating from early on an advanced understanding of the human mind, an absolute lack of empathy, and considerable use of psychoanalysis.

Shangri-La Feat

When Richtofen was transported to Shangri-La, he immediately recognized that the location was not a common excavation site. The area was directly affected by a large concentration of Element 115, which caused constant temporal anomalies. This is confirmed by the radios and by the structure of the map itself, where time resets cyclically.

Richtofen managed to adapt quickly to the environment. Unlike other scientists, he did not attempt to impose immediate control; instead, he first observed the behavior of the place and understood that Element 115 there did not function solely as an energy source, but as a catalyst for temporal distortion. This early understanding of the phenomenon is a central part of the feat.

Upon his arrival, he came into contact with the local tribes, who were impulsive toward him and viewed him as a threat. These communities were technologically underdeveloped and lacked the scientific knowledge to explain the anomalies occurring in Shangri-La. Richtofen took advantage of this knowledge gap. He introduced modern technology in a limited and controlled manner, which allowed him to position himself as a figure of authority. He did so carefully, however, since this tribe was dangerous, meaning that this divine-like figure had to be benevolent to avoid rebellions, lack of cooperation, or in the worst case, death. Although there is no explicit confirmation that he proclaimed himself a god, it is consistent with the context that he was perceived as a supernatural or superior figure due to his technological mastery.

Through this cultural and psychological manipulation, Richtofen managed to get the local inhabitants to cooperate in the exploration of the area, especially in zones near the Element 115 meteorite. This allowed him to obtain crucial information about the element and about the functioning of the location’s temporal loop, without directly exposing himself to the greatest risks of the environment.

The feat does not consist solely of manipulating people, but in Richtofen’s ability to exploit a completely unknown environment. Shangri-La did not obey normal temporal laws, and yet he was able to study it, use it, and extract valuable knowledge from it. This event expanded his understanding of Element 115 as an element capable of altering not only matter, but time itself.

Note: It should be clarified that Richtofen used signs, aura, and technology to achieve this, since he did not speak the same language as the tribe and verbal communication was impossible.

It is essential to understand that not everyone who comes into contact with 115 hears Richtofen. The element appears to act as a conduit, amplifying existing vulnerabilities. While Element 115 and Richtofen’s ethereal form provide the means for communication, they do not fully explain why Samuel, and not the other members of the Green Run crew, is the chosen vessel. The key likely lies in Samuel’s individual psychological profile. Dialogue in the games hints at a past riddled with mental instability and possible criminal activity. Samuel often makes cryptic remarks, showing a fascination with violence and a detached sense of morality.

This preexisting fragility likely made Samuel far more susceptible to Richtofen’s influence than his companions. Richtofen, with his manipulative and controlling personality, likely sensed this weakness and exploited it. He targeted Samuel not only because of his exposure to 115, but also because he knew Samuel was the easiest to control and manipulate for his own ends.

Richtofen’s Engineering of Element 115:

During the events of Origins, the discovery of Element 115 completely changed the research direction of Group 935. For most, it was an energy source. For Richtofen, it was the missing piece needed to understand how the Aether truly functioned.

From the very first contact, Richtofen noticed that 115 did not behave like a common physical resource. Its properties varied depending on the environment, the state of the crystal, and prolonged exposure. This led him to his first major conclusion: 115 did not merely power machines; it altered the very laws those machines obeyed.

Several stages were carried out for the collection of information and the use of this element, which I will now enumerate.

P1: The use of 115 as an energy source

Richtofen’s first objective was to stabilize the mineral in order to obtain a constant energy flow. The process was straightforward, but extremely dangerous:

1. Isolate the crystal in hermetically sealed chambers.

2. Regulate radiation emissions to prevent 115 from contaminating the environment.

3. Purify it to extract an “energy core” capable of powering any device.

This core would later serve as the foundation for all Wunder technology.

P2: Construction of weapons based on 115

Richtofen understood that 115 reacted differently depending on the structure into which it was integrated. This idea led him to experiment with metallic containment chambers, coils, and firing mechanisms.

The creation process was linear:

1. Purification of the crystal.

2. Insertion of 115 into a sealed chamber capable of withstanding the dimensional pressure it generated.

3. Channeling through reinforced conduits to direct the energy toward the firing system.

4. Stabilization of the energy flow to prevent it from fracturing the weapon or the user.

Through this procedure, the Wunderwaffe DG-2, the Ray Gun, and later even more unstable devices such as the Gersch Device and the QED were created.

In other words, Richtofen did not build traditional weapons; he constructed mechanisms designed to contain phenomena produced by 115.

P3: Creation of the giant robots

The colossal robots would not have been possible without Richtofen. Maxis designed the blueprints, but no conventional system could move structures of that size. Once again, the key was 115.

The process was nearly identical to that of a Wunder weapon, but on a much larger scale:

1. A massive 115 core as the heart of the robot.

2. A network of metallic conductors designed to contain the energy.
