Why distribution theory  is needed in quantum field theory
Distribution theory, also known as the theory of generalized functions, is a
mathematical framework that extends the notion of classical functions to
include objects that are not well-behaved in the traditional sense. These
objects, called distributions or generalized functions, are used to represent
certain types of singular or discontinuous functions that arise in various
branches of physics, including Quantum Field Theory (QFT).
There are some important aspects where it is required and is inherently
fundamental within Quantum Field Theory.
1)Handling Singularities:
Quantum Field Theory involves calculations that often lead to expressions
containing singularities, such as delta functions or distributions arising from
divergent integrals.

Distributions provide a rigorous way to handle these singularities, allowing
physicists to manipulate and evaluate expressions involving them without
encountering mathematical inconsistencies.
2) Regularization and Renormalization:
Distributions play a crucial role in regularization and renormalization techniques
in QFT. These techniques are used to deal with divergent integrals that arise in
perturbative calculations.
By redefining physical parameters (such as masses and charges) through
renormalization, physicists can remove infinities from physical observables,
ensuring that QFT predictions are finite and meaningful.

3) Field Operators:
In QFT, fields are represented by field operators that create and annihilate
particles at different points in spacetime. These operators are often
distribution-valued, particularly in interacting theories.
Distributions provide a way to define these operators rigorously, even when
they act on states that do not belong to the usual Hilbert space of quantum
states.
4)Feynman Diagrams and Scattering Amplitudes:
Feynman diagrams are graphical representations of perturbative calculations in
QFT, used to visualize and compute scattering amplitudes. These calculations
often involve integrals containing distributions.
Distributions are used to handle loop integrals and vertex functions appearing in
Feynman diagrams, ensuring that the resulting scattering amplitudes are well-
defined and finite
In summary, distributions are indispensable tools in Quantum Field Theory,
used to handle singularities, regularization, renormalization, define field
operators, and ensure the consistency and rigor of QFT calculations. They
provide a rigorous mathematical framework for dealing with the mathematical
complexities of QFT and obtaining meaningful and accurate predictions.
