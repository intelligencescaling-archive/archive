Kramer set up a game revolving around Amanda Young, a self-destructive individual. Her game was this: She had a reverse bear trap (which Kramer created) strapped to her head. The only way to get the key is to stab into a man’s stomach and pull it out within a certain timeframe. She was unaware the man was still alive, simply paralyzed. Amanda completes her game and survives, then, a puppet rolls out and talks to her. The puppet (named Billy) states that “so many people are ungrateful to be alive”, but not her. “Not anymore”. Kramer then emotionally appeals to Amanda like this to recruit her as a Jigsaw disciple. Amanda viewed Kramer as a father figure here, likely as an example of transference (a phenomenon in psychology where one will redirect feelings of an unresolved figure in their life onto a newer figure, in this case being a father/authority figure). Amanda feeling indentured towards Kramer especially after such a violent test showcases examples of trauma bonding and the Stockholm syndrome. Amanda’s immense pain from the game resulted in her seeking refuge within Kramer as a leader or father figure, believing the test truly was “rehabilitation”. Kramer successfully implored these psychological phenomenons to manipulate Amanda and recruit her as a disciple. Amanda is then picked up and taken to a police station (at the same time as Dr. Gordon is questioned, which Kramer planned). Amanda states that Jigsaw’s game worked, and she feels rebirthed (confirming Kramer’s manipulation methods worked). Amanda’s game acts as a recruitment device set up by Kramer, which Kramer later utilizes his newly established connection to start recruiting Gordon as an apprentice at the station. It is also shown in a later film that Amanda helped Kramer physically set up the bathroom trial, implying Amanda was already at least semi-recruited by the time she arrived at the station (further strengthening the theory that Gordon was always intended to be a possible disciple for Jigsaw).

Within Saw (2004), we see death games occur (notably two). Before the main game/trial is started, a pen belonging to Dr. Gordon (a main character) is found at the scene of one of these death games. Dr. Gordon is brought into custody and questioned, where he is shown Amanda Young, a survivor of a Jigsaw game.
We learn in a later film that Kramer had Hoffman (one of his disciples) plant the pen at the scene. However, what was Kramer’s thought process behind this action?
Well, first of all, it murkies the waters of the investigation. Kramer knew Gordon’s only alibi would relate to his affair, effectively trapping Gordon, prolonging the investigators’ actions. Second of all, Kramer knew Gordon would be shown Amanda. How do we know this? Well, later on, it’s revealed Dr. Gordon became Kramer’s secret disciple after the first film. Kramer’s first step in recruiting him was to show Gordon the effects of his “games”. By showing Gordon the case of Amanda Young (a successful trial according to Kramer), it slightly plants the idea in Gordon that Jigsaw’s methods work and are beneficial.
How did Kramer know Amanda would be the one the investigators showed Gordon, though? Well, that’s simple. Kramer’s last couple of trials were failures, resulting in death. He reasoned Amanda would be the one they showed Gordon as, simply put, she’s the only one they logically COULD. It’s also worth noting it’s highly likely Amanda was recruited by Kramer at this time (as in flashbacks in later installments we see Amanda actually helped Kramer set up the bathroom trial as a whole) making this even more planned and deliberate. Kramer feeling the need to acquire a new recruit (Gordon) to counter Hoffman is also consistent, as it’s revealed he set up the Gordon contingency directly after the two had officially started working together. Why would Kramer only see through Hoffman at the end of the film opposed to before, despite never seeing Hoffman after the pen plant for the entirety of the bathroom trial?
Kramer explicitly needed Gordon as he was planning on using him as a contingency against Hoffman (a disciple Kramer saw through and knew he was going to betray him). Gordon’s ultimate purpose by the end of the series was as a contingency in case of Jill Tuck’s (Kramer’s ex-wife) death. Meaning, Kramer predicted Hoffman would evade his test and kill Tuck even in the first film, as he started to anticipate it. By having Hoffman plant the pen, it eliminates the idea in Hoffman’s head that Kramer could be plotting something against him. As Hoffman simply reasons Kramer is trying to murky the information the investigators have, which Kramer understood. The proof of this emotional perception/reading is that Kramer kept Gordon a secret from Hoffman ever since he was officially recruited, meaning Kramer always intended to use him as a contingency against Hoffman. Kramer needed to conceal this from Hoffman, as Kramer knew he still needed to acquire information on the police force (specifically Detective Tapp) from Hoffman.

So, in conclusion, Kramer (by having Hoffman plant Gordon’s pen), achieved multiple things:
* Murkies the waters of the investigation (prolongs the investigation, assures by making sure Gordon’s only alibi would paint him as a cheater)
* Slowly begin to build Gordon as an apprentice to use as a contingency against Hoffman (ensured via Amanda prediction at the station)
* Embedded Gordon as a suspect in the Jigsaw investigation, making sure Tapp kept tabs on him as a result. This later resulted in Tapp turning to Adam to take pictures of Gordon after being discharged due to another one of Kramer’s plans (connecting Gordon and Adam while also implementing Tapp in the game, making sure Tapp has tabs on Gordon at all times)
* Conceals this from Hoffman himself by having HIM plant the pen (it is in character for Kramer to lie straight in front of Hoffman, as in Saw 4 Kramer blatantly ignores Gordon’s existence as an apprentice in the tape assigned to Hoffman), making Hoffman think Kramer trusts him and doesn’t suspect him, while misleading him with the framing aspects of the pen plant
This action Kramer took was extremely adaptable, as even if Gordon didn’t become an apprentice (unlikely given Kramer’s sheer lengths of the recruitment/manipulation methods), he still at least distorted the investigation and successfully concealed his counteraction of Hoffman from Hoffman himself. It fundamentally has no potential to harm Kramer, making Kramer’s risk calculation here incredible. Fundamentally, bare minimum, Kramer achieves a slight halt in the investigation against him, and at most, Kramer achieves four objectives at the same time. However, Kramer has no risk involved due to how he executed this action. This step also sets up his later actions in the film, effectively building his strategy.

The main trial in Saw (2004) is an extremely well-crafted plan devised by Kramer. I’ll start by providing the people featured in this game and their position:
* Dr. Gordon- Gordon is chained to a pipe in a bathroom. Gordon is a doctor who will be later recruited by Kramer. Gordon has been having an affair, and thus, he is in this game. Gordon is instructed that if he doesn’t kill Adam by the time the time is up, his family will die.
* Adam- Adam is chained to a pipe in the same bathroom as Gordon. Adam is a photographer. Specifically, he catches people doing bad things for a payment. He usually does this without a regard of morals, thus his placement in the game. Adam has taken pictures of Gordon, catching his affair.
FOR BOTH GORDON AND ADAM, THE ONLY PLAUSIBLE WAY TO FREE THEMSELVES FROM THE CHAINS IS TO CUT OFF THEIR FOOT (WHICH THE CHAIN IS BOUNDED TO).
[NOTE: The chains that bind Gordon and Adam are able to electrocute them via a remote. This was implemented as Kramer predicted Gordon and Adam would attempt to deceive Zepp by turning off the lights of the bathroom and fabricating Gordon’s victory within the dark. Kramer supplied Zepp with the remote to ensure this deception would never occur as a contingency]
* Zepp- Zepp is a hospital worker from the same hospital as Gordon. Zepp has a poison coursing through his veins that can only be stopped with an antidote Kramer has. Zepp’s role is to monitor the game via cameras near the bathroom (fitting the psychological profile of Jigsaw having a “front seat” to his games, meaning Kramer deduced the exact deductions the police force made on him) and to kill both Gordon (and Gordon’s family) and Adam if they do not escape their chains within a certain time period. Zepp is constantly monitoring Gordon and Adam, and is required to wear a Jigsaw cloak. Why? Well, Kramer knew the only info Tapp really gathered from their encounter was the cloak, so by placing it on Zepp, he disguises Zepp as Jigsaw (administering a poison to Zepp also matches the profile of Jigsaw being sick). This gives more incentive for Tapp to eliminate him. This also deceives Gordon and Adam into believing Zepp is Jigsaw. This works even better, as according to Tapp’s resources, he could deduce Zepp and Gordon work together, making it plausible Zepp was the one who planted the pen to frame Gordon and he is Jigsaw overalls Zepp has no idea Tapp is a part of this game.
* Gordon’s family- His wife and daughter are unknowing players in this game. They are apprehended and captured by Zepp, used as motivation to get Gordon to kill Adam and escape from his chains (possibly implemented to ensure Gordon’s survival, due to his later recruitment as a Jigsaw apprentice). Zepp will kill them if Gordon does not escape his chains in the time limit.
* Tapp- Detective Tapp acts as an adversity to Zepp in this game. Tapp is a discharged detective, and has been trying to catch Jigsaw. Tapp hired Adam to get pictures of Gordon (due to the fact Gordon was a suspect due to the planted pen). This means Tapp will be closely monitoring Gordon’s house, where Zepp will strike.
* Jigsaw- Kramer or “Jigsaw” disguises himself as a dead man in the middle of the bathroom, using makeup to appear as if he was shot in the head. Kramer took muscle relaxers to further the realism to deceive Gordon and Adam. Kramer did this to have a front seat to the game, allowing him to more accurately control (and later end) the entire game.

ADAM’S ROLE INVINCIBILITY:

The only way Adam could’ve escaped his chain without cutting his foot off, was the key Adam had at the start of the game when he woke up in the bathtub (the key was flushed down the drain due to Adam’s aggressive mannerisms when he woke up). It is revealed in a later film that Amanda rigged this portion of the game, meaning Adam was destined to forever be stuck in his chain for the entire game if he ever found a way to slice his foot off. However, I’m going to explain why Kramer’s entire plan wasn’t hindered nor hinged on this aspect.

Even if Adam’s key didn’t go down the drain and Adam freed himself, the front door likely wouldn’t be unlocked from the inside. This is because Gordon’s goal was to kill Adam before the timer went off, THEN Zepp would’ve intervened. This implies the door wouldn’t be unlocked 24/7 during this game, and the only way to escape the room would be to let Zepp enter it. This would naturally result in Adam needing to wait in the room to ambush Zepp. After this, Adam would likely leave the room and go find help. However this would leave Kramer and Gordon alone in the bathroom, and thus Kramer would simply reveal himself and recruit Gordon all the same. If Adam took Gordon with him, Zepp is still simply able to scapegoat for Kramer as both Adam and Gordon were under the impression Zepp was Jigsaw. If Gordon still manages to shoot Adam here, the timer would’ve still gone off as it did in the original film. Depending on the severity of the shot and whether or not it kills Adam would result in whether or not Zepp kills Gordon or not (as it would impact if Adam surprise attacks Zepp or not), fundamentally defaulting to Adam’s original purpose in the trial regardless.
If Adam somehow managed to cut off his foot (unlikely due to the rusted saw he was given by Kramer), the outcome wouldn’t change almost at all. Adam would still need to wait for Zepp in order to escape the room, Gordon would still have the goal of killing him (obviously), and hence the events of the film continue as originally seen. The only difference between —>
1. Adam frees himself from the chain, gaining the most mobility to move around the bathroom, fight Zepp (as the door cannot be opened without Zepp coming in to kill Gordon after the timer runs out), and later be given the chance to leave to look for help (which would still leave Gordon and Kramer alone in the bathroom, thus Kramer would recruit Gordon all the same). Even if Adam helps Gordon escape and leaves together, both are under the deception that Zepp is Jigsaw and thus Kramer’s identity is successfully concealed.
2. Adam cuts his foot off to escape his chain, gaining a bit more mobility but still heavily hindered. In this case, he would still need to ambush Zepp, and Gordon’s goals would remain. In this outcome, Adam would 100% be unable to save Gordon, so either way him and Gordon must split paths to find help. This guarantees Kramer will be able to recruit Gordon.
