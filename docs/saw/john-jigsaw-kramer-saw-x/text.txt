OVERVIEW
BACKGROUND INFORMATION: John Kramer, better known as Jigsaw, is the primary antagonist of the “Saw” series, which spans multiple films, video games, and a short comic. Jigsaw crafts complex games to test his subjects morally, trying to rehabilitate them via death games and mechanisms. Each game is specifically tailored to the subject he chose, and every game can be beaten, signifying the subject has been “reborn” by victory. This document will only cover Saw X, the second film in the series to occur chronologically.

To do so, Kramer converged three core principles within his method —>
1. By placing Diego in a situation that offers such a grave outcome as death if failure is a result, whilst providing the information that the reason Diego is in this situation is due to his affiliation with Cecilia (as a consequence), it psychologically tethers Cecilia to the situation at hand within the driver’s mind and causes him to detach from her emotionally (Kramer facilitated the driver’s emotions of blaming Cecilia for such an intense situation by associating the concept of death with his affiliation with her). This shows Kramer had applied of the concept of “Classical Conditioning”, which is an unconscious learning process where a previously neutral/positive stimuli (in this case being the Diego’s connection to Cecilia) becomes associated with a naturally occurring one within the subject’s brain (being the concept of death in this case). Kramer applies this basic psychological concept to associate Cecilia with the consequence of possible death within Diego and emotionally separate Diego from Cecilia, thus coaxing him into becoming his ally. (Source — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK470326/)
2. Kramer then confronts Diego after succeeding in his test, appealing to him emotionally. Diego now feels “betrayed” by Cecilia due to Kramer’s conditioning. Kramer, being a scammed patient of Cecilia’s, also innately feels betrayed. By causing this recognition within Diego, Kramer induces a mutual emotion between him and the driver (further emotionally separating Diego from Cecilia whilst also forming a connection with the driver by inducing mutual understanding, an aspect more potent for Diego in the current moment due to his intense feeling of being betrayed by Cecilia due to Kramer’s conditioning, seeking refuge in Kramer due to the induced feeling of mutual emotional distress).
3. Kramer’s manipulation of Diego is also further reinforced by the concept of “Trauma Bonding” in psychology, a principle that states a subject may become overly attached to an abuser or one who inflicted great pain/harm upon them (whether physically or mentally). It’s highly likely this response was triggered within Diego due to the severe pain and intensity Kramer had directed onto him via the game. (Source — https://www.sandstonecare.com/blog/trauma-bonding/)
These three psychological factors converged into one manipulation method, showcasing how Kramer psychologically ensured that Diego would become an ally upon succeeding in his test/game (which also displays how potent the method actually was). KRAMER’S REASONING: Kramer reasoned that the likelihood of Cecilia calling the police at the prospect of an intruder (Amanda or Kramer being seen via the cameras) was incredibly unlikely due to the fact Cecilia’s operation was highly illegal and she wouldn’t naturally want to draw any legal attention to herself by calling the police. By reasoning Cecilia wouldn’t be able to call the police, Kramer predicted her only route of escape would be her car/garage, of which he then targeted in his strategy. Kramer’s reasoning process (summarized):
Cecilia is cautious of law enforcement (psychoanalysis) —> Cecilia won’t call law enforcement if presented with an invader —> This indicates Cecilia’s only option would be to escape via her car/garage —> Target that escape route (the car/garage) in the strategy.

1. If Cecilia hadn’t paid attention to the cameras, the result would’ve been the exact same. 3. Amanda moving to the front of the garage after shattering the skylight ensures that even if Cecilia didn’t get into her car and simply tried to exit via the garage door, she’d still be met by Amanda and subdued all the same. Amanda standing in front of the garage also ensured Cecilia wouldn’t be able to drive away without killing or injuring Amanda, which would draw legal attention to Cecilia which would cause her operation to combust (shielding this strategy even more so).
Overall, Kramer foresaw Cecilia’s only two options of counteraction (law enforcement and her car) and enacted a strategy that takes advantage of this by cutting both options off and countering Cecilia’s extremely high surveillance, instead turning the cameras into an advantage for his strategy. TAXI DRIVER’S EYES: Kramer was able to memorize the taxi driver’s eyes to such a high extent that he was able to associate them with a pair of eyes he saw whilst suffering from a drug-induced fugue state. Anesthesia also heavily hinders a subject’s vision via transient confusion, making the memorization of perspectives or details incredibly difficult. Despite this, Kramer was able to associate a pair of eyes he saw for mere seconds during the surgery (whilst under anesthesia) with a pair of eyes he saw in the taxi (these eyes specifically were only viewed for less than a couple of seconds as well and were obscured by sunglasses). (Source — https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/anesthesia-impact-on-memory#other-side-effects)

This showing itself indicates how impressive Kramer’s memorization abilities actually are, being capable or memorizing even the smallest of details under inhuman circumstances and only given a small timeframe to execute the memorization, and he enacted all of this with passive memory, due to the fact he wasn’t actively trying to recall or note these details in both situations.
