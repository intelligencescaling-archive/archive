Jigsaw’s six-layered framing (SAW)

In SAW III/IV through SAW 3D, we see a plot-line concerning law enforcement (specifically the FBI) attempting to reason out who the Jigsaw apprentice outside of Amanda Young truly is. According to the timeline, this plot-line lasts for roughly six months and revolves around Kramer’s six-layered framing of multiple individuals - all interwoven into a tight web, where each layer is fabricated as having framed the previous one. Kramer enacted this scheme on his deathbed, with a majority of it occurring months after his death.

**[ LAYER #1 —> ERIC MATTHEWS ]**

- In SAW II, Kramer had indirectly manipulated Eric into abducting Kramer and carrying him away from law enforcement’s grasp - he did so through a fabricated Jigsaw trial framed to appear as though it was running concurrently, utilizing Eric’s son (a subject in the falsified trail - who was in no real danger at the moment) as leverage. Eric is then not seen in public for several months, which contradicts Kramer’s usual pattern of victim abduction (at most it’ll be a couple of days if not hours between the abduction and the actual test - as seen by Dr. Lynn, Amanda Young, Jeff Denlon, etc).

- Eric’s actions, in retrospect, strongly appear as though he was helping Kramer all along - even triggering his main strategy to evade capture in SAW II. Not only this, he seemingly “disappeared” afterwards - which contradicts the previous Jigsaw cases where subjects are tested almost directly after abduction.

- The conclusion drawn from these two facts is that Eric is operating in cooperation with Jigsaw, perhaps as a direct disciple. Either he was abducted by Kramer and conformed into an apprentice (much like Gordon), or he directly helped Kramer evade capture using the immediate SAW II strategy as an alibi (much like what Strahm’s theory about Rigg posits).

**[ LAYER #2 —> DANIEL RIGG ]**

- Rigg’s trial in SAW IV directly points  to him being Jigsaw’s apprentice at multiple points.

- For instance, his apartment is altered with dozens of pictures of future subjects - framing him as a conduit for Jigsaw to locate and abduct individuals. He is also pressured into taking the life of a woman via an altercation, increasing his perceived guilt. At one point Rigg is even forced to wear the infamous pig mask - a trademark of those who work alongside Jigsaw. Finally, Rigg’s fingerprints are detected on a bullet-casing from a recent Jigsaw game.

- Throughout all of Rigg’s tests (which are located around the city where law enforcement can view them), Jigsaw is seen “communicating” with Rigg - with lines such as “feel what I feel” or “see what I see” painted on several walls and structures. These phrases are not aggressive, but rather appear as though Kramer and Righ already have a mutual understanding.

- Lindsey Perez (an FBI agent) even states that the events are organized to frame Rigg - with Peter Strahm (another FBI agent) arguing it could simply be an alibi Rigg fabricated to grant him plausible deniability and that Rigg could truly be an apprentice (which is a canonically stated sub-layer to the entire multi-agent scapegoat).

- Finally, what cements this specific framing as the second layer is the fact Jigsaw orchestrated the events to seem as though Rigg is attempting to either frame or pass the focus towards Eric, who is the first layer. When Perez and Strahm are redirected onto the Jigsaw case, they witness Rigg owning and reading multiple magazines/texts about John Kramer - as well as the fact Rigg spends most (if not all) of his time trying to “find” Eric (with detective Hoffman stating it’s time to “let go”, showcasing the obsessive extent Rigg is going to here). Kramer had engineered these moments of suspicion to occur - Eric is notably Rigg’s closest acquaintance, as seen in SAW II when Rigg actively reinforces Eric’s actions and supports him. When Eric is abducted as a direct causation of Rigg’s oversight, it emotionally shatters Rigg and facilitates guilt within him - inducing his obsession with the case, which frames Rigg as admiring Kramer and hyper-fixating on Eric.

- These two traits resemble the work of an undercover Jigsaw apprentice attempting to redirect focus from their possible uncovering onto another party (Eric). With this, Rigg’s actions in SAW II also solidly his framing - he notably helped Eric escape with Kramer by halting Kerry (a detective) before she could intervene. In a holistic view of the events, it frames Rigg as a man attempting to “allow” Eric’s abduction to occur - thereby aiding in Kramer’s evasion strategy. This also ties into the narrative of Rigg attempting to “frame” Eric, with Kramer indirectly manipulating the events to appear as though Rigg allowed for Eric to fall into Kramer’s trap, as to set Eric up later as a possible framed Jigsaw disciple (as seen in the first layer).

Layer #3 —> Jill Tuck

(NOTE — Jill isn’t directly an “apprentice” per se, but it is revealed she is cooperating with Kramer ever so slightly. Her integration into this multi-agent framing acts as an assurance that her identity remains obscured - not only this, her layer specifically involves her being omitted as a suspect after being brought in for interrogation. This frees Jill of any future suspicion, which Kramer utilizes to counteract Hoffman)

- In Rigg’s apartment, among pictures of subjects, law enforcement finds a picture of Jill Tuck. After analyzing the context, it is revealed Jill is Kramer’s ex-wife (a close connection is a strong indicator of a possible cooperation between two parties).

- Notably, the school in which one of Rigg’s tests occur is attributed to Jill’s name - introducing the narrative she is attempting to frame Rigg, integrating her as the third fabricated layer.

**[ LAYER #4: ART BLANK ]**

- With Jill in custody and the plan still continuing, she is omitted as the actual third accomplice. However, Jill’s lawyer (Art Blank) is revealed as co-owning the school her name is tied to (the same school Rigg was at during the trial). Law enforcement looks deeper into him and notes that he also had a close connection to Kramer in the past.

- Not only this, Art acts as a legal representative to every subject in Rigg’s trial - amplifying his guilt massively and the motel room where the messages were found (“see what I see”) was registered in Art’s name. The narrative presented by this layer of the framing is that Art acts as Kramer’s information agent - granting him access to legal records (which explains how Kramer can know of so many crimes) along with resources (the motel room, school, actual physical access to the subjects, etc).

- With Jill as the co-owner of the school Art is tied to, it fabricates the story that Art was attempting to frame Jill (someone he knows closely - one of his clients, who Kramer also knows deeply). This is strengthened by the fact Jill is able to be contacted yet Art isn’t, and isn’t even present when she is brought in for questioning (which is the most common situation where a lawyer should be at play). Obviously, this is because Kramer forced Art as the monitor behind Rigg’s trial (which once again paints Art as having a role of “overseeing” the game), placing Art as the fourth layer in the framing.

**[ LAYER #5: MARK HOFFMAN ]**

(NOTE — This is the true Jigsaw apprentice, integrated into the multi-agent scapegoat. Hoffman isn’t the final layer, so it maintains shielding whilst allowing Hoffman to fall into a system where a multitude of suspects exist - decreasing the likelihood that Hoffman would be taken as the threat)

- If Rigg isn’t the true accomplice, how were his fingerprints obtained and found at the scene of a previous game? This indicates the existence of a mole within law enforcement, which extends past the previous layer of Art Blank (who doesn’t have the influence needed to perform this).

- The only individual involved in Rigg’s trial who is linked to law enforcement is Mark Hoffman. If Hoffman was the one behind the framing up to this point, the narrative is strengthened by the fact he is the sole survivor of Rigg’s trial (with the exception of Strahm, crucially) and also saved Jeff’s daughter at the same time, highly coincidental events for a random agent. Hoffman was also present when the bullet-casing was initially discovered, increasing plausibility.

- Multiple years ago, a Jigsaw test was performed on Seth Baxter - the killer of Hoffman’s sister. This grants possible motive for Hoffman being the mole and a reasonable “backstory”.

- Finally, Kramer’s wax-covered tape in SAW IV specifically hints towards an inside mole within law enforcement (“I am still among you”). In retrospect, this tape is truly a double-layered instruction code to this apprentice - on the surface, it’s a simple message to Hoffman and law enforcement hinting towards future plans. However, it also sends a message to the internal mole (“last man standing” refers to the last survivor, whilst also referring to the last apprentice).

**[ LAYER #6  —> PETER STRAHM ]**

- In SAW V, Hoffman enacts a complex scheme to frame Strahm as the culprit through multiple tactics (manipulating Strahm’s presence at several trials, placing Strahm’s belongings at said trials, etc). Hoffman’s scheme was foreseen by Kramer, and Kramer even indirectly manipulated Hoffman into enacting the plan by instructing Gordon to send an anonymous note to Hoffman after Kramer’s death - a note that would make Hoffman weary of Strahm. Notably, Hoffman utilizes Strahm’s fingerprints after Strahm’s death in SAW V to continue framing, fabricating the events as Strahm going AWOL as the final Jigsaw apprentice.

- It’s worth noting that the Strahm framing was set up in IV - Strahm was the agent behind the initial “other accomplice deduction” and given the later fabrication of Strahm attempting to frame Hoffman, this manipulates the event to seem like Strahm “exposed” a third party through a hasty yet rather precise deduction as to frame Hoffman as that third party. Kramer indirectly orchestrated Strahm’s aggressive behavior with Jill Tuck, who later reported suspicious behavior from Strahm in SAW V (Jill is notably the third layer - adding validity to the sentiment he was behind the previous layers of framing) during an interrogation between the two and generated multiple events that place suspicion on Strahm (an attack on Perez where Strahm is the only witness) and a specialized pathway seemingly for Strahm set up beside Kramer’s deathbed, granting Strahm a “way out”. Strahm fits the profile of an internal mole (as posited by the Rigg framing) as well as Kramer’s wax-covered tape applying to his status nigh-perfectly (Strahm is an agent for law enforcement and is also the “last man standing”, as Kramer directly stated in the tape).

- Finally, Strahm’s framing was intricately designed to fabricate the events to appear as though Strahm is not only the true third accomplice - but also attempting to frame Hoffman. Hoffman sliced a puzzle piece from a future subject using the same knife he used on Seth Baxter (the man who killed Hoffman’s sister) several years prior, and he didn’t leave the tape behind to break Jigsaw’s pattern of leaving tapes behind. Law enforcement notes the knife and is immediately redirected to Seth Baxter. This would incriminate Hoffman (Seth having a close antagonistic relationship with Hoffman), but with the evidence against Strahm, it constructs the narrative that Strahm was attempting to incriminate Hoffman. Not only this, but Hoffman fabricates the FBI task force killing in SAW VI to appear as though Strahm was behind it (still in hiding) and actively abducted Hoffman, finalizing Strahm as the final layer of the multi-agent framing.

**[ CATHARSIS ]**

In conclusion, Kramer’s multi-agent framing constructed a narrative formulated around six possible suspects - designed to be uncovered in such a way where the deeper layers seem to have framed the surface layers (similar to a Matryoshka doll).

(Arrows will be used to indicate the framing/implication of an individual)

- Peter Strahm —> Mark Hoffman —> Art Blank —> Jill Tuck —> Daniel Rigg —> Eric Matthews

This scheme persisted for roughly six months (mainly the Strahm & Hoffman layers) and was capable of stalemating (as well as completely dismantling via the framing of one of their highest esteemed agents) the FBI, an organization more advanced than its real-world counterpart technologically as shown by SAW VI. In fact, the FBI never caught Hoffman in the end - even his initial detection was orchestrated/anticipated by Kramer (via Perez) and Hoffman’s overall conclusion was a direct result of Kramer’s post-mortem planning. Kramer developed this scheme through multiple indirect manipulation tactics, controlling each agent (Matthews, Rigg, Tuck, Blank, Hoffman, Strahm, Perez, Erickson, etc) nigh-perfectly over the span of multiple months, all whilst being deceased (meaning the planning was formulated pre-mortem, with Kramer forecasting it past the point of his own death).
