The traps are either not intended for Amanda or Daniel, survivable if they are forced into them, or controllable through Amanda's presence and prior knowledge. The main violent threat, Xavier, is also heavily weakened by the gas and can be dealt with by Amanda. This means John can allow the game to proceed while still maintaining a strong ability to protect Amanda and Daniel, making their survival the most likely outcome across a wide range of possible developments. Even if the worst-case outcome occurred and both Amanda and Daniel died, John would still lose very little strategically. Amanda was sent into the game by John not only to fulfill the roles described above, but also because she needed to be tested in order to be “reborn.” John therefore knew that her death was a possible outcome, even if it was extremely unlikely. If Amanda were ultimately killed, her loss would not destroy his overall plan, as John could simply continue searching for and testing another potential apprentice. Daniel's death would similarly cause no lasting strategic damage. John would simply avoid allowing himself to be discovered by the police and could later find another way to construct a game around Eric. The specific method used to draw Eric into the original situation would be lost, but the larger objective of testing and manipulating Eric could still be pursued through a different approach.

Eric’s Abduction
John now knows the actual outcome of the nerve-gas-house game, with only Amanda and Daniel surviving as he anticipated. He therefore allows himself to be found by the police and begins his game with Eric. The entire game is built around a simple rule: Eric must remain with John and continue speaking with him if he wants to get his son back. Eric believes that the footage being shown on the screens is happening live, when in reality it is prerecorded and Daniel is already safely secured elsewhere. John also knows Eric's history of violence. Eric has previously broken a man's jaw with a flashlight, shot an unarmed person, and repeatedly allowed his anger to override his judgment. John therefore designs the game specifically around this weakness. If Eric breaks the rule by losing his patience and resorting to violence, he fails the test. However, John would simply tell him that he will lead him there, allowing Eric to believe that this is simply the next stage of the game while Daniel remains safely hidden at the original location. As John anticipates, Eric eventually loses his patience and attacks him. John has already made sure that they are alone at this point, so he can immediately proceed with the next stage without revealing the true purpose of Eric's actions. John tells Eric to activate a hidden mechanism that moves the small section of the room where they are located downward like an elevator, revealing a prepared vehicle underneath. He then uses it to transport Eric to the nerve-gas house, where Eric is placed into his own game. John also accounts for the possibility, however unlikely, that Eric never loses his patience and continues following the rules. Daniel is already safe, so John does not actually need Eric to fail for the larger plan to work. John has ensured that he is alone with Eric throughout the conversation, meaning that when Daniel is eventually revealed to be safe, the revelation would naturally draw everyone's attention, including Eric's. This would give John the opportunity to remain behind unnoticed, activate the mechanism himself, and leave the location even if Eric never breaks the rules.

Saw 3 & 4
Gideon Meatpacking Plant Scheme
This will be an analysis of the scheme and events in both Saw 3 and Saw 4, since they happen at the same time.
The Gideon Meatpacking Plant operation is best understood as one large, interconnected scheme rather than several unrelated games. John simultaneously places Strahm, Lynn, Jeff, Rigg, Amanda, Hoffman, and Matthews into circumstances designed to interact with one another. Jeff's game, Amanda's secret test, Rigg's trials, Hoffman's involvement, and Strahm's investigation are all separate threads deliberately constructed to converge at the same location and time. John first eliminates the possibility of Jeff reaching him with anyone capable of disrupting the intended sequence. The woman from the opening trap is not a meaningful complication: even if Jeff succeeds in saving her, she is left naked, severely cold, physically weakened, and unlikely to move efficiently. With only two hours to complete his trials, Jeff would have to carry or assist her, substantially slowing his progress, while her survival is not necessary for the purpose of his test. The Judge presents a different but equally manageable variable. John understands his personality well enough to anticipate what he will do after Jeff saves him. When Jeff later retrieves the key needed to free his son's killer, the Judge interferes with the trap, causing the gun to fire and kill him. John can likewise anticipate Jeff's reluctance to save the man responsible for his son's death. Through these circumstances, the people accompanying Jeff are gradually removed, leaving him to continue toward John by himself. John also gives Jeff a gun containing a single bullet, knowing it will become important later. Amanda's test is running alongside Jeff's game, although Amanda herself does not realize that she is being tested. Lynn is the central piece of it: she is forced to keep John alive, while Amanda must ensure that Lynn remains alive if she wants to survive. John has deliberately placed Amanda under increasing psychological pressure, particularly by showing affection toward Lynn while becoming increasingly distant toward Amanda. ***He then intensifies that pressure through the letter supposedly written by Hoffman, which threatens to expose Amanda's involvement in John's death if she refuses to kill Lynn. Under the interpretation that Hoffman wrote the letter at John's direction, Amanda is pushed into precisely the kind of decision John wants to examine: whether she can preserve another person's life instead of deciding that person is unworthy of it. John appears to have already accounted for Amanda's probable failure. Jeff's arrival is therefore synchronized with the final portion of her test. Crucially, Amanda does not know that Lynn is Jeff's wife, so she cannot anticipate the consequences of killing her. Once Amanda shoots Lynn, Jeff uses the single round John gave him to kill Amanda. The arrangement effectively makes Jeff an unwitting counter to Amanda: his own emotional state becomes the mechanism through which her test reaches its conclusion. Jeff is then confronted with John's final test. He can either let John live or kill him. After everything John has put him through, combined with the apparent deaths of his son and wife, John's prediction is that Jeff will choose revenge. John has prepared for that decision in advance, leaving a recording that activates after his death and seals the room, preventing Jeff from simply leaving and preserving the location for what comes next. Moreover, John remains remarkably composed even after having his throat cut by Jeff. Despite suffering a fatal injury, he stays conscious and in control long enough to present Jeff with the prerecorded message he had prepared, maintaining his composure until his death and even appearing to smile in his final moments. At the same time, John is running a second major operation through Rigg. Rigg's weakness is his compulsive need to intervene and rescue people who should instead be left to deal with their own situations. His trials are therefore constructed around forcing him to stop interfering. John gives him an additional incentive to continue by telling him that progressing through the trials will eventually lead him to Eric Matthews, his missing partner. Hoffman is essential to this part of the operation because he is secretly working for John from within the police. His apparent status as another participant allows him to remain close to the investigation while concealing the fact that he is actually assisting John. Art Blanc is similarly forced into the operation through the device attached to his back. His connection to John, his legal background, and his knowledge of several people involved in the games make him useful, while his apparent position as another victim helps obscure the true structure of the operation. John simultaneously manipulates the FBI investigation so that Strahm and Perez are drawn deeper into the case. John has already targeted their police insider, Kerry, and provides Strahm with a key that will later become important. As Rigg's trials progress, John leaves behind enough misleading evidence to encourage the investigators to consider Rigg as a possible Jigsaw apprentice. The cryptic messages and markings support this interpretation, while the physical demands of certain traps make the theory appear plausible because they seem to require considerably more strength than Amanda possesses. The investigation then begins accumulating connections to people close to John. A photograph of Jill Tuck discovered during one of Rigg's trials causes the police to focus on her. Art Blanc's name is also discovered at the hotel connected to the investigation. Because Art is John's former associate, a lawyer, and someone who knew several individuals connected to Rigg's trials, the investigators begin considering him another possible accomplice. This suspicion leads them toward Art's properties and further reinforces their interest in Jill. Their next major lead comes from another Rigg crime scene at a school, reached after Rigg triggers an alarm to summon the police. John has prepared a doll specifically for Strahm and Perez, indicating that he has anticipated which investigators will arrive. The doll tells Strahm that he will soon shoot an innocent man implying to be Jeff and provides Perez with a separate cryptic message that draws her closer before the shoots and injures her. Strahm, now furious, returns to the station and confronts Jill. From there Strahm is able to verify the location of the game and heads there. By the time Strahm reaches the Gideon Meatpacking Plant, the different branches of John's plan are beginning to overlap. Rigg's final instruction is for him to wait outside the room where Hoffman, Art, and Matthews are located instead of forcing his way inside. Rigg fails to follow that instruction and enters at the last possible moment, activating the mechanism that kills Matthews. Art attempts to stop Rigg by shooting him, but Rigg then kills Art before dying himself. Hoffman survives, which allows him to continue the role John has assigned to him and maintain the appearance of being another victim of the operation. Strahm reaches the plant at virtually the same point that Jeff's portion of the scheme reaches its conclusion. He follows the route toward the room where John, Amanda, Jeff, and Lynn are located. The entrance has already been secured, but John's earlier clues concerning keys and locked doors give Strahm the means to enter. He remembers the key and uses it, arriving precisely where the previous stages of the operation have converged. Inside, Strahm encounters Jeff after the collapse of his game. Jeff is psychologically shattered by Lynn's apparent death and still believes that his daughter is in immediate danger. After obtaining Amanda's gun, he points it at Strahm and demands information about his daughter. John has effectively converted Jeff's grief into another mechanism of the scheme: Jeff's predictable reaction forces Strahm into shooting him, removing Jeff from the sequence while placing Strahm exactly where John needs him. Hoffman then secures Strahm inside the room. Once the lights are extinguished, a concealed message written in ink becomes visible and reveals the next route. This is another example of the environment itself being prepared around Strahm's expected behavior: the clue only becomes useful after the precise sequence of events has occurred. Strahm's investigation has therefore been engineered to intersect with Jeff and Amanda's game rather than merely coincidentally arriving at the same location. Rigg's individual result is not the critical factor in Strahm's arrival. His game serves several purposes simultaneously: it tests Rigg's inability to refrain from interfering, brings Matthews into the operation, gives Hoffman a legitimate reason to be present, and generates the chain of evidence that helps direct the investigation toward Gideon. Strahm's own path, meanwhile, has been arranged so that the investigation ultimately converges with the other games at the required moment. The resulting sequence leaves John with the circumstances necessary for the next stage of his plan. Amanda has failed and died; Jeff has killed Amanda and John before being killed by Strahm; Matthews and Art have died during Rigg's game; and Strahm has been delivered into his own test. Hoffman is then positioned to emerge as the apparent surviving officer who was caught in Jigsaw's operation and ultimately rescues Jeff's daughter. From the outside, he appears to have survived the games and saved the child, preserving his credibility within the police while concealing his role as John's accomplice.
What makes this scheme particularly impressive is John's ability to understand and predict the behavior of dozens of people, including their small decisions and reactions, to an extremely precise degree. The scheme depends on numerous individuals making specific choices at specific moments, yet John anticipates these decisions and builds the entire plan around them. What makes this even more impressive is that John never directly met or interacted with most of the people involved. Instead, he was able to construct detailed psychological profiles of them through information obtained from other people, their previous actions, and existing information about them. From this indirect information alone, John was able to infer their personalities, vulnerabilities, likely reactions, and future behavior, allowing him to account for their decisions throughout the scheme with remarkable precision. Even a single small detail being overlooked or misjudged by John could have caused the entire scheme to fail or only partially succeed. The plan relies on an enormous number of interconnected predictions, meaning that one unexpected decision or deviation could disrupt multiple stages of the scheme and prevent later events from unfolding as intended.

Saw 5
Hoffman’s Manipulation
Hoffman wakes up after being kidnapped by Jigsaw and finds himself tied to a chair with a gun pointed at him. John toys with him before pulling the trigger, deliberately frightening Hoffman. However, the gun does not fire, creating the impression that it contains no bullets. This makes Hoffman believe that, despite his situation, John has ultimately placed himself in a position where Hoffman can decide what happens next. John's manipulation of Hoffman is closely tied to his trauma over his sister's murder and his growing belief that the existing system is fundamentally flawed. Hoffman had already demonstrated this himself by killing the man responsible for his sister's death rather than allowing the justice system to deal with him. John uses this history to attack Hoffman's faith in the system. He also exploits Hoffman's deteriorating lifestyle, particularly the fact that he spends his nights and everyday life drinking, and presents his own philosophy as a superior alternative: a different system of rehabilitation that John believes can succeed where the existing one has failed. John then unties Hoffman and seemingly gives him a choice of his own. Hoffman can either kill Jigsaw immediately or accept John's belief in a better system. John deliberately structures the situation so that Hoffman feels he has arrived at the decision independently rather than being forced into it. This is important because it allows John to strengthen Hoffman's belief in the new system afterward: Hoffman can view his continued involvement as something he personally chose to accept. Despite making Hoffman believe that he is in control, John has actually prepared two layers of protection against being killed. The first is the information he gives Hoffman directly: John claims that he possesses proof of Hoffman's involvement in his sister's murder and that it will be released if Hoffman kills him. This gives Hoffman a clear reason to reconsider his decision. The second safeguard is completely unknown to Hoffman. Although John has already pulled the trigger once without firing, making the gun appear empty, the weapon is actually loaded. The initial trigger pull was therefore deliberately staged to create a false sense of security and convince Hoffman that the gun presents no real danger. Hoffman consequently believes that John has surrendered control of the situation and that he is genuinely being allowed to choose between killing Jigsaw and accepting his philosophy. In reality, John has controlled both sides of the decision: the threat of exposure discourages Hoffman from killing him, while the supposedly empty gun hides an additional safeguard that Hoffman does not even realize exists. John therefore makes Hoffman feel as though he is exercising free choice while secretly ensuring that the outcome remains under his control.

Saw 6 & 7
Hoffman’s Test
With John's death, he nevertheless has one final objective: to test Hoffman. He deliberately conceals this purpose behind what appears to be his final set of instructions. Before dying, John leaves Jill a tape explaining what she must do and gives her the Reverse Bear Trap 2.0, presenting it as part of his final plan involving Hoffman. The people Hoffman is instructed to test therefore serve as a cover for the real purpose of the arrangement: placing Hoffman himself into a test. The structure of the plan also allows John to know where Hoffman will eventually be, since following the instructions naturally brings him into the position where Jill can place him into the trap. John believes that everyone deserves a chance, meaning Hoffman's test was intended to be genuinely survivable. However, an unforeseen event disrupts this. Shortly before Jill carries out John's instructions, a reporter shows her the letter Hoffman had written for Amanda. This changes Jill's intentions, causing her to abandon the idea of giving Hoffman a fair chance and instead deliberately set out to kill him. Even this does not completely undermine John's preparations. He had already established one final contingency through Doctor Gordon, whose existence as John's disciple had been kept secret from everyone except Jill. Gordon's role was to intervene if something happened to Jill or if the original plan failed to produce the intended outcome. Jill leaves Hoffman in the trap, but Hoffman manages to escape by using the door to jam the mechanism before it can kill him. He then pursues Jill for revenge, triggering the final contingency John had prepared. Gordon intervenes and takes Hoffman out, allowing John's plan to ultimately succeed even after his death.
