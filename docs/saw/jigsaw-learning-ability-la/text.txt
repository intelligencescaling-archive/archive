CONTEXTUAL TIMEFRAME:

Before delving into the content of the feat itself, an idea must be formed of the exact timeframe in which this feat was performed. Kramer himself stated in the comic that he “discarded sleep” and “neglected meals”. There are two statements from Kramer pointing to the idea he slept little to none during the timeframe, those statements being —>
1. “I discarded sleep” — According to the Merriam Webster dictionary, discarded means to to get rid of especially as useless or unwanted.
2. “The hours I didn’t spend puking with my guts on fire were consumed by planning” — This implies Kramer didn’t dedicate any remaining time to sleep, even if he did sleep, it would’ve had to be under an hour each day given the statement, which would still result in immense sleep deprivation.
With these two statements taken into account (showing a doubly reinforced narrative), it’s heavily implied Kramer slept almost no time at all during the timeframe. With this, we can reasonably conclude how long the duration actually was by utilizing the law of human anatomy.

According to Guinness World Records (which has been recording the longest duration a human has gone without sleep since the 1950s), there seems to be a barrier preventing any recorded human from reaching 19 consecutive days without sleep. If we use the highest estimate of about 18 days for the duration Kramer went without sleep (supplying the longest timeframe possible for the feat), we can utilize this information to do more precise calculations in relation to the information he absorbed during this time. (Source —> https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/1/whats-the-limit-to-how-long-a-human-can-stay-awake-733188).
Now, does this estimate align with other data we know of Kramer’s state at the time? As stated previously, Kramer had skipped multiple meals everyday for this duration. Even if we conclude he ate absolutely nothing (which likely isn’t true), Healthline states that the average human may survive 8 to 21 days without food or water (this figure was taken from records of previous instances of humans attempting to survive without food or water). This lines up very well with our previous estimate of about 18 days, being just under the maximum of 21 days given by the data. Given that Kramer likely did eat a couple meals during this timeframe, and the absolute starvation data given by Healthline even at the lowest would fit our estimate of 18 days, it’s reasonable to conclude that Kramer’s lack of eating at any level wouldn’t affect the given 18 day figure. (Source —> https://www.healthline.com/health/food-nutrition/how-long-can-you-live-without-food)
Finally, we are given one final statement from Kramer in the comic that may point to how long the timeframe was. It was stated Kramer still went to work once in a while during the duration, meaning it had been a large enough timeframe to encompass enough days to comprise a pool great enough for a fraction of it being spent at work. A perfect timeframe of this would indeed be the previous 18 day estimate, as it would supply Kramer enough days to only show up to work for a fraction of them (perhaps one or two days every week, which would total around five total days at work during the 18 days, which matches the statement very well) whilst still granting him enough time to consume the information he did.

Even if the 18 day figure is discredited, Kramer still stated he spent bare minimum hours vomiting and still went to work once in a while, further narrowing the timeframe regardless of the inference’s narrative standing.

So with all the information gathered and statistics compiled, a reasonable estimate of 18 days at the most would fit all three statement requirements given by the narrative and would also fit the biological maximum output the human body can exert. By granting Kramer a timeframe of 18 days at the most, we are given a conservative calculation of the information absorbed due to selecting the highest possible amount of time Kramer could’ve had to complete such a feat.

INFORMATION ABSORBED:
Before beginning, it’s worth noting Kramer worked at a toy factory before undergoing his immense studying. This shows how he had almost zero real knowledge in any field before the feat took place, solidifying its validity. In the comic, it’s shown that Kramer studies a wide range of subjects in the 18 day timeframe (or simply a short duration). Kramer himself states he absorbed a “vast” amount of knowledge in various fields, implying a great value of understanding absorbed for each subject (it is also stated everything was researched, tested, and refined). In the comic itself, we were shown multiple of the exact subjects he educated himself on via books. Off the subjects the  books covered we see alone, we can conclude Kramer had absorbed knowledge from the following subjects —>
* Botany (studying a book detailing Dionaeo muscipula plants. It is confirmed he genuinely studied this as we are given a direct panel of him studying it).
* English (the uppermost book seems to state English contents).
* Cartography (seen with a book containing the study of maps. It is proven that he studied this subject as he would’ve needed this knowledge in the first film to map out the underground bathroom, which itself is a labyrinth of hallways,  and the trial in general).
* Visual-Effects (the lowermost book seems to be detailing some subject based around visuals. This subject is proven to have been studied by Kramer as in the first film he evidently had knowledge of it via distorting audio to untraceable levels, manipulating footage, and professional makeup skills).
* Mathematics (Kramer is shown reading a book based upon mathematics. This one is confirmed to have been learned by Kramer as we are given a panel of him studying it and engineering in general hinges upon mathematics).
* Medicine (an obscured book seems to be revolving around the study of poison, a trait commonly seen in medicinal practices. This subject was proven to be learned by Kramer via his ability to administer poison to Zepp in the first film that had no known curable cure, a cure only Kramer had uncovered).
Assuming Kramer spent an equal amount of time on every subject throughout the 18 days (supported as no subject was shown to have had a repeated book, narratively implying each subject was studied equally. We can reasonably assume Kramer spent three days on average studying each subject dividing his days equally among the six subjects). Kramer stated EVERYTHING was researched, tested, and refined. This points to the idea each subject was dwelled upon deeply, and thus equally distributed within the timeframe. This also implies every subject was equally absorbed, meaning each subject’s complexity and quality matched each other. Given the fact we know Kramer’s engineering skills (deriving from his mathematical studying) is at advanced level due to the fact the comic isn’t a new continuation and simply a backstory for Kramer, he should have the same level of engineering skills regardless of adaptation (which is proven as we later see comics Kramer construct the exact same device/mechanism as films Kramer). His engineering skills were shown to be at the level of a professional civil engineer, showing advanced skills in the subject (and thus the rest of them due to the previously inferred equality).

INTERPRETATION CALCULATION: Kramer, in the film series, is a professional civil engineer and architect. Disregarding work experience on a lowball, becoming a civil engineer typically requires 4-6 years of education. Assuming the comics version and the films version of Kramer both have the same level of engineering skill (supported as the comic was supposed to simply be a backstory for Kramer, not a new continuity and also the fact both iterations created the same mechanism), implying Kramer had absorbed this knowledge in the 18 day timeframe, Assuming his engineering prowess was derived from the mathematics he studied, and he spent an equal amount of time on every subject throughout the 18 days (as no subject had a repeated book. This isn’t to say each subject only had one book, but they were all portrayed as narratively equal and proven to be learned by Kramer in the first film via representation), we can reasonably assume Kramer spent three days on average studying each subject. Given the logical equal distribution of each subject, Kramer had processed and applied this information roughly 487-730 times faster than the average person (via 4-6 years of educational value Kramer learned and applied in the span of these three days).
