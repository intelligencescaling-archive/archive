+++++++++++++Introduction

John Kramer is the primary antagonist of the Saw series of films and games. After losing his first child in a tragic miscarriage, his wife in a divorce, getting diagnosed with cancer, and nearly dying in a car accident, civil service engineer/architect John Kramer gained a new appreciation for life, one he saw far too little of in others. He devoted the rest of his life to “teaching” others to cherish their lives through various games and traps as the criminal mastermind “Jigsaw.” Though he was killed halfway through the series as part of his plan, his final plans and ideology would be carried on by apprentices Mark Hoffman, Lawrence Gordon, and Logan Nelson, as well as copycat killers inspired by his ideology

General

John is generally able to design and build several elaborate traps. The video tapes corresponding to these traps often have subtle verbal clues and explain the victim’s reason(s) for being in the trap. Though Gordon was helping him for much of the series,it’s likely for only a few victims as Hoffman (it’s stated in the director’s commentary) didn’t recognize him as a disciple despite being extensively involved with Jigsaw. It’s also implied he could treat whatever injuries the scammers would’ve gotten in Saw X, despite Gordon’s absence - which includes a man carving out a piece of his own brain (VSI, VCI, Creativity, Crystallized Intelligence. Knowledge)

John was also able to pass down this knowledge  such that even characters with no engineering or medical background could tinker with, finish, or even design traps of their own. Most impressively, the first apprentice to receive John’s guidance, Logan Nelson, was able to construct a laser collar that did not harm him and triggered fake blood upon use, as well as a voice modifier that had the mayor and police genuinely entertaining the possibility that John Kramer had resurrected (VCI)

Jigsaw generally performs extensive research on his victims and people even tangentially related to them, most prominently seen in Saw III where he ties in the game from Saw I by capturing all of the people related to the death of Jeff’s son or somehow knowing the exact amount of money a thief in Jigsaw robbed a woman over. (Information Gathering)

John manipulates several people of different kinds to his cause - from the vengeful Detective Mark Hoffman to Amanda Young who desperately seeks a mentor and father, and more. What makes this impressive is that one usually needs a good, if at least neutral, rapport with someone to befriend someone, let alone gain their loyalty. John manages to do this from the position of a serial killer who horrifically mutilates and traumatizes the people he later recruits to his cause and twisted philosophy. (Emotional Engagement, Awareness, and Understanding, Social Skills and Intuition, Leadership, Influence Building, Charisma, Sophistry,  Persuasion)

John dies in Saw III, but Saw IV-VII, taking place months to years afterwards, are his games, albeit with his apprentices carrying them out. Saw V is the exception with Hoffman improvising after Strahm survived a trap that was supposed to kill him. I leave out most of Hoffman’s stuff for Jigsaw since he’s sort of improvising for much of his films, but include FBI Agent Strahm’s feats since Jigsaw’s prediction involving him in Saw IV is so precise that John must’ve predicted his exact moves and thought processes and Logan Nelson’s feats since his whole motive was trying to live up to Kramer, implying inferiority.

Narratively, undoubtedly being the smartest guy in the verse, John should likely scale above apprentices like Mark Hoffman (who he calls “inferior” in Saw V and is quickly caught onto in Saw VI by the police), survivors who come up with inventive ways to survive his traps, and FBI Agent Peter Srahm.

Tobin Bell has described him as part “philosopher, part scientist” with a touch of theology. He’s very detail oriented and always considers the worst case scenario, having a second plan just in case. He’s a very good judge of character. Producer Bousman has described him as “extremely educated.” (Existential Intelligence, Knowledge, Academics, Crystallized Intelligence, Emotional Understanding, Cold Reading, Divergent Thinking, Planning Contingencies, Planning Coverage)

SAW II

The film opens with Jigsaw killing an informant of Detective Matthews and goading him into the case via a taunt written on a wall of the crime scene. Jigsaw leaves a clue in the form of a logo for “Wilson Steel” found on the trap and he is tracked down to an abandoned steel mill. There, several SWAT officers are non-lethally incapacitated with a false stair and an electrically charged wall  before Jigsaw is finally confronted. By allowing them to seemingly discover his location on their own and seemingly not going down easy, he lulls them into a false sense of security (Setting Traps, Short Term Strategy, Indirect Manipulation, Info Control)

The police investigate the area and discover several monitors showing a group of people, one Matthew’s son, somewhere in a game. Matthews asks where he is, and John responds, “He’s in a safe place.” Literally, his son was in a safe in that very room, but due to Jigsaw’s taunting and the seemingly high-stakes time limit at hand. Not one officer caught on. And what they don’t know is that the game had already taken place, the monitors show a recording and are not live. He had effectively given the police the position to cleanly arrest him, but he knew they wouldn’t call his bluff.
(Misdirection, Mass Manipulation, Bluffing, Strategic Deception, Planning Risk Calculation)

The test was for Detective Matthews to keep his cool and play along. as Jigsaw had profiled him as a workaholic, brutish, short-tempered man based on a police brutality incident 5 years prior. If he did, the games would’ve ended there, but knowing he wouldn’t, Jigsaw’s taunting, elaborating on his philosophy, and subtle challenging of his power broke him to the point that Matthews would do anything up to beating John to a pulp and agreeing to go with him alone to an undisclosed location to find his son. (Fear Inducement, Hot Reading, Information Gathering, Psychological Profiling, Emotional , Direct, and Verbal Manipulation, Prediction)

John reveals the moment he decided to become Jigsaw. He had driven his car off a cliff, impaling onto debris, but survived, pulling the debris out and going to the hospital. In that moment, he learned to truly cherish his life. (Perseverance, Fortitude, Metacognition)

The game itself involves several people falsely or otherwise imprisoned by Matthews alongside his son.

Matthews viciously beats John and breaks a finger, but John doesn’t stop taunting him, only feigning surrender to lure Matthews to the warehouse where Amanda is. Eric by that point had become so mindbroken he agreed to John’s demand that they go alone and snuck John past his fellow officers. Anticipating this freak out, John had positioned himself in the same room as an elevator so that they could bypass the police. And the police were able to only track down the house’s signal when he allowed them to, implying extensive knowledge in computer science, thus he gave Eric directions to where Amanda would ambush him rather than where the games were held, the latter which the police were actually sent to. Ultimately, this was one huge strategy to kidnap Eric, which itself is just another part of a bigger plan to make Hoffman look good, as revealed in the sequel (Emotional Management, Mental Fortitude, Prediction, Misdirection, Information Control, Short Term Planning, Knowledge, Planning Coverage, Strategy, Strategy Aim, Strategy Resilience, Strategic Foresight and Success Rate, Linear Thinking, Crystallized Intelligence)

SAW III

Months later, Jigsaw captures Dr. Lynn, a colleague of Gordon, and easily sees through her attempts at verbally running him over with “graduate school” jargon; this might also imply he has some knowledge in the field, which isn’t a surprise as he does show some skills in anatomy and physiology in poisoning and operating on his victims. The main game of this film involves grieving father Jeff, who must go through several tests designed to help him move on from the death of his son. (Emotional Perception, likely Knowledge)

On his deathbed, Kramer reveals that he knew Amanda had been rigging her traps. Now, John since Saw II has been bedridden, and this film takes place 6 months later. Thus, his only way to deduce this was knowledge of his intended subjects' deaths, his interactions with Amanda, and his analysis of her. It is possible that the police released details regarding the traps suddenly being unbeatable, but it’s unlikely they would do so lest they give the killers a clue as to their investigation. Amanda’s test was to keep Lynn alive and John tested her by showing affection for Lynn, challenging Amanda’s paternal feelings for him and, what he saw as her weakness: her emotions. (Emotional Manipulation, Indirect Manipulation, Emotional Perception, Abductive Reasoning)

In a flashback, John personally approached Amanda in her apartment after she survived her trap, identifying her as someone willing to join his cause. In his words, he convinced her to give everything to him, “every cell” in her body, leaving her old addict self as a different person entirely (Emotional Perception, Brainwashing)

John proceeds to undergo brain surgery without anesthetics, never once showing any signs of pain even as Lynn carves a large hole into his scalp and pokes around his brain (Emotional Management, Composure, Impulse Control)

Over the course of the film, Jigsaw also manages to engage with Lynn, getting her to vent about her husband. Jigsaw implies he knows about her affair, enraging her. Later, when Amanda breaks down; he warns her to keep her cool as this is all part of her game. She does not and he sheds a tear since he knows his pseudo-daughter/protege is about to die, but proceeds with the game anyways. Amanda shoots Lynn, Jeff’s wife, failing her test. (Indirect Manipulation, Emotional Engagement, Emotional Understanding, Emotional Management, Love Bombing)

In his last moments, Jeff enters the room. John could easily pacify Jeff by telling him that should his heart stop Jeff’s wife will die, but he does not. John stays committed to his games to the very end, giving Jeff one final test by pointing out various weapons in the room and telling Jeff not to use them but instead to forgive. Jeff does not , killing Jigsaw. While bleeding out to death, John pulls out one last tape: the doors lock and it reveals John had prepared for this exact outcome and with another game to rescue Jeff’s daughter, who he had kidnapped prior (Divergent Thinking, Planning Coverage, Determination, Mental Fortitude, Planning Contingency, Strategy Foresight)

Psychological Manipulation - A given with Kramer. As explained above, his plans as a whole require him to perfectly know everyone’s psyche and precisely how they’ll react in fixed and dynamic situations, and he manipulates them through traps, clues, apprentices,etc, accordingly.

Scapegoating/Misdirection - Leading the police to chase after Riggs for a while before focusing on Art Blanc

Information Control - Inducing the police to interrogate Jill, knowing she’d eventually give up critical info about John’s past. Letting Art Blanc be discovered through his name on motel records, knowing they’d discover he’s a friend of Kramer and connected to many of the victims in Riggs’ trial

White Knighting - Hoffman escapes with Jeff’s daughter, gaining the reputation of a heroic cop.

All foresight - Self evident.

Deductive/Abductive/Inductive/Abstract Reasoning: Such planning requires very strong reasoning abilities. Deductive (“If this is true, then this necessarily is true”): It would be too complicated to describe the countless variables Kramer would have to consider, but the perhaps strongest case of this is Kramer reasoning out how to make it such that Strahm would shoot Jeff. in the event Jeff failed. Inductive and Abductive (“If this has been true thus far, this will most likely be true”; “This is probably the best explanation”): Used to gain the initial premises which Kramer could use to deduce outcomes within his plan. This would involve his psychoanalyses of people based on their past behavior in relation to how they’ll react to new situations. Abstract Reasoning (as seen in analyses of dreams and heavily relies on the symbolic associations humans make); this should also heavily contribute to his psychoanalysis of people. Also involved is Jigsaw being able to predict not just what people will do but when. For example, Strahm deducing Jeff’s game location was reliant on Jill giving him the information, which she had been withholding. Predicting when she would do this so precisely means John would have to understand not just that she’d be reluctant to do that, but also how she’d react to the situation, considering countless factors like her loyalty to her husband (himself), her desire to do good, her fear of implicating herself, etc.
