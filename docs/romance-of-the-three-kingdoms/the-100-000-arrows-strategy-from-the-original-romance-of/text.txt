The 100,000 Arrows Strategy from the original RoTK

"First organise the inner, then organise the outer. First organise the great, then the small. First organise yourself, then others."

\- Zhuge Liang

This strategy is from the original Romance of the Three Kingdoms, a novel that has a surprisingly good amount of outsmarting and stuff despite being hundreds of years old. Anyways, to understand the strategy, here's some context:

**This all takes place in the Three Kingdoms period, which I am not going to explain now, but to get a basic idea of what's happening, a warlord named Cao Cao is attacking the Southlands where Zhuge Liang and Zhou Yu are allied (ZL serves Liu Bei while ZY serves Sun Quan but this isn't important). What's more important is that they are fighting on the river, so Cao Cao is approaching from the North, trying to take down the Southern alliance.**

**Cao Cao's army is not used to naval battles however, so Zhou Yu uses a scheme to have his best naval officers executed and to have inept officers take their places (I will not be explaining this scheme here but let me just say that Zhou Yu is also super smart). Zhou Yu knows that Zhuge Liang will easily see through his scheme and wants to have Zhuge Liang executed. He makes a bet with ZL and tells him to produce 100000 arrows in ten days or else he'd be executed.**

The strategy:

1. Given this almost impossible task (100000 arrows is logically impossible to be made in just 10 days), Zhuge Liang of course accepts but says he only needs 3 days. Now, this is just my interpretation but I think this move was not only to align the strategy with the incoming thick fog, which Zhuge Liang predicted beforehand, but also to show his confidence and throw off Zhou Yu to make him think that Zhuge Liang was doomed.

2. After two days of doing nothing, Zhuge Liang finally goes to Li Su (Zhou Yu's friend and assistant) and borrows some boats, straw, soldiers, and battles equipment (war drums). Li Su reports back to Zhou Yu, who was puzzled at ZL not asking for resources to manufacture arrows in the normal way, but he didn't care anyways. As long as Zhuge Liang failed, it didn't really matter. (strategic shielding as it prevents Zhou Yu from getting suspicious and interfering)

3. On the third day, Zhuge Liang invited Li Su to go with him on the ships where he had previously prepared the straw to look like soldiers. Together, they travel across the river to Cao Cao's river base in the north. Li Su panics and asks why they're travelling to the enemy at night like this but Zhuge Liang tells him to chill.

4. As he had predicted days ago, a very dense fog covers the river, making the visibility so bad, the soldiers couldn't even see each other on the same boat. As they neared Cao Cao's base, they were ordered to beat the war drums and make it seem like they were going to attack the base in the fog. Cao Cao, not knowing that this is a trap, is reduced to a few options: giving up the camp, sending out boats to intercept them, and shoot arrows at them from a distance. Of course, giving up the camp was unthinkable, so he didn't do that; sending boats to intercept them might have been good if it were not for the dense fog and the inept officers in charge (see the context), so of course, Cao Cao used archers and shot at the straw dummies from a distance, unable to see if they were actual people or not.

5. Zhuge Liang then commanded the ships to face the other way, so the weight of the arrows would not sink the ship due to their weight. Both sides of the ships were then full of arrows, which he carried back to their camp. This allows him to avoid execution, gain Li Su's admiration, stun Zhou Yu into not trying to kill him, gain arrows to use for later, and take valuable ammunition from Cao Cao. (divergent thinking, strategic coverage)

It might not seem like a very impressive strategy but y'all got to remember that this was written in the 14th century way before even Sherlock got written, which makes this the first 'classic' to be scaleable in SCD. Also, RoTK is based on history (70% history and 30% fiction as somebody said), so this is already super impressive for an IRL person to think of.
