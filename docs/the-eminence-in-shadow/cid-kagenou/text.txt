IMPORTANT INFORMATION
The Bipolar Power Structure in the Capital:
Mitsugoshi Corporation: The public commercial front operated by Shadow Garden.
The Trade Alliance (Garter < Gettan): The economic front secretly controlled by the Diablos Cult.
Security Condition: The connections behind both sides' business fronts are kept completely secret from the public and their opponents.
Available Resources:
He cannot know the Cult's exact internal schedule or every market variable with 100% certainty. Instead, he employs a strategy of building a position in which, regardless of how the enemy responds, the final outcome is always brought back onto the most advantageous track for his side.
Saving Yukime's life earlier (the owner of the White Tower in the Lawless City) was no coincidence. It was preparation for a legal gray area and an unrepayable debt of gratitude, ready to be activated as a covert logistical base.
SETUP
Cid notices that the rivalry between Mitsugoshi and the Trade Alliance has escalated to the point of assassination attempts and bombardment. Expecting the enemy to launch even more vicious and underhanded attacks, Cid deliberately adopts the identity of John Smith to approach Yukime. She has established the 'Snow Fox' company as a supposed third faction that intends to sit back and watch the two sides fight, but she knows she needs someone like Shadow because simply waiting for both sides to exhaust themselves is not enough.
Creating an Intelligence Firewall: Yukime only knows that John Smith is Shadow. She has no idea about his connection to Mitsugoshi. Pretending to join forces with Yukime's 'Snow Fox' company allows John Smith to establish the appearance of a third faction.
Effect: This keeps Mitsugoshi completely outside the scope of legal suspicion while also cutting off any possibility of being traced by either the Diablos Cult's intelligence network or Shadow Garden.
=> Fabrication, Information Control, Strategy Building, Long-Term Planning, Resource Utilization, Indirect Manipulation.

I. ALLY MANIPULATION
1. Yukime's Faction
During their strategic discussion, John Smith performs four or five tasks simultaneously: manipulating the plan through misdirection, manipulating her psychology, setting up a reasoning test, and planting incentives for his partner.
A single gold coin can be multiplied many times over. But... in truth, it is merely a vague illusion called trust.
People think those pieces of paper are money, but they are not actually money. They are simply certificates proving that money has been deposited in a bank. For example, if we deposit 10,000 Zeni in a bank, we receive a certificate (paper money instead of gold coins) worth 10,000 Zeni, which can then be used for transactions. However, something interesting happens here. The 10,000 Zeni deposited in the bank and the certificate worth 10,000 Zeni... Haven't they effectively doubled into 20,000 Zeni? If the original 10,000 Zeni simply stays locked away in the vault, there is no problem. Even though the money supply has doubled, there will be no contradiction as long as the original 10,000 Zeni does not return to the market. But the bank still uses that 10,000 Zeni as collateral and lends out additional paper money.
The capital's economic boom originates from Mitsugoshi's business expansion.
Not wanting to miss out on the opportunity, merchants will want to borrow money. Banks respond by printing more paper money. Nobody wants to carry heavy gold coins around. Paper money is becoming widespread, while its true value is obscured. The 10,000 Zeni in the bank has been multiplied many times over and distributed throughout the capital, while Mitsugoshi is making enormous profits from the interest.

(Simply put, the original gold coins are lent out at high interest, and that interest is then used to cover the additional paper money being created.)

John Smith explains the mechanism of the 'illusion of trust': Paper money is merely a certificate for deposited gold. When banks lend out the deposited gold at high interest and issue more paper money than the amount of gold they actually hold, the amount of money in circulation doubles even though the underlying collateral value remains unchanged.
He throws down two banknotes: Mitsugoshi's (finely printed, with intricate watermark designs) and the Alliance's (crudely printed, poorly cut, and easy to counterfeit). The problem is this: the Alliance's banknotes are only circulating in the capital. Printing on a small scale would bring in only petty profits, while printing on a large scale would be exposed immediately. So how should they handle this?
An ordinary person would usually think of counterfeiting money for profit, but remember that his real objective is to make Mitsugoshi the only faction dominating the market.
Even if only for a short time, we will flood the market with a large amount of counterfeit money. Of course, it will be exposed. Immediately afterward, we will spread rumors about it among the people. They will then begin to suspect the money in their own wallets. Trust in paper money will collapse.
Being exposed is the key point.
Those who doubt the value of paper money will immediately rush to the banks to exchange it for gold coins.
If we move one step ahead, we will monopolize all the profits!

At this point in the discussion, John deliberately withholds the solution. He stops just before answering her question, 'The association's banknotes are only circulating in the capital...' and does not elaborate further.
First Intimidation
At this exact moment, John launches the first intimidation tactic. He crushes the wine glass in his hand, releases an oppressive killing intent, and growls, 'You... really think so?'
Behavioral Psychology Manipulation: The pressure of survival forces Yukime's brain to operate at full capacity and connect the dots on its own: Mass counterfeiting -> Deliberately allowing it to be exposed -> Spreading rumors -> People lose faith and rush to exchange their paper money for gold (Bank Run) -> Both banking systems collapse because they lack sufficient gold reserves. Psychologically, people tend to trust answers they have worked out for themselves more.
The Logic That Makes Yukime Trust Him: John Smith deliberately leads Yukime to understand that when the inflation attack begins, the major trading companies supporting the national economy will collapse simultaneously. The government will fall into chaos and devote all its resources to dealing with riots and the financial crisis. Taking advantage of this window of chaos, John Smith and Yukime will seize all the real gold they can exchange, erase every trace of the printing operation, and disappear without leaving anyone able to track them down.
After Yukime works out the market-collapse scenario, she begins presenting her logistical plan: 'I'll use the mansion in the forest in the Kingdom of Midgar as the printing factory... It's far from the city, so it will attract little attention.'
Second Intimidation
Noticing the flaws in this plan (it could easily be discovered by bandits, and its location within Midgar could be traced), John does not bother explaining or arguing against it. He immediately follows up with a second intimidation tactic. He approaches her, radiates an even more terrifying pressure, and repeats his threatening words. With no way out under this pressure, Yukime is forced to reveal the best trump card she has been keeping to herself: moving the entire printing operation and distribution network to the Lawless City, Yukime's own territory, into an abandoned mine. It lies beyond the reach of Midgar's law enforcement and can conceal the counterfeit operation completely. This is exactly what Cid had originally planned when he saved her during the earlier Lawless City arc.

RESULT 1
He forces his opponent to independently deduce the Bank Run scenario (while also testing whether his partner is trustworthy) and upgrade the logistical plan herself by relocating it to the Lawless City.
He plants the motive of 'erasing all traces and disappearing amid the chaos' to put Yukime at ease and establish absolute compliance. He turns her into someone completely subdued, both fearful of him and willingly offering up the entirety of Snow Fox's infrastructure, without daring to consider betrayal because of how dangerous he is.
He deceives Yukime into believing that both Mitsugoshi and the Alliance will be destroyed, while he himself has secured victory for Mitsugoshi. Yukime believes John Smith is planning to destroy both banks and establish a third faction with her to take over everything. In reality, this is Cid's strategic thinking under conditions of limited information:
- Primary objective: Protect Mitsugoshi and elevate it to a position of absolute monopoly (through the coded letter he leaves behind later).
- Contingency plan for the worst-case scenario (tear it down and rebuild): Cid understands that under conditions of informational uncertainty, the enemy may introduce unforeseen variables that cause the current Mitsugoshi to be brought down. If the worst-case scenario occurs, this counterfeit-money attack will still ensure that the Cult's entire old financial infrastructure is flattened. Cid only needs to create an entirely new Mitsugoshi 2.0 and then recruit all of Shadow Garden's personnel again.

2. Shadow Garden's Faction
The Identity Exposure Variable:
John Smith eliminates all of Gettan's elite troops sent to intercept the counterfeit-money shipment.
Alpha enters the battle to protect Mitsugoshi. During the clash, John Smith's mask breaks, revealing that he is Shadow (Cid).
Manipulation and Information Security:
When Alpha breaks down emotionally because she believes she has been betrayed, Shadow does not comfort her or offer an explanation. He immediately plays the role of a traitor and declares that their relationship is over. From that moment on, Shadow's strategic priority is no longer defeating Alpha. It shifts to controlling the information damage.
The important point is that Shadow does not have enough information to determine the consequences of his identity being exposed. He cannot know whether Alpha is the only witness to the battle, or whether there are also observers, reconnaissance magic, or the Diablos Cult's intelligence network monitoring events from behind the scenes. Under such uncertainty, any explanation or action could inadvertently reveal additional unwanted information.
Therefore, Shadow chooses the option with the lowest level of information leakage. Instead of explaining the plan or trying to regain Alpha's trust at the scene, he immediately continues playing John Smith and accepts that Alpha will believe he has betrayed Shadow Garden. This decision ensures that all the new information generated by the incident stops at 'John Smith = Shadow', rather than expanding into 'Shadow is still cooperating with Shadow Garden and secretly protecting Mitsugoshi'. This is also an intelligence-disruption trick, as it creates the possibility that any observers will return and spread the information that 'Shadow has just betrayed Shadow Garden'.
This causes Diablos to:
Shift its focus toward internal conflicts within Shadow Garden
Underestimate the possibility that the two sides are still cooperating
Have no reason to expect Shadow Garden to receive support from John Smith

3. Staging and Setup
Shadow withdraws, but the day before, specifically after the first counterfeit-money shipment, he had left a letter written in 'ancient characters' during a secret meeting with Beta. After Eta deciphers it, Shadow Garden bursts into tears as they realize the whole truth:Shadow had been dozens of steps ahead of the Cult, using the counterfeit money itself to drain the enemy's gold reserves. The letter specifies the coordinates of the massive hidden gold stash (obtained in exchange for the counterfeit money), allowing Shadow Garden to collect it and provide the backing reserves Mitsugoshi needs to weather the Bank Run. Thanks to this gold, Mitsugoshi not only survives but also buys up all the Alliance's bankrupt trading companies at rock-bottom prices, achieving its goal of financial monopoly.
The letter preserves information security all the way to the final stage. Without it, Alpha would not have thrown herself to the ground crying during the identity-exposure incident, making it possible to deceive Diablos.

RESULTS 2 AND 3
Gaslighting: He temporarily sacrifices emotional ties to deceive the Diablos intelligence network in case someone is watching.
Completely protects the secret of Mitsugoshi's identity from the enemy.
Leaves behind an encrypted letter revealing the gold stash, turning the risk of bankruptcy into an opportunity to seize 100% of the market.

II. ENEMY MANIPULATION
1. Gettan's Original Plan
The Trade Alliance is the Cult's financial front. Gettan's original plan consists of two steps:
Secretly recover and withdraw all the real gold stored in the Alliance's vaults, transferring it to a secure location controlled by the Cult. This takes a long time because it has to be done covertly.
Only after withdrawing all the gold does Gettan personally release counterfeit Alliance banknotes into the market for two purposes:
1. Trigger a credit collapse to push Mitsugoshi Corporation to the brink of bankruptcy.
2. Make the public believe that the Alliance went bankrupt because of the counterfeit-money disaster, completely concealing the fact that the Cult had secretly stolen all the real gold from its vaults beforehand.
2. Countermeasure
John Smith accurately reads this process and executes a move that cuts it off:
He floods the market with counterfeit money while Gettan is still preparing for Step 1.
Gettan is completely shocked and horrified when he discovers unfamiliar counterfeit money circulating ahead of schedule. Triggering the market collapse early freezes all of the Cult's assets, putting the enormous amount of gold that has not yet been withdrawn at risk of disappearing entirely along with the Alliance's bankruptcy.

Manipulation Effect:
Having fallen into a panic after seemingly losing all of the Cult's assets, Gettan is pushed by John Smith's attack into a false-attribution trap:
Gettan convinces himself that the only party with both the ability and the motive to pull off such a suicidal move must be Mitsugoshi. He believes Mitsugoshi is willing to collapse the market itself because it has secretly stockpiled an enormous reserve of gold deep in its vaults, ready to revive itself after the crisis.
Desperate to compensate for the Cult's assets that John Smith has just trapped and rendered inaccessible, Gettan orders all his remaining forces to raid Mitsugoshi's gold reserves. In his desperation, he forces them to reveal themselves and commit their full strength to attacking the gold vault in person, making them easy for John Smith to eliminate.
