Manga Fyodor Doc (Chapter 129)

Introduction:
This doc is meant to establish manga Fyodor’s feats up until the latest chapter, currently Chapter 129. The doc does not take into account the BSD Light Novels or the Anime. The SCD categories used are from here. For simplicity’s sake, I did not include all possible categories that contributed to each feat, only the categories I think benefited the most from the feat. Thus it does not mean that categories not found within this document results in Fyodor being feat-less in that category, but rather it is simply not a key aspect of the mentioned feats.

Note: This doc, while attempting to be comprehensive, is not authoritative. Analysis of feats may differ from person to person, and how high each feat scales may also differ. This doc was done to the best of my ability, with each analysis made so as to be the most logically consistent with his character, while also ensuring my analysis of his feats are logical and well-supported by the provided evidence.

Chap 42:
Overview: Fyodor let himself be captured by Ace. Before his capture, he told the kidnapper to tell Ace that the key to freeing himself was to remove his consciousness from the mind space. When confronted and told to be Ace’s underling, he said no (Certified baller move). Ace called him a big dumb stinky pants and left, while Fyodor was being cleaned. During this time, an underling came by, and Fyodor said that his ability let him control space and consciousness. When the underling left before Ace arrived, Fyodor used the wine to destroy the clock and the intercom, and stuffed cork into the keyhole. When Ace returned, Fyodor proposed a game to settle the issue. The game was to take turns drawing a card, and then the other would guess whether the next card would be higher or lower. Once the guesser guesses wrongly, the other will become the guesser. Fyodor won by memorising all the blemishes on the cards, but due to the unmoving clock, broken intercom and the lock that won’t work, Ace assumed that Fyodor created a mind space, and thus hung himself to free his consciousness.

This feat contributes to Short Term Planning, Observation, WMI and Indirect Manipulation. The plan to kill Ace was fairly solid, making him believe that he was in a mind space and thus making him kill himself without exposing too much. Observation was important, by looking at all the blemishes on the cards, and the memory needed to remember all of them in the card game. Indirect manipulation was due to Fyodor manipulating the surroundings to make Ace think he is in a mind space. (I’m not too sure if this is indirect manipulation or fabrication, but I feel it leans towards indirect.) However, there are some questions I have to raise regarding the validity of the feat. The clock seems to be a grandfather clock, which runs on a pendulum and gears.

This text implies that he destroyed the clock by splashing wine on it, as he did with the intercom.

This would be impossible with a normal grandfather clock. It could be some digital version that requires circuits but considering the personality of Ace, who likely enjoys a lavish lifestyle, this is most likely a real mechanical grandfather clock.

The other issue is the intercom. The wine used is likely red wine, and after splashing on the intercom the wine should be visible. He could have wiped the wine away, but Ace’s instructions to his underling was to dry Fyodor, and any recent stains from red wine should be fairly visible and cause some alarms to be raised. The clock shifted 5 minutes from before Ace re-entered, but due to the clock being broken we cannot estimate how long Ace took. However I doubt it would be for very long, as a huge difference in actual time and the time on the clock would be noticed by Ace, and due to the dungeon being poorly ventilated, and likely cooler than above ground, it would take quite some time for the wine to evaporate. Ace would likely notice, considering his fairly good observation skills.

The weakest argument I have is about the cork. Wine corks can be between 38mm to 55mm long, but most door locks are ~5cm or 50mm long. This means that if Fyodor stuffed the cork in the door lock, it would not be accessible from the other side. Ace would not have been able to unlock himself and enter the room. Ace could have not locked the door, but it is unlikely because it's Fyodor, and Ace knows he’s a threat. The issue with this argument is the door may not be ordinary.

The door shown is slightly less wide than a brick, and bricks are usually 110mm wide. Since these seem larger, I will give the benefit of doubt and say they are 180mm wide. This means the lock can be larger, giving it space for 2 locking mechanisms. This does seem redundant as most doors only have one, thus by obstructing it from one side it obstructs it from both sides, but it’s the most likely possibility of this strategy working. While strange, it could be a special kind of door that exists in this world, and thus I am willing to suspend my disbelief at it working.

However, it should be noted that these aren’t foolproof arguments either, as each can be answered by simply assuming that that was how things worked in the setting of BSD.

Overall, this feat has 2 interpretations. If we assume my arguments are valid, the planning coverage and invincibility would be negatively affected. Otherwise, it would be as interpreted initially.

This is where one of Fyodor’s most misinterpreted feats takes place. Fyodor and Dazai are playing a number guessing game. If I assume that they only use 6 digit numbers, there would be 900000 combinations. Most would assume it’s 999999 combinations, but most of the time when people guess with 6 digit numbers, they tend to not use numbers lower than 100000, even though the case could be made that numbers like 069420 are technically valid. This means that the probability of guessing is 1 in 900000, which is a 0.0001% chance. Of course, this is a low ball, as we do not exactly know the rules upon which this game was played. They could have more numbers, they could have less. Due to such a low probability of guessing it right, many use this as a proof of his intelligence.

However, this isn’t the case. While they say it’s a number guessing game, it’s more likely about guessing the number that the other would pick. Meaning instead of a game about probability, it’s a game of mind reading. Remember what happened in Chapter 64? Fyodor stated that unless someone has equal intellect to him, they cannot understand. Dazai was able to communicate with him in code, meaning that they are capable of understanding each other. But it doesn’t stop there. There were also moments such as in chapter 52, where Dazai ignored the other cars because he knew they were bait as “It was what he would do.”

This means that Dazai and Fyodor not only have similar intellect, but have similar thought processes, thus making the number guessing game less “What would the other person do?” and more “What would I do?”. This means that not only is this feat not a simple guessing/intuition feat but a mind reading one, most of Fyodor’s mind reading feats of Dazai are not as impressive as initially assumed and are probably going to scale lower.

Side note: Fyodor’s statement is somewhat faulty as even if people have the same IQ or the “same level of intelligence”, they may not have the same thought processes, which means they might end up with different conclusions. Even if people have similar types and levels of intelligence, the way they think may still be different. This further reinforces my interpretation that because Dazai and Fyodor have similar thought processes, it isn’t that hard for one to read the other’s mind, thus reducing the scale of their mind reading feats against each other.

Analysis of Fyodor’s Master Plan:
The summary above was done to the best of my knowledge of the manga’s characters, what is seen in the manga, and what I can deduce without making excessive logical leaps.

Prelude:
As stated above, everything theorized in the Prelude was all hypothetical, and thus it is hard to definitively assign categories. However, if I had to assign cats, it would be manipulation and foresight. There is not enough information to properly assign subcats, only the cats in general.

Act 1, Set up:
Fyodor gets Indirect Manipulation and Mass manipulation from this act.

Genchiro likely had no shortage of brilliant minds to help him plan out his goal, but why did he choose Fyodor? My best guess was that Fyodor somehow mentioned that in the prophecy Fyodor made up, he mentioned to Genchiro that he was the best person to do it, despite the risks. This thus gives him indirect manipulation. However, this is a theory and in no way backed by official proof.

Tricking people into thinking his ability was touch based is Mass Manipulation.

Act 2, Confrontation:
Fyodor gets Long Term Planning, and Planning Invincibility and Planning Effectiveness.

The plan takes place over a long period of time, thus LTP is obvious.

Planning Invincibility as it was unlikely for people to discover the plan until the very last moment, as Genchiro was the mastermind, thus making people focus on him, leaving Fyodor free to carry out his plan.

Fyodor gets Planning Versatility as the plan would be achievable even with drastic changes, with the exception of what happened in Prison Game. As long as Fukuchi follows Fyodor’s plan, which he would, and Fyodor gets killed by a vampire, which is also very likely, Fyodor would win, thus also contributing Planning Effectiveness.

It is unclear if Fyodor’s plan accounted for Genchiro sending him to Mersault, and thus making a plan that allowed him to not be directly involved after the Cannibalism Stage. If so, it would contribute Prediction. If not, Fyodor gets Planning Contingency instead.

Act 3, Resolution:
Fyodor gets Strategy Resilience.

He only gets strategy resilience due to being able for the strategy to succeed whether or not Chuuya was a real vampire, or if Dazai truly died or not.

The biggest problem is that Fyodor only convinced people that touch was the activation condition. This meant that as long as Dazai was at a distance, he would be safe. Thus, Dazai could have just killed Fyodor using something like a gun, and didn’t have to go with using a vampire. With Chuuya’s gravitational ability, they didn’t have to kill him at all. Chuuya could lock Fyodor’s movements using gravity, and leave him be until the whole vampire situation is sorted out. Even if Fyodor’s inside vampires tried to free him, they are no match for Chuuya. This heavily affects his Strategy as any of these other outcomes would result in failure of his entire plan, thus hindering Planning Coningency and Planning in general.

Fyodor not bothering to check if Dazai’s dead should have signaled to Dazai that something is wrong, as Dazai clearly stated himself,

So Fyodor trusting Chuuya, who is under Bram, who is under Genchiro and not Fyodor, to kill Dazai should have sus as hell. The strategy would have fallen apart if Dazai actually realised this, so even the Strategy Resilience is not great, but it’s the only cat I can give Fyodor here.

Fyodor was also not actively foreseeing what Dazai would do, and instead reacting to what happened, confident that either way he would be able to get a vampire to kill him.

This does not affect Fyodor’s initial plan, as he really only left the part with the most variables, getting himself killed by a vampire, up to improvisation. The plan itself is fine, but his strategy in the prison game is terrible. Even if you could argue that he predicted that Dazai would do all that, then he won not because he was good, but because Dazai fumbled hard.

Contingency:
This gives Fyodor Planning Contingency and possibly deception. Planning contingency is obvious, but I’m unsure about deception as the sniper and false stealth plane are more to buy time, which doesn’t seem like active deception, but can be argued as such.
Conclusion:
Fyodor is not a complete narrative merchant like how some would believe, but isn’t a high tier either. While demonstrating some solid planning feats, the lack of clarity on what was his original plan, a contingency or an improvised move lead to his scaling being rather unclear. He is also weighed down by a poor showing in the Prison Game, making it seem like he doesn’t do that well when things aren’t preplanned.

His best category by far is planning, which while simple, was very effective and hard for people to notice. It isn’t infallible, as Dazai was being rather passive with waiting for Fyodor’s schemes to come to light rather than actively trying to figure out his plans or intercept his moves. His only real attempt at this was during the Prison Game, in which he didn’t do that well in. His plan could probably be foiled by someone smarter and more proactive than Dazai, or at the very least someone who could see through the suspicious actions Fyodor took.

The thing that weighs Fyodor down the most is the fact that aside from Planning and FSIQ, most of his other categories are somewhat mediocre, and even nonexistent. His STP for example cannot be scaled as he has zero feats of seeing through someone’s deception. All the actions he foresees do not have any form of deception involved, and the only time someone actively deceived Fyodor was Dazai in prison game, which Fyodor ended up not seeing through. This heavily weighs down his scaling as anyone who has better deception than Dazai might cause Fyodor to slip up and predict wrong.
