Character Overview:
* Fyodor Dostoevsky: One of the main antagonists in BSD. He is the leader of a group of thieves known as “Rats in the House of the Dead.”
* Ace: One of the executives of the Port Mafia. He previously caused the downfall of the Mafia’s casino.
* Mori Ougai: The boss of the Port Mafia.
After orchestrating the fall of the aircraft Moby Dick, Fyodor Dostoevsky was discovered and targeted by Ace. Ace hired the most professional kidnapper in the country to capture Fyodor. Everything seemed to be going smoothly. Fyodor, tightly wrapped in a prisoner’s uniform and bound with leather straps, was completely immobilized.
Ace reported to Mori Ougai and requested permission to personally interrogate Fyodor. Mori approved. Ace went to meet Fyodor and ordered his servant to unbind him and dress him.
Ace and Fyodor had a conversation in which Ace demanded that Fyodor serve him in his plan to overthrow Mori. Fyodor refused and even threatened to kill Ace, enraging him. Ace smashed a wine bottle on Fyodor’s head and stormed out, saying he would return later.
A young boy, Ace’s servant, approached Fyodor, cleaned the wound on his head, and told him his story.
Later, Ace returned, and Fyodor invited him to play a game: if Fyodor won, he would go free; if he lost, he would become Ace’s servant. Fyodor won the game flawlessly, sending Ace into a state of panic. Fyodor then manipulated Ace into committing suicide.
In the end, Fyodor revealed that his true purpose in coming there was to obtain the list of Port Mafia members and their abilities.

6.1. The rules of the game that Ace proposed can be summarized simply as follows:
   * Each turn, Ace will draw one card.
   * Fyodor must guess whether the next card Ace draws will have a higher or lower value than the current one.
   * If Fyodor guesses correctly, he continues guessing. If he guesses wrong, then it’s Ace’s turn to draw, and Fyodor has to guess his card again.
   * When all cards have been drawn, the one with the most correct guesses wins.
6.2. Ace would be the one to draw the first card (a decision he made on his own). The first base card was the 9 of Spades.
At that moment, Fyodor commented: since the cards range from 1 to 13, the probability of the next card being lower than 9 was slightly higher. He chose "Lower."
Ace then drew the 4 of Hearts. Fyodor guessed correctly and retained his turn.
Fyodor continued by guessing lower again, and the next card was the 3 of Clubs,  correct again.
He guessed lower once more, and the next card was the 2 of Diamonds,  again correct.
Ace, watching, thought to himself that Fyodor was just getting lucky, and that by the second half of the deck, he (Ace) would surely win; after all, he was a gambler specializing in card counting and probability.
However, Fyodor kept guessing flawlessly:
Higher – 6 of Clubs
Higher – 9 of Diamonds
Lower – 5 of Hearts
...and so on, as if he could see the future.
Final result: Fyodor 52 – 0 Ace.
Fyodor talked about randomness and probability when guessing the first card to make Ace believe that Fyodor was truly playing based on intuition and luck. Additionally, in the anime during this scene, we can see Fyodor continuously saying his guesses,  “Higher, Lower...” ,  without pause, all the way through the entire deck. Between his guesses, after confusing Ace, there were no time gaps.
This indicates Fyodor’s extremely fast information processing speed. On average, a person speaking 120 words per minute takes about 60 seconds; that is 0.5 seconds per word. This means Fyodor spoke 49 words (corresponding to 49 cards,  excluding the base card and the first two guesses, during which he was distracting Ace by boosting his confidence and pride), starting from the fourth card, he gave non-stop answers.
That means Fyodor processed all the information for 49 cards in about 24.5 seconds.
It may seem like it’s just 49 cards, but in reality, the amount of information being analyzed is much greater. Here's why:
   * For example, suppose there are 7 cards in play. Let’s divide the brain's process into two parts while playing:
   * A: “Cards already guessed.”
   * B: “Cards remaining to be guessed.”
So initially, it’s A: 0 – B: 7.
   * When guessing the first card, you move it to part A and remove it from part B. So now it’s A: 1 – B: 6.
→ This means the brain has done 2 processing actions:
1 (the card being guessed) × 2 (actions: recognition + elimination) = 2 total processes.
   * For the second guess: A: 2 – B: 5. Now, 3 processes take place:
1 (current card being guessed) × 2 (as above) + 1 (previously guessed card now in A) × 1 (comparison) = 3.
   * For the third guess: A: 3 – B: 4.
→ Total processes:
1 (new card) × 2 + 2 (previous guesses in A) × 1 = 4.
   * Continuing this pattern up to 7 cards results in roughly 35 information-processing operations.
So while the number of cards is small, the cognitive workload compounds,  and Fyodor doing all of that in real-time with perfect accuracy is a major feat.
So with 49 cards that Fyodor calculated, he actually performed 1,421 information-processing actions in 24.5 seconds. Sounds kind of absurd and over-the-top, but hey,  this is an unrealistic character, so that’s just how it is 😅.
Some might argue that Fyodor didn’t actually do it this way since there’s no concrete proof, but I believe this is the only viable method if you want to win this game flawlessly across all 52 turns. (I’ve played this game with some friends, and when I asked how they played it, they all said they did raw mental calculation,  eliminating the remaining cards and remembering them to guess the next one.)
Later on, there are details showing that Fyodor really did do mental calculation without cheating or using any special method.
Moreover, it’s not just calculation; Fyodor also had to memorize and mentally manipulate the full set of 52 cards (this time, I’m counting all the cards since this feat relates to PSI, so I’m starting from the moment Fyodor began true mental calculation).
Across all 52 turns, each time Fyodor had to manipulate his internal mental deck, distinguishing between the cards that had already been drawn and the ones remaining. That means 52 rounds of memory manipulation, and each time, he had to handle all 52 cards in his head (because failing to maintain the entire dataset would risk error, but as we can see, he guessed everything perfectly).
So essentially, Fyodor performed 52 memory manipulations, each involving 52 cards:
52 × 52 = 2,704 instances of memory data manipulation, all within 24.5 seconds.
In conclusion, this feat demonstrates an insane level of CPI (PSI + WMI) from Fyodor, in my opinion.
