For Honor’s Plot:
        Apollyon is the main antagonist of the video game For Honor. Among a cast of rather lackluster and underwhelming characters within the game, Apollyon is one of the few fleshed out and interesting characters. The aura she exudes is larger than the Empire she commands, with an ideology that serves as the incentive for the actions that are underway throughout the main story. To understand Apollyon’s ideology we must first understand the setting of the story and her background. The story takes place in a fictional region known as Heathmoor during a medieval time period. The land of Heathmoor was home to the Knights and Vikings, who rarely interacted at this point in time. This was until a natural disaster known as the cataclysm occurred. Earthquakes of immeasurable magnitude spanning across the entire planet, meteor showers, and massive volcanic eruptions destroyed the lands and permanently changed the topography of Earth. The destruction was so far and wide, Japanese warriors from the East traveled all the way to the swamplands of Heathmoor in search of a new home. By the end of it, the lands were divided between the three factions. The Knights owned Ashfeld, the Vikings owned the frozen lands of Valkenheim, and the Samurai inhabited the swamplands known as the Myre. The disaster was so catastrophic it's a miracle that anyone managed to survive. Naturally, resources became scarce, and eventually the factions met and fought for centuries. At first it was a war for resources. As time went by, the reason they fought became less and less clear, and the once motivated and fierce warriors became complacent and searching for a sign of peace. Any semblance of peace however, was shattered when Apollyon arrived.

Apollyon’s ideology and goal:
        Apollyon grew up in Ashfeld, the home of the Knights within Heathmoor. The Vikings began pillaging towns all across Ashfeld when she was young. She watched as the townsfolk cried for help, only to be met with a Lawbringer[1] known as Dante, who deemed their village unworthy of defending and fled to safer lands. Apollyon watched as her village was plundered, the inhabitants killed and raped, and from all of the death and destruction she came out of it with a lesson learned. For one, the world is separated by two groups of people; predator, and prey. Wolves, and sheep. The strong prey on the weak, they exert their power in any way they see fit, living by their own hand and carving their own path. The wolves are warriors, those who starve for war and conflict, some revel in this fact while the others will deny it and claim to be fighting for abstract concepts such as duty, honor, or kinship. Those who fight, not for the sake of fighting but for ideas such as peace and a world without conflict, are hypocrites who deny their true calling. Fighting for principles and ideas antithetical to their warrior nature, however their actions still make them wolves in their own right. Then there are those who enjoy the benefits of the concepts some of the warriors fight and die for. Those who crumble under pressure with no resistance, and those who ignore their calling to conflict. Prey. Prey don’t fight for their own ideas, they don’t risk their lives in spite of what they may preach, they’re sheeps awaiting their own slaughter. Humans are unique, as they are the only animal that can willingly choose to be either predator or prey. Immediately, Apollyon sought out to live by the laws of her own ideology. She spent her youth training in combat, then lived as a criminal, then a soldier, and then a warden[2]. When she turned 16, she had finally found Dante who was by now a frail old man. Mercilessly. She dragged him onto the streets and publicly executed him, she proceeded to take his armor and had it refitted by a blacksmith. She brandished that armor for the rest of her life, wearing it as a warning for those who dare to use the law against the strong. From that day forward, her ideology was truly cemented into her well being. Until the day she died, she worked towards one goal, a plan set in motion to create the ideal world for the wolves to rise among the weak. A world plagued by eternal war.

Narrative:
Throughout her journeys, Apollyon has come across warriors of all different factions, even exchanging pleasantries. She noted a conversation with one female Viking warrior in an observable, which implies that she can speak and understand Icelandic. She also speaks later to multiple Samurai warriors implying that she can also speak and understand Japanese. One may point out that the different factions are showcased speaking English, however this is a design choice done specifically when members of a faction are speaking to their own members. During the Viking segment of the campaign, Runa remarks that she doesn’t speak Japanese after being asked if she managed to get directions from Samurai combatants. Showcasing that the factions speak almost always in their respective native language, and English isn’t a lingua franca in this universe. Apollyon is not only able to speak the languages of the three distinct factions, she is also particularly knowledgeable in their history and cultural customs/ideals. Something she takes into account when developing her plans and strategies.
(VCI, knowledge)

The Blackstone Legion had many warlords prior to Apollyon taking power, all either too weak or incompetent to lead, resulting in their deaths. Apollyon would be the 8th and final warlord of the Blackstone Legion. Shortly after assuming control, she destroyed 17 "permanent" Viking settlements in Ashfield, showcasing her expertise in military affairs and how quickly she adapted to becoming a Warlord.

Chapter 1.1: Warlords and Cowards
In chapter 1.1 we are introduced to the primary protagonist of the Knights campaign, a Warden serving under a Warlord named Hervis Daubeny. Daubeny was a former member of Apollyon’s Blackstone Legion, who deserted after stealing some supplies. A lawbringer known as Holden Cross was sent to punish Daubeny for his treachery. After Daubeny refused trial by combat, the Warden was chosen to fight Cross’ second. As the Warden beat and executed the Blackstone warrior, Cross saw that the Warden was a potent and skilled combatant being led by a coward, a wolf led by a sheep. Holden Cross then accepted the Warden as part of the Blackstone Legion. A decision Apollyon agreed with. Showcasing that Holden Cross on the surface knew what Apollyon wanted. He was, in fact, her second in command and was regarded as her most stalwart ally.

Chapter 1.2: And Stay Out
Swiftly after the Warden is recruited, he is sent by Apollyon to save the Iron Legion from an invading Viking force. The Iron Legion was once the most powerful Knight faction, however over the years it became weak and complacent. Their defenses began to crumble, and their resistance against the now invading Vikings was a futile effort which would inevitably end in defeat if they weren’t provided aid. The Warden was sent by Apollyon as their relief force for a few reasons. For one, to test the Warden. His resolve, his combat skills, and to see if he sticks to his oath. The second reason, one warrior wouldn’t be enough to fill the gaps within the Iron Legion’s lines no matter how powerful, and as a result the Iron Legion would suffer more casualties and become even weaker in spite of the aid. A weaker Iron Legion would be more susceptible to pressure. Lastly, the Warden served as leverage, showcasing the might of warriors who belong to the Blackstone Legion, subliminally encouraging the Iron Legion soldiers to accept their protection. Shortly after the battle of Harrowgate had been won, the Iron Legion had been integrated into the Blackstone Legion. Even though now the Iron Legion was officially disbanded, its old image remained as a once noble, honorable and fierce fighting force in the lands of Ashfeld. And now with its soldiers under a new Legion that exemplifies strength and action, the seeds had been laid for a new Iron Legion to be birthed in the near future.

While Ragnar embodied the oppressive, beast-like nature of the Vikings, the Raider contrasted with a more honorable and morally righteous approach. While Ragnar did good to invoke the urges that deep down they all had hidden, he wouldn't be an ideal leader for the Vikings due to his lack of empathy for those who don’t directly serve him.
