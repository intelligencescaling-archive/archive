To understand the scope of Sparkle's implementation, its plan is broken down into five strategic pillars:

1. Deception: Sparkle constructed a narrative divided into two opposing poles (the evil streamer vs. the abandoned mask). By giving the protagonists a visible conflict and a just cause to defend, she prevented them from questioning the legitimacy of the event or the lack of a real motive behind Sparxi's attack.
2. Planning: He designed a narrative architecture with contingencies at every stage. He knew that the mimetic infection would force the intervention of the Astral Express, that the use of the imagined landscape would allow the battle to be isolated in a visually dazzling setting, and that Sparxi's defeat would grant the Pathfinder a massive influx of wish power from the audience.
3. Emotional Intelligence (EI): He tapped into the Astral Express's sense of responsibility and heroism. He knew precisely how Himeko would react to memories of the academy, how March 7th would act in the face of imminent danger, and how the Pathfinder would respond to a direct challenge.
4. Theory of Mind (ToM): Sparkle simultaneously read the minds of both the Arcadia Plan audience and the protagonists. He understood what the masses expected to see (a streamer show with drama and heroic resolution) and what the Pathfinder needed to feel to become fully engaged without suspecting he was inside a pre-written script.
5. Forecast: He calculated the long-term repercussions within the Fantas Games. By positioning the Pathfinder as the victorious winner over the region's most viral streamer, he guaranteed him a privileged place in the eyes of the masses and the Exaltation itself, ensuring his relevance in the competition without risking anyone's life.

All of this can be understood in 4 premises that explain the mental process of this phrase.

Premise 1: The Disintegration of the Ego and the Void of Identity.
For most characters, a mask is a disguise that conceals a "true self." In Sparkle's thinking, the "true self" doesn't exist; beneath one mask lies only another previous mask. By asserting that we are all "shed masks," he establishes that individuality is a narrative illusion and that the individuals on stage are merely playing roles that a higher entity or mind (a Director) used in the past and then discarded.

Premise 2: The Metaphor of Ecdysis (Shedding the Skin).
The act of "discarding" directly evokes the biological process of ecdysis (when a snake sheds its skin). The old skin is left dry, hollow, and abandoned, but it served a vital purpose at one time. Following this logic, the weakened "Sparkle" that the Astral Express finds at the school wasn't the suffering mastermind, but rather a shed skin, a residual shell that Sparkle deliberately removed to generate empathy, curiosity, and propel the hero's investigation.

Premise 3: The Dynamics of Monodrama in Plan Arcadia.
If the school Sparkle was just a discarded mask, the hypothesis that Sparxie "defeated" or "replaced" her in an actual contest falls apart. Sparxie and the discarded Sparkle aren't rivals, but rather two pieces of the same costume. Sparkle operates through a three-phase cycle:

1. Discard a skin (the victim version) to ignite the intrigue of the Astral Express.
2. Equip a new skin (Sparxie) to take on the role of the viral antagonist and capture massive attention.
3. Reintegrate both masks once the Pathfinder completes his role as "Protagonist".

Premise 4: The Provocative Mirror towards the Pathfinder.
The phrase isn't just a personal reflection, but a barb aimed at the nature of the Pathfinder. As an artificially created entity, with sealed memories and wielding a Star-shaped object to fulfill Elio's script, the Pathfinder is itself a vessel or "mask" designed by someone else. Sparkle subtly mocks the fact that everyone in Plan Arcadia is acting out inherited roles, but only she is aware of being part of the charade.
