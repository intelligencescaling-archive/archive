OVERVIEW:
BACKGROUND INFO: Frederick “Charles” Krueger, better known as Freddy Krueger, is the primary antagonist of the Nightmare on Elm Street series, which comprises multiple films, novels, comics, and video games. Freddy, within the story, was a serial child murderer. Eventually, his deeds were discovered and he was arrested. However, due to a technicality, Freddy walked free without punishment. As a result, the wrathful parents of the children banded together in a mob and killed Freddy via burning him alive. Before he died, Freddy made a deal with the dream demons, granting him the ability to intrude the dreams of anyone in his own personal “dream world”, where he has complete dominion. Freddy utilizes this power to continue his string of murders, the legacy of the Springwood Slasher now eternally burning in a blaze of supernatural revenge. This document will specifically cover an interpretation for his full-scale plan in the Freddy vs Jason vs Ash timeline, which is notions convoluted and unclear. This interpretation creates an established and focused series of events, clearing up any discrepancies within the plot itself (due to the numerous retcons and contradictions in the continuity itself). This interpretation posits that Freddy had enacted a plan that lasted for 21+ years (and possibly decades worth of preparation process, which would stretch the plan to 40-50 years) and had five major contingencies to ensure his revival around these multiple decades (with an countless amount of miniature contingencies and shieldings present throughout).

FREDDY’S PLAN (C1):

1988 — ALICE CONDITIONING/FIRST CONTINGENCY
In 1988, after the events of “The Dream Warriors”, Freddy is made clear of Kristen’s unique ability to draw people into the dream world. Here, he begins his first formal revival contingency within the following film - “The Dream Master”.

A year after the events of the third film, Kristen, Kincaid, and Joey (the main trio in the previous film) were released from the institution where Freddy terrorized them and returned to their normal lives. However, when Kristen dreams that she is in Freddy's old boiler room, she summons Joey and Kincaid into the dream world, fearing that Krueger might have returned. Joey and Kincaid, angered, insist that Freddy is gone. The next day, Kristen meets with her boyfriend Rick Johnson, and their friends - Rick's sister Alice (whom Freddy will build his contingency on), Sheila, and Debbie.
Kristen has become popular, while Kincaid and Joey are considered outcasts in school. Kincaid and Joey confront Kristen at school for pulling them into her dream and tell her that they don’t want to be involved anymore, and she might end up bringing Freddy back by mistake (Freddy anticipated this and implored this as a strategy to isolate Kristen).
That night, Kincaid falls asleep and awakens in a junkyard, where Freddy has been accidentally resurrected. Though Kincaid puts up a good fight, and unable to contact Kristen to pull him out of the nightmare due to Freddy’s isolation strategy, Freddy kills him. Joey is up late watching TV and listening to music but eventually falls asleep. Inevitably, he is killed as well. Kristen is heartbroken to learn that Kincaid and Joey are dead, feeling guilty that she could not save them realizing Freddy is back to finish the three of them off. At dinner, she realizes that her mother has put sleeping pills in her meal because Kristen has not been sleeping, out of fear of Freddy (it’s likely Freddy had predicted this and utilizes it to ensure Kristen falls asleep). She tries to resist but eventually falls asleep. Once Kristen falls asleep, Freddy enacts the strategy behind his first contingency —>
* Freddy then taunts and torments Kristen (saying he’s going to use her to get to the other kids), as to eventually bait her into pulling Alice into the dream world (abusing and manipulating her into using her dream powers).
* This strategy works, and Alice is pulled into the dream world by Kristen.
* Freddy then lunges for Alice (misdirecting Kristen into believing his earlier words were true). But then, Kristen takes the blow and transfers her power to Alice (as per Freddy’s prediction). No matter how this played out, it would’ve worked in Freddy’s favor. 1) If Kristen didn’t save Alice, Freddy would still eliminate an enemy and could continue deceiving and using Alice to eliminate different individuals. 2) If Kristen did die, but chose to not pass on her power, Freddy gains the opportunity to kill them both and Kristen’s power (of pulling people into her dreams) is forever deleted, subtracting a major method of counteracting Freddy from the equation. 3) If Freddy chooses to simply kill Kristen outright and never aims for Alice, it still defaults to the same two outcomes (if Kristen passes on her power before she dies, outcome one occurs. If Kristen chooses not to, outcome two would occur)  - With Kristen giving her power to Alice, Freddy predicts (and ensures) it will pass through him first (this strategy/plan is confirmed via Freddy strategically killing Kristen directly behind him and by preparing his body with souls beforehand).
* With the power going through Freddy first (as per Freddy’s goal), he attaches a piece of himself into it and smoothly sends it to Alice, deceiving Alice into believing the power transfer worked seamlessly (Freddy does this as it now makes Alice carry a piece of Freddy, making Freddy fundamentally immortal no matter the result of this film, setting up a concealed contingency).
* Freddy, by attaching a part of himself to Alice, ensures he bare minimum survives after the events of this film, fundamentally implementing a contingency.
After Freddy’s strategy, he continues eliminating Kristen’s friends in the dream world. Alice, now possessing Kristen’s ability, decides to fight against Freddy to halt his killings (this confrontation is one Freddy predicted and ensured via killing Alice’s friends gradually, forcing her to take action). Alice and Freddy have a showdown within the dream world, and no matter the outcome, Freddy knew it would benefit him —>
1. If Alice wins and “defeats” Freddy, he has already set up a revival contingency in the form of attaching a piece of himself onto the power that was passed from Kristen to Alice. This allows for Freddy’s spirit to live on through Alice regardless of his victory/defeat in this film, establishing his first major revival contingency in his holistic plan.
2. Once again, if Freddy loses - He still would’ve dealt massive damage to Alice’s life by eliminating her closest friends and subjecting her to trauma. This aids in his later contingency, as it influences Alice into forming a neat obsessive relationship with Freddy’s defeat built on hate, ensuring that when given the chance - She would join a hypothetical group destined around keeping Freddy contained upon its creation (which is a step Freddy had predicted and implemented into his holistic plan). This group of course, would end up being a mole operation actually intended to acquire the Necronomicon for Freddy, meaning this outcome would indirectly influence Alice into aiding in Freddy obtaining his ultimate goal within a later contingency/step of the plan.
3. If Freddy wins, he permanently removes Kristen’s ability from ever being utilized against him again. This is crucial, as it would severely limit the level of counteraction that could ever be taken against him in the future - Fundamentally deleting his most glaring current weakness.

=< 1999 — “WILL ADMINISTRATION”, BUILDING THE THIRD CONTINGENCY
Freddy’s plan started before his planned encounter with his only kin, Maggie. As this encounter took place in 1999 and resulted in Freddy’s death (likely also planned by Freddy), it’s reasonable Freddy’s holistic plan started before that point.

Freddy’s main plan started with his strategy to administer Will into the mental institution. Why did Freddy want this? —>
1. Will is Lori’s boyfriend (who currently lives in the main Elm Street house). Will naturally cares for Lori extensively, and upon hearing of Freddy’s return years later, he’s likely to break out to help her. By doing this, Will would access the knowledge of the sleeping medication present at the institution, which he’d attempt to share with Lori in order to ensure she won’t be harmed by Freddy.
2. Will breaking out would also strengthen the validity of Freddy’s influence upon his revival, fostering more influence whilst also indirectly manipulating Lori and her boyfriend (Will) into returning to the mental institution under the guise of the unique medication that is held within.
This fundamentally allowed Freddy to set up a trap years in advance, predicting the exact actions of Will roughly 3-4 years before they occurred.

Now, how did Freddy exactly go about enacting this step of administering Will to the mental institution? —>
1. Freddy had killed Lori’s mother whilst disguised as her father, ensuring Will would see the altercation occur.
2. Will would then blame Lori’s father for the death of Lori’s mother, attempting to convince everyone of the “truth”.
3. Lori’s father is a high-ranking member of the police force. He’d naturally have the influence to administer Will to the local mental institution if persuaded to, which he would be in this case due to the false accusations.
4. Lori’s father knows of Freddy’s existence and is a part of a group of adults within the town that attempts to conceal Freddy’s existence by administering teenagers who have survived Freddy’s attacks to the local mental institution.
5. Due to the fact Lori’s father didn’t truly kill Lori’s mother, Will would have no proof of the murder and would appear nearly insane to the general public and thus Lori’s father could never be incriminated.
6. Lori’s father would be given the secondary reason to administer Will to the institution due to the fact he’d likely deduce Freddy actually killed his wife if he himself didn’t. To conceal this fact and prevent Freddy from gaining influence, he’d be forced to administer Will to the institution regardless.
7. If Will wasn’t administered, Freddy’s influence would’ve grown exponentially and Lori’s father may have lost his job. This forces Lori’s father to administer Will (showcasing excellent shielding of Freddy’s strategy), as well as showcasing that even if Will wasn’t administered, it still works into Freddy’s hand (via growing his influence and getting Lori’s father kicked off the police force and stripping him of his influence, Lori’s father being a core member of the squad of adults assembled to conceal and weaken Freddy’s existence).
By specifically choosing Will to administer, Freddy indirectly manipulated Lori’s father via a doubly reinforced manipulation tactic (that even if compromised would still highly benefit Freddy to roughly the same level) that forced Lori’s father to take advantage of his status and administer Will to the institution (whilst also ensuring Lori’s father couldn’t ever be traced back to the crime), crafting an incredibly fortified strategy. This strategy ensures that after his possible death in the next installment, Freddy will have a contingency set in place for his revival. It’s stated that “Freddy’s Dead” (the last installment in the series until Freddy vs Jason) took place 10 years after the previous film, implying this film takes place in 1999.

4. To help John gain his memories back, Maggie takes him back to his hometown (Springwood).
5. They discover the town is run-down and ruined due to Freddy’s influence, where they also discover via the townsfolk that Freddy has a child.
6. The logical conclusion up to this point is that John is Freddy’s child, for several reasons — 1) Freddy was attacking John in his dreams but not killing him. Why would he spare John if he was simply another random teenager? 2) Freddy’s goal was deduced to be his need to widen his influence, and Freddy manipulating his kin into leaving Springwood and bringing people back to the area fits that deduction perfectly. Through these two fabricated reasonings, Freddy successfully misdirected the cast into believing his kin was John.

8. John lives just long enough to inform the group of the gender information revealed to him (as per Freddy’s prediction).

10. Maggie then enters the dream world to confront Freddy, where he attempts to recruit her.

Freddy’s plan in this film successfully misdirected the cast from the identity of his child up until the very moment where he needed them to be aware of it. No matter the outcome of the plan, it benefited Freddy entirely —>
1. If Maggie accepted the offer, Freddy would be able to extend his influence outside of Springwood and thus regains his complete influence without needing to enact his Will administration contingency (without discarding it, keeping it as a passive contingency in case of his future downfall). In this hypothetical scenario, it would act as Freddy’s official first revival contingency.

If Maggie killed Freddy, he has successfully planned his own death and takes advantage of his previously established Will administration contingency (which leads to Maggie helping Freddy in the future regardless, which will be elaborated on later on in the document). By killing Freddy, it directly sends him to hell where he’ll meet Jason (as per Freddy’s prediction). This will enable the Will administration contingency and allow for Freddy to revive and possibly obtain the Necronomicon (his overall goal).
This interpretation would provide reasoning for why Freddy seemed almost uncaring and playful in his final moments (his last words were quite literally a joking remark). If Freddy had foresaw this outcome, it would make complete sense for him to not be overly concerned at his downfall in this film (especially considering that anytime Freddy is truly defeated, he becomes desperate and overly serious, such as in the first two films and at the end of the entire comic run).
Overall, Freddy’s plan to lure his true child to Springwood was capable of misdirecting the cast until the very moment Freddy wished for his kin to be revealed. No matter the outcome of this plan, it heavily benefited Freddy in both trajectories and continued his holistic plan without fail. (+STRATEGY, +PLANNING, +FORESIGHT, & +COUNTERACTION)

“I've been waiting for a long time. I've been searching for a way back. And then, I found him. I found the one who could make them remember. I had to go into the bowels of Hell to find him, but I found him. He's a little slow, and he's not very pretty... but he’s got a real knack for killing.”
The simple explanation for this is that this actually benefits the theory’s posit. Freddy is only referring to PHYSICALLY finding Jason here, in Hell. This is also strengthened by Freddy’s word choice, where he only refers to his search as physical (needing to “go into” the bowels of Hell, which is notably a certain depth and not Hell as a holistic place - If Freddy had never anticipated using Jason as a contingency, he should’ve simply referred to Hell as its whole existence, not just a specific part. All evidence points to this being a preplanned contingency, especially given Freddy’s wording in this opening statement and the post-credit scene in “Jason Goes to Hell”. With that out of the way, we can continue.

As the dream demons can’t expect Freddy to know that rule unless they explicitly show it to him, it’s highly likely Freddy gained some knowledge on the Necronomicon from the rule itself (showcasing the fact he did in fact know of its existence long ago).

ASH WILLIAMS
The crossover event Freddy vs. Jason vs. Ash: The Nightmare Warriors (FVJVA NW) further expands the concept of predestination. By Freddy consistently outmaneuvering the cast (notably Ash), Freddy is overcoming the laws of fate with his strategic ability alone.

It’s notable to consider that despite Freddy’s supernatural prowess, the sheer scope and scale at which he operated at is impressive. Not only this, Freddy had to constantly overcome and counteract the dream demons - Literal gods within the story. These very gods were the ones who granted Freddy his abilities, yet were still outplayed by his schemes along the way.
