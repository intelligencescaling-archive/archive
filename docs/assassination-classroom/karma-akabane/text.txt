Atomic Crystalline Lattice:
Karma perfectly deduces the solution to a complex math problem:

The Problem: "In the figure below, cubes with side length "a" are stacked and arranged repeatedly, with an atom placed at each vertex and in the centre of each individual cube as shown, in the form of a body-centred cubic crystalline lattice. Assume that the structure is composed of sodium, potassium, or some other alkali metal. Within the body-centred cubic crystalline lattice, the atom at the centre is point A{0}. Inside the cube, the region that is closest to A{0} than any other atom is D{0}. Find the volume of D{0},"

The problem given above has been asked to find the volume of D[0], which is a region that is described as being closest to the atom “A[0]” as compared to “any other atom”. There are fragments of atoms (that border each vertex of the cube in the diagram) inside of the cube containing the central atom A[0].
It can be deduced that the volume of D[0] would have to be the sum total of the volume of these fragments, as they are the only known entities given in the question that surround the central atom A[0]. Thus, the total volume of the cube given in the question is the sum total of the volumes of the regions A[0] and D[0].
Karma mentally highlights the portion “cubes with sides of length "a" are stacked and arranged repeatedly”. From the diagram given next to the question, it shows that the atoms on the vertex of the cube containing A[0] are surrounded by cubes on all 3 dimensional faces.
The question also clearly states that the cubes have the same sides “a” and take the form of a lattice structure which, by definition, consist of atoms that are perfectly identical to each other in their properties. Therefore, it can be concluded that all the cubes given in the diagram must be identical to each other in terms of their facets and properties.
From this fact, Karma further deduces that, due to the identical nature of the cubes, the volume of the portion of the atom that are on a vertex of the cube containing A[0] and D[0] must be equal to the portions of the atoms that are on the vertices of other cubes that they have been surrounded by.
This is because the identical properties of the cubes surrounding the vertex atom on all three dimensional sides have made it so that the atom on a specific vertex contains equal portions of itself inside of the cubes that said atom has been surrounded by.
There are 8 different three dimensional hemispheres in total, therefore, Karma deduces that one fragment of an atom on the vertex of the cube would contain exactly 1/8th of the volume of the entire atom that it is a part of inside of that one particular cube.
Each cube has fragments of atoms on all 8 of its vertices in total. From the deduction made two points earlier, it can be further deduced there are a total of 8 different portions of vertex atoms, each containing 1/8th of the original volume of the respective atoms that they were initially a part of, inside of the cube.
The question further states that the cube containing A[0] and D[0] is also noted to have a structure of the form of a body-centred crystalline lattice that has been composed akin to that of alkali metals such as Sodium and Potassium. By definition of such a crystal structure, the atoms inside of a body centred crystalline lattice are identical in their properties, which means that the total volume of each singular atom given in the diagram must all be equal to each other.
From this observation, it can be further extrapolated that the fragments of the atoms on the vertices of each cube must therefore be identical to each other in volume. This is because the fragments on the vertices of the cube consist of exactly 1/8th the original total volume of the atoms they were a part of, which have now been proven to be equal to each other in their properties and thus, the total amount of space they occupy (i.e. volume). Thus the volume of each fragment of a vertex atom inside of the cube has to be exactly 1/8th of its own value i.e. 1/8th of one atom.
A cube consists of a total of 8 vertices and each atom has been placed bordering said vertices of the cube. There are a total of 8 different atoms that border the cube and thus 8 different fragments of atoms with an equal volume of exactly 1/8th of one whole atom inside of one cube. Therefore, Karma finally deduces that the sum total of the volume of all 8 fragments given inside of the cube (i.e. the volume of D[0]) is exactly that of one singular atom. This happens to be equal to the volume of the central atom A[0], which also occupies the volume of exactly one atom.
Karma concludes his analysis by visualizing that the entire volume of the cube is solely occupied by regions A[0] and D[0] (refer back to the beginning of the answer) both of which have the same volumes and are thus in the ratio in the ratio 1:1 inside of the cube. This means that each region must occupy exactly one half of the total volume cube, which has a side of “a” that has been given in the question. Therefore, the total volume of region D[0] is (a^3)/2.
During this process of fleshing out his analysis of the problem, Karma was able to analogize the components of the solution to the personal experiences at Class 3-E for the past year and the monologue about “talent” that he had a scene prior. Karma analogizes his life as the cube, himself as the central atom A[0] and the people around him as the atoms on the vertices of the cube. Karma reflects upon his life and cognizes that from inside of his cube he could only see bits and fragments of other people’s invisible parts called “talents” when in reality, he analogizes these “talents” as the portion of the vertex atoms that are outside of the cube something which he couldn’t see inside of his cube.
However, in the same way that all atoms in an atomic crystalline structure are identical, so are the talents of his classmates within Class 3-E, he just couldn’t see it before and had to think outside of the cube. However, when we combine the smaller fragments of the different people we interact with in our life, we find out that the territory held by those fragments is the same as the territory we ourselves hold in our life i.e. the fragments of different people consist of exactly half of our lives. This line of reasoning is analogical to the solution of the problem, the sum of the fragments of the different vertex atoms inside of the cube and finding out that the volume they occupy is equal to that of the central atom A[0] and constitutes exactly half the volume of the cube.
