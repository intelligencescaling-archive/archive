Neat Psychological Feat From Parker

im on book 3 of the parker series (The Outfit) and there has been a lot of clever heists and robberies this book, but the one that interested me most is this one:

keep in mind the one doing this feat is not Parker himself, but rather a minor robber character from his same underworld just for this chapter alone, but im willing to bet that Parker himself could have done the same thing since they all belong to the same professional thievery world. it is stated that they are all professionals when working together even if they arent necessarily allies, they work together for mutual interests. Parker himself has by now shown enough feats that he could pull this off, so ill count it as a potential feat for him as well. no spoilers for the plot btw this is just only one chapter out of 25

ill call it ***The Brown Jacket Feat***

so, **The Main Arrangement**:

it's the 1970s.

The Outfit is an American institution/organization that does illegal activities. organized crime basically. this specific activity of theirs consists of a drug-money transfer from a courier to another.

LaRenne is wearing a brown jacket with 75,000$ sewn into the linen, and is supposed to take a plane to an intermediate airport where he meets Hanks, who is also wearing a brown jacket. they secretly switch jackets, LaRenne continues his trip to Miami as normal, and Hanks goes to his own destination at an Outfit office.

the reason LaRenne doesnt complete the trip is that here are actually more than these two couriers. its just that these two are the ones involved in this robbery. but basically The Outfit limits their information so if any of them just so happened to be caught or arrested, they wouldnt be able to snitch on The Outfit. thats why LaRenne doesnt know any better than to just fly to that specific airport and switch jackets and then stay in Miami for 1-2 days as normal.

the robbery itself is actually rather simple but elegant enough to be mentioned on this sub i feel

Kobler, our robber, sits next to LaRenne the whole plane flight with a briefcase. he doesnt talk to him until the plane is landing, and he opens the briefcase and shows LaRenne the gun in it, then essentially tells him; Kobler is his bodyguard sent by The Outfit, and The Outfit has discovered that Hanks has wants to take the 75,000$, and LaRenne is instructed to carry the money this time.

LaRenne believes him. i think this is because Kobler knows both LaRenne's name and Hanks', because he knows about the money in the jacket, and because that he might know Hanks mightve already contemplated doing it. the book itself states that LaRenne has privately considered doing it but was too scared of the Outfit hunting him, but now is pissed that hanks considered doing the same thing because its entirely believable. if he has thought of it, then hanks has too. so Kobler manipulates larenne by thinking of his own incentive

but then LaRenne asks why he should trust Kobler, and Kobler tells him that when the plane lands, he can simply call a higher boss of The Outfit to verify that Kobler isnt lying. he gives him an easily verifiable procedure because he knows that LaRenne would think an Outfit employee would know LaRenne would be suspicious

the plane lands, LaRenne signals Hanks to visit him later as he himself goes to the phone booths, and Kobler approaches Hanks and then tells him a different story thats kinda the same:

"LaRenne is planning to steal the $75,000. We've discovered it. I barely managed to get onto the plane with him. Where is he?"

Hanks is stunned because the book again states that Hanks himself also considered stealing it. Kobler uses Hanks' incentive against him like he did with LaRenne. he then urges Hanks to go and call the boss and additional men (at another phone booth so LaRenne doesnt hear him obviously) because he doesnt know if LaRenne has accomplices or not. Hanks obeys and runs. this is so that Hanks isnt at the scene or near it for the next part

Kobler goes to the phone booths, sees LaRenne trying to contact the boss, and knocks him unconscious with his revoler before the call could go through. (it kinda does. \[A tinny voice sounded, "Hello?"\], but thats the operator.) he then takes the jacket and puts it and the revolver into the suitcase and leaves.

by the time Hanks realizes something is wrong and runs back, Kobler is already gone with his driver. he never had to fire his revolver

maybe its not that crazy but this book is just filled with neat social intelligence feats like that. its really fun and addicting to read. please read Parker.

as for how they got the information to begin with, thats from something else. but yeah, using the fact that they have both thought of taking the money to pit them against each other is a great feat, i dont exactly know which category that goes to, but its just cool
