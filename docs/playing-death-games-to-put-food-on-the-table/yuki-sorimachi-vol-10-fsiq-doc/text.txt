Echolocation:

Now, what is Echolocation, and how is it used in the brain?

Echolocation is, by definition, the production of sounds purposely created by something, that are then processed by the brain to create a mental image of what is around them, creating a fuzzy spatial image in the brain for the one that produced said sounds

How is it used/what processes need to happen in the brain for it to work, and what animals use it, and how?

Let's start off by the process of Echolocation

1. The animal/person/machine etc. produces a sound (for example: clicking of the tongue, specialized organs producing a sound, and many more).

2. That sound then bounces off of their environment.

3. That sound is then retrieved by the animal/person/machine that originally made that sound, using a specialized organ or intensified/focused hearing (bats have specialized ears due to the specialized cartilage in their ears that allows them to pick up those specialized clicks/echoes that were earlier produced).

4. The brain then uses those retrieved signals to reproduce a mental image that gives the animal/person/machine a sense of what is happening around them, comparable with a fuzzy 3D mental model of their surroundings.

Now that we have the process down, let's map what a pattern is, for effective Echolocation

1. Distance is measured by the time delay between the time of emitting the original echo, and the perceived return of it, the longer the time delay, the farther the distance.

2. Size is measured by the intensity of the echo, the bigger the size of the environment, the more intense the echo.

3. Movement is determined by the pitch of the retrieved sound. Through that, they determine whether something/someone is coming towards or away from the receiving party of the signal

Now, we can finally move on to explaining the feat and what makes it so ridiculously difficult:

She is not locked in her room practicing, nor in a controlled environment, this is in a high pressure situation that involves more than just listening to her own sounds and the echoes that return from it, but focusing on her opponent as she does this.

What is PSI?:

PSI, or Processing Speed Index, refers to how quickly someone can perceive information, process it, compare it to what they already know, and produce a correct response.

In this feat, Yuki is not simply reacting quickly with her body. She is rapidly processing an entire chain of logic while being chased, attacked, and physically knocked away by her Phantom. This makes the feat a combination of perception speed, reasoning speed, and plan-formation speed.

The Feat:

While running away from her Phantom, Yuki looks back and sees that the Phantom is already next to her. The Phantom is in an attack posture, rotating her body in mid-air to deliver a roundhouse kick.

In the instant before the kick lands, Yuki notices the stairs behind the Phantom. The neighborhood map and the path Yuki had taken immediately come to mind, allowing her to realize that the Phantom used the stairs as a shortcut. This means the Phantom did not catch up through some vague supernatural power. It caught up by using a logical route with less distance.

Just as Yuki assembles this logic, she is hit in the left flank and sent rolling into a guardrail. Even while being beaten again, she confirms the important point: the Phantom used a shortcut through the stairs.

Logic is the keyword.

The Phantom is created by Yuki’s own head. It is modeled after her old self. It is not a mysterious monster. Therefore, if it caught up through logic, then it moves according to logic. If it moves according to logic, then it can be bound by logic.

This is where the strategy is assembled inside Yuki.
