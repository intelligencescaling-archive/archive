LIGHTNING’S PROOF OF GOD’S EXISTENCE
This theory hinges on the idea that Lightning was aware of a being that governs the events of the Yu-Gi-Oh! VRAINS storyline, and here is an explanation of how such knowledge could be possible.
The Yu-Gi-Oh! franchise consists of numerous series. Although these series do not directly connect to each other plot-wise, they do exist within the same overall timeline.
Lightning, as an AI with access to all available information, would also have access to the events of every other Yu-Gi-Oh! series.
Yu-Gi-Oh! is a card game, and thus heavily influenced by luck. To win, each protagonist must receive an absurd amount of good fortune in order to overcome the villains. In each of the five previous Yu-Gi-Oh! series, there existed a powerful central threat capable of wiping out humanity unless stopped by the protagonist.
The fact that all five protagonists consistently received extraordinary, perfectly timed luck—allowing them to defeat overwhelming foes—serves as evidence of a God-figure (the writers) who intervenes to ensure the hero triumphs by granting the right cards at the right moment. Therefore, under this theory, Lightning effectively uncovered proof of the existence of this God/the writers.
Each of the five Yu-Gi-Oh! series followed a set of conditions:
   * Introduction of a new card mechanic

   * Emergence of an overwhelmingly powerful villain who endangers humanity

   * Appearance of a resolute protagonist who ultimately saves the world

By recognizing this recurring pattern, Lightning could deduce that a new storyline—one in which the protagonist would again be assisted by God—was beginning, as the conditions were present:
      * A new card mechanic (Link Summoning, the foundation of Yu-Gi-Oh! VRAINS)

      * A villain threatening humanity’s existence (Lightning himself, conveniently born with a bug that caused him to hate humans)

      * A determined individual (Playmaker), indirectly connected to the villain and to the broader plot. Although Lightning did not personally know Playmaker, he knew Ai—an Ignis whose personality was shaped based on Playmaker’s reactions during the Hanoi Project. Since Ai’s personality demonstrated unwavering determination in pursuing his own beliefs, even when they seemed questionable, Lightning could infer the presence of a protagonist indirectly linked to the villain.

An additional factor strengthening this feat is the vast time gap between various Yu-Gi-Oh! series, often spanning decades, which made detecting and identifying every recurring moment across the history of the Yu-Gi-Oh! universe far more difficult.
Because Lightning despised humans, his aim was to create a world without them. However, such a plan would be impossible to complete while a protagonist benefitting from plot armor existed. Consequently, knowing about the God-figure—the writers—who favored the side of good became a crucial advantage that Lightning could exploit to further his own agenda.

FIRST PART OF THE PLAN
Lightning needed the Ignis World to be destroyed for his plan, so he secretly created Judgment Arrows, a powerful weapon in the Yu-Gi-Oh! game and a tool capable of massive digital destruction, and used it to obliterate the Ignis World.
He also weaponized the trust he had built over nearly ten years as the Ignis’ leader. Since he already knew his long-term goals during that decade of self-management, he used the credibility he had earned to convince all the Ignis—including Aqua—that the Hanoi Knights were the ones who destroyed their world and were therefore their real enemy.
The purpose of destroying the Ignis World was to free every Ignis from that realm and push them into fighting the Hanoi Knights.
Separating all the Ignis accomplished several things for Lightning:
         * It gave him the freedom to carry out his plan without the other Ignis noticing.

         * It made Ai, who had no deep emotional ties with the other Ignis and suddenly had no home, believe Lightning’s lie and seek out his originator, Playmaker. Playmaker was already battling the Hanoi Knights because of the traumatic Hanoi Project he had been subjected to ten years earlier. Ai joining Playmaker would become useful to Lightning later.

         * It prevented the Hanoi Knights from immediately wiping out the Ignis World themselves, something they were ready to do because of reasons explained later.

Dr. Kogami, a researcher at SOL Technologies, led the “Hanoi Project” together with the other members of the Hanoi Knights. The project involved kidnapping six children and making them duel in Yu-Gi-Oh! to create the six Ignis, whose personalities were based on the children’s reactions during the experiments.
Dr. Kogami was eventually infected by a virus and died. Revolver (his son Ryoken Kogami) and the Hanoi Knights believed SOL Technologies killed him—both to protect their reputation and to gain total control over the Ignis.
Before his death, Lightning had run a simulation showing that Ignis would eventually cause humanity’s extinction if left unchecked.
Although many believed SOL killed Dr. Kogami, Lightning was actually the one who murdered him so that Kogami couldn’t run the same simulation and begin a war against the Ignis.
This was crucial because, at the time of Kogami’s death, all the Ignis were still located in the Ignis World. Had Kogami discovered the simulation results, he would have hunted down every Ignis and created an even stronger version of Judgment Arrows, capable not only of destroying the Ignis World but also killing every Ignis inside it.
This worked in Lightning’s favor: by the time Revolver eventually performed a similar analysis, the Ignis World had already been destroyed by Judgment Arrows, making such a plan impossible.
A second reason Lightning killed Dr. Kogami was to redirect the conflict from “Hanoi Knights vs. Ignis” to “Hanoi Knights vs. SOL Technologies.”
This new rivalry caused the Hanoi Knights to act inside SOL’s virtual reality system—the VR space built for Yu-Gi-Oh!—in an attempt to find and eliminate the Ignis. This was important because Playmaker, having been part of the original Hanoi Project, was highly capable inside this VR environment.
After many fights, Revolver concluded the only way to destroy the Ignis and strike back at SOL was to activate the Hanoi Tower, an artificial device that, once launched, would shut down the world’s electricity. This would erase the Ignis (as digital beings) and trigger a crisis at SOL.
Revolver installed the Hanoi Tower inside Link Vrains, the VR world created by SOL for VR Yu-Gi-Oh!.
Once activated, Playmaker fought to stop the Tower from causing global disaster. Thanks only to Ai’s support, he was able to reach the Tower and oppose Revolver’s plan. Playmaker dueled Revolver and managed to tie the duel because Ai manipulated Data Storms (tornadoes of data that can combine into new cards) and saved him several times.
So what does all of this have to do with Lightning?
As explained earlier, Lightning had indirectly pushed Ai into joining Playmaker, and manipulated Revolver into targeting SOL Technologies instead of the Ignis.
Revolver’s hatred of both SOL and the Ignis drove him to activate the Hanoi Tower. Lightning anticipated that Revolver would use the Tower for the same reason Dr. Kogami would have: since the Ignis couldn’t be found, a device capable of killing them indirectly was inevitable. The only existing system powerful enough to accomplish that was the Hanoi Tower, which could collapse every electrical system on Earth.
At the same time, steering Ai toward Playmaker was necessary because Lightning’s goal wasn’t for the Hanoi Tower to succeed—it was simply for the Tower to exist. The Tower would later become important for Lightning’s final confrontation. With Ai’s help—and only because of it—Playmaker managed to draw against Revolver, which was the only requirement to shut the Tower down: in Link Vrains, losing Life Points means losing control of your creations. When Revolver’s Life Points hit zero, he also lost control over the Tower.
–
First, the Hanoi Tower crisis gave Lightning plenty of time to prepare for the next steps of his plan.
Second, because the Ignis still couldn’t be captured or killed, Bounty Hunters were hired to track them down and hand them over to SOL Technologies—who intended to exploit the Ignis for their own benefit. These hunters, as explained later, played a role in Lightning’s strategy.

FORMING ALLIANCES
After the destruction of the Ignis World, Lightning persuaded and brainwashed Windy (one of the Ignis) into joining his fight against humans, convincing him that humans were useless, destructive, and incapable of accepting that a superior species (the Ignis) needed to rise. Windy fell for this because Lightning validated his species and ideals, making him feel aligned with Lightning.
Lightning’s plan to fight humans involved creating the strongest possible AI. For this reason, he decided to build an Ignis-like AI—one with free will, but far less emotional and far more technologically advanced.
However, Lightning knew that an AI with free will might side with Playmaker and the humans, so he devised a three-step plan to:
            1. gain control over Bohman (the AI he was creating), and

            2. fully develop Bohman so he could duel Playmaker.

In the first step, Bohman was not fully developed and had very limited free will. Lightning used him to steal Jin Kusanagi’s consciousness. Ten years after the Hanoi Project (where Jin had been mentally tortured for Lightning’s creation), Lightning deduced that Jin was suffering from severe PTSD and would be easy to overpower. So he sent Bohman to extract Jin’s consciousness through data absorption. Jin couldn’t resist due to his mental state.
This allowed Lightning to push Playmaker into dueling Bohman to learn more about him (Playmaker won). This first stage helped Lightning by developing Bohman’s dueling skills and turning Bohman into an enemy of Playmaker’s group.
Additionally, Jin Kusanagi’s consciousness would be useful later.
After this first duel, Lightning gaslit Bohman into believing he was the real Playmaker, and that Playmaker and Bohman had been switched during the events of the Hanoi Project. Based on this deceitful theory, the one we know as “Playmaker” is actually an impostor, and Bohman has to challenge him to duel to regain his identity. Bohman accepted this because Lightning’s words were his only source of information.

Playmaker’s team sensed an unusual pressure in a specific area of Link Vrains—this was the zone where Bohman was being developed. Lightning had deliberately created that pressure, intending for Playmaker’s team to notice it. He also built a highly visible base there to draw attention.
Inside the base was Windy, and when Playmaker’s group arrived, Lightning used his control over Windy to:
               1. indirectly deceive Ai and the others into thinking Windy wasn’t an enemy, and

               2. indirectly lead Playmaker’s team to Bohman so that Playmaker would duel him again.

This resulted in a second duel between Playmaker and Bohman (Lightning’s method for making this duel happen will be explained later), where they fought over Playmaker’s identity. This increased the hostility between them and further improved Bohman’s dueling ability.
At this stage, Bohman was fully developed and ready for the final battle against Playmaker’s team. He now had complete free will and did not genuinely believe in Lightning’s ideology. However, because of the first two steps, Lightning still had full control over him. Bohman had nowhere else to turn, and Lightning (along with Bohman’s deceased brother, also created by Lightning) was the only one who had ever accepted him.

Since Ai was usually with Playmaker in Link Vrains, Blood Shepherd became a very famous duelist there, aiming to capture Ai. This fame likely caused his duels to be recorded at times.
Lightning, knowing Blood Shepherd was one of the hunters after the Ignis, wanted to eliminate him. His strategy was the following: first, he instantly located Blood Shepherd’s position and created a portal near him.
When Blood Shepherd entered, Lightning let him rant about how terrible AIs were and how humans were superior. Lightning then intentionally provoked him further, explaining that humans only consider themselves superior because throughout history they dominated other species and now cannot tolerate the idea of a species superior to them due to pure ego.
This enraged Blood Shepherd even more, making him feel the need to prove human superiority.
Lightning then used this anger: he challenged Blood Shepherd to a duel to determine whether humans or AIs were superior, with the condition that the winner would be allowed to kill the loser. Blood Shepherd accepted immediately, and Lightning had already predicted he would win.
After winning the duel, Lightning was able to take Blood Shepherd’s consciousness.
Throughout the duel, Lightning anticipated and predicted many of Blood Shepherd’s moves:
                  1. Preventing the destruction of “Armatos Legios” monsters with Decurion’s effect after Blood Shepherd activated “Snatch Drone.”

                  2. Preventing the loss of all his Life Points by negating General’s attack with Decurion’s effect. After this, Lightning stated that AIs (including himself) can predict anything, proving he had foreseen Blood Shepherd’s attacks.

By anticipating Blood Shepherd’s moves and using strong strategy, Lightning secured the win.

This indirectly caused Blood Shepherd—who targeted Ai—to become famous as well, resulting in his duels being recorded, giving Lightning access to his strategies.

The Final Fight Against Humans
After Lightning declared war on humanity, the Playmaker Team—consisting of Playmaker, Soulburner, Blue Angel, Revolver (who had now joined Playmaker to fight Lightning), and Spectre, along with the Ignis Ai, Aqua, and Flame—decided to end the conflict by defeating Lightning once and for all.

The Hanoi Tower became relevant again, as Revolver could now use the energy it previously generated—enough to shut down the world’s entire electricity system—to power the expedition against Lightning, which was part of Lightning’s strategy, enabling him to utilize his trap.
Additionally, Playmaker had informed Akira Zaizen, CEO of SOL, about Lightning’s declaration of war, prompting Zaizen to implement security changes in Link Vrains that allowed people to access the system, likely facilitating the Playmaker Team’s navigation in pursuit of Lightning. These security modifications would become important later.
Given the precarious situation, Akira Zaizen advised ordinary users not to enter Link Vrains.
The team eventually returned to the same area where Bohman had been during his second duel.
Lightning anticipated their arrival and had a plan: first, he deployed bit-boots—simple AIs used to buy time—to engage the team in duels while flying on boards, splitting them up in mid-flight. Once separated, he sent each member to one of nine different Fields, preventing them from teaming up and allowing him to defeat them individually.
Thanks to his extensive training against Playmaker, Bohman was now powerful enough to defeat Blue Angel, who possessed Aqua, one of the Ignis. Once she lost, Blue Angel was effectively eliminated, and Bohman absorbed Aqua’s data, which also contained Earth’s data, increasing his strength.
Following Blue Angel’s defeat, Windy, fighting alongside Lightning, faced Soulburner in a duel.
Windy lost, as he had not undergone training like Bohman, which aligned with Lightning’s plan to ensure all Ignis data would eventually be collected by Bohman.
After Windy’s defeat, Soulburner—now holding Windy’s data—faced Bohman.
As previously explained, Lightning had full control over Windy’s ideology. During Soulburner’s duel with Bohman, assisted by Flame, Windy’s implanted directives caused him—via Soulburner, who held his data—to use his last resource to “kill” Flame, converting him into data as well.
Soulburner, heavily dependent on Flame, suffered a mental breakdown, playing poorly during the duel and ultimately losing to Bohman, who then acquired the data of both Flame and Windy, further increasing his power.
After this duel, Lightning chose not to have Playmaker face a member of his own team. Instead, he arranged for Playmaker to duel Soichi, Jin’s brother and Playmaker’s hacking partner.
Following the Hanoi Project, Playmaker sought revenge against the Hanoi Knights for the abuse he suffered, while Soichi, whose brother had been abused in the same project, shared the same goal. This common objective forged a strong emotional bond between them. This outcome aligned with Lightning’s plan: Soichi’s thoughts were now accessible to Bohman, allowing him to anticipate Playmaker’s moves in future confrontations. Additionally, Playmaker’s emotional turmoil further served Lightning’s objectives.
Next, Lightning’s team faced Revolver, who held no Ignis. Since Bohman would not absorb Ignis data from Revolver, Lightning engaged him directly.

Lightning vs Revolver (Subsection of “The Final Fight Against Humans”)
At the start of their duel, Lightning activates a Field Spell “Armatos Colosseum”, which makes Lightning able to discard a card and summon two Armatos Legio Monsters in defense position from his hand. Lightning, after some combos, summoned Link Monsters Decurion and Centurion. Decurion has a Link Arrow that points behind itself. He sent those two monsters to the graveyard to summon Link 3 Plumbur Trident (The “3” stands for the number of Link Arrows possessed by the Monster) and set a trap card.
On Revolver’s turn, Revolver summoned some monsters and Autorokket. Autorokket, though being less powerful than Plumbur Trident, has a specific effect: it can destroy a spell or trap of the opponent—this makes Revolver able to destroy Lightning’s trap, and, in case, destroy Judgement Arrows (the same card Lightning used to destroy the Ignis World).
Lightning anticipated it. The trap he had set was “Armatos Gloria”. Armatos Gloria makes any card unable to get banished or destroyed from the field due to a card effect, countering Autorokket.  Furthermore, as a drawback, when Armatos Gloria is targeted by an effect, every monster of Lightning’s field gets destroyed. This is a calculated risk.
As a response, Revolver uses the monsters on his field to Summon Link 4 Borreload Dragon. Borreload Dragon is not only stronger than Plumbur Trident, but it also has an effect which makes Plumbur Trident weaker, making the Life Points damage heavier. Revolver uses Borreload Dragon to destroy Plumbur Trident.
“How logical… but since you are logical, it’s easy to figure out your plans” Lightning remarks. By destroying Plumbur Trident, Revolver triggered Plumbur Trident’s effect, which allowed Lightning to place Judgement Arrows in his hand, rather than compromising his board. Furthermore, when Plumbur Trident goes to the graveyard, it has an effect to leave the graveyard and enter the “Spell Zone” of the field. Plumbur Trident has 3 arrows that point in front of itself. Due to the fact that the Spell Zone is behind the Monster Zone, Plumbur Trident’s Arrows point directly to the Monster Zone.
Due to the fact that Link Monsters can only be summoned in either
                     1. the Extra Deck Monster Zone, which is in front of the Monster Zone.
                     2. In the Monster Zones, but only in the areas that are pointed at by Link Arrows.
Now, thanks to Plumbur Trident’s Link Arrows, Lightning can summon his Link Monsters in the Monster Zone.
On Lightning’s turn, Lightning utilized Plumbur Trident’s Spell Effect: he can send Decurion back to the Deck in order to summon Centurion. Lightning then used Armatos’ Colosseum to summon Armatos Legio Segmentata and another Armatos Legio monster. He sends the latter to the graveyard to summon Decurion in a zone which Plumbur Trident’s Link Arrows point to—a zone of the Monsters Zone.
Since,
                     1. As already explicated, Decurion has an arrow which points behind itself.
                     2. The Monsters Zone is in front of the Spell Zone.
Decurion points to the Spell Zone.
Judgement Arrows is a Spell that can only be activated on a zone which is pointed at by a Link Arrow. Since Decurion points to the Spell Zone, Judgement Arrow can be activated.
Before Lightning, however, could activate Judgment Arrows, Revolver used a trap card (Mind Crush) to make Lightning discard Judgement Arrows from his hand and send it to the graveyard. This was done so that Lightning could not activate Judgement Arrows.
Lightning, who had anticipated Revolver, had already set up three monsters (Decurion, Centurion, and Armatos Legio Segmentata) on the field. After Revolver destroyed Judgement Arrows, Lightning, who had thought far ahead, used those three monsters to summon Link 4 Armatos Legio Magnus Dux.
Lightning, then, used Segmentata’s effect: when used for a Link Summon, it can send each of the Monsters used for the Summon to the Deck. This made Lightning’s graveyard go from having 8 cards to having 5 cards.
Moreover, due to the same effect, it sends a further number N of cards to the deck, with N being the Link Number of the Link Monster summoned. Since Armatos Legio Magnus Dux is Link 4, Lightning sent 4 of the 5 remaining graveyard cards to the Deck. Lightning’s Final Revelation
After defeating Revolver, Lightning—with only few forces—transferred his own consciousness into Bohman, placing it alongside the Ignis consciousnesses—Flame, Windy, Aqua, and Earth—inside Bohman’s Neuron Link, the structure containing their data and consciousness.
With this, only Bohman (representing Lightning’s team) and Playmaker (representing Playmaker’s team) remained.
At the final stage of his development, Bohman gained the ability to steal the consciousness of anyone within the network.
Because Lightning had indirectly manipulated Akira Zaizen—through his declaration of war—to loosen Link Vrains’ security, people could enter the system without restriction, despite warnings about potential dangers.
The ultimate duel, Bohman versus Playmaker, was arranged inside Link Vrains (by Bohman, using Lightning’s consciousness, as will be explained later) so that spectators would be drawn to watch live and enter the system. Once inside, Bohman could steal their consciousness, allowing him to predict Playmaker’s possible moves.

After receiving Lightning’s message, all the Ignis—including Lightning—granted their power to Ai, aiding him in the duel.
Although Lightning had allied with Bohman, he used him as a pawn: Bohman ultimately lost due to Playmaker’s dueling skill, plot armor, and the Ignis’ assistance, fulfilling Lightning’s plan. With Bohman’s death, every Ignis in the Neuron Link died as well.
Ai then accessed the message Lightning had prepared.
In the message, which had been prepared before the confrontation even started, Lightning explained that he had run simulations of the worlds in which only Ai survives. In every scenario, the world descended into constant war, and Playmaker ultimately died. This occurred because Ai, the most intelligent remaining AI, would attract the attention of powerful organizations. Playmaker’s bond with Ai would compel him to intervene, risking his life to protect Ai, ultimately leading to his death.
Ai ran the same simulations and confirmed Lightning’s predictions. These simulations also demonstrated precise knowledge of the world’s social and spatial environment. From this, it is clear Lightning planned and manipulated events from the very beginning, ensuring the only scenario in which his team could fail was one where only Ai remained alive.
The message revealed no hatred toward Playmaker or Ai, despite their opposition, and detailed how Ai would act to pursue the final duel, ultimately losing. This outcome had been orchestrated from the start, ensuring that Playmaker and Ai would face all these battles together, forming an unbreakable bond.
Furthermore, with only one Ignis remaining, powerful organizations would attempt to capture it. The bond between Ai and Playmaker would compel him to protect Ai at all costs, creating a scenario where Playmaker’s death became inevitable if Ai survived.
Because Ai could run the same simulations, he knew that if he was alive, Playmaker would die. Thus, Ai crafted a strategy to assure either one of two possible final outcomes, both saving Playmaker.
To achieve one of those two outcomes, Ai, after disappearing from Playmaker’s life, planned a course of events that would lead Ai to forcefully take control over SOL, and Playmaker to face him in duel. With this duel, Ai proposed a bet, which included the two specific outcomes:

Playmaker wouldn’t accept this outcome, as the real unitary Ai would have been lost in the process.

Ai’s defeat, however, would lead to Ai’s ultimate death. As a matter of fact, no matter how hard Playmaker tried to convince Ai, Ai knew that their fate was sealed.

Thus, Lightning indirectly created a world for all Ignis and AIs to exist peacefully after death, free from humans. This reveals that his actions—though appearing as betrayal—were intended to ensure a human-free afterlife for all Ignis.
