Aoi Tsukiyo, Great Detective (名探偵 )

Character Introductions :
* Kozushima Tarou: A billionaire in his 70s and the eccentric owner of the Glass Tower. He is a former professor of biotechnology at Teito University who made his fortune by inventing the patented "TRIDENT" gene delivery system. He is an collector of mystery novels and crime memorabilia.
* Ichijou Yuma: A young medical doctor who serves as Kozushima Tarou’s personal attending physician. He secretly harbors resentment toward Kozushima because Kozushima’s TRIDENT patent lawsuits blocked a life saving treatment for his younger sister, who suffers from a fatal neuromuscular disease.
* Aoi Tsukiyo: A eccentric young woman in her mid-20s who dresses in a Sherlock Holmes style deerstalker cap and cape coat. She introduces herself as a self-proclaimed "Great Detective" who dedicates her life strictly to solving ‘Honkaku’ style complex mysteries.
* Kagami Tsuyoshi: A veteran detective from the Nagano Prefectural Police Department’s First Investigative Division.
* Kuruma Koushin: A 73-year-old veteran honkaku mystery novelist who wears traditional Japanese robes and is famous for authoring locked-room murder novels.
* Sakyo Kousuke: The editor-in-chief of Super Mystery Monthly Magazine.
* Yumeyomi Suishou: A TV psychic and spiritual medium who claims to possess the ability to perceive residual energy left by spirits.
* Sakaizumi Taiki: A professional guest chef hired to cook gourmet meals for the gathering.
* Tomoe Madoka: A timid young woman in her late 20s who works as the maid of the Glass Tower.
* Oita Shinzo: An elderly man who serves as the butler of the Glass Tower.
About The Glass Tower :
The Glass Tower is a spire located in the deep snows of Mt. Chogatake in the Japanese Alps. Designed as an 11-story glass-and-steel building shaped like a giant trident. The structure is organized as follows:
* Basement (B1): Houses the central kitchen, main dining room, living room/salon, and entertainment spaces.
* Floors 1 through 10: Feature single guest rooms spiraling around a central staircase and elevator shaft. Room "First" on the 1st floor serves as Kozushima Tarou’s private quarters. Rooms "Second" through "Tenth" are assigned to the visiting guests and staff.
* Key System: Each individual room features its own unique mechanical key card/lock, which are not duplicable. Butler Oita Shinzo holds the master key, designated as key "Zero," which opens all guest rooms and service areas.
* 11th Floor (Top Cone): A glass display room located directly under the spire’s summit. It contains Kozushima’s personal collection of rare crime memorabilia, and a sealed cabinet containing a lethal poison.

The First Murder, Kozushima Tarou
The Invitation and Yuma’s Plan
Kozushima Tarou sends out invitations to a select group of guests : Dr. Ichijou Yuma, detective Aoi Tsukiyo, police detective Kagami Tsuyoshi, novelist Kuruma Koushin, editor Sakyo Kousuke, psychic Yumeyomi Suishou, and chef Sakaizumi Taiki to assemble at his remote Glass Tower on Mt. Chogatake. The official reason given for the gathering is that Kozushima plans to unveil an unpublished mystery manuscript written that could overturn the history of the genre, and host an "Challenge to the Reader" deduction game for his guests.
However, Dr. Ichijou Yuma arrives with an ulterior motive. Yuma’s younger sister is suffering from a fatal, degenerative neuromuscular condition (ALS). A clinical drug produced by Shiota Pharmaceuticals was capable of halting her disease, but Kozushima filed a patent lawsuit claiming infringement on his TRIDENT gene delivery technology. Kozushima refused to settle or allow the drug to reach the market purely out of personal vanity and greed. Yuma took the job as Kozushima's attending physician specifically to find an opportunity to kill him, allowing the patent lawsuit to lapse so his sister could receive her life saving medicine.
Knowing Kozushima suffered a severe myocardial infarction five years ago and takes daily prescription heart capsules, Yuma plans to poison him in a way that mimics a fatal cardiac arrest. Days prior to the gathering, Yuma secretly accessed the locked poison display cabinet on the 11th floor and extracted a lethal dose of tetrodotoxin (pufferfish poison) in powder form, packing it into an opaque capsule identical to Kozushima’s heart medication.

Kozushima’s Murder (Day 1)
At approximately 7:00 PM on the first evening, all guests gather in the basement dining room to wait for dinner to be served by chef Sakaizumi Taiki, butler Oita Shinzo, and maid Tomoe Madoka. Kozushima Tarou remains in his private room "First" on the 1st floor.
Dr. Yuma Ichijo excuses himself under the pretext of delivering Kozushima’s heart medication to his room. Yuma enters the "First" room, hands Kozushima the capsule containing the lethal tetrodotoxin, and watches Kozushima swallow it with water. After tricking Kozushima Tarou into swallowing the capsule containing the lethal powdered tetrodotoxin poison, Yuma locked the door of room "First" from the inside using Kozushima's personal room key , the "First" key,  and dropped the key directly onto the floor near the work desk inside. He then exited the room, pulled the door shut, and quickly fled up the spiral staircase to his own assigned guest room "Fourth” on an upper floor to establish an alibi. As the paralysis set in, Kozushima realized he was dying and grabbed the room's internal telephone handset to call the butler extension line in the basement. He gasped for breath and made frantic, muffled sounds of distress over the line to butler Oita Shinzo before collapsing. The terrifying noises broadcast over the internal phone line startled everyone relaxing in the basement dining room. The guests and staff immediately rushed up to the 1st floor to reach room "First". Yuma joined the back of the group as they arrived, where they found the door locked from the inside, forcing butler Oita to unlock it using the master key "Zero".

On Kozushima’s desk sits a small plate holding gourmet chocolate truffles. Kagami points out that the frantic phone call and the strange state of the room mean the scene must be preserved intact for a formal police investigation and autopsy.

A little context for this ;

Thirteen years prior, a series of young women mysteriously vanished near a small ski resort on Mt. Chogatake. The culprit selectively targeted isolated women who were estranged from their families and traveling alone on unplanned trips, committing only one or two murders a year to avoid detection. The case came to light when a young woman in her twenties escaped from the basement of a local guesthouse after being held captive for several weeks.
The guesthouse owner, a middle-aged man named Fuyuki Daisuke, fled into the forest as police arrived. A sudden snowstorm and a subsequent massive avalanche hit the area. Although a search party of 100 officers never recovered his body, police concluded Fuyuki died buried in the snow slide. A thorough search of Fuyuki's guesthouse revealed the skeletal remains of eleven young women embedded in the concrete walls of the basement. The horrific scandal ruined the local tourist economy, forcing the resort and neighboring accommodations into bankruptcy and leaving the land abandoned. A few years prior to the current events, Kozushima Tarou bought the cheap, ruined land and built the Glass Tower directly on the exact site of Fuyuki's guesthouse.

Kozushima’s Murder
Aoi first establishes that Kozushima did not die of natural causes. The killer stole powdered pufferfish liver (tetrodotoxin) from the 11th-floor display room and administered it to Kozushima in the First room.
Aoi disproves the existence of a duplicate key or complex mechanical trick. The locked room was created through a psychological trick.
   1. The culprit poisoned Kozushima in the First room, locked the door from the outside using Kozushima's First key, and hid in their own room nearby to let the household pass on the spiral staircase before joining the group as they rushed up.
   2. When butler Oita unlocked the door with master key Zero and everyone fixated on Kozushima’s body, the culprit silently dropped the First key onto the carpet.
   3. Aoi proves the key was dropped after entry by showing photo/video footage of the crime scene carpet. Fine cigarette ash scattered when Tomoe accidentally knocked over an ashtray while making an emergency phone call was found underneath the First key. Had the key been on the floor prior to entry, the ash would have landed on top of it.
Oita's Murder
Aoi explains that the killer did not intend to burn down the Glass Tower or destroy Oita's body. the fire was a timed trick to lock the door and cover up physical evidence.
   1. The large east-facing dining room window is built as a giant convex lens (a magnifying glass). As the morning sun reaches a specific angle due east, it refracts sunlight into a intensely focused, scorching oval on the dining table.
   2. The killer painted the tablecloth with blood to write "SPIRITED AWAY FROM CHOGATAKE." Because dark colors absorb heat while white reflects light, the dark red blood retained the focused solar heat. Flammable poplar fluff, soaked in kerosene, was placed on the dark bloodstain as kindling.
   3. Once the morning sun hit the dark blood-painted area, the fluff instantly ignited, setting off the heat-sensitive ceiling sprinklers. The water doused the fire before it destroyed the message, while washing away fingerprints, footprints, and altering body temperature to obscure time of death.
The Cube Latch Trick
Inside the dining room door is a 270-degree rotating brass latch.
To lock the door automatically from the outside, the killer rotated the latch arm upward and wedged a large sugar cube from the dining table into the narrow space between the latch and the wall.
The sugar held the heavy brass latch upright in a near-vertical position. The killer shut the door from the outside and left.
When the solar-ignited fire triggered the ceiling sprinklers, the cascading water rapidly dissolved the sugar cube. Without the sugar prop, gravity caused the heavy brass latch arm to swing downward in its arc, falling directly onto the door's locking protrusion and securing the room from the inside.
Tomoe’s Murder
Aoi explains the third locked room in the Sixth room, revealing that the Sixth room was not the actual crime scene.
She notes that all guest room windows in the Glass Tower automatically open fully when the main fire alarm is triggered (to vent smoke). The killer set a timed candle ignition device in the sub-kitchen specifically to set off the building’s fire alarm. This was necessary because the killer could not find the Sixth key (which Tomoe had hidden inside her headdress) to enter her room and press the interior window-open button manually. Kagami attacked Tomoe, dragged her to the Second room, and tortured her to extract information before stabbing her to death.
To move her corpse across the building without leaving a trail of blood, the killer dressed her body in the thick, elaborate wedding dress taken from the display room. The heavy fabric acted as an impromptu sponge/wrapping, absorbing all blood from her wounds temporarily.
The Exterior Glass Wall Trick
The Glass Tower is cone-shaped, meaning its exterior glass walls are gently sloped rather than vertical.
The guest rooms are aligned directly above one another every four floors (at 90-degree intervals). The Second room sits directly above the Sixth room.
After the fire alarm forced open all the room windows, the killer pushed Tomoe’s body,wrapped in the smooth wedding dress out the window of the Second room. Driven by gravity, her body slid smoothly down the sloping exterior glass wall and funnelled directly into the wide-open window of the Sixth room below.

The name "Mashu Shinju." In English translations, "Shinju" translates to Pearl, or Margaret, and "Kagami" translates to Mirror, or Millar. The name is an intentional homage to famous mystery novelist Margaret Millar.

Margaret Millar was a female American mystery novelist who, together with her husband Ross Macdonald, produced many wonderful works focused on psychological suspense. She was a master author who won the Edgar Award for Beast in View in 1956, and also received a Grand Master Award from the MWA.

Aoi remarks that Kagami told them the other day he was divorced, questioning him if his ex-wife took his daughter with her.
