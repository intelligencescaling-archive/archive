Firstly, what is the definition of objectivity?

**Merriam-Webster Dictionary:** expressing or dealing with facts or conditions as perceived without distortion by personal feelings, prejudices, or interpretations.

**Wikipedia:** Something is ***subjective*** if it represents first-person subjective experience (such as perception, emotions, opinions, imaginary objects, or conscious experiences). ***If a claim is true exclusively when considering the claim from the viewpoint of a sentient being, it is subjectively true.*** For example, one person may consider the weather to be pleasantly warm, and another person may consider the same weather to be too hot; both views are subjective. Something is ***objective*** if it can be confirmed through observation, experimentation and intersubjective dialogue. The two are usually regarded as opposites; beyond that sources differ: both ideas have been given various ambiguous definitions.

Wikipedia is usually the better source for questions like this rather than basic dictionary definitions which are usually made for colloquial rather than philosophical usage, although both work in this context.

I already made a post on it, with three example valid viewpoints towards scaling systems, and one of the examples was that the scaling systems can be treated as a premise for the matchup or evaluation framework.

Let me make it easier by giving an analogy to understand how "premise" and "objectivity" work together, using an actually objective example:

**Statement 1 (which may be true OR false) states the following:** "X + 5 = 15."

Now suppose X is equal to 3. Would statement 1 be true? Nope. Because statement 1 would become 8 = 15 which is a mathematically incorrect statement. An objectively false equation.

Next, suppose X is equal to 10. Would statement 1 be true? Yes! Because statement 1 would become 15 = 15 which is an identity. Objectively true by necessity if we (subjectively) interpret the mathematical symbols by their default usage (subjectivity always plays a part but the statement is internally objective given the premises and constraints and definitions).

As you can see, statement 1 may be true, or it may be false. But it is either OBJECTIVELY TRUE or OBJECTIVELY FALSE given the value of X. Our subjective feelings do not play a part once the premise (the value of X) has been determined.

Now suppose we have another statement. Notice how it's slightly different from statement 1?

**Statement 2 states the following:** "If X is equal to 10, then X + 5 = 15."

Let's say X is equal to 3, is statement 2 true?

The correct answer is yes! The value of X doesn't matter because statement 2 has the implicit assumption that the claim it is making "X + 5 = 15" is dependent on the premise that X is equal to 10 as opposed to anything external to the statement. It is objectively true! What matters is its internal consistency because it does not rely on additional values the way statement one did, due to its premise. This is why statement 2 is objectively true.

Whether X is 10, 1000, 5, 99, or undefined, statement 2 is true because it depends on its premise (X is equal to 10) rather than the actual value of X in real life whatever it may be.

This is how premises work. This is how scaling systems work when they are treated as the premise for the scaling matchup. "Just scale the words on the page" is an oversimplification of how a system is treated, it's like choosing to ignore the IF conditional in statement 2 and saying "just determine whether X + 5 = 15 is true!" which would be stupid.

This is one of the viewpoints for scaling systems, and under this viewpoint it can actually make scaling systems more objective then not using scaling systems, just as the premise "if X is 10" collapsed the true/false possibility of statement one into absolute truth by making it internally reliant rather than externally reliant.

A second viewpoint is that the scaling (like methodology scaling) can be considered as a separate independent scaling of its own such as power scaling, writing scaling, looks scaling, morality scaling, in which case it again is not more subjective because it has to be viewed independently from intelligence scaling. Given the rigid constraints that do not rely on imaginary interpretations and personal opinions to the same extent as intelligence scaling (which under this interpretation is different to methodology scaling), it is again more objective!

A third viewpoint is the subjective interpretation of the term "Intelligence Scaling" which you yourself use in line with "Diegetically determining which character outsmarts who." (although you don't put it into words, you call it "just scaling" or whatever). However, given the broad meaning of the word ***intelligence*** and the ambiguity of its focal point (which does not even need to be on the smartness of the characters themselves within the story), whether you define it one way or another way is equally subjective. Even with this third viewpoint, the objectivity of scaling a particular way (non-diegetically like Methodology Scaling) is not detracted from and the only thing that matters is their internal objectivity.

Under all three example viewpoints I gave, scaling systems are not automatically rendered unobjective or less objective. If you think that under any of these viewpoints, you are distorting facts by using "personal feelings, prejudices, or interpretations," you have still not understood the usage of premises. For a statement to include "If X is equal to 10," such as statement 2 in the previous analogy I gave, the conditional does not detract from the objectivity of the statement regardless of how the conditional itself may have been formulated (subjectively or otherwise).

A framework or system or scaling definition can be treated as a set of premises, the way the conditional "If X is equal to 10" can be treated as a single premise for the statement that ends up making it less subjective.
