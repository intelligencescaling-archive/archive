Author's advantage over characters

Clearing out what Authorial Scaling actually is.

Authorial Scaling is a scaling that likely developed independently, although the concepts are different and both arrive at the same results most of the time.

Authorial Scaling scales the intellectual effort(in outsmarting context) that the author placed in a character's perspective. If you've noticed, that is non-diegetic.

Surprise! that gives the same result of Baku destroying Dazai and Fyodor from Bungo Stray Dogs that you also see in Methodology Scaling.

**\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_**

The OC writers can take advantage of these, these are basically things that we have that the characters we write don't.

**Time:** You have days to weeks, for a feat that your character will do in seconds. (Although this doesn't mean much in Methodology but the next one means a lot...)

**Number of Tials:**  You can do things over and over again, see all the failures while the character you write effortlessly does things in perfection.

**Knowledge Access:** While your character is in a life-death situation forced to solve sum crazy formula or game theory problem... technology is already so advanced, just search it up or ask experts. Don't be like the authors who get the Monty Problem wrong. Don't be like the authors who make chess games that don't make sense and get destroyed by a 1,000 elo player.

**Notes:** Very clear advantage. The character has to store everything in his/her memory, or create heuristics. All you need is a clear general understanding of the story and some good note app like Obsidian.

**Force the outcome:** An underrated technique is reverse-engineering. If reasoning/strategy doesn't make sense, then just manipulate the environment around it so that it makes sense. The characters cannot do this, but you can.

**Specialized areas:** Related to number of trials but talking about a different point. In short, if you played chess for 10 years and can achieve high tier level strategies in pure process alone, that doesn't mean much in most scaling. However, if a fictional character does it the moment he steps on a game, that would be a crazy feat!

**Charts:** I heavily recommend creating charts using softwares. It helps you visualize strategies very clearly.

**Connecting Events:** People are specially good at this so you'll find it as one lf the easiest methods and also why I suggest writing first and the  going back on the story. Basically when you write two arcs you might first not have something that connects it deeplg in mind but after writing the second arc you found a way to connect it to the previous one allowing for a longer and more complex scheme.

(The BATICS author along with many others abused this)
