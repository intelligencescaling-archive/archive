A VOCABULARY AND CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK DESCRIBING AND CATEGORISING EMOTIONS

1. Basic Emotions
Basic emotions are often considered universal and biologically hardwired. According to Paul Ekman, there are six primary emotions:

1. Happiness: Joy, pleasure, contentment
2. Sadness: Grief, sorrow, unhappiness
3. Fear: Anxiety, apprehension, terror
4. Anger: Rage, frustration, irritation
5. Surprise: Astonishment, shock, amazement
6. Disgust: Revulsion, contempt, disdain

2. Complex Emotions
Complex emotions are combinations of basic emotions and are influenced by cultural and social factors. Examples include:

1. Love: Combination of joy and trust (intimacy and passion)
2. Jealousy: Combination of anger, fear, and sadness
3. Guilt: Combination of sadness and fear
4. Pride: Combination of joy and satisfaction
5. Shame: Combination of sadness and fear (often with a social or moral component)

3. Emotional Dimensions
Emotions can be categorized along different dimensions:

1. Valence: Positive (e.g., happiness) vs. Negative (e.g., sadness)
2. Arousal: High arousal (e.g., anger) vs. Low arousal (e.g., calmness)
3. Intensity: Mild (e.g., annoyance) vs. Intense (e.g., rage)

4. Emotional Domains
Emotions can be grouped based on contexts and experiences:

1. Self-conscious emotions: Guilt, shame, pride, embarrassment
2. Social emotions: Empathy, jealousy, envy, gratitude
3. Moral emotions: Guilt, shame, pride, empathy
4. Achievement emotions: Pride, satisfaction, frustration, disappointment

5. The Circumplex Model of Affect
This model, proposed by James Russell, positions emotions on a circular arrangement along two dimensions: valence (pleasure-displeasure) and arousal (activation-deactivation).

1. High Arousal & Positive Valence: Excitement, enthusiasm
2. High Arousal & Negative Valence: Anger, anxiety
3. Low Arousal & Positive Valence: Contentment, relaxation
4. Low Arousal & Negative Valence: Sadness, depression

6. The Plutchik's Wheel of Emotions
Robert Plutchik's model identifies eight primary emotions, each with varying degrees of intensity, and proposes their combinations to form more complex emotions.

   1. Primary Emotions: Joy, trust, fear, surprise, sadness, disgust, anger, anticipation
   2. Secondary Emotions: Formed by combinations of primary emotions (e.g., joy + trust = love)

7. Emotional Regulation
The processes by which individuals influence their emotions, including:

   1. Cognitive Reappraisal: Changing the way one thinks about a situation
   2. Expressive Suppression: Inhibiting the outward signs of emotions
   3. Mindfulness: Awareness and acceptance of one's current emotions
   4. Problem-solving: Addressing the source of the emotion

8. Emotional Intelligence
[1]The ability to identify, understand, manage, and utilize emotions effectively:

   1. Self-awareness: Recognizing one's own emotions
   2. Self-regulation: Managing one's own emotions
   3. Social awareness: Recognizing emotions in others
   4. Relationship management: Handling interpersonal relationships judiciously and empathetically

9. Cultural Influences on Emotions
Different cultures express and interpret emotions uniquely, affecting emotional experience and expression:

   1. Display rules: Cultural norms about how and when emotions should be expressed
   2. Emotion lexicon: The vocabulary a language has for emotions, which can influence emotional awareness and expression

10. Physiological Basis of Emotions
Emotions are linked to physiological responses in the body, involving:

   1. The brain: Amygdala, prefrontal cortex, and other areas involved in emotion processing
   2. Autonomic nervous system: Sympathetic and parasympathetic responses (e.g., heart rate, sweating)
   3. Hormones: Adrenaline, cortisol, and others affecting emotional states
