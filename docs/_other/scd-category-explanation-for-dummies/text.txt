Full Scale Intelligence Quotient
General Ability Index: A subcategory of FSIQ focusing on the individual ability in reasoning and problem solving skills. Includes VCI, FRI, and VSI.

Cognitive Proficiency Index: A subcategory of FSIQ focusing on the Processing Speed and Working Memory of an individual. Includes PSI and WMI.

Working Memory Index: A measure of short term memory and ability to manipulate information

Processing Speed Index: A measure of the processing speed of an individual in all five senses and how quick they can pick up and perceive something.

Verbal Comprehension Index: A measure on understanding, linking, and identifying words, letters, etc. Also includes vocabulary

Fluid Reasoning Index: A measure on individual ability to think and reason logically, understand abstract concepts, identify patterns and use logic in situations.

Visual Spatial Index: A measure on an individual’s ability to visualize and manipulate spatial info.

Quantitative Reasoning Index: Ability to understand quantitative concepts including problem solving and numerical reasoning.

Total Reading Index: Ability to understand text based concepts including reading (Crazy)

Emotional Intelligence
Emotional Perception: Ability to identify false and real emotional expressions, understand how emotions are portrayed through culture and perceive emotional content in voice, facial expressions, language and behavior.

Emotional Understanding: Ability to understand and recognise emotions alongside more complex and mixed emotions.

Emotional Facilitation: Ability to use emotions to generate varying perspectives and support memory and judgment

Emotional Engagement: Ability to connect emotions to an activity, task, person or environment. Helps support motivation.

Emotional Management: Managing one’s emotions

Empathy: Ability to understand and share feelings with another person based on their situation or issue

Social Awareness: Understanding and relating to others alongside community

Social Intuition: Using experience and gut feeling to understand and navigate social situations alongside how people act in specific scenarios

Relationship Management: Developing and sustaining relationships with other individuals and dealing with conflict between groups.

Cultural Intelligence: Adapting to varying cultural differences in society as a means to handle and navigate social situations.

Intelligence Concepts
Crystallized Intelligence: Using previous knowledge, experience and memory to understand something.

Fluid Intelligence: Ability to reason and understand new problems without knowledge and memory.

General Factor: Idea of general knowledge where if an individual is good at one specific field, they are generally good in intelligence. This includes reasoning ability, memory, processing speed, verbal reasoning and spatial ability.

Gardner’s Theory: Idea of multiple varying intelligences where a person cannot be good at each subcategory of intelligence. This includes:
* Visual-Spatial Intelligence: Ability to visualize and blueprint objects and ideas

* Logical-Mathematical Intelligence: Using logic and calculation to understand something

* Naturalistic Intelligence: Understanding and knowing the structure and nature of the environment including wildlife, plants, etc

* Musical Intelligence: Understanding meaning of sounds and music alongside identifying rhythm

* Existential Intelligence: Thinking about the meaning of life

* Interpersonal Intelligence: Understanding themselves

* Intrapersonal Intelligence: Understanding others

* Bodily Kinesthetic Intelligence: Understanding the human body and coordination

* Linguistic Intelligence: Learning language and understanding words.

Thinking
Logical Thinking: Employing deduction, induction and abduction to arrive at a conclusion in a sensible manner

Critical Thinking: Analyzing and evaluating information and arguments in a logical and systematic manner while being devoid of fake information or personal opinion

Systematic Thinking: Methodical thinking done through a specific linear system.

Analogical Thinking: Employing analysis and logical inference to support a specific style of thinking

Associative Thinking: Ability to link and create connections between objects

Holistic Thinking: Applying a belief that two objects are connected through explaining by only reference to the whole concept itself.

Decompositional Thinking: Breaking down a single concept into varying smaller ideas or groups

Convergent Thinking: Ability to think through moving to a specific point and employ abduction to reach a specific conclusion.

Divergent Thinking: Ability to think through moving away from a specific point and employ abduction to reach a specific conclusion

Metacognition: Understanding one’s own thought process and how they reached a conclusion

Abstract Thinking: Using pattern recognition and inductive thinking

Illogical Thinking: Using reasoning that ignores facts or is fallacious

Logical Reasoning: Using sound and sensible reasoning to make a conclusion or way of thinking

Deductive Reasoning: Subtracting or narrowing down options to reach a specific conclusion based on observations.

Inductive Reasoning: Making specific observations in a topic and creating a general conclusion

Abductive Reasoning: Using outside reasoning due to limited reasoning or observations in order to draw a conclusion.

Quantitative Reasoning: Using numbers and statistics to support a method of thinking

Causal Reasoning: Using reasoning related to an informal manner rather than logic
