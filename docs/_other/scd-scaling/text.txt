1.FSIQ - (Full Scale Intelligence Quotient)
·        VCI (Verbal Comprehension Index) - Measures a person's ability to understand and use spoken language.
·        VSI (Visual Spatial Index) - Assesses a person's skills in visual perception and spatial reasoning.
·        FRI (Fluid Reasoning Index) - Measures a person's ability to think logically, solve problems, and apply reasoning skills.
·        WMI (Working Memory Index) - Assesses a person's ability to hold and manipulate information in their mind.
·        PSI (Processing Speed Index) - Measures a person's speed and accuracy in processing visual information.
2.EI - (Emotional Intelligence)
·        EE (Emotional Engagement) - The ability to connect emotionally with others or oneself. Reflects how deeply one can invest feelings into an experience, interaction, or idea — and sustain that engagement meaningfully.
·        EP (Emotional Perception) - The sensitivity to detect and interpret emotions in oneself and others. Determines how accurately one reads emotional signals through tone, body language, or micro-expressions.
·        EU (Emotional Understanding) - The cognitive depth behind emotional awareness — understanding why emotions arise, how they evolve, and what causes emotional shifts or contradictions in human behaviour.
·        EF (Emotional Facilitation) - The skill of using emotion as a strategic tool — recognizing and applying emotional states to guide thinking, motivate others, or influence the atmosphere of a situation.
·       EM (Emotional Management) - The capacity to control, stabilize, or redirect one’s own emotions under pressure. Represents emotional composure, restraint, and resilience when navigating conflict or stress.
3.SI - (Social Intelligence)
·        SOA (Social Awareness) - The perceptual skill to read a social environment — recognizing hierarchy, group dynamics, intentions, and the unspoken norms guiding social interactions.
·        SOM (Social Management) - The tactical control of social environments — navigating, shaping, or manipulating relationships and group outcomes through strategic action, influence, or diplomacy.
·        SOS (Social Skills) - The practical execution of social behaviour — how effectively one communicates, adapts tone, persuades, empathizes, and establishes rapport in real-time interaction.
·        SOC (Social Cognition) - Analytical understanding of social structures, motives, and dynamics.
·        SOE (Social Engineering) – Tactical manipulation of people and systems through psychological and interpersonal methods.
·        SAD (Social Adaptation) - Adjusting social approach, communication style, or behaviour for situational fit.
4.AC - (Adversity Capacity)
·        LC (Learning Capability) – The ability to extract lessons, skills, or insights from challenges, failures, and adversity, and apply them effectively in future situations.
·        MF (Mental Fortitude) – The capacity to endure prolonged psychological stress, trauma, or difficult circumstances without losing clarity, focus, or functionality.
·        ST (Stress Tolerance) – Ability to maintain performance and composure under pressure, high-stakes environments, or sudden challenges.
·        IM (Impulse Management) – The ability to regulate immediate emotional or instinctive reactions, delaying gratification or suppressing harmful impulses in favour of strategic action.
·        Perseverance – Sustained determination to continue pursuing goals despite setbacks, obstacles, or prolonged adversity.
·        Cognitive Discipline – Consistency and rigor in thought processes, reasoning, and decision-making under stress or distraction.
·        Composure – Maintaining calmness, emotional stability, and rational thinking in high-pressure or chaotic situations.
5.Overall Intel -
·        Fluid Intelligence – Knowledge and skills accumulated through experience, education, and cultural exposure. Can be developed over time through learning.
·        Crystallized Intelligence – Ability to reason systematically, identify patterns, and make conclusions based on deductive and inductive reasoning.
·        Logical intelligence – Ability to reason systematically, identify patterns, and make conclusions based on deductive and inductive reasoning.
·        Learning Ability – Capacity to acquire, retain, and apply new knowledge or skills effectively.
·        General Knowledge – Breadth and depth of factual, cultural, and practical knowledge across domains.
·        Memory – Ability to encode, store, and retrieve information accurately and efficiently.
·        Adaption – Cognitive flexibility; the ability to modify thinking, behaviour, or strategies to fit new conditions or challenges.
·        Creative intelligence – Capacity to generate novel, original, or unconventional ideas and solutions.
·        Intuitive intelligence - Subconscious or instinctive synthesis of information leading to insight, predictive understanding, or rapid decision-making.
·        Kinesthetic Intelligence - Understanding and controlling physical objects or one’s own body in space; coordination and manipulation.
·        Spatial Intelligence - Ability to visualize, comprehend, and reason about spatial relationships, structures, and patterns.
·        Academic Intelligence – Proficiency in formal learning environments; ability to understand, analyse, and apply theoretical concepts.
·        Musical Intelligence – Sensitivity to rhythm, tone, pitch, and musical patterns; ability to create, interpret, or reproduce music.
·        Linguistic Intelligence – Skill in understanding, using, and manipulating language effectively, including reading, writing, and verbal expression.
·        Intrapersonal Intelligence – Awareness and understanding of one’s own thoughts, emotions, motivations, and behaviour.
·        Interpersonal Intelligence – Ability to perceive, interpret, and respond effectively to the emotions, intentions, and behaviours of others.
·        Potential – The innate capacity for cognitive growth, learning, and mastery — representing one’s upper limit of intellectual and strategic development if fully realized.
6.Overall Thinking -
·        LAT (Lateral Thinking) – The ability to approach problems from unconventional or indirect angles, generating solutions through creative reframing rather than straightforward logic.
·        LIT (Linear Thinking) – Thinking in a direct, sequential path where each conclusion is derived logically from prior facts or established reasoning.
·        CT (Creative Thinking) – The generation of novel, original, and imaginative ideas that diverge from standard patterns of thought.
·        AT (Analytical Thinking) – The ability to deconstruct complex problems into smaller, logical components for examination and understanding.
·        ST (Strategic Thinking) – The skill of formulating short-term tactics and situational strategies that maximize advantage within given constraints.
·        ABT (Abstract Thinking) – The ability to think conceptually, beyond concrete objects or immediate experiences, and to understand symbolic, theoretical, or metaphysical concepts.
·        CIT (Critical Thinking) – The process of evaluating information, arguments, or beliefs through logic, scepticism, and evidence-based reasoning.
·        Metacognition – Awareness and regulation of one’s own thought processes — thinking about how one thinks and adapting mental strategies accordingly.
·        Quick Thinking – The ability to rapidly process information and make effective decisions under pressure or time constraints.
·        Detailed Thinking – The capacity to focus intently on specifics, precision, and accuracy within complex systems or ideas.
·        Conceptual Thinking – The ability to form, manipulate, and understand abstract ideas or frameworks that explain complex phenomena or systems.
·        Innovative Thinking – Applying imagination and insight to create entirely new methods, perspectives, or solutions that advance existing systems.
·        Systematic Thinking – The ability to organize thought logically and methodically, ensuring structured progression toward a conclusion.
·        Associative Thinking – The ability to form connections between seemingly unrelated concepts, ideas, or experiences to uncover patterns and relationships.
·        Problem Solving – The capacity to analyse a situation, identify obstacles, and generate effective, adaptable solutions through logical, creative, or strategic means.
7.Overall Reasoning -
·        LR (Logical Reasoning) – Reasoning that follows structured, rule-based logic and can be clearly explained through rational progression.
·        ILR (Illogical Reasoning) – Reasoning that defies conventional logic yet may still reach accurate or intuitive conclusions through nontraditional thought processes.
·        CFR (Counterfactual Reasoning) – The ability to imagine and evaluate “what if” scenarios by altering variables or events to explore alternate outcomes and their implications.
·        ANR (Analogical Reasoning) – The process of drawing parallels between different concepts, situations, or systems to transfer knowledge or infer solutions.
·        NMR (Numerical Reasoning) – The ability to reason effectively using numbers, data, and quantitative patterns to draw conclusions or make predictions.
·        DR (Deductive Reasoning) – Reasoning from general principles or multiple premises to reach a single, logically certain conclusion.
·        PR (Perceptual Reasoning) – The integration of perception and reasoning to interpret patterns, relationships, and visual-spatial information for problem-solving.
·        ABR (Abstract Reasoning) – The ability to reason through conceptual or symbolic patterns beyond direct observation or tangible logic; essentially creative reasoning.
·        IR (Inductive Reasoning) – The process of forming general conclusions or theories based on specific observations, examples, or patterns within a single topic.
·        AR (Abductive Reasoning) – The ability to infer the most likely explanation from incomplete or uncertain information — forming plausible hypotheses under ambiguity.
·        Pattern Recognition – The ability to identify regularities, structures, or trends within complex information — recognizing connections between ideas, events, or data points that may not be immediately apparent.
·        Deduction – The structured application of logical principles to eliminate false possibilities and reach the most rational, evidence-based conclusion.
8.Overall Deception -
·        MD (Misdirection Deception) – The art of diverting attention or focus away from the truth by manipulating perception, timing, or context.
·        FD (Fabrication Deception) – The creation of entirely false information, narratives, or evidence to mislead or control belief.
·        CD (Concealment Deception) – The act of withholding, obscuring, or disguising truth without necessarily fabricating falsehoods.
·        Unpredictable – The ability to remain erratic or unreadable in behaviour, emotion, or strategy, preventing accurate anticipation by others.
·        Acting Skills – The capacity to convincingly assume alternate identities, emotions, or intentions through performance and behavioural mimicry.
·        Disguise – The use of physical, vocal, or behavioural alterations to conceal true identity or intent.
·        Fabrication – The strategic creation or alteration of information to construct a false yet logically consistent narrative that supports a deductive conclusion.
·        Bluffing – The deliberate presentation of false confidence, weakness, or information to manipulate decisions or reactions.
·        Poker Face – The ability to suppress visible emotional or psychological cues, maintaining a neutral expression under pressure.
·        Info Control – The mastery of managing information flow — knowing what to reveal, distort, or conceal to guide perception and outcomes.
9.Overall Manipulation -
·        Direct Manipulation – The ability to influence or control another person’s thoughts, emotions, or actions through direct communication, presence, or interaction.
·        Indirect Manipulation – The capacity to influence individuals or groups subtly through unseen or background actions, often without direct confrontation.
·        Individual Manipulation – The skill of targeting and controlling the decisions, perceptions, or emotions of a single person through tailored methods.
·        Mass Manipulation – The ability to shape or control the behaviour and beliefs of large groups through influence, propaganda, or emotional contagion.
·        Event Manipulation - The strategic alteration or orchestration of events, circumstances, or timing to produce desired outcomes or psychological effects.
·        Logical Manipulation – Using rational argumentation, structured reasoning, or data-driven persuasion to guide others’ decisions or beliefs.
·        Illogical Manipulation – Leveraging irrational, abstract, or counterintuitive methods — such as confusion, unpredictability, or paradox — to disrupt logic and influence outcomes.
·        Emotional Manipulation – The ability to exploit or utilize emotions to influence others’ perceptions, behaviours, or loyalties.
·        Psychological Manipulation – The application of psychological insight, behavioural conditioning, or cognitive distortion to subtly guide another’s mindset or choices.
10.Overall Manipulation Methods -
·        Cognitive Reprogramming – The process of systematically altering how a person perceives, interprets, and responds to information, effectively rewriting their mental frameworks, emotional triggers, and decision-making pathways.
·        Gaslighting – A form of psychological manipulation that makes a target doubt their memory, perception, or sanity by denying facts, minimizing experiences, or presenting false narratives as truth.
·        Guilt Tripping – Using guilt or moral obligation as leverage to control another person’s emotions, decisions, or behaviour.
·        Social Ostracism – Deliberate withholding of social contact, support, or inclusion to punish, control, or coerce a target. It operates by cutting off emotional, informational, or practical resources
·        Seduction – Using charm, attraction, and emotional/sexual intimacy to lower a target’s defences and gain influence or access.
·        Love Bombing – Overwhelming someone with affection, attention, and gifts early in a relationship to create quick attachment and indebtedness, later used to control or manipulate.
·        Black Mailing – Threatening to reveal damaging information (true or fabricated) unless the target complies with demands.
·        Scapegoating – Projecting blame or responsibility onto another individual or group to deflect accountability and preserve power or image.
·        Triangulation – Controlling dynamics or perceptions by manipulating communication and relationships between three or more parties.
·        Strategic Influence – The calculated use of persuasion, framing, and psychological leverage to guide beliefs or actions toward a desired outcome without overt coercion.
·        Threat-Based Manipulation– The art of controlling behaviour, extracting compliance, or enforcing obedience through the deliberate use or implication of fear, loss, or punishment.
11.Overall Strategy -
·        Logistics – The coordination and management of resources, timing, and movement required to ensure a plan’s functional success.
·        Effectiveness – The degree to which a strategy achieves its intended objectives and produces the desired outcome.
·        Efficiency – The ability to accomplish goals with minimal time, effort, or resource expenditure.
·        Adaptability – The capacity to adjust strategies and methods dynamically in response to new information or changing conditions.
·        Strategic Shielding – The act of protecting one’s core strategy from interference or exposure through concealment, misdirection, or layered planning.
·        Length – The temporal scale and sustainability of a strategy, determining whether it is optimized for short-term gains or long-term endurance.
·        Strategic Foresight – The ability to predict future developments and prepare adaptive responses before they occur.
·        Short-Term Strategy – Formulating and executing quick, flexible tactics that yield immediate results.
·        Long-Term Strategy – Designing sustainable strategies that maintain effectiveness over extended periods.
·        Resilience – The endurance of a strategy under pressure, reflecting its capacity to recover and maintain function after disruption or failure.
·        Strategic Coverage – The comprehensiveness of a plan, measuring how well it accounts for all possible factors, risks, and variables.
·        Complexity – The structural depth and interconnectedness within a strategy, balancing sophistication with manageability.
·        Strategic Execution – The precision and effectiveness with which a plan is implemented in real-world conditions.
·        Success Rate – The consistency and reliability of strategic victory across multiple attempts or scenarios.

·        Planning Preparation – The extent of groundwork, research, and pre-emptive setup undertaken before plan execution.

·        Short-Term Planning – The skill of creating immediate, adaptable plans that produce quick and effective results.
·        Long-Term Planning – The capacity to develop enduring strategies and roadmaps that remain effective over extended periods.
·        Planning Practicality – How well a plan functions in real-life conditions, balancing ambition with feasibility.
·        Planning Coverage – The range and inclusivity of a plan, measuring how many potential variables, risks, and contingencies are accounted for.
·        Detailed Planning – The thoroughness and clarity of a plan, ensuring that every component and phase is precisely defined.
