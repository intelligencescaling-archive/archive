The argument needs to be destroyed since it's BAD and dishonest.

# 1.

**\[First\]**, one side of the Manipulation category in SCD is literally about controlling people through their weaknesses and vulnerabilities.

>Manipulation differs from general influence and persuasion.\[13\]\[14\] Manipulation, unlike persuasion, typically involves exploiting the vulnerabilities of an individual.

\~ wikepedia\~

All manipulation by SC is literally about exploiting either the weaknesses, vulnerabilities, or both of others for their goals.

Saying *"MR Placeholder only manipulates weak/mentally vulnerable characters"* exalts the manipulation point, not decreases manipulation potency.

Even including character aspects like age, sex, career, stage, etc., doesn't automatically invalidate SC's manipulation. It's about exploitation and methods.

These poor points are used to only downplay the process and ignore the whole context and scope.

All of this just to get a quick thrill because their fav character has better manipulation. 😈

# 2.

**\[Second\]**, it's desperate to attempt to attack said SC's feat framework, kit, and modus operandi.

Characters who have information networks, proxies, or sensory systems from which they perform their operations and exploit others get cancelled by this poor logic and looked down upon.

This is often due to the opposing side not accepting the X character's strengths for the matchup. They don't wanna acknowledge that those strengths contribute to their manipulation feats, or they desire an equalized matchup instead of a general outsmarting situation between characters.

# 3.

**\[Third\]**, downplaying the scope & scale of manipulation and how it plays into the story overall.

Arguments are usually like this:

"While Master X manipulated a few people in a room throughout the  three arcs, those people were canon fodders. Meanwhile, Mage Y was manipulating bigger fishes."

Despite those "bigger fishes" barely playing a role beyond one arc.

Ignoring the scope of what that manipulation contributes to the arc and how it plays into scaling and ranking. This is due to feigning dishonesty & ignorance and not accepting the intricacies of another character.

It's about decreasing MAC(multi-agent complexity ) of the story and character

# 4.

**\[fourth\],** This is the one argument that is actually valid, but it's frequently misapplied.

Opponent complexity matters. Manipulation is about exploitation and methods, but WHO is being manipulated and WHAT makes the manipulation difficult are also relevant.

Whether the opponent is genuinely difficult to manipulate and whether the circumstances make it difficult to manipulate things in one's favour are legitimate factors to consider.

However, this argument is often used as an excuse, mainly through scale and appearance fallacies, to undermine the exploitation and methods involved.

People focus on the perceived status, intelligence, or importance of the target while ignoring the actual process, whether it involves exploiting other people's perceptions of themselves, a system, or a situation.

One thing: opponent complexity is already part of the feat framework. So talking about opponent complexity isn't that simple when you're simultaneously denying a character's manipulation feats, ignoring the story, and disregarding all three points mentioned above.

You can't dismiss a character's manipulation, ignore the methodology  and context, and then conveniently bring up opponent complexity as an excuse to downplay the feat.

# VERDICT

The "X manipulates fodders, while Y manipulates non-fodders" argument is a shallow and dishonest way of comparing manipulation feats.

Opponent complexity matters, but it doesn't automatically invalidate manipulation feats simply because the targets are labelled fodders.
