Akiyama Shinichi from Liar Game and Ayanokōji Kiyotaka from Classroom of the Elite are both portrayed as brilliant strategists, but when comparing their intelligence in terms of narrative function, depth of tactics, and realism, there are strong arguments for why Akiyama is often considered "smarter" than Ayanokōji. Here's a breakdown:
________________

1. Realism of Intelligence
* Akiyama is a former psychology student and con artist whose strategies are rooted in real-world psychological manipulation, game theory, and logic. The Liar Game often showcases plausible deception tactics and psychological pressure that could feasibly be applied in real life.

* Ayanokōji is presented as a near-omniscient, superhuman mastermind with almost no flaws. His intelligence, while entertaining, often stretches credulity, relying on convenience or narrative control.

✅ Advantage: Akiyama — more grounded and believable.
________________

2. Complexity and Depth of Strategies
   * Akiyama solves intricate, multi-layered games involving hundreds of players, trust manipulation, and high-stakes consequences. His moves are often slow-burn, requiring precise prediction of human behavior and planning across many steps.

   * Ayanokōji often wins through dominance, using superior resources, surveillance, or manipulation, but rarely needs to explain his full logic unless the story chooses to reveal it. His strategies are more about control than complexity.

✅ Advantage: Akiyama — deeper, more nuanced strategic execution.
________________

3. Use of Psychology
      * Akiyama's greatest strength is his deep understanding of human nature, often outwitting opponents by exploiting cognitive biases, emotional weaknesses, and group dynamics.

      * Ayanokōji, while intelligent, approaches people more like tools. His detachment makes his strategies feel more mechanical, with less psychological richness.

✅ Advantage: Akiyama — more psychologically astute.
________________

4. Stakes and Pressure
         * In Liar Game, failure can mean financial ruin or psychological breakdown, which makes Akiyama’s composure and consistent success more impressive.

         * In Classroom of the Elite, the stakes are often academic or social, and Ayanokōji is never truly at risk. He's so overpowered that tension is minimal.

✅ Advantage: Akiyama — higher stakes, more pressure.
________________

5. Narrative Bias
            * Ayanokōji is written to be "untouchable" — he's the central force behind every event, often without effort. That can feel less earned.

            * Akiyama struggles, loses occasionally, and adapts. His victories feel like the result of real thinking rather than narrative fiat.

✅ Advantage: Akiyama — more believable intellectual arc.
________________

Conclusion
Ayanokōji is a brilliant, borderline-superhuman strategist in a controlled environment. Akiyama is a human being who outwits others through real, explainable intellect and empathy-tinged manipulation.
Therefore, Akiyama is arguably "smarter" in a more meaningful, realistic, and grounded sense.
