『What is strategy?』

If we are going to divide strategy and planning into two individual definitions, we must identify an agreeable difference.

Strategies and plans are very similar, in that their sole purpose is to reach an objective of some kind. However, the similarities end in the fact the role they both have are played differently.

Where have we heard strategy before? Ah, right, while you're playing video games you'll often hear "strategies" thrown around by the community. For example, one of the most basic strategies known in gaming, is when you're fighting a grass-type opponent in a turn-based game, and you use a fire-type attack for more effective damage.

Does it involve planning? No, not really, but it is strategic because it involves a purpose consistent to achieving an objective. Therefore, it is a strategy. A strategy is an idea, decision, or action with the purpose of reaching a goal.

Now, why do we plan things? Because it helps us progress toward our goals. It does so by establishing possible steps necessary to do so. Every step is strategic, but not exactly a strategy, rather a tactic as it serves a micro purpose essential for moving to the macro. They function like a subsidiary strategy.

A reason most people have them all turned around is because they work together seamlessly. Every good plan is strategic, and every good strategy is compatible to all sorts of plans.

What makes one a good strategist is their ability to do both.

『Alternative Subcategories』

There are three subcategories. That's right, three. An incredible improvement from the numerous subcategories. The intention here is to scale how good of a strategist the character is, rather than the strategies themselves.

I see complexity, length, and success rate alot, but they don't actually have anything to do with a character's ability to strategize. These categories should be used to scale strategies, not characters.

Awareness — Evident from the name, it is the degree of awareness the character has in regards to their strategic position, whether that be what each possible action and decision they can take, what effect those actions and decisions might lead to (both positive and negative), and the current adversary's position as well. Scaled based on range of awareness, and how quickly they can become aware of it relative to how much information and time they have.

Adaptability — How well a character can respond to circumstances changing, whether it be being pushed to a corner, or unexpected factors. Scaled by how quickly the character can bounce back strategically relative to difficulty of new circumstances.

Optimization — How quickly a character can find the most optimal or consistent strategy. Scaled based on speed relative to the amount of time and resources available.
