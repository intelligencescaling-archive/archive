What is a Holistic Point of View?

Just as the name suggests, you analyze everything. But unlike normal scaling not everything is valid until proven to be consistent within the established narrative; congruent with a character's methodology, and demonstrably within the range of a character's abilities.

1. To properly evaluate a feat, using this specific point of view, you need to eliminate cognitive bias.

If you go in thinking: “This feat has a flaw.” you will find problems with things that don't even have any.

The same logic applies in the event you go in thinking: “This feat is impressive.” or “Is this feat valid?” Because now you're subconsciously hoping it is and that will muddle your perception.

The right mindset to have going into a story is: “What is the feat?” and “Does the narrative support it?”

It might sound as though “is this feat valid” and “does the narrative support it” are the same thing, but they aren't.

Not necessarily.

2. Why?

With the former, you hold out subconscious hope for an interpretation you believe to be viable before going into the analysis with the full details of the feat.

For the latter, you analyze the full feat first and then see if your interpretation is supported by the narrative. So it is a matter of the order in which these two actions are taken.

If you're still confused, then, simplified, it looks like this:

Is this feat viable? (Question) -> Where is the feat in the story? (answer) ❌

Eg. X-strategy is overrated -> looks for the feat while thinking it is unimpressive -> unintentionally conjures up false evidence to validate that logic.

👈You first evaluate from a lack of knowledge. Thus declaring validity BEFORE establishing a coherent and non-convoluted support structure. ❌

Where is the feat in the story? (answer) -> Does the narrative support this interpretation? (question)✅

Eg. Where does the X-strategy begin and end? -> looks through the volumes and comes to a conclusion -> searches for narrative evidence that supports it.

👈You first evaluate from a presence of knowledge. Establishing a non-convoluted yet coherent support structure SHOULD be the cause of validity. ✅

Thing to note: Above all else, you MUST be trustworthy for this to work. If you are unsure about something, recheck or at least keep notes if you can't be bothered to reread the source material.

You are never to intentionally misconstrue a passage or gloss over important details to validate your opinion. If you do, then you've already violated the system by intentionally stepping into the “question -> answer” state.

Narrative Consistency

Narrative consistency is basically when the feat a character pulls off is always given an explanation within the story itself. Be it in the form of show don't tell, subtle clues, hidden plot points or situational context.

If the feat is stated to be true and yet there is no way to explain it without violating the character's established methodology or proven ability, then it is invalid in the sense of becoming an outlier that is only applicable in “in-verse” scaling match ups(e.g L stock market feat)

It is never to be used in cross verse match-ups as that would be disingenuous to both the scaling system itself and the other character.

For there to be true narrative consistency, a feat must never be analyzed in isolation.

This isn't to be confused with isolating a feat for the sake of a matchup.

Analysis requires as much objectivity and access to information as possible. Isolating a feat from the analysis for the sake of a match up comes after you've correctly analyzed its correlation with the previous feat that was executed.

This is to ensure that you yourself are aware of what went on before that feat took place so that you will be better able to defend your take with actual canon consistent evidence.

If the feats do not have any connection to any others (is an outlier or one off) then you can simply evaluate a match up as you always have in isolation.

Character Methodology

This encompasses aspects of Character writing in relation to the actual process behind their feats. Meaning, categories such as counteraction/countering has to be taken into account.

For that to be possible you'll need to have an understanding of the character's personality, ideology OR flaws. You do not need all three, though it may be preferable for a more accurate scale.

The reason why it may be preferred is not because you'll be doing the usual static scale of, “Does he beat him?” You'll be doing it by placing them in actual situations of your choosing.

As a bonus, for those who don't usually include categories like counteraction, you only need to do this while using the methodology aspect specifically.

Proven Ability

This is how likely it is for a character to actually execute a feat based on their on-screen representation. Whether or not the feat comes in the form of a statement or a highly detailed explanation, it has to be taken as fact until it has been proven wrong or invalid.

When you are aware of the extent of a character's abilities, you will be better able to scale them against other characters according to their display.

For the sake of an example:-

Ayanokoji vs Light (High diff)

Abandoned Building Baku > Tutorial Chen Ran (High diff)

To ensure the correctness of your takes, do not be biased in your analysis of these characters. Yes, you will be required to have the same depth of, or at least a basic understanding of both’s peak ability whether or not you isolate a feat for the sake of a match-up or are doing a full scale analysis.

Rant: If you have a take for the sake of pushing an agenda or are intuiting it then it's fine, however, when you actually put effort into the analysis to prop up a flawed premise then that would be disappointing, truly. Not only are you spreading misinformation, you are doing so with utmost confidence without considering the issues that may crop up because of it.

If your analysis has some ambiguous areas that you've filled in with the most accurate interpretation you can muster, LABEL IT.

Q&A

Do I need to use Methodology and Proven Ability in this scaling?

Yes.

Though they are meant to be filters that allow greater analytical accuracy, you do not need to use both of them at once when you want to do a match up.

Using methodology is most appropriate when you apply the characters to a specified scenario (Character  vs Situation etc).

In contrast, Proven Ability enables you to intuition scale based on (only on) the perceived level of the characters being evaluated (Full Scale Match-ups etc).

How do you scale using Character methodology actually? Like you use methodology for writing and the thought process, what about the feat?

The Character Methodology is you making use of methodology scaling (logic and actual methods) as well as character personality (flaws, personality or ideology)

If two characters with opposing ideologies, say A finds life meaningless and B finds life meaningful, the potency of their respective ideologies will determine who has a psychological edge.

If A has a narcissistic personality and B has a pragmatic personality then B will have an edge in countering seeing as you'll automatically couple that with their character flaw once you're made aware of it.

<->

You'll determine which of the two are able to come up with a working counteraction by putting them inside a situation and developing a plan of action that is consistent with the mindset of the character.

Here, since PA is not necessarily needed, you have leeway to make the character perform better than their canon counterparts. Just be careful not to mis-characterize (glaze) them too much.

So what I'm saying is, you get the chance to develop a feat for them in this case. You just have to put them in a situation of your choosing and decide if they win or lose and explain how they'd go about it.

It seems like a hassle, but it's really not. It's meant to allow people to express their creativity, especially writers. Funnily enough.

What about the reasoning behind the feats? Like, do you analyse the feats or not? Or just character vs situation?

When you're using Character Methodology on its own you create your own feats to evaluate. This is explicitly character vs situation on its own (you can make it character vs character vs situation if you want)

When you're using PA on its own you *isolate* the character's actual feats in story and evaluate them for the match up you have in mind. This is for character vs character match ups on its own (tournaments, gauntlets, 1v1, teams etc)

If a feat is an outlier even within the verse, then the character can only scale to the feat when you isolate it. You cannot use it in conjunction with any other feat as it will be unfair for the others in their verse.

How does CSM deal with DOTA?

That one's fairly simple, actually. If whatever the author says does not match with the narrative in a coherent manner then it is not to be taken as valid.

If it actually is coherent and there is sufficient narrative evidence to support it then it is to be taken as valid.

If it is coherent, but is an outlier feat then it is to be treated as an outlier is treated—only useful in in-verse match-ups. It is also to be isolated.
