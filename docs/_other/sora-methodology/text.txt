Understanding the Prisoner's dilemma
The prisoner's dilemma is a fictional situation present in game theory. Sora uses this analogy to analyze his situation so I will note down the key parts of this situation relevant to this story.
The following is the situation, two prisoners in different places both are trialed for a certain crime, and the police is midway through an interrogation when both prisoners bring up the same certain deal.
If two of the prisoners confess both get sentences for a full year
If both prisoners remain silent both get only a month
If one confesses and the other one does not, then the one who confessed gets out of jail free, the other one 3 years in prison.
A classic situation where it tests trust between people and what gives the best benefits for the individual.
To analyze this situation there is a concept of nash equilibrium. Which is a state where both parties execute their optimal choices, in the prisoner's dilemma case it is for both to confess.

Sora analyzes the situation further, saying that all players have their intentions, if the police had some part in making the deal it is likely that the one who is at the edge is the police as they do not have any other option but to wait for the choices of the prisoners, and the nash equilibrium is likely a misdirection to lure in the prisoners. The one who has the initiative is the prisoners. This is similar to the scenario with Sora and the others players in this game, Sora made everyone betray each other to put everyone on the same page, so they do not participate in the game of doubt the police or in this case Old deus set up, allowing him to maintain initiative.
