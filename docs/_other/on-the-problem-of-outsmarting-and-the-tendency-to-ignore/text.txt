On the problem of “outsmarting” and the tendency to ignore causations

**On the problem of “outsmarting” and the tendency to ignore causations.**

In SCD two paths are usually possible:
1- scaling the most intelligent character
2- scaling who would win, among the characters, in a context that predominantly requires intelligence and cunning.

Now, the vast majority of scalers choose the second path. Now (leaving aside the fact that cunning is a rather vague concept) the way in which they pursue it (this option) presents a series of relevant issues.

In essence there are three paths to scale “outsmarting”:

**First Path:** it is thought that the final outcomes of “outsmarting” (e.g. capacities of manipulation, deception, foresight, analysis, adaptability etc.) are purely caused by intelligence + personality + experience + third-party genetic advantages (looks, voice etc.) + third-party environmental advantages (familiarity, luck, emergence with one’s abilities more than proportional etc.).

In this case it would make sense to measure only the causal factors underlying these outcomes.

Obviously, only intelligence and personality should be counted, since the other causal factors are advantages that have nothing to do with cunning and intelligence (I am talking about experience, third-party genetic advantages, third-party environmental advantages) which would lead to misleading evaluations.

**Second Path:** thinking that the “final outcomes of outsmarting” are not caused by anything. Causations are completely ignored.
This is the model that perhaps comes closest to the way many people actually do scaling.

That these outcomes are not caused (at least in part) by the elements I previously mentioned is objectively false, therefore route 2 can be avoided in principle.

**Third Path:** thinking that the final outcomes of outsmarting are caused *also* by the previously mentioned factors, but that they have weight only up to a certain point (for example roughly around 50%).
This is the model that remains closest to the scaling tendencies of most scalers (leaving aside the second), but it still presents major problems.

This route presents 4 problems:

**Problem 1** — If they were not *only/for the vast majority* caused by the previous components I mentioned, exactly what should these final outcomes of outsmarting be caused by?
If the model is not even described, the whole thing becomes weak and random.
Moreover, they could be a multitude of variables, not necessarily just one.

**Problem 2** — To what extent should these variables (still not described) weigh in determining the value of these outcomes?
(For example) Maybe for manipulation 80% is already explained by social cognition and lack or low affective empathy, but as long as an approximate measure is not known, assigning value to this unknown variable becomes very arbitrary.

(obviously on the components of manipulation I am greatly simplifying but it is to make myself understood).

This problem is very relevant especially in the context of fiction. This is because in fiction many fundamental causal abilities (such as working memory) can be decided in a completely arbitrary way according to narrative needs, with the consequence (very often) of an alien level of capacity.

This is a problem because once it is admitted that these, even partially, contribute to forming the skills of the final outsmarting outcomes, they should in turn distort the score of the latter, but this very rarely happens in the community.

Example: Shiro, who manages to calculate all the moves in chess and always find the best response (therefore crazy WMI + PSI + pattern recognition), is evaluated in planning at very low levels.

Another example: Nambong Baek, who is almost always assigned extremely high FSIQ, thinking, reasoning, cognitive empathy, social cognition, but with strategy, planning (and sometimes “deception” and “manipulation”) very mediocre.

**Problem 3** — As long as things remain this vague, how do we distinguish between “advantage factors” (those previously mentioned such as looks, luck etc.) and these unknown variables?

**Problem 4** — How have causal variables that have never even been expressed been evaluated so far? Is it not more plausible that aesthetic judgment has been used? (again: assuming they really exist)

In short, the model becomes extremely arbitrary and vague.

**\[DISCLAIMER: in this text I use “intelligence” incorporating the following list of cognitive abilities (I specify otherwise it seems I merely mean FSIQ): Working Memory; Attention; Pattern Recognition; Long-Term Memory (episodic, procedural, semantic); Processing Speed; Metacognition; Error Monitoring; Decision Making (probabilistic / trade-off); Goal Management; Temporal Foresight; Cognitive Flexibility; Working Memory Updating; Impulse Control; Motivational Regulation; Inhibition; Social Cognition (including cognitive empathy); Strategic Reasoning; Critical Thinking (bias suppression + evidence evaluation); Planning; Abstract / Analogical Thinking; Creative Thinking; Causal Reasoning\]**
