Why Normal Scaling Is Dumb

A deliberately provocative title which I don’t really mean. What I do mean, however, is that the attitude of normal scalers towards scaling is absurd.

First, I want to make it clear that I take no issue with how they scale. In fact, no one has been as clear or as consistent as them in their goal with scaling: evaluate a character’s ability to outsmart. That’s it. That’s been their motto for the entire duration of SCD since 2022.

The problem is the chest-thumping and boasting that accompanies their scaling, as if the characters they deem capable are praiseworthy for scaling high, while the ones incapable mock-worthy for scaling low.

They have such lofty praise for their high tiers, hyping them up with every superlative in the dictionary - they sound so starstruck you almost begin to wonder if they realise they’re reading fiction? That these feats weren’t actually performed by anyone, that these statements aren’t the testimony of real existent talent but rather the fiat of one lone writer. Does it dawn on them how ridiculous it is then to make this the object of their adoration?

As far as evaluating characters goes, as far as establishing a hierarchy goes, there’s no issue - but when emotions seep into your analysis, I can’t help but ask: what on earth are you dazzled by? Something a toddler could scribble down in five minutes, is that how easy it is to impress you?

You can look anywhere you want in life, never do we seriously compete or fanaticise about something so shallow. Football - it’s not easy to be Ronaldo or Messi. Literature - it’s not easy to come up with Shakespeare. Miss Universe - not just anyone can look that good.

There was even once a competition over who could write the biggest number on a blackboard, and observe: they made a rule that you *couldn’t make naive additions.* For example, just adding one was not allowed, as that would be both uninteresting and unimpressive. It’s not skilful to just increment whatever’s already on the board. It gives a bigger number, sure, but it goes against the entire spirit of the competition. The point was to come up with creative ways to grow the number. Notice how that now demands real skill as it isn’t easy to do, it takes ingenuity. And indeed eventually, someone didn’t, *couldn’t*, come up with a bigger number, thus ending the game.

The point is that a competition over that which takes no skill is no competition at all. Imagine a competition over who can think of the biggest number between 1-100, that’s a good analogue for how absurd the normal scaling scene is. They praise characters for scaling high when it took zero effort to write them that way, and they ridicule characters for scaling low when the author wasn’t even participating in their senseless competition.

So why single out normal scaling? This criticism applies elsewhere too, but it pertains most to normal scaling because of how open the definition is. Recall that normal scaling wishes only, only to establish how capable a character is, nothing more. There are no filters of needing to be onscreen or “realistic” or “logical” or anything else. This lack of restriction makes it extremely easy, trivial in fact, to make a character scale as high as you wish. It’s no longer a question of skill or ability now but rather choice - how clever does the author *want* their character to be.

As I said earlier, a competition over what doesn’t require skill is no competition at all. When the author can make his character arbitrarily intelligent and folks are in awe at his creation, in truth they are in awe at nothing except the author’s fancy that day - that he chose to write those words, a simple decision, a thoughtless keystroke. Needless to say marvelling over this is senseless. Perhaps had he, on a whim, chosen some different words, all their adoration would be gone, vaporised into thin air. What shallowness! Hence why I drew the analogy with competing for the biggest number 1-100. There is nothing impressive about picking 100, it isn’t deserving of praise, it isn’t a show of finesse, it’s a mere *choice*. Competitions don’t concern choice they concern ability, otherwise why wouldn’t everyone just choose to win?

By comparison, take methodology scaling, and I’m aware everyone has their own version of it. However we should be able to agree that a character’s scaling in methodology is tied to what their feats indicate (even if loosely) of their author’s own intelligence and effort. It's no longer based on preference or choice. A character cannot scale high without indicating and thus requiring something likewise high of his author’s own wit and hard work. Therefore, competition makes sense here because the character’s scaling is withheld by authorial ability, it’s actually something *worth* competing over.

Of course, I don’t advocate for toxicity in any scaling, but *if* you are going to glaze a character while insulting others, it would at least make a lick of sense to do it with respect to their standing in methodology, as that’s an actual point of competition. It makes no sense to do so in normal scaling for the reasons already outlined.

So to conclude, normal scaling serves to evaluate, it’s a tool to grade characters. As soon as you make it about prestige however, as soon as you boast about how your character “outreasoned a super computer”, “made a 20 000x return on the stock market”, or “manipulated all of Germany”

then with all due respect… who gives a shit?
