**Shuffle! Tactic:-**

The tactic itself was made with the purpose of using everyone **(3 people)** in his vicinity to influence the decisions Deiondre would make.

The first and third tactics were built by Monty to use Reina especially to cause discord within Deiondre's mind.

Deiondre likes Reina and Monty knows this. This is why in the first tactic he purposefully revealed the card he was playing.

His reasons were:

1. Get Reina to reprimand him for doing something foolish
2. Instill doubt in Deiondre's mind as to which card Monty will actually play in the first round.
3. Plant “Scissors” in Deiondre's head

Because of Reina's involvement Deiondre will unintentionally begin to overthink Monty's words by over analyzing *Reina's* physical restraint.

The reason why this works is because Monty had already been in two situations where Reina refrained from hitting him. Not because she couldn't or didn't want to. But because she felt it wasn't worth the trouble due to how frustrating it all was.

Monty, saying he'll play **‘Scissors’***x2* instills minor doubt in Deiondre. But when Reina went over to Monty all fired up as if she were going to hit him—then suddenly decided against it after seeing his card, that stirred Deiondre's doubt further.

This led him to overanalyze Reina and Monty's actions and ultimately refrain from playing the card he initially intended *(rock)*

Keep in mind, in this round, both of them played their cards simultaneously. This will play directly into the third layer of the tactic.

\[To expand, Deiondre starts to suspect Monty of lying cuz Reina didn't hit him. Her not hitting him is taken as her either figuring out that Monty was bluffing mid-swing and playing along or her not bothering to hit him simply because she thinks there's a reason not to *(too frustrating to bother).*

Both are equally plausible; however, he follows the first interpretation because Reina had **NOT** refrained from hitting Monty in front of him before this. *(3 times)*

*Within this context, her hitting him would be taken as a sign of Monty playing the card he said he would and Reina being impulsive.*

This is why he sighs *(because he can't tell if Monty's lying)* and switches cards. And it is why he regrets his decision when he ultimately loses the first round.\]

**Second Layer** of the tactic:

1. Tell a lie to induce further doubt.
2. Simulate a time crunch to limit thinking time.
3. Force Deiondre to play on regret, frustration and instinct *(reverting to System 1 thinking)* subsequently putting the ‘Scissors’ suggestion on the back burner.

The lie was when Monty said he would play **‘Paper’** in the *following* round with the actual intent of playing **‘Paper’** in the *current* round.

He never told the lie to make Deiondre **believe** what he said. He wanted Deiondre to **doubt** what he said.

It looks counter-intuitive because it is.

Monty's honesty in the previous round will be viewed as a lie in the current round and because of that, the doubt that Deiondre feels will fester very quickly since this is playing off of his feelings of regret for second guessing himself and not playing Rock in the previous round.

Monty begins a calm but slightly sped up 4 second countdown instead of the typical 3, 5 or 10 for two reasons.

1. It instills a sense of urgency
2. It prevents Deiondre from catching on to what Monty had been doing by utilizing the *unfamiliarity* of the countdown to distract him.

This was done to limit thinking time via overstimulating Deiondre's brain *(cognitive loading)*.

Deiondre can't focus on three things at once and because he deems all three equally necessary at various points in time, Deiondre ends up blanking due to how quickly he's switching between thoughts.

He ends up having to play a card and frustration compels him to be frantic -> regret compels him to grab rock ->  and instinct compels him to do so without conscious thought.

This is **System 1** thinking at play.

Note: In the moment, Deiondre *unconsciously* believed that halting the game wasn't an option so he didn't exactly have a choice regardless.

**Third Layer** of the tactic:

1. Play first three times in a row due to perceived arrogance.
2. Lose due to said perceived arrogance
3. Act nervous.
4. Tell Deiondre to play first in the following round and draw his card back to subtly push the narrative that Deiondre was cheating in Reina's mind *(context bias)*.
5. Let Deiondre think he's planning something *(context bias)*.
6. Let Deiondre himself become unsure of the card Monty's holding.
7. Use Deiondre's suspicions to incite Reina to act.
8. Re-establish ‘Scissors’ as a viable option.

Monty played three times back to back in quick succession because he let the audience think that the two earlier wins had gotten to his head. Him accepting Deiondre's proposal to pick cards at random from then on reinforces that image of arrogance.

When Monty lost the first round, he intentionally repeated the tactic from the second round of the previous layer to lead Deiondre into believing that he was still feeling confident he could trick him; further consolidating his perceived arrogance.

When Deiondre wins the second round, Monty started to show concern at the start of the third round with his delayed reply + unsettled look in response to the question he was asked *(to Deiondre it seemed like Monty had begun thinking he might lose actually lose the game),* along with tapping the desk impatiently.

Monty pushing his stacked cards forward and telling Deiondre to play first didn't seem off initially. It was when he pulled back his cards and suddenly seemed adamant that Deiondre comply with his request. To Deiondre it seemed like Monty was making a desperate attempt to trick him into going first so Monty could swap out his card depending on what Deiondre played.

Deiondre then suspects Monty of having **"the Ultimate Hand"** and becomes wary of him, because if he has that card, Deiondre has to choose his own carefully or he loses.

The doubt Monty had instilled in Deiondre in the previous two rounds fully blossomed right here because now Deiondre thinks of him as a shrewd/cunning player at the game *(context bias/schema violation).*

 This is not to be mistaken with him thinking Monty himself is shrewd/cunning.

This is how Monty determined that Deiondre had scissors in his hand. Deiondre not playing at all was noted *(if he had paper instead of scissors he'd be guaranteed a draw at the very least)* and taken as an indicator that he was afraid of losing since Monty went out of his way to establish a subconscious pattern of **“Paper, Scissors, Paper”** play within Deiondre which triggers his instincts.

Deiondre trusts his instincts. It's the very reason he turned the game into blindly *‘guessing’* what to choose like an actual RPS game instead of guessing what to choose from the *provided* information in the first place.

Monty realizes this and plays on it by positing information in such a way that Deiondre's instincts tell him that something is wrong. *(Schema Violation. Monty continuously goes first then suddenly stops and demands Deiondre do the same, which caused Deiondre to instinctively suspect something is up when coupled with Monty's actions. This schema violation was also meant to highlight the* ***‘Scissors’*** *suggestion)*

Because of this, Deiondre starts to question if Monty wanted him to play a specific card (scissors) so that if Monty beats it in the current round with **"the Ultimate Hand",** Deiondre himself would win the next round by default with the remaining one.

This quickly leads Deiondre to question Monty's motives. He asks him if he'll play the card he has and when Monty implies that he's unsure if he should, Deiondre amicably encourages him to play first this round as well.

Reina picks up on this like Monty had expected her to and starts suspecting Deiondre of trying to cheat *(cognitive + contextual bias).* She posits the evidence of Deiondre suddenly winning three rounds back to back after Monty had gone first in all of them.

Reina believed there was really no way to explain the streak when Monty had dominated the first two games so easily; so the whiplash of seeing Monty suddenly on the back foot in conjunction with her not wanting him to lose was bound to make her voice her thoughts.

Because of that, the other two spectators started to question Deiondre's legitimacy. They didn't really pay attention to any of those things initially because they lack the information on Monty that both Deiondre and Reina possess. To them, it was just a normal game between two strangers.

Even though their doubt was to a lesser degree than Reina's, this frustrated Deiondre because he, like anyone else, did not like being falsely accused. Thus, he tells everyone who wasn't playing to butt out after saying he had no reason to cheat.

Monty knew Reina wouldn't listen to Deiondre and that she would advise Monty to not play first because of the cheating allegations being pinned on Deiondre.

The game was effectively put into a stalemate that had three outcomes, each being unfavorable for Deiondre.

A) If Deiondre won another round after Monty went first then they'd be convinced he was cheating.
😎 If they wait out the game then his refusal to go first will be placed under scrutiny and they'd come to believe he was cheating.
C) If he goes first he risks losing the game due to lack of information.

Deiondre eventually gave up on waiting for Monty to play first because of the first two options and decided to take a chance by going with the third.

Monty had wanted Deiondre to go with this option from the start so that he could get the outcome he had desired from the beginning of the game. Monty wanted to ensure that he gets to that outcome by seeing which card Deiondre plays first and then respond accordingly.

Monty does that and draws the second game like he intended, losing 4000№s like he had planned.

Basically, Monty created a situation where Reina was left nothing but Satisficing *(Satisficing Theory).*

**Further explanations:-**

Satisficing Theory goes like this: *"settling with an answer* ***that is good 'enough'*** *rather than searching exhaustively for* ***the absolute best choice****."*

In the end of the third tactic, Monty made Reina unable to determine for certain whether or not Deiondre was cheating by pushing his cards forward and covering the last card Deiondre had post-victory. When she looked at Monty's behaviour again, she also realized that he had never affirmed or denied whether or not Deiondre was cheating.

Add that to her hearing Deiondre out at the end for why he lashed out, and her settling with "good enough" was really the only thing she could do. Although, this little interaction actually boosts her image of Deiondre since it shows that he's trustworthy even in unfavorable situations and rightfully lashes out when wrongly accused (this is inline with Reina's own psychology so she feels he's relatable)

\_\_\_

Schema Violation occurs when new information or an experience contradicts a person's existing mental framework (schema) about how the world works, disrupting expectations and often sparking curiosity or discomfort.

For this, Deiondre had already profiled Reina as someone who gets angry when people do stupid things and if they're close enough then she will hit them (*he never sees when she refrains from hitting Monty because that wasn't done in public)*

Since that is the established Schema, Monty takes advantage of it to disrupt what Deiondre perceives as "the truth" by inducing a "truth" that Deiondre never had the privilege of seeing.

Thus, this new experience fosters cognitive dissonance within him and leads to him doubting Reina's intent. Since her actions are directly violating the established Schema (profile).

So basically it's a matter of information asymmetry.

The schema Violation didn't end there tho. Monty's tactical layers THEMSELVES are a Schema Violation.

In the first two, he was overtly inducing mistakes *(Deiondre thinks this is how he is. Monty acting arrogant by accepting Deiondre's guessing game proposal reinforced the image of blind overconfidence).*

In the third, Monty was covertly manipulating everything **\[this was only possible because the first tactic had been successful\]** by using body language and open ended wording that can be interpreted multiple ways by different people depending on how they perceived him.

Deiondre thought Monty was trying to be crafty at the end so he was convinced Monty was planning something.

Reina thought Monty was afraid of playing so she reasoned he was it was because Deiondre was cheating -> She believes Deiondre was cheating because his win streak only became possible when Deiondre initiated the Guessing Game -> She concludes that Deiondre was DEFINITELY cheating because Monty always played first for the three rounds Deiondre had won.

But she cannot prove Deiondre was cheating because Monty hadn't actually said that's what he thought. That was just her interpretation of the situation. Deiondre had a valid crash out when she viewed it all in retrospect *(without seeing his final card)*

The third tactic couldn't be avoided at all once Deiondre fell into the first one or even if he evaded the second one because all the groundwork was dependent on the success of the first.

**Further explanations x2:-**

The second tactic is actually simple psychology. Monty just overloaded Deiondre cognitively by forcing him to think of 4 things at once.

But since people can't think about 3 things simultaneously, he was forced to task switch between the four thoughts Monty had forced him to think:-

1. "I'll be playing paper next round" is not relevant because Monty is talking about a moment in the future, not the present.

2. "Time is running out" the unfamiliarity of the countdown simulates a genuine time crunch while somewhat disabling pattern recognition (count from 4 - 1 slightly faster than normal)

3. "What will I play this round?" Deiondre was thinking about this, but because of the other two factors in addition to regret bias, hindsight bias and frustration, he could not use his time effectively.

4. “I regret not playing Rock last round” Rock is the most common first move for players, especially for beginners or those who make a “panic” or quick, unthinking choice. Him lingering on the thought further misaligned his priorities.

Thus Monty forced Deiondre to rely on instinct -> but since that instinct was influenced heavily by Deiondre's emotions -> he played Rock and lost.

Hindsight bias (now that the situation has ended in a draw, Reina has time to look back on everything leading up to it and realized that Monty never actually did anything that supported her suspicions at all. And Deiondre wasn't acting as suspicious as she though \[she misread his caution and sudden display of skill for cheating because Monty had begun losing immediately after this happened\])

**Key Note:** Monty did not simulate the outcome of this game in advance. He also did not predict all of the actions each individual would take **PRIOR** to the implementation of a layer.

In layer 1, Monty ONLY predicts what Deiondre would think and do after he'd gotten Reina involved. Reason being, Reina herself had 3 separate options:

1. React passively (least likely)
2. Flair up and hit him (likely)
3. Flair up but don't hit him (likely)

Depending on the option she chooses, Monty will be able to assess Deiondre's thought process and would've adjusted the tactic accordingly.

The reason Monty involved Reina is because nothing **Monty** **says** specifically holds any weight in Deiondre's mind. Deiondre will interpret whatever ***Monty*** says as unreliable and trust his instincts like he initially intended if Reina had remained passive or had actually hit Monty.

Seeing as Reina DIDN'T hit Monty like Deiondre had expected, he started to second guess the validity of Monty's words *through his belief in Reina's actions*.

Her not hitting Monty for stupidity is an anomaly in the sense that Reina may have been trying to cover for Monty. Her not hitting him was **not** taken as her slipping up.

Reason being, Reina had immediately voiced a coded message to Monty after she slipped up. THIS satisfied Deiondre's expectations of Reina's intelligence *(that she wouldn't slip up)* and led Deiondre to the wrong conclusion concurrently.

If she had hit Monty and NOT tried to rectify the situation, then that would have seemed suspicious as well.

If she had hit Monty AND tried to rectify it afterwards then Deiondre would still find it suspicious. Because, either way, he sees her as intelligent.

And even though it does not violate the pattern of her hitting Monty for making a mistake, had she made that choice to hit him, there is a high POSSIBILITY she would tell Monty to change his card which would've landed Deiondre back at step 1: **Figure out which card Monty will play.**

If that happened, Monty would've done something else to get Deiondre to lose in the second round. Because now that Deiondre's trust in Reina's reactions was established, Monty would proceed to violate that trust in the next round by playing on it in a non-obvious way *(like his initial first tactic)*
