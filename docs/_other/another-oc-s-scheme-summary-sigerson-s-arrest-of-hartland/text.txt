Another OC's scheme summary

Thought I might do a short summary of my OC's scheme this time :)

**CONTEXT:  Sigerson is currently in a cat-and-mouse battle with Elysius Blight, one of the Custodians (the elite of the country), and has the opportunity to arrest Hartland, one of Blight’s henchmen. This is how he does it with limited resources even as he’s being targeted by the Enforcers (secret police figures) from a false charge (all of this was planned while before he even reached Sodor):**

1. The first thing he does is get his cousin (an actress) to disguise herself to prevent people from recognising her. Of course, if someone did recognise her, it would be trouble for him as well, since it is public knowledge that they are escaping the Enforcers together. After she’s perfectly disguised, they go in trains that are set for completely opposite locations. When Sigerson arrives in Sodor, the first thing he does is purposely reveal himself where he has many secret escape routes (the city is like London or something like that with a lot of alleyways, etc). This is so if the Enforcers suspect a diversion (because he revealed himself so obviously after a long time of hiding), they won’t be able to catch Evelyn, who is disguised, nor can they catch him even if they want to, since he has many escape routes unknown to them. Grock (the Enforcer’s director) decides that this sudden reveal by Sigerson is a diversion, so he searches elsewhere with no result.
2. Next, Sigerson needs allies to help him with the next few tasks, since he has no money, no resources, etc. He also has a few goals to accomplish being: 1) Arrest Hartland 2) Evade Grock 3) Evade Blight, so he doesn’t intervene to help Hartland 4) Inform Grock about Blight, so Grock will go after Blight instead of him Sigerson has an old acquaintance who is from the criminal underworld and he knows that this criminal would be willing to help him, so he sneaks over to the gambling den the criminal frequently goes to (the guy’s name is Turner).
3. He meets Turner and persuades him to help but with one condition: he must help him recover his debt. This also works out perfectly fine for Sigerson, since he would need money anyways to buy food, etc, so he looks for a person within the gambling den to scam. The person he finds is a confident, proud gambler who has made quite a fortune through gambling, so naturally, Sigerson challenges him to a game of simple card memorisation. The gambler doesn’t refuse because of the crowd’s pressure (which Sigerson already knows) so he agrees to play. The game is very simple; they merely have to look over 4 groups of cards and memorise the groups, but Sigerson set this game up knowing it would be easy to cheat by marking the cards. He lets the gambler handle the cards last (so Sigerson cannot alter any marks after him), and pretends to keep losing every round. He then makes a bet to the crowd, guaranteeing their earnings if he loses, so when the final round comes, he stops pretending and fully memorises the cards during the last round, gaining sufficient money for Turner’s debt and for any other expenses. *CONTINGENCY: Even if Sigerson lost, he could’ve blackmailed the gambler, since he knew what method of cheating he was using (making fingerprint marks on the cards).*
4. After gaining enough money, Sigerson seeks out another accomplice using Turner’s influence, this time leading to a boy named Harvey, who is an expert thief. He then tells Harvey (who has his own mini strategy) to rob Hartland’s residence to increase his paranoia. During the robbery, however, Sigerson prevents Hartland from notifying Blight by slipping a piece of evidence that connects him (Hartland) and Blight. Hartland is then unable to contact Blight due to fear of being killed, since somebody knew the connection (since he knows Blight would not hesitate to kill him to cover up evidence).
5. Now that Hartland’s paranoia has increased, Sigerson prepares the next step, which involves another trap at another gambling den. He previously profiled Hartland and knew that he was an impulsive gambler, so he set up the gambling den beforehand; when Hartland arrived to gamble, the tables were all filled, leaving only one open (the tables were filled with people Sigerson had already hired with the money they got from robbing Hartland’s home). This forces Hartland to go to the seat Sigerson planned for. There were a bunch of mirror-like objects (windows, glass lamps, etc) around the table, set up by Sigerson. The mirrors would encourage Hartland to cheat (Sigerson already knew Hartland used this kind of cheating method before, so it was safe to assume he would attempt it again), which Sigerson could use to his advantage. He then made Turner play against Hartland (who was cheating), lose, and blackmail him for cheating, telling him to meet up at the Crown Hotel at a specific time. *CONTINGENCY: Even if Hartland never showed up at the gambling den, Sigerson could always blackmail Hartland using the evidence between him and Blight. This would be a bit riskier though, since they couldn’t arrange a specific “blackmail” location, unlike the gambling den blackmail*  Sigerson then gave this information to the Enforcers through a bunch of intermediaries, since he was in no position to perform the actual arrest himself. The people he sent to deliver this information to Grock were all civilians, high ranking Enforcers, or just generally trustworthy people (that Sigerson knew of). This was to make Grock reason this out: Why would Sigerson use high-ranking Enforcers even though it would be riskier than low-ranking-ones? → The higher ranking ones are more loyal. → Why would he choose the more loyal ones? → There is someone within the Enforcers leaking info.
6. After the place and time was prepared, Sigerson further increased the possibility of Hartland going to the place as promised by leaving behind a real gun, giving Hartland the opportunity to silence his blackmailer (the gun is real, but there are replicas of it that are not, so to ensure that Hartland does not really shoot, they intend to make a last minute switch if needed). Sigerson then had an abandoned apartment block set up so that he could witness the Enforcers entering the Crown Hotel on time to arrest Hartland during his confrontation with Turner. *CONTINGENCY: In the likely event that Blight already knew that Sigerson would be waiting there to watch the scene unfold, he prepared a simple flour trap. The four trap is basically a bag of flour tied to the ceiling with a bunch of fuel on the floor, so that when the floor falls to the ground and gets into the air, a fire from his torch would ignite and light up the entire apartment room, attracting the Enforcers’, people’s and police’s attention. This happens so if Blight sent people to kill him, he could make an escape and have the Enforcers get the henchmen, since there will also be a crowd of people.*
7. After Grock has arrested Hartland (which weakens Blight’s influence since if he wants to replace his pawn, he’ll attract attention), he will know that Sigerson has accomplices. Sigerson knows this of course, so he switches accomplices again to confuse the enemy, and after negotiating with a famous assassin gang, he earns their help. The next step of his plan is to escape Sodor, because he has successfully sent Hartland to jail (though indirectly), but there’s a problem: the entire city is surrounded by the Enforcers after Grock stalled time and reorganised them.
8. To escape past the cage of Enforcers, Sigerson decides to create a crime that looks as if Blight were the one controlling it, sends protection to Blight’s real target, and clears a path through the Enforcers so he can escape the city. Even if Blight wanted to stop the ‘fake crime’ set up by Sigerson or claim it wasn’t him behind it, it would never work, because that would be the same as exposing himself.
9. Sigerson sends the Eiter and his gang (the assassin gang leader is Dexter Eiter btw) to several ammunition factories and to set up bombs secretly, because Blight would most definitely want to destroy the ammunition factories. This is why (Blight’s thoughts): I manipulated two countries to fight because I want to cause suffering between them → the war is very one-sided at the moment → if the war is one-sided, it will end quickly, which I do not want → I have to weaken the stronger side in order to prolong the war → destroying ammunition factories will weaken the stronger sides resources and capability to fight
10. Some of the bombs are fake, but some are real, so with these bombs set up in the factories, when Grock goes to investigate, he will be uncertain and be stalled for even longer. This move by Sigerson is still not enough for Grock to retract his all the Enforcers however (only a few are needed to search for the bombs since the bombs won’t explode for sometime so there’s plenty of time), since Grock still has no idea where he is. Sigerson then sends messengers to Grock (Eiter’s gang disguised as factory workers) and eventually to provide him with enough data for Grock to deduce that Sigerson is also investigating the factories sabotage secretly nearby. This is the data Sigerson gives through the gang: 1) A man with the exact description as Sigerson was found lurking nearby 2) A footprint was found that could be used later to confirm if it belonged to Sigerson 3) A letter with a deduction using information only someone near the factory could obtain in such a short amount of time (the letter is given very quickly after new pieces of information were uncovered so it was plausible to think that Sigerson had some way of getting the information extremely quickly, either in person at the factory or through an accomplice at the factory, which was a lot more unlikely due to the fact that no communication method was available. In short, this letter confirmed Sigerson’s presence at the factory)
11. Now that Grock knows approximately where Sigerson is, **he loosens the security around the city in order to close in on him**. After Grock reads the letter and as he defuses the bombs Sigerson set up through the gang, Sigerson makes him deduce this: There are a ton of bombs hidden around the ammunition factories, both real and fake → The bombs are also set to go off after a very long time → This all points to a diversion as a person wanting to sabotage the factories would not act like this → Who is the main target? → Mr Paulos is the owner of the factories and is the sole thread connecting Sardis (Grock’s country’s ally) and Ecclesia (Grock’s country) together → If Mr Paulos is assassinated, Sardis will no longer ally themselves with me, leading to us losing a lot of military power → We will also be put into Blight’s control, because Blight controls many coal factories → Therefore, Blight has a motive to kill Mr Paulos → Mr Paulos is the target

This leads to Grock sending back-up Enforcers to act as additional guards to protect Mr Paulos (who is on a journey), which is what Sigerson wanted, so if Blight did plan to kill Mr Paulos, he wouldn’t be able to do so without using a lot of effort.

1. Grock then goes to where Sigerson had been staying (Grock actually just went to his accomplice’s place, which was given by Hartland whom he had arrested) and finds a footprint there that matches the one (Sigerson’s supposed footprint) at the factory (the footprint also had unique marks so it was impossible that two exact shoes existed in the world, meaning the footprints at the place were definitely Sigerson’s (or at least what Grock thought). This, along with a few strands of bleached black hair, an obvious misdirection attempt at making Grock think he would be escaping by ship, and a warm stove (which meant that someone was there not long ago) were set up so Grock would think that he (Sigerson) would be escaping by train with his hair bleached blonde as a disguise.
2. What happened in reality was that when Grock retracted the Enforcers from the cage he had created around the city (**the bold words above**), Sigerson escaped by cab. The cab had nobles seated in it to make it less suspicious, while Sigerson hid in a tiny compartment right beneath the floor of the cab.

So this is the scheme that Sigerson devised to arrest Hartland, provide Grock with a lead on Blight, and escape at the same time. This is only a base for the scheme I am writing for the future though, which will be connected to this scheme as one single chain of schemes.
