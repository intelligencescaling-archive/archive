Obliterating arguments against antifeats

I will obliterate them so decisively, nobody with an IQ over 114 will continue to think anti-feats are useless or stupid.

Argument 1: What is the point of including antifeats? Why not include the best feats alone?

Your anti feats determine the limits and truth of your abilities. They prevent headcanon/overhyping, exaggerating, taking metaphorical statements as literal like powerscalers always do, and they distinguish the character's consistent intelligence from plot armour moments.

As an example, Kamishiro in Tomodachi Game supposedly has the "God's eye" that allows him to see through things, read peoples minds, and look at the future. But it is because of his antifeat at the end of the arc that it was proven his eye was FAKE and he was only pretending.

If you ignore the antifeat that disproves his ability and assume he can actually predict the future and see through things, you're going to judge him based on a false assumption.

Antifeats are just limiting explanations that clarify something's true potential. A feat inflates it, an antifeat prevents the inflation from going out of control and becoming illogical by the reader's interpretation. They prevent cherry-picking and biased scaling.

As another example, Paul crying after his first kill serves to clarify that the countless emotions he experienced was not on the level of a true experience. This debunks the misunderstanding that 'he can never be fazed emotionally and he has infinite AC because of the infinite emotions he experienced'.

Argument 2: What if the character does an antifeat but later overcomes it?

This is called growth. The character becomes more intelligent, or overcomes his weakness. To give an example from Reverend Insanity, Fang Yuan trusts someone due to desperation in one of the volumes, which is an antifeat that results in a huge problem. However, later in the novel he experiences a similar situation but he learns from his past experience, which he explicitly states. He grows more intelligent, but that still means his past self committed an anti-feat, only that his future self outgrew the weakness. A feat can become an ANTI-anti-feat if there's a logical reason to believe the previous anti-feat was overcome/negated.

Argument 3: What if the anti-feat is only on the surface?

Then it's no longer an antifeat. For example Ayanokouji gets manipulated by Horikita, eating her food so that he owes her a favour. But from the way he acts throughout the novels and what precedes the event, it's clear Ayanokouji most likely lets himself get manipulated. How can it be considered an anti-feat if he knew what was happening and wanted it to happen?

Or for another example, Fang Yuan in chapter 55-57 "suppresses his shock" and everyone in the room is able to see it. The reality is, Fang Yuan only pretended to suppress his shock to make them think he sucks at acting. So it was only on the surface that he was bad at hiding his expressions. He did it on purpose, so it counts as a feat rather than an anti-feat. If there's proof that an anti-feat was done on purpose to achieve another goal, it is no longer an anti-feat and may even become a feat.

Argument 3: Context matters.

Facts 💯

As an example, Fang Yuan says "the road to the future will be very interesting" to his brother after his own talent is revealed to be C rank. This ends up becoming a point of suspicion when the security recordings are checked years later.

But the context is: Fang Yuan experienced an insane psychological standoff for 6 hrs, then spent the whole night planning with only 2 hrs sleep after regressing to his powerless child body, then walked through icy waters with his body drenched, while being mocked and sneered at on all sides by the people watching. Even after all this, he only whispered it to his brother while pulling him up, with his back facing the rest. And even then, the phrase is ambiguous and does not compromise him greatly, which is shown when the phrase is misinterpreted later in the novel.

Or when he trusted someone and got betrayed, the context was that he was in a desperate situation and didn't really have many alternative options.

So context definitely matters, and it can reduce or increase the impact of an antifeat.

Argument 4: What if the antifeats are overcome by a feat?

This is also possible. For example, Yuuichi Katagiri could NOT beat Satone in a gamble, which he knew, because of her superior intuition and luck. This antifeat only proves that Yuuichi Katagiri's intuition + luck is less than Satone's intuition + luck. However, he instead came up with a plan so that he wins the overall game even if he loses the mini gamble against Satone. So while he may be worse at gambling than Satone, the feat he did proves that his overall capability is higher than Satone's due to his outsmarting, acting, deception, and manipulation.

Argument 5: What if a smart character acts a lil retarded sometimes?

This is definitely possible. Antifeats do not completely cap someone as retarded. They only provide clarity about their true abilities.

For example, Isaac Newton lost a significant amount of money in a type of stock investment. This anti-feat does not prove that he is suddenly limited to a mere fool, but rather it clarifies that he is prone to error, whatever the reason may be (overly trusting, cannot predict future, etc.)

Argument 6: Antifeats can be misused.

This is true. Conclusions for anti-feats need to be tame. If someone uses Newton's antifeat to conclude he's a fool, they are stretching it.

Antifeats are for deflation, not complete limitation. If someone tries to flip heads on a coin 100 times and gets 95/100, the 5 serve as antifeats that show the person isn't *perfect* in intuiting coin flips, but the 95 show that he is significantly above other people.

These are the main arguments I've seen so far. If there's something I missed, give it to me so I can obliterate it too and prove once and for all that antifeats are not useless.

Tldr: Antifeats need to be considered, but they should not be misused.
