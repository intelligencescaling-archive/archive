Important:
To properly understand the analysis and feats presented later in this document, it is essential to first understand the protagonist's time-loop ability and how it functions.

The time-loop ability is a supernatural curse that traps time within a single calendar day. Only the original host of the curse, Makoto, can advance the timeline. To do so, he must cause the death of at least one person before the end of the day. If he succeeds, time progresses normally to the following day. If he fails, the timeline resets to 00:00, the beginning of the day, erasing all events that occurred during the previous loop while allowing Makoto to retain his memories.
The curse is infectious, but only when a reset occurs. If the day ends without Makoto causing a death and another person is in direct physical contact with either Makoto or the curse's secondary host at the exact moment of the reset, that person also becomes infected. Once infected, they retain their memories after future resets and become bound by the curse. However, if Makoto successfully advances the timeline by causing a death, the curse cannot spread, regardless of physical contact. Furthermore, infected individuals cannot advance the timeline themselves. Even if one of them kills someone, the day will still reset unless Makoto is the one whose actions directly caused a person's death.
When a reset occurs, infected individuals do not return to the locations they occupied at the beginning of the day. Instead, they remain at the exact location where they were standing when the reset happened, even though the rest of the world returns to its original state. For example, if an infected person begins the day at home but is standing in a hospital when the reset occurs, they will reappear in that hospital after the reset rather than at home.
Another property of the curse is that anything an infected individual is physically holding or touching at the exact moment of a reset is transported into the new timeline together with them. This applies to both objects and people. Rather than creating duplicates, the transported object or person is removed from the previous timeline and transferred into the reset one, meaning duplicate versions never exist.
Finally, the curse recognizes indirect causality. Makoto does not need to personally kill someone. As long as his actions are the direct cause of another person's death before the deadline, the condition for advancing the timeline is fulfilled.

Before the deadline expires, he secretly obtains the hospital's floor plans and evacuation routes, leaving them in his home so that, once his three-day plan fails and the police search his residence, they will discover them. He predicts that when he later announces a bomb threat, the police will remember these materials and interpret them as evidence of prior preparation, making the threat appear completely credible. Thus, the hospital operation is never intended to replace his original investigation but instead serves as a backup if Makoto's automatic killing mechanism prevents him from proving the existence of the time loop. Ultimately, Tsukumo's fears prove correct. The three days expire without a single reset because Makoto's mechanism successfully causes the timeline to continue advancing each day. As a result, Tsukumo fails to convince anyone of his theory. Already framed as a criminal, he ultimately escapes from police custody, even taking Mizuno hostage, further destroying his reputation in the eyes of the authorities. With his original plan having failed exactly as anticipated, Tsukumo immediately proceeds with the contingency he had already prepared. Since he knows his reputation has been completely destroyed, he also knows that any extreme action he takes afterward will be considered believable by the police. Using this to his advantage, Tsukumo reports that he has planted a bomb inside the hospital. Given that he is now considered a dangerous criminal, both the police and the hospital staff treat the threat as genuine. As a result, every patient - including Shiori - is evacuated from the hospital. Tsukumo deliberately arranges for the evacuation to take place shortly before midnight. His plan relies entirely on the mechanics of the time loop. If Makoto does not cause anyone's death before midnight and the timeline resets, Shiori, as an infected individual, will not return to her hospital room. Instead, she will remain at the exact location where she is at the moment of the reset. Because she is in a coma and is being transported outside the hospital, she would be left helpless in the middle of the road after the reset, placing her life in immediate danger. Tsukumo intends to use this situation to force Makoto into killing him so that the timeline advances to the next day and Shiori survives. However, Tsukumo cannot know with certainty whether Makoto's automatic killing mechanism still contains victims. Instead, he also bases his plan on Yae. Tsukumo reasons that Yae has never had the opportunity to speak with Makoto after his arrest and therefore cannot know whether the mechanism still has anyone left to kill. Even if she somehow knew it existed, she could not know how many victims remained inside it. Tsukumo also concludes that the mechanism must have been constructed only after Yae disappeared, when Makoto began preparing for the possibility of being arrested. Consequently, Yae could not possess detailed knowledge of it. From this, Tsukumo predicts that if Yae learns Shiori's life is in danger, she will sacrifice herself to indirectly cause Makoto to fulfill the condition for advancing the timeline, thereby saving both Makoto and Shiori. Before revealing the details of his plan, Tsukumo first observes Makoto's reaction. When he tells him that Shiori is being evacuated from the hospital, he immediately notices fear and realization in Makoto's expression. From this reaction alone, Tsukumo concludes that Makoto's mechanism has already run out of victims. If it had not, Makoto would have had no reason to react with such alarm. Even if this deduction proved incorrect and the mechanism still contained victims, Tsukumo could simply remind Makoto that Yae does not know that. He would reason that Yae would still sacrifice herself to ensure Makoto survives. Knowing how deeply Makoto and Yae care for one another, Tsukumo concludes that forcing Makoto into a situation where Yae dies because of him would psychologically devastate him. In Tsukumo's eyes, it would be equivalent to Makoto killing Yae himself. He believes that such emotional pressure would inevitably cause Makoto to kill him, which would still achieve Tsukumo's objective by ensuring Makoto is convicted of murder. Even if Makoto somehow endured Yae's death without reacting - which Tsukumo considers impossible - Tsukumo also has one final measure. Instead, he would directly threaten Shiori's life, making it clear that if Makoto refuses to kill him, he will kill Shiori himself. Faced with the certainty of losing either Yae or Shiori, Tsukumo believes Makoto will inevitably choose to kill him. As Tsukumo predicted, Makoto ultimately attempts to kill him. Shortly afterward, Makoto learns that Yae has committed suicide. Overwhelmed by grief after losing the person he loved most, he completely loses control.
