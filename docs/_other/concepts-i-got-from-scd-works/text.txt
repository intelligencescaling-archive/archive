Concepts that I got from SCD works

(in no relevant order)

\#1. Akuma no Shoumei (Umineko)

Also called "Devil's Proof", it basically says that it is impossible to prove a negative statement, because you would need to search the entire space, which normally implies having infinite amd perfect knowledge.

The common example is that it is easy to show that a devil exists: just show them the devil.

But proving that it \*doesnt\* is impossible, because even if you havent seen it/havent seen evidence about it, you can always say that you didnt look hard enough, or it is actively hiding his traces.

I think that the strongest claim is that evidence of the \*negative\* is astronomically superior to the corroborative evidence, so the paradoxical lesson is that you should work with what you haven't, instead of what you have.

\---

2. The Magician's Choice (Usogui)

Other media must also have this concept, but the first time I saw it was in Usogui.

This is the idea that a magician will let you \*think\* that you chose something in free will. But they secretly guides you to their preferred choice.

\---

3. You Only Think About What You Can Perceive (Moriarty  The Patriot)

Its in the first mystery-solving case about a serial-killer that kills child beggars (or poor children in general? I dont remember).

Moriarty gives the ingenious logical jump that if the assassin kills these kinds of people, then they can see them frequently, because you can only think of things you perceive frequently.

This scene flashes into my mind constantly from time to time.

\---

4. There is Always Multiple Solutions, Think For Yourself. (World Trigger)

You might think that World Trigger is too battle focused to be SCD, but it has a lot of strategic and political maneuvers. Enough that it being atleast low tier is an uncontesteable fact.

These concepts are very basic, something that any self-help slop could say, but the emotional impact of it being said by a character that strikes me with awe in every appearance makes it hit harder than normal.

And, for that reason, this dialogue always flashes into my mind when my mind gets trapped between options, or when I think I cant do it alone.

\---

5. Review Your Games (18loh)

Another basic one, but I admire Chen Ran so much that it sticks more than it should.

The scene that is specifically glued in my mind is from the end of first instance and the result of the Infinite Killing situation.

His influence made me try to copy this self-reflection habit when possible, and it only grew in me as I indulged in competitive games like Mobas and Chess.

\---

6. Everything is a Narrative (TsukumoJuku's Philosophy/JDC/Maijoverse)

(This is partially my interpretation from Maijo's reimagining, Reviews from Seiryouin work, and "Let's Read" from a certain tumblr user. This is because the JDC universe is untraslatable)

This got me demotivated the first time I noticed it.

The gist of it is that Juku Tsukumo is a detective that does not particularly \*care\* about the truth.

If a case is impossible, too outlandish, or the solutions would be too absurd, he simply lies and gives a compelling narrative that explains everything.

This has a certain nihilistic tone that the truth doesnt matter, but it started to mature on me as the idea of "everything is a narrative" and bayesian updating got clearer throught the time.

What I mean is that forces any belief you have to be held just as it is -- a belief.
