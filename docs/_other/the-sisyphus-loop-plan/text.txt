Analysis of this clue: Erik Erikson's theory of psychosocial development outlines eight stages of life.
Stage 1: Trust vs. Mistrust (Infancy: Birth to 18 months) – Children learn to trust caregivers based on how well their basic needs are met, leading to the virtue of hope.
Stage 2: Autonomy vs. Shame and Doubt (Early Childhood: 2 to 3 years) – Children gain a sense of personal control and independence, developing the virtue of will.
Stage 3: Initiative vs. Guilt (Preschool: 3 to 5 years) – Children assert power and control over their environment through play and interaction, fostering the virtue of purpose.
Stage 4: Industry vs. Inferiority (School Age: 6 to 11 years) – Children cope with new social and academic demands, building competence and the virtue of confidence.
Stage 5: Identity vs. Role Confusion (Adolescence: 12 to 18 years) – Teens explore their independence and develop a sense of self, leading to the virtue of fidelity.
Stage 6: Intimacy vs. Isolation (Young Adulthood: 19 to 40 years) – Young adults form intimate, loving relationships with others, resulting in the virtue of love.
Stage 7: Generativity vs. Stagnation (Middle Adulthood: 40 to 65 years) – Adults create or nurture things that will outlast them, often through family and work, gaining the virtue of care.
Stage 8: Integrity vs. Despair (Maturity: 65 to death) – Older adults reflect on their lives and feel a sense of fulfillment or failure, acquiring the virtue of wisdom.
The antagonist's life, like other humans, also follows this framework, though his knowledge of said framework advances him to the 5th stage much earlier than others. This will be a recurring theme as the novel further develops.
