SCD MEDIA TYPES IMO

*Warning: This is not meant to say X is not TRUE SCD or vice versa, whatever. It's just a simple distinction based on the idea that most characters/series in the SCD community aren't made for SCD reasons and complementing  on that idea*

There are common talking points I've heard from time to time, such as how most series that are scaled and ranked aren't made for SCD reasons, or how a character isn't primarily written for SCD/outsmarting reasons. Most media in SCD aren't actually SCD or made for it.

Despite believing that most of this is wrong, there's an underlying shared trait that is true for other reasons when considering what makes a series have SCD elements: how much wit, scheming, and outsmarting it contains. This leads to conclusion that presence of  good outplays and high competence that can be analysed is mostly what is scd is. This means a variety of works can be included in SCD overall, from Beyblade and Toy Story to Project, DDLC, etc., despite them looking like non-SCD media at the surface level.

If any media has explainable wit, outplays, and intelligence-wise feats, it can be included in the SCD community.

Due to this ,SCD  can include many types of genres and stories  like animaga - shounen ,ln , fantasy, movies, sci-fi, detective, thriller, action, etc.

Now, with that information and definition, let's divide SCD into TWO types.

**TYPE - I**

These media are, in fact, made for SCD (SCD media). The media cater to an SCD/intelligence-seeking audience only, since the authors know there's demand and a target audience for it.

They prioritize and put overall logic above storytelling. The whole process and methodology are laid out to be seen, judged, or directed accordingly.

In scaling systems, they would have very excellent methodology for scaling and ranking (both strict and non-strict) and brilliant feats. The whole scaling could suffice with methodology without going into other areas. Normal scaling-wise, they are also good.

This can be further divided into two camps:

1. Background

Authors who have a background in game theory, mystery, and logic, but are only part of certain traditions/trends, such as escape rooms, closed-room mysteries, cat-and-mouse, fair-play mysteries, whodunit variations and whydunits, Honkaku mysteries, spy and espionage story lineages, etc.

2. Non-background

These are works made by authors who taught themselves game theory, mystery, and logic but don't have any background or lineage. These authors write the work for the genre they want to write. The works can be slow in their introduction, as the authors are trying to get their footing in the craft while writing and developing the storytelling.

**TYPE - II**

These media are not made with SCD in mind. The media cater to a certain genre and target audience, whether because of the medium or the trends at the time.

They prioritize the story over possible logic errors and use wit and outplays to advance the plot, tell the story, and characterize characters. Oftentimes, they don't focus on SCD elements the way we want them to.

In scaling systems, they sometimes have decent methodology (both strict and non-strict). However, they are perhaps really good at normal scaling. Their feats aren't necessarily mind-blowing but are still impressive when pieced together within the story.

This can be further divided into two camps:

1. With intention

The works/media have wit and feats with the intention of showcasing character(s)' abilities and telling details story-wise. The series has aspects related to wit/outplays as central themes or as one of its themes, serving as thematic points that contribute to the overall themes. This is simply catered to their preferred type of writing, where the outsmarting style is important.

2. Non-intention

The works/media have wit and feats, however, the author/writer didn't have any idea that said character is accidentally cracked in SCD when you take everything in the story into account. It was simply catered to their preferred type of writing, where the outsmarting style isn't necessarily important.

**NOTE:** Initially, I thought about dividing them into SCD Prime, SCD-lite, and SCD-like, taking the terminology from how people discuss gaming, particularly Soulsborne, Souls-like, and roguelite games. However, Type I and Type II were sufficient in conveying the distinction simply, which is why they were selected. Plus, people would probably abuse terms like SCD Prime, SCD-lite, and SCD-like.

# TAKEAWAY

It is important to see the characters and their respective works in an unbiased light and not be biased or dismissive.
