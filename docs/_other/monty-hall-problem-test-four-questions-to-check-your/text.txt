Monty Hall problem test (4 questions to test your understanding)

I want to move away from this topic, but just like complexity systems, it's so counterintuitive that most people simply can't understand how it works. It's really pretty simple when you've understood how the problem works beyond just knowing an answer that you've memorized without understanding the theory.

**Rather than the switching behaviour, you need to understand that the problem is about how probabilities are shifted!**

Why don't you take a guess at these 4 questions to see whether you understand how the Monty hall problem works?

**Question 1:**

There are 3 doors. 1 has a car. 2 are empty.

You decide to select the first door. The host suddenly reveals something: "I will reveal an empty door between door 1 and door 2."

The host then reveals that 2 is the empty door! Now he gives you the opportunity to switch. Will you switch to door 3 or stay at door 1 if you want to maximize your chances of winning the car?

Does switching affect the probabilities? Is it better to switch or to stay? Answer the question then check the spoiler yourself.

Correct answer: >!Stay! Most people who memorize the answer to the Monty Hall Problem without understanding how it works will choose to switch here. If you picked switch, you don't understand how the monty hall problem works and probably memorized the answer without understanding the concept deeply. There's nothing wrong with that, but you shouldn't be running around giving your opinion on whether the probabilities are valid for a particular character or not because you clearly don't understand how it works unless you didn't read the question carefully. Staying maximizes your chances at 2/3 while switching reduces it to 1/3. The key point in question 1 here is that the host revealed the empty door between 2 and 1 rather than between 2 and 3. If the host instead said "I will reveal an empty door between door 2 and door 3 (or between the doors that you did NOT choose)," the probability would have been 2/3 for switching, but in this case the probability is 2/3 for staying! This is assuming things like the host being truthful, not exploiting a loophole or employing trickery, etc., obviously. Also, the host doesn't even need to say what rules he is following out loud, the probability in hindsight differs depending on his internal decision of what to remove the empty door between (either 1 and 2 or 2 and 3), even though the external choice (revealing 2 as empty) is the exact same result in both scenarios.!<

**Question 2:**

Same premise as before, you pick door 1 and the host opens door 2, revealing it to be empty.

However, internally, the host only decided to pick one of the empty doors (1, 2, or 3, whichever is empty) randomly regardless of whether you had chosen it or not, and regardless of what number the empty door appeared at, and it just happened to be door 2 that he revealed as empty.

Is it better to switch or to stay?

Correct answer: >!Either! 50-50 whether you switch or stay. The host is not forced to reveal from among the ones you didn't choose, so him picking door 2 was a mere coincidence irrelevant to your choice! !<

**Question 3:**

There are 3 doors. 1 has a car and 2 are empty. You pick door 1. The host decides to reveal an empty door between the 2 you did NOT pick (in this case between 2 and 3) and he would have followed this rule regardless of which door you picked. However, he does not announce what rule he was following to you. Is it better to switch or to stay in hindsight?

Correct answer: >!Switch! Staying means 1/3 probability of getting the car and switching means 2/3 probability. This is the classic monty hall problem where the host is restricted by those specific rules I mentioned.!<

**Question 4:**

Same premise as the previous one EXACTLY (classic monty hall problem), except this time the host can decide himself on whether he reveals an empty door between the ones you didn't pick OR just stays silent.

You pick door 1 and the host doesn't stay silent, he suddenly reveals "I will remove an empty door from between door 2 and 3" and the host thus reveals 2 as empty, so everything on the surface is the same as the original monty hall problem but this time the host chose to reveal door 2 as empty by choice rather than by being forced due to the rules. Is it better to stay at 1 or switch to 3? Does switching affect the probabilities or do nothing? Think of the answer carefully, then check the spoiler when you are ready.

Correct answer: >!Stay! If the host suddenly revealed that fact and could otherwise have stayed silent (such as if you hit an empty door), it is probable that he is trying to get you to switch to the wrong answer so that he doesn't lose the car! If we assume the host wants you not to win the car, then the probability of switching would have resulted in you getting nothing 100% of the time since the host would only have revealed the option for you to switch if you actually were about to win the car. !<
