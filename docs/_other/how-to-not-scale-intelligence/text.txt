How to NOT scale intelligence

1: Make a doc where you focus on spamming categories for every instance and action rather than expanding upon the feats to clarify the validity and depth of the feat. What a relatively useless doc. I'd rather read the novel than read 99% of the docs out there. If your doc doesn't make the feat easier to understand (such as 18LoH docs linearizing the timeline to make it easier to understand) OR expand on easily overlooked depth OR condense the feats that determine the character's scale without fluff to make it more efficient than going through the story itself, your doc is almost certainly useless.

2: Scale a character by the quantity of their average human level feats rather than focusing on the peaks after realizing that there are normal humans who would be just as capable of performing the feats you are overly glazing. You can have a million normal human level feats due to the quantity of extended media but this won't help you scale high compared to a severely lower quantity of higher quality feats, especially narratively, but also methodologically.

3: Wrap your entire life around a single character to the point where your entire mode of scaling is determined entirely by whether or not the character is glazed. Neither using Narrative Scaling because your fodder character gets horribly victimized by dozens of mainstream "statement merchants" NOR using methodology scaling because characters largely perceived as "fodders" by mainstream scalers (Akiyama, Tokuchi, Baku) either come too close to or surpass your fave! Unless you are consistent and objective without glazing, extremism in modes of scaling can be far more logical than striking a balance.

4: Discarding someone else's entire scaling based on unrelated nitpicks about them such as whether they are considered a virtuous or evil person under your subjective moral framework!

5: Being inconsistent with how you treat the logical validity of different feats. For example calling a particular feat illogical or unrealistic or an antifeat, then jumping to the defense of another despite both being equally subjectable to that same criticism.

6: Fake reading. Overly extrapolating. For example assuming that because a character is top 1 in methodology, they would struggle in narrative scaling even though it should be obvious that an author who is smart enough to make a character top 1 in methodology is likely competent at keeping their narrative intellect intact as well. Another example would be seeing the growth within 5-30 chapters of a webnovel character and assuming the growth remains as linear throughout the rest of the story, being overly confident that you can cognize the intellect of the character without reading it. This is especially true in narrative scaling where a single paragraph can turn a mid tier character into an extreme tier character.

7: Policing the mode of scaling people use (Narrative vs Methodology). This goes both ways. If someone says they have Johan > Light, your immediate reaction should not be "But Light is more realistic and has better methodology so he low diffs that unrealistic offscreen fodder!" because it's clear and obvious that the person is scaling them both narratively. Similarly if someone gives a tier list where they are obviously using methodology scaling and are ranking the characters by their extradiegetic methodology, your immediate reaction should not be to berate them for scaling something different to what you scale, but rather to understand their purpose/standard. Reflect and think!

8: People who get angry and threatened and irritated at the truth, turning off their minds from understanding anything too complex or counterintuitive when it goes against their previous ideas, arguing for the sake of arguing, people who dumb down the opponent's argument to treat it like a joke, people who strawman every time they are corrected despite knowing they are misrepresenting an argument beyond just a simple misunderstanding, people who pull up any point they can from thin air like an AI to argue against every point, point by point, rather than accepting what they know is correct and refuting what they know is incorrect.

9: If you get annoyed because all these new characters that drop your favorite verse's relative position pop up (such as the Chinese ones) rather than rejoicing at the fact that the intellectual ceiling of fiction is experiencing uptrends, so you intentionally look for points to hate on them without having seen the works yourself (such as by claiming it has bad writing without reading a single chapter or arguing irritatedly that they probably don't scale as high as your fave without having enough information to make this judgement), this kind of aversion to truth is unintelligent behaviour.

10: If you hate something because it feels "bad" rather than actually being "incorrect" so you refuse to try and understand it because it contradicts your core biases/beliefs, thus closing off your mind off to it and treating it as a joke or invalid without pondering on why and reflecting deeply before deciding on your stance (an example of this could be "evil" characters that scale high but you intentionally downplay them), you are being closed minded! Closed mindedness is not as simple as "Not supporting everyone being able to do whatever they want as long as they don't directly harm other people without their consent."

11: Jumping to conclusions when there isn't sufficient evidence. Abduction, induction, and adduction is useful, but you don't live in the fictional world for causality to become so oversimplified that every conclusion with some backing that you pull out of thin air becomes the truth. This is the primary flaw with many SCD stories as well. If the narrative clarifies they don't think on such a granular level that the alternatives and strategic flaws fall within their consideration, the success in reasoning can become hard-carried by luck and causality oversimplification. "I think that you think X, but you probably guessed I think this way, so you'll think Y instead which I'll counteract by thinking Z." for example. If the opponent is not exactly that smart, or if they are slightly smarter, they'll just think on a different layer and your entire understanding flops hard. Imagine how badly some of your favourite characters' cold reading feats would flop if they operated in real life.

12: Overrelying on one particular standard or aspect when determining how high a character scales. For example rigour for methodology scalers or system complexity for narrative scalers. Both might be relevant and important to consider, but the wider context is far too significant for that to become what the matchup hinges on, especially for system complexity (regardless of how you define it) which has almost 0 correlation to the result in most matchups.
