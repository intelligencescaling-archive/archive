I think a core thing we should do when scaling characters and see who does something is scale them by situations. You can adjust all your categories yip yap sure, but AFTERWARD...I think it's best say:

"OK, I think x character beats y...now let me think of a situation where they face each other and how I think x would win".

If you CAN think up the situation and have it make sense logically(of COURSE without saying "they just win") then sure! Go for it!

But if you can't...then you should honestly realize you arent scaling off of anything concrete.

I'm not saying you should literally formulate a whole strategy FOR them...but based off what you SEE them do..put them in situations(based off their own two verses ONLY, as adding a bunch of different situations creates alot of chaos honestly) and based off what they did in each situation justify if they could win.

Now a final big mistake I think people do when doing this...is they assume there is only one main route possible. If you think your character could find ANOTHER way to win than what happened in the situation from the story you're using to compare them...then sure!

For example(just throwing this out there)... I could think up Hannibal beating Kanade in Danganronpa Another 2 Case 3.

My reasoning would be he could reason through all of her Misdirections given his acute senses throughout the series and novels, granted the same amount of evidence Sora has he should be able to solve the case in my eyes.

But THEN I'd go one step further and say he wont go the main route of accusing both Kanade + Hibiki. He could accuse Kanade alone! As (via his surgery experience) Hannibal should be able to realize that just because two people stabbed the same person...via Danganronpa rules...the person who stabbed more vital organs SHOULD instead be the true culprit.

Via that, Monocrow would HAVE to reveal more information to help narrow down the culprits further or the case is unsolvable thus the game ends!

"This is headcanon"..sure..but it's justifiable headcanon. I believe Hannibal can see through Kanade via everything pointing to the fact that he can! I believe Hannibal's established knowledge of surgery counters Kanade's double culprit strategy!

You could also flip the situation to Kanade visiting Hannibal for therapy...psychoanalyzing him(via her psychoanalysis feats on Syobai) to find out he's evil and requesting a search warrant of some kind. Of course it would make sense that Hannibal realizes she does this and kills her off before she gets a chance.

I think going by situations makes it all the more inventive and useful, compared to mere categories. I think categories should be the stepping stone and THEN we assess by situations primarily with justified explanations to support why we think that. This nullifies impossibilities such as characters "no diffing" another character when I don't think that's possible in intelligence scaling.

Now some people might say "we already do this", but I don't think we do with full awareness and knowledge of WHY it should be done like this. Nor do I even think everyone does actually.

Tldr;

Categories should be the first half(appetizers) of your evaluation, then situations the main dish. You should justify why Character A beats Character B in a situation(preferably using situations from only Character A and B's verses).

For example, in a debate of Akiyama vs Ayanokoji. Categories should be the appetizer, THEN you should put in your mind a scenario of Ayanokoji facing off Akiyama in say..Musical Chairs..and claim who wins then justify why.
