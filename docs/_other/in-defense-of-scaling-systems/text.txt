In defense of scaling systems.

There are valid complaints to have about scaling systems, but the majority of the arguments I have seen against them are complete BS.

Most of the arguments are premised on a severe misunderstandings of why people even scale intelligence, or an incorrect understanding of terms like "objectivity."

The complaints primarily stem from comparing intelligencescaling to its source, powerscaling, so people scale the intelligence only in the singular aspect of how smart the character is story-wise, which is a valid goal to have, but you are severely deluded if you think this is the only aspect of intelligence that can be scaled.

Firstly, you need to understand that intelligence can be scaled under different premises, just like powerscaling.

For example, Hal with perfect memory vs Fang Yuan as an example matchup. Is this matchup a valid one to have? Hal doesn't have perfect memory, but the matchup is valid because "perfect memory" is the premise and even if a premise is not necessarily true, the overall statement can become internally true under the assumption of the premise being true.

Here's a simple math example to illustrate this:

Statement A: 5 + X = 12

Person 1: "Statement A is true, given X = 7"

Is person 1 telling the truth, or is he lying, or is it undeterminable?

The correct answer is that person 1 is telling the truth.

If X = 7, Statement A is true and Person 1 is telling the truth.

If X = 35, Statement A is false (since 5 + 35 is not equal to 12), but Person 1 is still telling the truth! Person 1's statement was conditioned on X being equal to 7, so the statement is true regardless of the truth value of statement A.

This is why a matchup like Hal with perfect memory vs Fang Yuan is a valid matchup to have even if Hal does not have perfect memory.

Just as conditions like "perfect memory" can become the premise of the matchups, so too can scaling system conditions.

The second thing you need to understand is that intelligence scaling is not just about scaling the intelligence of the characters. Intelligence is an umbrella term that encompasses many things, and methodology is one of these things that are encompassed by it.

If you are scaling the methodology of the feats in a series, you are scaling an aspect of intelligence.

Arguments like "Normal scaling is the best and scaling systems are there just for fun." make no sense because normal scaling was created to be "just for fun" as well. Its focus is actually far more on fun than methodology, since it views the work through the fictional lens rather than through a more academic game-theoretical lens.

Same with the argument that scaling systems are not objective. This misunderstanding comes from people not being philosophically adept, so they assume objectivity is simply about what they find intuitive or not, which is a very intuitive and colloquial way to think about objectivity. Normal scaling is intuitive and easy to understand, compared to a non-diegetic form of scaling like pure methodology which is detached from the narrative, so there is far less pressure on your mind to think about who would outsmart who if they were thrown in the same university than to completely detach the method from the story and evaluate the methodology of the feats non-diegetically.

Something is subjective if it represents first-person subjective experience (such as perception, emotions, opinions, imaginary objects, or conscious experiences). If a claim is true ONLY when considering the claim from the viewpoint of a sentient being, it is subjectively true. For example, one person may consider the weather to be pleasantly warm, and another person may consider the same weather to be too hot; both views are subjective.

This has nothing to do with the object of evaluation. If the object of evaluation is the diegetic intelligence of the character, then obviously an attempt should be made to see things through purely the lens of the story. Meanwhile if the object of evaluation is the methodology viewed non-diegetically, then what the feat implies about the diegetic intelligence of the character is objectively irrelevant.

The latter form of scaling is not less objective, it simply focuses on a completely different aspect of intelligence. It's that simple.

You need to try and understand what objectivity means. Objective does not mean true or false, and you are misunderstanding even harder if you think truth and falsehood comes down only to the fictional lens through which the character's intelligence is evaluated.

1 + 1 = 3 is more objective than "ice cream tastes good."

The former is objectively false. Yes, something can be objective whilst still being false. The latter is subjective.

Going back to the analogy I gave before,

Statement A: 5 + X = 12

Statement B: Statement A is true, given X = 7

You need to understand that statement B is objectively true regardless of whether or not Statement A is true. It is true regardless of whether or not X is 7. This is how methodology scaling works. It is internally true even if it doesn't match the diegetic implications of the feats.

Comparatively, statement A is either objectively false or objectively true, or indeterminable. Either way, it is OBJECTIVE.

Something more subjective would be "X person looks hotter than Y person." Even if it's true for you, it's subjective.

You need to understand how objectivity works, I swear 90% of the people here misunderstand it severely.

Normal scaling is far more subject to our personal experiences and interpretations than methodology scaling, and this makes it a more subjective endeavour. This doesn't mean normal scaling is wrong or bad. You're just scaling for fun anyways, and it isn't meant to be an objective academic pursuit, but you need to stop deluding yourself into thinking that you're somehow being objective when you're not.

So... why do people even use scaling systems? Why doesn't everyone just decide to agree on scaling the same way?

The truth is, this question applies to everyone. Why do you scale intelligence? Why not just scale power instead since it's the umbrella that intelligence falls under? Is there something special about intelligence that makes you want to scale it rather than just powerscaling? If there is something interesting about intelligencescaling that separating it from powerscaling makes it more suitable for you to scale, then you need to understand that there is similarly something about methodology that makes people want to scale it separate from how smart the character is story-wise. Of course, some people also use scaling systems only because they want to glaze their favourite character by forcing limitations on other characters, but the primary reason is because people want to scale something they are interested in (whether it is power, intelligence, methodology, looks, writing, or morality, people have their preferences for what to scale).

This is why scaling systems are not invalid, and as a matter of fact, they are more objective than normal scaling, more precise than normal scaling, more academically oriented than normal scaling, while achieving the detachment from powerscaling that conventional intelligence scaling could never achieve.

That being said, scaling can become very convoluted too quick if there are too many scaling systems. The discussions become more surface level because more breadth results in less depth. Also some people excuse their horrible takes by just saying something like "this is my personal secret scaling system" without giving any framework for how they scale to objectively determine if their takes are valid under the particular system they use.

These are valid criticisms to have, but some of the extremely common arguments I've seen about how scaling systems are just for fun and not "proper scaling" or that they are subjective, or that they only "restrict other characters" without any other purpose are nonsensical arguments that don't invalidate them at all.

Scaling systems are valid. If they aren't valid, then intelligence scaling as a whole is also completely invalid.
