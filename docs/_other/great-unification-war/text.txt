Fresh personnel immediately replace them on the frontline.

Questioning the Timeline After Waking Up
After collapsing near a village, Li Gang woke up in an old woman’s house. He asked her the date and how long he had been unconscious.
She initially said it was the 17th and that he had fainted only a few hours earlier. This seemed consistent with his recollection: he had visited Jin Huang’s cabin on the 15th, fainted there, eventually travelled away, and then collapsed near the village.
Li Gang felt something was wrong, so he asked for the complete date, including the month and year. The woman replied that it was February 17, 42058.
This exposed a calendar discrepancy. When the new calendar was introduced, the change had been announced four days before the end of year 42042. Most people treated the year as ending on the announcement date, but remote areas could still recognize the remaining four days. Since every month had 30 days, the date used locally could be four days behind the corrected calendar: February 17 would correspond to February 21.
This created a six-day gap between February 15, when Li Gang had visited the cabin, and the corrected date of February 21. Li Gang then asked when he had arrived at the village. The woman said he had been found outside her house the previous day, even though she had initially described his collapse as having happened only a few hours earlier.
He considered whether he had simply misjudged how long he had been unconscious. His injuries and prolonged lack of sleep made that possible. However, he remembered that his first collapse at Jin Huang’s cabin had occurred on the 15th. The calendar discrepancy, unexplained passage of time, and confusing account of when he had been found made him suspect that something had happened while he was unconscious.
He considered the possibility that his body had been taken over without his knowledge. He suspected that one of the two Rank 8 warriors he had fought had survived and used a method to parasitize or control his body. He believed an Emperor might be involved because he considered such an ability beyond ordinary warriors.
His theory was that the entity controlling him had deliberately delayed him. It could have taken control while he was unconscious, waited until the next day, and then allowed him to awaken only a few hours before the old woman spoke to him. Because the village used an older calendar, this would make the dates seem consistent at first and reduce the chance that he would notice the discrepancy. Distinguishing Between Two Possible Tracking Mechanisms
Li Gang considered an alternative: perhaps his location was revealed only when the entity inside him took control. The two possibilities had different implications.
If his body was permanently marked, the enemy might track him directly. In that case, returning to the cabin would not be necessary for them to find him.
If his location was revealed when the entity took control, the enemy might initially search the cabin and learn his whereabouts only after the entity awakened. They could then pursue him and leave someone watching the cabin in case he returned.
This distinction mattered because assuming the wrong mechanism could lead him into danger. If the second possibility was true, returning to the cabin could expose him to enemies waiting there. If the first was true, moving farther away might not help. Since he could not determine which explanation was correct, he had to account for both.
Deciding to Recover Rather Than Travel Far
Li Gang concluded that he needed to heal quickly but should not travel too far. His injuries prevented him from properly investigating his condition, detecting a possible mark, or defending himself. Travelling farther would consume energy and could worsen his situation. At the same time, if the enemy could track him directly, distance alone would not guarantee safety.
