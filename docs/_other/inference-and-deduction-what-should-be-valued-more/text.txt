Inference and Deduction - what should be valued more?

I'll start by listing my personal definition for an inference. As the sub has already talked about deduction before.

An inference is simply a well-educated guess or an idea of understanding based on information given and information \*\*taken\*\* there is a difference, unlike a typical deduction usually seen as an assertion, an inference is subjective to change and able to adapted and expanded easier without hurting story or narrative. My point is if the information for Y is given by X and X's information is false, the initial inference can be adapted and changed, allowing for a more fluid line of reasoning and modality to be used in this situation.

Imagine you and your friends are playing a social deduction game like among us, a popular game everyone's seen. Your friend C is the impostor, and he poisons the well giving you false information, and you make an inference that is initially wrong. This is information given to you. But later during the game, you adapt the initial framework of reasoning to fit a broader lense of logic based on anything. For example, X avoided lights being fixed or was late to a body report etc etc. You can now build up a better inference and thus arrive at a better guess or new line of action.

But a basic definition of a deduction is: starting out with what you know and logically what working what \*\*must be true.\*\* for example:

All humans need oxygen to breathe and to live (this will be true to make it easier to understand, but yes, this is general fact.)

John breathes.

It is safe to assume John is a human.

 so if C is able to give you information that's false and your deduction is either C isn't the impostor or C is safe your deduction is wrong as your conclusion is not analytically true and you COULD adapt it don't get me wrong but under this traditional and widely accepted definition it would be harder to adapt to it.

It essentially affirms the condition and affirms the result. But the condition could be false.

If C (P) gave me this information and this information is true (Q), then C is safe (P-> Q)

However, if the condition is false, then the result also has to be false. For example, if a character manipulated toward a certain result. For example, Ayanokoji with Ryuen, Light with the 13-day rule. Then, the person they've tricked now has anti-feat within their reasoning or scaling their reasoning. However, if this was inference, then it wouldn't be such a heavy anti-feat. I'm not saying guesswork>deduction, and I'm not gonna fully debate because that position is shaky to uphold anyway. This is just another opinionated post of mine for anyone to debunk, analyse, read, or enjoy.
