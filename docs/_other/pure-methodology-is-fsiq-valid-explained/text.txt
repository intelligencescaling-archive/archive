Pure Methodology: FSIQ valid? Or Invalid? Explanation.

Pure Methodology is a non-diegetic scaling of the methodologies of a character's feats. If you wanna know how it works, this post explains how: [https://www.reddit.com/r/IntelligenceScaling/comments/1vv9d2u/methodology\_scaling\_frequently\_asked\_questions/](https://www.reddit.com/r/IntelligenceScaling/comments/1vv9d2u/methodology_scaling_frequently_asked_questions/)

This post is for FSIQ in **pure methodology** by the way. This scaling isn't for most people since it's very counterintuitive and people who don't understand the purpose of scaling systems will usually struggle to even comprehend why it isn't less objective just because it has a different criteria for evaluation compared to conventional scaling of intelligence.

This is why I recommend most people stick to scaling narratively (diegetically). It's far easier and intuitive; you can act like a sophisticated intellect by larping on about how you're such an objective person who never uses systems to limit characters because putting limitations on a matchup is subjective or bad. If you can't even understand how premises can make a system internally true, how can I expect you to be able to use a system this counterintuitive?

Here's the post (it's a repost of a post from almost a year ago, with a few minor wording changes to increase the explanatory prowess and accuracy):

**Processing Speed (PSI):** INVALID.

Reasoning: processing speed is completely invalid because it is fully dependent on the timescale. As the timescale is decreased towards 0, the processing speed becomes arbitrarily large. If time = 0 seconds, processing speed = infinite. Therefore processing speed is completely dependent on the narrative and thus an INVALID category in pure methodology scaling. Scaling processing speed is valid for scaling the in-story intelligence of the character, but NOT for the methodology of their feats which is what pure methodology focuses on! Processing speed can be considered one of the most important categories in conventional scaling of intelligence, but it is completely irrelevant in methodology.

**Working Memory (WMI):** the working memory required to understand the explicit logical structure of the character's feats with its distinct variables explicated is VALID. However, the narrative magnitude of their working memory implied story-wise from the feat is INVALID. Only the explicated structure affects the scale of the character's working memory. In this case, a character parallel processing a hundred homework questions and solving them all simultaneously scales lower than explicitly being shown to solve a problem that requires a specific working memory capacity to solve.

Reasoning: the reason why narrative magnitude is invalid for evaluating the methodology non-diegetically is because similarly to processing speed, it can be made arbitrarily large just by changing its magnitude without any effect on the explicit logic. For example "a hundred equations ran through his mind" can be replaced with "a million" or "a trillion" or any higher number that is arbitrarily larger without any effect on the actual reasoning or logic that can be derived and analyzed outside the bounds of the story. Thus it is INVALID. Only the working memory required for understanding the explicit logical structure of the feat is VALID.

**Verbal Comprehension (VCI)**: only the direct verbal competence shown by the character through their explicit words are VALID. An example of this is L using Japanese grammar to trick Light while interrogating him at the cafe. Another example is Chen Ran purposely using double negatives in his speech to avoid telling an objective lie while speaking. Both of them are VALID. However, narrative verbal comprehension such as the scope of knowledge of a character or how many languages they know is INVALID.

Reasoning: Same as the arguments from before, narrative VCI is INVALID for non-diegetic scaling of feat methodology because it can be upscaled to infinity without changing the logical structure. For example a character knows 2 languages vs a character knows 99999 languages vs a character knows infinite languages. Since it can be narratively upscaled infinitely, it does not contribute to the character's VCI in methodology scaling.

**Visual Spatial Index (VSI):** only the spatial reasoning contributes and is VALID. The visualization itself is only narrative magnitude and thus INVALID.

Reasoning: a character can be said to have visualized an apple. And then a universe. And then a multiverse, and then visualized it down to the tiniest atom. This is a completely narrative claim without any significant change to the explicit logical structure of the feat from a non-diegetic lens. Thus INVALID. Only the visual spatial reasoning contributes. For example, a character is told that the distance between the bottom edge of one side and top edge of the opposite side of a room is 20 meters, and that the length of the room with the prior mentioned edges at the ends is 12 meters. then using the laws of trigonometry, he concludes that he needs to build a ladder of height 16 meters if he wants to climb onto the roof. In this case, it would be VALID and scale higher than something like visualizing a multiverse. (just a random simple example, obviously it's not a great feat)

**Fluid Reasoning (FRI):** VALID. Not how good they're narratively supposed to be at reasoning, but rather their actual reasoning feats logically evaluated. The opinion of the author or character is also irrelevant, since only the methodology matters. For example a character could have poor reasoning, but coincidentally arrive at the correct answer because the author said so. This does not upscale the character, you could even argue it downscales them if the reasoning is bad enough and the success probability can be analyzed as low, with superior alternatives.

There are more subcategories, but this explanation should allow you to extrapolate the standards onto the rest of the subcats and decide for yourself whether it can be considered valid or not. I don't even use conventional categories when I scale feat methodology, I prefer categories like \[ methodological complexity, rigour relative to complexity, premise interconnectedness, etc. \] but if you prefer to use conventional categories, this post helps to identify the validity of specific ones.

I personally switch between narrative scaling (conventional scaling of intelligence) and pure methodology scaling (non-diegetic scaling of feat methodologies) depending on who I'm talking to. This opens up room for the most discussion. It's not some kind of agenda against narrative, I am even a narrative scaler, I use narrative scaling in discussions with 70% of people (and also pretty much 100% of debates) because that's what people tend to find intuitive, so I operate within their framework (kinda like speaking someone's language because he cannot speak yours).

By the way, this post should for the most part apply to common methodology scaling as well. However, it does NOT apply to proven ability or normal scaling.
