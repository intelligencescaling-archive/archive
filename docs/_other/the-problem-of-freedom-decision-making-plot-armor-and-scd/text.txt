The problem of freedom/decision making

In SCD, it's pretty obvious that some characters make outright insane strategies in such short amounts of time. For example, Liar Game's author spends at least a week or even more coming up with the ideas for each arc, but Akiyama figures out loopholes and solutions in mere minutes. Now you might say, "yes that's obvious, they're SCD characters so they're geniuses". But are the conditions for these strategies, tactics, plans, etc really that straightforward to understand? Think about it; you probably know that these characters have to be aware of the possible threats, unpredictable circumstances, other people, and sometimes even their own limitations. But when the characters does win because of a solution that they create, people don't realize that a whole lot of plot armor is included in the process. Theoretically, if an Author made a character that takes over the world/outsmarts some world leader(like many SCD characters have), why doesn't he just replicate the plan in real life? Technically, if there was no plot armor and these characters really did outsmart their opponents, why doesn't the author apply those methods to real life?

You might've already found the solution; it's the conditions, as I was previously talking about. As I've said, these characters have to analyze their conditions and surroundings and make plans from then on. So, the authors can't replicate these strategies and plans that they make for the story in real life, because the conditions are different. It's like if an author writes a story about some smart guy making a time machine; the author will probably come up with some fake, fictional blueprint to explain the construction of the time machine. And obviously, we don't consider the author a supergenius, because he didn't even invent a time machine; he created a bullshittery plot convenience moment to keep the story going, which was the time machine's blueprint, because it obviously is just for the story and won't work in real life. If it did, then the author could've made it.

Now, why the hell am I yapping about all this? Well, think about this; if the author created a fictional solution to make a black hole, the the SCD Character's author also created a fictional solution to their character's problems in the story. Which means that a character's solutions, thinking process, and anything related to their mental abilities could possibly be outright plot armor. If you're having a hard time understanding, think about this; when an SCD character outsmarts in a situation, there's always some convenient element in their situation that lets them win. For example, the people around them could be just stupid enough, their surroundings might have just the right amount of specific conditions beneficial to them, and their opponent might have just the right amount of weaknesses to let them win. But in real life, it's nothing like that; the real world is unpredictable, with multiple opponents, surrounding conditions, and people with different personalities.

Think about how the characters in these verses are more corny and cliche sometimes. How Yamauchi was screaming when he got expelled in COTE, how the average COTE student seems stupider than the average person in real life. Think about it; do you really believe a normal person from real life would have that same reaction like Yamauchi? Sure, they will be shocked, sad, mad, confused, but definitely not screaming and shouting and trying to throw things. For a real life person, they know it's not that serious. Real life people have more aspects to their thinking process, to their observations, and to their perception of things in general. You might consider some fictional verses to be harder than real life, but in reality, the real world is a way colder, harsher, unpredictable, and dangerous environment than a lot of these fictional verses. Anything can happen in real life, unlike in the stories.

So what does this mean? Well, when we scale characters and compare them against each other, we tend to put them in a realistic situation. Like the real world. But, would a character's strategies and plans really work in the real world, where no plot armor nor convenience exists? And don't even get me started on unrealistic characters, we all know they're just pure bullshittery for most of their thought process. Like for example, let's say there's two people, one fictional and one real. Both somehow start a drug empire that evades all government systems and authorities. Now, we can all agree that both are smart for pulling that off. But which is smarter? You might think it's equal, because they did the same thing, but it's actually the real life person that's smarter, and for the same reasons I've been stating; real life is harder than fiction. Authors cannot replicate the infinite unpredictability of real life into fiction, and plot connivences will always exist so that the character can conveniently win and the story can continue.

Now don't get me wrong, I'm not complaining. The stories are still awesome and I have no problem, but this is just an interesting thought I've had.
