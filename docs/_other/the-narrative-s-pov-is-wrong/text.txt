“Narrative POV is wrong” explained simply.
Before starting, Narrative POV is defined as: the interpretive stance in which the story is approached as if it were a separate and epistemically autonomous world.
Problem number 1: Violation of evaluative coherence.
To talk about intelligence, we deductively require our causal system due to the dependency problem.
Both to assign value and to identify variables, a reference is necessary, and that reference can only be our causal system.
A causal system is a set of variables and the rules that determine how those variables influence each other.
Everything we call “probability”, “intelligence”, “plan success”, “coherence” depends on how causes produce effects.
Consequently, it is not possible to claim that something is “intelligent” because it considers certain variables, and at the same time claim that it is not less intelligent when it omits other relevant variables.
Example with the Memory Loss Plan.
Person A says: “it is intelligent because it predicts the behavior of X, Y and Z” and person B responds “yes but it cannot predict the behavior of Q, M and P”.
As person A, you CANNOT respond “Yes but Death Note is not realistic so it doesn’t count”, because the same variables that you initially used to evaluate the intelligence of the plan ARE based on “realism”.
Now I use the term “realism” but what I mean is “a causal system coherent with ours”.
What scalers almost always do is consider ONLY the parts of causality that would boost the character’s scaling, ignoring those that go AGAINST it or heavily downplaying them, when (ex ante) they are just as valid as the “PRO” ones.
________________

Problem number 2: Arbitrary management of causal incoherence.
Now we ask the question: “Can the causal system of fictional works be entirely incoherent within the work?”
The answer is: yes, it can have such incoherence since it is produced by a fallible agent (a writer, a human being).
There are two ways in which a causal system can be “broken”:
2a- “what causes what” is broken. Interaction
2b- “how much something causes something” is broken. Magnitude
In fiction, the vast majority of cases fall into the second case, not the first.
3- The more this magnitude is distorted, the higher the causal incoherence, the more: either we are measuring something that is not intelligence OR we are making a VERY specific choice about what we accept in terms of causal incoherence (which I will now explain).
Now I will try to summarize (even though in the original document they are explained in a much more exhaustive way) the main ways in which magnitude breaks:
Mode 1: a capacity that is pushed to extreme levels would be causally connected with other things, which however are not pushed at all in the same way.
Example: absurd working memory > absurd planning and reasoning.
Mode 2: within the capacity itself there are absurd inconsistencies of skill that would be impossible in a causally coherent system.
Example: someone with absurd cold reading abilities (like PJ), and then fails to notice a detail that would be trivial relative to those abilities.
Mode 3: correlations (which are VERY likely causally connected, but for which we do not have tangible proof) between abilities are completely ignored.
Example: in our world, someone with 160 processing speed is EXTREMELY likely to have at least “good” levels in other abilities such as VCI, FRI, WMI, etc.
The point is that regardless of how this causal incoherence manifests, scalers will always choose the best results while ignoring the clearly inferior ones.
This no longer becomes “scaling a character” but “scaling SOME characteristics of a character in SOME scenes”.
In essence: if we accept that a character, given their previous feats, instead of having to do 100 (as would be causally coherent with our world), does 1,000,000, despite 1,000,000 being causally incoherent, and we keep 1,000,000 as a valid result, we are making a deliberate choice to ignore causal coherence.
Ignoring causal incoherence, however, ONLY in this specific circumstance, that is ONLY when the character’s scaling would increase.
“But just because instead of doing 100 they do 1,000,000 does not in itself mean there is a causal problem. It could simply be that the causal system of the fiction is different from ours.”
Let’s even concede and say “Well, if the goal is to scale and compare fictional characters, if everyone has this advantage, what’s the problem”.
The problem remains because the advantage is almost NEVER coherent, and if we consider cross-verse it is not even proportional.
This is because it would require two things:
1- that intra-verse causality is coherent.
So that every time something should do 100 according to the causality of that verse, it does 1,000,000 within that work.
Extremely implausible due to “authorial impossibility” and “practical arguments”.
2- (given point 1 as true) that inter-verse causalities are proportional (if they get the magnitude wrong, they do so by the same amount) AND coherent.
So that every time something should do 100 according to the causality of all verses, it does 1,000,000 in ALL verses.
If the first was impossible, this becomes “exponentially impossible” for obvious reasons.
In essence: if based on previous information B should get 100 according to our causality, but gets 10,000, C should have the same advantage, which almost never happens, especially if from a different verse.
In abstract: if we want to make inferences about who would win in an intelligence matchup, the only usable field (as shown in point 1) is our causal system. But using our causal system, two things happen:
(fundamental) 1- magnitude incoherences occur in the characters’ abilities, often very significant, which heavily impact scaling (therefore they cannot be treated as simple “rounding”), since it is extremely likely that characters have magnitude incoherences — relative to our reality — that differ significantly.
(much more secondary) 2- intra-character scaling incoherences occur, which creates a problem of identity: “is it really possible to estimate what a character is capable of while accounting for significant fluctuations in magnitude (often occurring at different times), without at the same time making the mistake of drastically overestimating or underestimating their abilities?”
Considering these issues, ignoring such incoherences and allowing characters with much higher magnitude deviations to be advantaged constitutes an arbitrary choice.
Problem number 3: Assumption of causal validity of the result.
The Narrative POV is potentially fallacious at a logical level.
Let me explain better: when dealing with complex systems, or complex solutions, and only the result is shown, it is implicitly assumed that such result is causally and logically valid.
Of course, from a human perspective, everything becomes a big question mark, but it is factual that the more extreme the claim, the higher the probability of running into causal logical issues — this time not so much in the abilities themselves, but in the possibility of the result itself.
Example: L, at 7 years old, invests in the stock market and obtains a 20,000x return in 2 years (with no further explanation other than “access to a computer”).
________________

Problem number 4: Loss of generalized inference.
Due to the dependency problem, when those abilities are “inflated” without also inflating all the effects that such inflation would entail (e.g., 10,000 working memory > extremely complex reasoning > off-scale planning ability — I am obviously simplifying), the meaning of that ability itself changes.
In essence, you lose the generalized inferential capacity with respect to that ability EXCEPT in the WAY in which it is shown.
But at that point, what counts as the “way in which it is shown”? Then a lot of methodological problems arise.
For example: to define specificity, do we refer to the structure of the problem? etc.
This would require a methodological assumption that is not universally justified.
Example: you can have one person saying “Look, Sherlock has not really demonstrated much in planning, but since he managed to simulate an entire city in his head, which requires extraordinary working memory, this ability is causally convertible into planning” and another saying “No, it remains only pure calculation ability”.
And here I want to clarify: neither of the two is “universally” right — both would be right within the methodological choices adopted.
________________

So, in essence, the Narrative POV is based on:
1- the assumption that everything is causally coherent
2- that such causality is coherent and proportional across every verse
3- the acceptance of a double standard in evaluation (real-world causality is used, but sometimes yes and sometimes no)

PERSPECTIVE PRESENTATION

Narrative Perspective (or “Narrative POV”) Explanation

The narrative perspective is the interpretive stance in which the story is approached *as if* it were a separate and epistemically autonomous world.

Metaphorically, one may describe it as “seeing the story with the very eyes of the story itself.”

Aside from other purposes (personal enjoyment of the story and so on) the aim of this work will be to examine whether this POV is methodologically flawed (or not) for the evaluation of fictional characters’ intelligence and cognition.

The step to step scheme of this document will be the following:
1- proving how there is a methodological dependence (Dependency Problem) - meaning it can’t be epistemically autonomous
2- how from such dependence a structural incoherency problem arises (Causality Problem + Authorial Impossibility Args)
3- how *empirically* the chances of such a problem are relevant (Practical Problems + Unrealistic Capability Problem + Auth Imp Args)
4- how deductively, even with few intra-verse problems, this methodological approach collapses (Overlapping Problem).
________________

The Overall Thesis of This Work:
Using the narrative's POV, as a perspective used to evaluate intelligence and cognition, has extremely high chances of producing results *not* evaluating such qualities but instead other variables (such as aesthetics, dramaturgical effects…).

CAUSALITY PROBLEM

Causality Problem Definition.

The Causality Problem says that the Causality Systems within each moment of each fictional work (if such systems exist that is), have extremely high chances of being incoherent with each other.
________________

Reminder:
causality systems aren’t just about what is “possible” or not, and so about having or not having 0% probability. They are also about probabilities within the 0-100% range.
Meaning there are 3 ways a causality system can be incoherent:

1- Violation of impossibility — something occurs even though, according to the previously established or implied logics, it should have been impossible. This one is decently common.
2- Direct probability incoherence — in nearly identical scenarios, the same action yields very different outcomes without explanation. Obviously really rare.
3- Indirect probability incoherence — in structurally similar or analogical situations, events happen with outcomes that contradict what the established system would predict. This is far more common.
Example: a character is described as having an extremely high Working Memory, yet in practice their reasoning remains basic and trivial, far below what such cognitive capacity would realistically allow.
________________
⚠️Don’t forget that the Causality Problem is *probabilistic*.
⚠️Don’t forget that Causality System’s incoherencies can be within the 0-100% range.
________________

Before diving into the arguments, let’s clarify a few foundational concepts.

CAUSALITY SYSTEMS
Causality systems regulate how “things” (agents) within these systems have to work. You could see them as functions, determining how the world operates.
This means that they regulate 2 fundamental aspects (especially for what concerns us, and so cognition and intelligence):
A- Interactions. They determine the logic behind “what causes what”, so only the mere existence of the relationship.
B- Magnitude. They determine “what is caused by what”, it basically regulates the “weight” of the “result”.
________________

A derivative form of empirical observation that emerges from the interaction of these two traits can be called “Frequency”.
Frequency revolves around questions such as:
1. How often does a given interaction occur?
2. How often does a specific magnitude follow a given interaction?
3. How often does a certain interaction arise after a given magnitude?
________________

CAUSALITY ENVIRONMENT
Definition: the set of agents operating within a given causal system.
________________

Pay attention: since we specified that CS’s (Causality Systems) functions are defined by these two factors (interactions and magnitude), an incoherency in even one of these factors results, by definition, in a causality incoherency.

Authorial Impossibility Arguments

Definition: arguments that use, as their common basis, the fact that a fallible agent (the author) built the stories.

a. Authorial intent deeply diverges from causal coherence.
The primary goals of authors are almost always aesthetic-personal meaning-commercial-entertainment based.
In most cases, these objectives are indifferent to causal coherency, on the contrary they increase the chances of incoherency.
Even in the best case scenario, (intent is perfectly in-line with causal coherence), biases would still arise.

b. Authors generally lack a comprehensive understanding of how our Causality System operates.
This becomes especially evident in cognitive-intel based fields, given their complexity and constant nuance.

c. Even if they had such knowledge, a constant and coherent application of it would require exceptionally high intelligence.

Practical Problems

DEF: The Practical Problems are a set of empirically-grounded reasons (drawing from Cognitive Science and the common behavioral patterns of authors) that explain why an author is extremely likely to fail at constructing and maintaining a coherent system of Interactions, Magnitudes, and Frequencies.

1 – Lack of Causality and/or Correlation Coherence (main point)
When authors construct their characters, three common scenarios arise:
(1) they lack sufficient knowledge of how real cognition / intelligence operates (and mind you again, this is an *insanely* hard topic);
(2) they understand it but choose to disregard it; or
(3) they understand it but cannot maintain consistency because the narrative requires extraordinary or unrealistic elements.
As a result, correlations and causation between different cognitive / intelligence capabilities and their expected outcomes are frequently ignored.
One of the most recurrent themes of would be:
Point A) Certain capabilities being much weaker than others, just because they are much “harder to write”, even though these “others” heavily correlate or are straight up caused by the first ones.
Classic case: working memory through the roof, but not one single reasoning the character makes (on-screen) ever backs it up, even though WM is strongly correlated with and (partially) the cause of it, in real life.

Point B) “Easy to write” capabilities being extremely fluctuating (within the same field).
Classic case: pattern recognition being so strong to the point the character can just look at a person for 1 second and would be able to make the most accurate profiling one could ever make. But at the same time he has dozens of other situations where he cannot mysteriously do the same.
Why this is the case:
1- authors can’t just keep the audience filled with incredible performance from the character, so in order to build emotion they keep the consistency at bay
2- the more they “blow it big” the more likely these situations are to come.
Point C) “Easy to write” capabilities being insanely fluctuating (outside of the same field of expertise, but within others that are semi-analogical).
Classic case number 1: pattern recognition being super strong, but only within a specific field (strictly to profiling people, but stops being so strong for finding strategies).
Classic case number 2: detective with insane divergent thinking (when rediscovering strategies culprits used) but with little to no showing of such skill when having to make strategies of his own.
Why this is the case: a mix of dramaturgical effects + hard to write skills are usually required.
2 – System Manipulation (main point)
Complex systems can be implicitly and silently simplified at will (or even unwillingly of course).
The more variables a system should require, the more likely it is that fiction will simply ignore them.
Prime scenario where this happens: non-fixed scenarios. Non-fixed scenarios, due to their very nature, lack (or mostly lack) the explicit “rules/variables” that constitute them (due to the complexity of such systems).
Obviously not all non-fixed scenarios are the same (some have more explicit rules, some have the few explicit rules being really important meaning the complexity is lower, some in which the few explicit rules are not important meaning the complexity is higher and so on).
In such systems, authors will just need to create the illusion of taking into account variables. Suspension of disbelief will do the rest.
Obviously this can easily happen in fixed scenarios too (though this is less frequent).
Classic example: the typical “genius” chess match, that when is on-screen is actually revealed to be pretty lame compared to what the narrative portrays it to be. (The Mentalist, Classroom Of The Elite, Code Geass and so on).

因果性問題
因果性問題の定義
因果性問題とは、各フィクション作品の各瞬間に存在する因果性システム（もしそのようなシステムが存在するならば）が、互いに非整合的である可能性が極めて高いことを指す。
注意：
因果性システムとは、単に「可能か不可能か」、すなわち確率が0％かどうかだけの問題ではない。それはまた、0〜100％の範囲における確率についての問題でもある。
したがって、因果性システムが非整合的になる方法は3つある：
1- 不可能性の違反 ― 先に確立または暗示された論理によれば不可能であるはずのことが発生する。これは比較的一般的である。
2- 直接的確率の非整合性 ― ほぼ同一の状況において、同じ行動が説明なしに大きく異なる結果を生む。これは明らかに非常に稀である。
3- 間接的確率の非整合性 ― 構造的に類似、または類推可能な状況において、確立されたシステムが予測する結果と矛盾する出来事が起こる。これははるかに一般的である。
例：
あるキャラクターが極めて高いワーキングメモリを持つと描写されているにもかかわらず、実際の推論は基本的で些細なものであり、そのような認知能力が現実的に許容する水準を大きく下回っている場合。
⚠️因果性問題は確率的であることを忘れないでください。
 ⚠️因果性システムの非整合性は、0〜100％の範囲内で起こりうることを忘れないでください。

因果性システム
因果性システムとは、そのシステム内に存在する「もの」（主体）がどのように機能しなければならないかを規定するものである。
それらは、世界がどのように作動するかを決定する関数として捉えることができる。
これは、2つの根本的な側面を規定することを意味する（特に本論、すなわち認知と知能に関して重要である）：
A- 相互作用（Interactions）
 「何が何を引き起こすのか」という背後の論理を決定する。すなわち、関係性の存在そのものを規定する。
B- 大きさ（Magnitude）
 「何が何によって引き起こされるのか」を決定する。基本的には、結果の「重み」を規定する。
これら2つの特性の相互作用から派生する経験的観察の一形態は、「頻度（Frequency）」と呼ぶことができる。
頻度は、以下のような問いを中心に成り立つ：
1. ある特定の相互作用はどれくらいの頻度で起こるのか？

2. ある特定の相互作用の後に、特定の大きさがどれくらいの頻度で生じるのか？

3. ある特定の大きさの後に、特定の相互作用がどれくらいの頻度で生じるのか？

現実では、ワーキングメモリは推論と強く相関し、かつ（部分的に）その原因であるにもかかわらず、である。
ポイントB)
 「書きやすい」能力が（同一分野内で）極端に変動すること。
典型例：
 パターン認識能力が非常に強力で、キャラクターが人を1秒見るだけで、考えうる限り最も正確な人物プロファイリングを行えるほどである。
しかし同時に、なぜか同じことができない状況が何十も存在する。
これが起こる理由：
 1- 作者は、キャラクターの驚異的なパフォーマンスで観客を常に満たし続けることができないため、感情を構築する目的で一貫性を犠牲にする
2- それを「大きくやりすぎる」ほど、そのような状況が発生しやすくなる
ポイントC)
 「書きやすい」能力が（同一分野の外にありつつ、半ば類推的な他分野内で）狂ったように変動すること。
典型例その1：
 パターン認識能力が非常に強いが、特定の分野内でのみ（人物プロファイリングに限定され、戦略を見つける場面では急に弱くなる）。
典型例その2：
 犯人が用いた戦略を再構築する場面では驚異的な発散的思考を示す探偵が、自身の戦略を立てる必要がある場面では、その能力をほとんど、あるいは全く示さない。
これが起こる理由：
 劇的効果と、書くのが難しい能力が必要とされることの混合である。
2 – システム操作（主要点）
複雑なシステムは、意図的に（あるいはもちろん無意識のうちに）暗黙かつ静かに単純化されうる。
システムが要求する変数の数が多いほど、フィクションはそれらを単純に無視してしまう可能性が高くなる。
これが起こる代表的な状況は、非固定シナリオである。
非固定シナリオは、その性質上、それを構成する明示的な「ルール／変数」を欠いている（あるいはほとんど欠いている）。これは、そのようなシステムの複雑さによるものである。
もちろん、すべての非固定シナリオが同じではない（より多くの明示的ルールを持つものもあれば、明示的ルールが少なくてもそれらが非常に重要で、結果として複雑性が低いものもあり、逆に明示的ルールが少なく、それらが重要でないため複雑性が高いものもある、など）。
このようなシステムでは、作者は変数を考慮しているという錯覚を作り出すだけでよい。
あとは「不信の停止」がすべてを補ってくれる。
当然ながら、これは固定シナリオにおいても容易に起こりうる（ただし頻度は低い）。
典型例：
いわゆる「天才」のチェス対局が、実際に画面上で描写されると、物語が示唆しているほどには全く大したものではないことが明らかになるケース。
（The Mentalist、Classroom Of The Elite、Code Geass など）。
このパターンは、当然ながらチェスに限った話ではない。

私たちの世界では、確率は主体によって歪められることはない。

ここで2つの「乗算的問題」が登場する
1 – 非整合性は事実上重なり合うことがない
もし2人の異なる作者が因果性の誤りを犯したとしても、同じ誤りを犯す確率は実質的に無視できる。
なぜか？理由は3つある：
1- 相互作用・大きさ・頻度において、（かつ同時に）同じ誤り、あるいは類似した誤りを犯すことは極めて起こりにくい。
2- 因果性環境が異なる（ある宇宙には異星の機械、能力、性能などが存在するかもしれない）。
3- 作者的不可能性論証が、この主張をさらに強化する。
例えば、各作者は物語の中で達成したい目的が異なる可能性が高い（人間スーパー計算機を書くのか？心を読む存在を書くのか？常にすべてを予測する計画者を書くのか？）。
キャラクターのアーキタイプは数多く存在し、それが結果として頻度の差異をより大きくする。
要するに、能力が重なり合うためには、全く同じ文脈において、全く同じ誤りを犯す必要がある。
ここで本題に戻ろう。
（極めて寛大に見積もって）各作品世界が5〜10％の因果的非整合性しか含まないと仮定しよう。
非整合性は重ならないため、
作品世界を足し合わせていくと、システム全体にわたる非整合性は増大する。
比較される作品世界の数が増えるほど、この非整合性は増大し、最終的には完全な複合的因果性非整合性に至る。
2- 非現実的な能力も、ほぼ常に問題を孕んでいる
作者は、これらの能力が（そもそも存在しうるとして）実際にどのように機能するのかについて、経験的観察も証拠も文字通り一切持っていない。
この特定の問題は、すべての「作者的不可能性」論証によって、特に強化される。
例えば、作者は物語上の目的のために、非現実的な能力を押し出す必要がある。
この事実（経験的証明の完全な欠如）＋特に強力な作者的不可能性の強化を踏まえると、
これらの能力が適切な因果性システムの整合性を欠いている可能性は、現実的な能力よりも指数関数的に大きい
（そもそも現実的な領域の能力ですら、最初から問題が多くないわけではないのに、である）。
つまり、これらの非現実的な能力こそが、
そのような「わずかな」（5〜10％の）非整合性の中に最も含まれやすいものである。
したがって、最良のケースシナリオ（重複問題がなく、非整合性が5〜10％しか存在しない場合）ですら、
キャラクター間に劇的な差異を生む要素は、スケーリングから除外されるべきである
（なぜなら、それらはほぼ確実に純粋な美的要素に依存しているからである）。
（ここを二重に強調したい：現実的な能力を持つキャラクターであっても、依然としてこの問題に強く、かつ圧倒的に悩まされている。
ここで私が言っているのは、キャラクターがより非現実的になるほど、この問題が指数関数的に悪化する、という点にすぎない。）

フィクション作品が長くなればなるほど、因果性システムの非整合性を含む可能性は高くなる。

確率論的推論
因果性問題は次のように述べる：
「因果性問題とは、各フィクション作品の各瞬間に存在する因果性システム（もし存在するならば）が、互いに非整合的である可能性が極めて高いということである。」
論理的連鎖：
P1- 知能という概念は、因果性を前提としている
P2- 因果性の非整合性が増えれば増えるほど（特に知能そのものに関わる領域においてであり、これはすでに論証した）、
私たちが評価しているのが知能である可能性は低くなり、代わりに別の何かを評価している可能性が高くなる
C1- 因果的非整合性が大きいほど、観察された行動から知能を推論する能力は弱まる。
________________

立証責任
物語のPOVを擁護するために、反対者が負うべき立証責任は（最も妥当と思われる主張を取るなら）以下のとおりである：
ルート1
「因果性システムとは無関係に、定義可能かつ評価可能な架空の知能は存在しうる」
立証責任：
依存性問題の結論C1またはC2が誤りであることを証明する。
ルート2
「依存性問題の結論は受け入れるが、架空の因果性システムは完全に整合的である」
立証責任(1)：
すべての作者的不可能性論証を一つ残らず反駁する。
立証責任(2)：
比較されている作品世界が、現実世界の因果性システムと同一、もしくは類似していることを示す。
ルート3
「依存性問題の結論は受け入れ、非整合性の存在も認めるが、それは誇張されており、事例は多くない、あるいはそれほど重要ではないと考える」
立証責任(1)：
すべての作者的不可能性論証が低確率であることを示す。
立証責任(2)：
実践的問題が低確率であることを示す。
立証責任(3)：
「誇張されている」という主張は、ある程度の非整合性を認めている以上、乗算的問題を完全に反駁する。
立証責任(4)：
架空世界の整合的な部分が、現実世界の因果性と一致、または類似していることを示す。
小さな注記：
文書を終える前に指摘しておきたいのは、
「物語が、認知／知能の認識および評価のための代替的パラメータを明示的に確立している」場合、
それ自体が演繹的に、その作品世界が非整合的な因果性システムを持つことを意味する、という点である。

Practical Problems

DEF: The Practical Problems are a set of empirically-grounded reasons (drawing from Cognitive Science and the common behavioral patterns of authors) that explain why an author is extremely likely to fail at constructing and maintaining a coherent system of Interactions, Magnitudes, and Frequencies.

1 – Lack of Causal-Correlational Coherence (main point)
When authors construct their characters, three common scenarios arise:
(1) they lack sufficient knowledge of how real cognition / intelligence operates (and mind you again, this is an *insanely* hard topic);
(2) they understand it but choose to disregard it; or
(3) they understand it but cannot maintain consistency because the narrative requires extraordinary or unrealistic elements.
As a result, correlations between different cognitive / intelligence capabilities and their expected outcomes are frequently ignored.
One of the most common themes of “lack of correlations” would be:
Point A) Certain capabilities being much weaker than others, just because they are much “harder to write”, even though these “others” heavily correlate or are straight up caused by the first ones.
Classic case: working memory through the roof, but not one single reasoning the character makes (on-screen) ever backs it up, even though WM is strongly correlated with and (partially) the cause of it, in real life.

Point B) “Easy to write” capabilities being extremely fluctuating (within the same field).
Classic case: pattern recognition being so strong to the point the character can just look at a person for 1 second and would be able to make the most accurate profiling one could ever make. But at the same time he has dozens of other situations where he cannot mysteriously do the same.
Why this is the case:
1- authors can’t just keep the audience filled with incredible performance from the character, so in order to build emotion they keep the consistency as bay
2- the more they “blow it big” the more likely these situations are to come.
Point C) “Easy to write” capabilities being insanely fluctuating (outside of the same field of expertise, but within others that are semi-analogical).
Classic case number 1: pattern recognition being super strong, but only within a specific field (strictly to profiling people, but stops being so strong for finding strategies).
Classic case number 2: detective with insane divergent thinking (when rediscovering strategies culprits used) but with little to no showing of such skill when having to make strategies of his own.
Why this is the case: a mix of dramaturgical effects + hard to write skills are usually required.
2 – System Manipulation (main point)
Complex systems can be implicitly and silently simplified at will (or even unwillingly of course).
The more variables a system should require, the more likely it is that fiction will simply ignore them.
Prime scenario where this happens: non-fixed scenarios. Non-fixed scenarios, due to their very nature, lack (or mostly lack) the explicit “rules/variables” that constitute them (due to the complexity of such systems).
Obviously not all non-fixed scenarios are the same (some have more explicit rules, some have the few explicit rules being really important meaning the complexity is lower, some in which the few explicit rules are not important meaning the complexity is higher and so on).
In such systems, authors will just need to create the illusion of taking into account variables. Suspension of disbelief will do the rest.
Obviously this can easily happen in fixed scenarios too (though this is less frequent).
Classic example: the typical “genius” chess match, that when is on-screen is actually revealed to be pretty lame compared to what the narrative portrays it to be. (The Mentalist, Classroom Of The Elite, Code Geass and so on).
This pattern extends well beyond chess obviously.
3- Meta-Identity Influence (main point)
In fiction probability is influenced by the meta-identity each character has.
This variable systematically warps the Frequency and Magnitude of stochastic events (luck, coincidences) around character based on the purpose the author has for them within the story, violating the core real-world principle of probabilistic impartiality.

From here on out I will just evidence some particular cases which are a mix of these first 2 points.

4 – Simulation of Human Mind and Behaviour (secondary point)
This problem is almost self-explanatory.
Even within the scientific community, there is no strong consensus on how the human mind (particularly subconscious processes and personality dynamics) interacts externally or how it operates internally.
As a result, narrative portrayals of thought and behaviour often collapse into oversimplifications, stereotypes, or outright contradictions.
The most problematic area is about suggestibility: to what extent should a character, given their pre-established personality type and cognitive profile, plausibly respond to another’s attempts at influence/manipulation?

Here come the 2 “Multiplicative Problems”.
1-  Incoherencies are virtually impossible to overlap
If two different authors make causality mistakes, the probability that they make the same mistake is practically irrelevant.
Why would they not make the same mistakes?There are two reasons:
1- making the same mistake / analogical mistake in interaction, magnitude and frequency which is extremely unlikely.
2- The Causality Environments are different (one universe can have alien machines, abilities, capabilities and so on).
3- The Authorial Impossibility arguments strengthen this argument. E.g. every author probably has different objectives he/she wants to reach in the story (writing a human super calculator?writing a mind reader?writing a planner who always predicts everything?) there are plenty of character archetypes, which then turn into higher frequency differences.
Essentially: they would need to make the *exact* same mistake in the *exact* same context, in order for the capability to overlap.
Now going back to my point, let’s assume (extremely generously) that each verse contains only 5–10% causal incoherencies.
Because incoherencies do not overlap:
When verses are added together, incoherencies multiply across systems.
As the number of verses increases, the adjusted incoherency approaches near full composite causality system incoherency.

2- Also unrealistic capabilities are virtually always problematic
Authors literally don’t have any empirical observation nor proofs under hand as to how these capabilities (given they could exist in the first place) actually work.
This specific problem is especially enhanced by all the “Authorial Impossibility” arguments. E.g. authors need to push unrealistic capabilities due to story purposes. >
Given this fact (complete lack of empirical proof) + particularly strong Authorial Impossibility enhancement, the chances of these capabilities lacking proper causality system coherency, are even exponentially bigger than realistic capabilities (as if capabilities in the realistical realm already didn’t have too many problems to begin with). >
Meaning that these unrealistic capabilities are the ones MOST likely to be in such “few” (5-10%) incoherencies. So EVEN under the BEST case scenario (no overlapping problem, and only 5-10% incoherencies) the things that would make drastic differences between characters, should be objectively excluded from the scaling (since they would, most likely, rely on mere aesthetic).
(I wanna double-underline this: characters with realistic capabilities STILL suffer from this and overwhelmingly so. Here I am merely saying that the more unrealistic one character goes, the more exponential the problem gets).
