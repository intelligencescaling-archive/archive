Explanation

Strategy is scaled by the quality of a scheme once executed, a strategy can include multiple different tactics and/or smaller strategies, but all of them will have to be mapped out beforehand for it to be one coherent strategy. Generally, characters that exceed in strategy will be harder to counter than characters who only exceed in planning.

Planning serves as a flexible blueprint when developing a scheme; multiple different strategies and/or tactics being formulated at different periods of time can be compiled into one plan, but all of them must be centered around clear and defined goals. A character exceeding in planning will usually be more flexible and versatile than those who only exceed in strategy.

This way of differentiating strategy and planning aims to equalize the importance of the 2 categories.

For example, if we were to define strategy as tactical implementations aka the actions the character takes in order to achieve a goal, it would mean that this one singular category scales the character’s adaptability and on the spot critical thinking on top of the quality of the scheme they come up with. As we can see, that would limit planning to only conceptualizing predetermined events, ultimately making strategy a much more versatile and important category since there is little point in thinking of a plan when the character lacks the ability to implement anything.

The example above is the common definition for strategy and planning found on Google which is strictly applied to a business model, and so this document was made with the intent to help provide a more scaler-friendly model that can be applied to any system of scaling smart characters.

Tactic is one move only, the move can either accomplish one goal or a set of goals. Unfortunately, it’s quite tough to bring tactics up to the same level of importance as strategy and planning. Exceeding in tactics whilst lacking the ability to chain them together with strategy and planning would limit the character to a more passive/reactive style due to lacking the ability of mapping out everything beforehand. Although tactics are still important, exceeding in strategy and or planning would instead help the character to adapt and perform better in a lot more situations.

Categories

These are some suggestions for categories that can be used to scale planning and strategy. It is not required that scalers have to use all of these exact categories but I highly recommend implementing some more important ones into any scaling system.

Red - Very Important, Applicable In All Situations
Orange - Moderately Significant, Helpful In Many Situations
Green - Situational, Dependent On Context

Overall Planning

Planning Coverage: The coverage of a plan shows the character’s ability to take into account as much as possible when developing a scheme. This includes various outcomes, variables, potential hazards as well as minimizing any uncertainties within the plan’s lifespan through ensuring guarantees.

Planning Acclimation: The acclimation of a plan is determined by the character’s ability to formulate, build upon and or alter a plan to be more befitting for the current environment, condition and potential hazards. Even when faced with new or unforeseeable circumstances, competent planners should be able to come up with a new scheme and or adapt their existing ones to make it so their plans function as initially intended.

Risk Calculation: A character’s ability to assess risk gives us a look at their potential to think of and evaluate the different choices they have to take when presented with a tougher situation.

Planning Formulation: This category scales the character’s ability to come up with a logically coherent plan within a short amount of time.

Preventative Measures: A character can take countermeasures to prevent enemy tactics from hindering a plan’s setup.

Long-term Contingencies: A plan may include variables that could help in dealing with the possibility of an unfavorable situation in the long-term.

Overall Strategy

Strategy Fortification: Often referred to as Castling or Success Rate, the fortification of a strategy is scaled by the quality of steps taken to make sure that strategy would achieve whatever the character is aiming for. This category does not place emphasis on the limitations the character faces, it just focuses on the substance of variables the character included in the strategy to maximize its resilience and efficacy. Note that any resources utilized to make a strategy more fortified are not wasted resources when it comes to outsmarting.

Strategy Building: We can evaluate how well characters build their strategies based on their ability to gain comfortable control over it whilst working off the limitations they face. Even when presented with a finite amount of time and/or resources, it's still important for a character to try to get as much of a grip over the situation as possible in order to lessen the chances of being countered.

Strategy Orchestration: A character’s ability to orchestrate within their strategy is determined by their ability to manipulate the field in which the strategy is applied to control all of the elements within it. Essentially, this category is Logical Manipulation and/or Setting Traps within a strategy.

Strategy Adaptability: Rather than scaling the character's ability to adapt, strategy adaptability is determined by the character's ability to design a strategy that can leave room for adjusting in case of an unfavorable situation. Adjustments can still be counted into one strategy as long as the character had already accounted for the possibility of a situation where they’ll have to adjust the strategy by using resources that were already intended to be utilized in case of an unfavorable situation, doing this means they aren’t exactly creating a new strategy since adjusting is already within the character’s intent beforehand.

Strategy Efficiency: Efficiency within a strategy is defined as the character’s ability to execute their strategy and achieve their goals with the least amount of time and resources sacrificed when the situation calls for needing to reserve time and resources.

Strategy Complexity: The addition of layers and overlaps within a scheme can help make a strategy harder to decipher and ultimately has a chance of lessening the possibility of getting countered as it would be harder to trace the different components within a strategy.
