So what does "scaling objectively" actually mean?

I've become quite the controversial figure here as of recently mainly because of my disagreement with scaling systems as a whole. I usually get asked the same sorts of questions whenever I bring up "scaling objectively" even in posts that don't necessarily have to do with that topic such as my last one, so I feel like I should clarify everything here instead of writing the same comment 12 times

The Merriam-Webster dictionary defines "objective" in this sense to mean "expressing or dealing with facts or conditions as perceived without distortion by personal feelings, prejudices, or interpretations" or to put it more simple, facts that exist outside the mind. When you apply this to scaling, you're only left with one thing truly objective: What's on the page

That is it, that is the only thing you can ever use to determine the intelligence of a character and still follow the definition of "objective", everything else is determined by one's own mind and not facts outside of it AKA subjective.

So what does this rule out, what isn't objective when it comes to scaling? Lemme run down the list

* All scaling systems
* All categories. While they're a useful metric, they can't ever be used to truly determine who outsmarts who
* Labels such as "realistic", "semi-realistic" and "unrealistic"
* The distinction between on-screen and off-screen
* The distinction between "feats" and "statements" (all statements are feats)
* Tiering (As in Low/Mid/High tier)

Yea that's all I can think of but there's probs more.

Any attempt to try and claim that you're scaling objectively while using any of the above is a blatant category error, as only what's on the page objectively speaking is truly mind-independent.

So how do you scale objectively? Easy, just look at what's actually in the story you want to scale, and then scale it. Cutting out all the fluff that this community has built for itself and scale the raw implications of exactly what's written (provided there are no direct contradictions)

This doesn't mean that you should stop using all of these fanmade terms for fun, obviously using this for fun is fine, the only thing you cannot do is claim that any of it is "objective"

**Rebuttals:**

1: "But what if the author contradicts themselves?

I saw this most often in my previous post where people tried to bring up examples of a character scaling high in "narrative/statements" but then being not as strong when it comes to direct on-screen showings of feats. To put it bluntly, performing at a lower level does not literally erase the previous statements/narrative off the page entirely and if you think so, you're being subjective. So any other explanation that accounts for a worse performance (ego, mentality, simply being bored) works better than trying to meet the impossible burden of suggesting that a worse performance = a feat no longer exists.

There is one exception I can think of: When it really is a DIRECT contradiction. AKA, the author writes a textbox pointing towards a Red Circle that reads "This circle is blue". In terms of intelligent characters, I cannot think of a singular example within the actual text of any story, only fan interpretations

Examples:
\- Will Graham having "perfect EU" according to my friend, which is directly contradicted by him being unable to read Hannibal in S1
\- The Vices in Usogui being smarter than STL Baku by a wide margin. This is different from the "lower performance" example I said before, because it's not just a claim about the intelligence of the Vices, but a direct, testable claim of being *smarter* than a certain character (STL Baku and Hal by extention). Furthermore, the narrative doesn't even suggest intelligence anyways and is more inline with power/influence so this is kind a bad example but you get the idea

I can't think of any other noteworthy rebuttals

**Conclusion:**
Most people don't like scaling objectively because it means that characters with "unrealistic" or even just "boring" feats often scale higher. All I have to say to that is "too bad". IF your goal is to scale as objectively as possible (which I assume is most people's goal when trying to determine who wins in a matchup) then this is what you're left with. What's said and drawn on the page and what it's implications are, that's all SCD comes down to when you disregard the subjective fluff of the community. Scale using those for fun if you want, just don't try and say it's objective when by definition it's not and can never be

Thank you for reading
