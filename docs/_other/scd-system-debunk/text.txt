Nowadays in SCD, victory is often determined by a simple arithmetic calculation: “Character A took 12 categories out of 20 (FSIQ, EQ, SQ, Planning, Deception, Spatial, Reasoning, etc.), and Character B took only 8, so Character A won.”

Problem 1: Equal Weighting Fallacy. In reality and in science, categories are NOT equivalent. The predictive power of Gf (fluid intelligence) in solving unfamiliar problems is ~70-80%, while a category like "Observation" or "EQ" without Gf produces around 0-5%. Equating 1 point for Gf to 1 point for "Spatial Awareness" is like equating 1 kilogram of gold to 1 kilogram of feathers simply because they are both "1 kilogram."

Problem 2: Multicollinearity and Category Inflation. The current categories in the SCD are critically correlated with each other. If a debater gives a character points for Fluid Intelligence, Deduction, Induction, Abduction, Pattern Recognition, and Analytical Thinking, they've actually given a point for the same characteristic (Gf) six times, artificially inflating the score in favor of their favorite.

Problem 3: Lack of incremental validity. If Character A has a colossal advantage in Gf (e.g., Gf = 160 vs. 110), then the additional categories "taken" by Character B, such as SQ, Emotional Control, or Tactics, have zero incremental validity – they simply cannot compensate for the gap in base processing power.

Debates often ignore the fact that intelligence in real-world situations operates according to the limiting factor principle (Liebig's Law), not according to the principle "the higher the total score, the better." The point for the document: To solve a specific problem or outsmart an opponent, a character needs a minimum threshold of Gf and Gc. If the task difficulty threshold is Gf = 140 and Character B's Gf = 120, then no matter how many "points" they have in other artificial categories (EQ, Charisma, Cold Reading), they will physically be unable to construct the necessary logical chain. They will stumble over the "bottleneck" of their intelligence. Conclusion: Evaluation should be based not on the number of pluses, but on the principle: "Does the character's psychometric profile exceed the difficulty threshold of the task posed by the opponent?"

The true assessment should not be additive (adding points), but systemically probabilistic.
