Scaling Systems and their definitions

Someone already made a post on this last year but the post was inaccurate. Another person made an updated post on it a few months ago but I believe he deleted the post and his account as well. So I made a new one.

Keep in mind that the goal of most scalers is to determine either how intelligent the character is (raw IQ) **or more commonly** how good they are at outsmarting other characters (which includes the remaining categories like strategy and reasoning).

# Narrative Scaling

All information is used from the story (narrative) to determine how the character would do in a true intellectual or outsmarting matchup. A misunderstanding people have is that narrative scaling means you take all statements literally which is not true. If the statement is a joke or figurative speech, then it does not make sense to take it literally. Narrative scaling takes into account everything from the story including antifeats, implications, statements, etc., in order to arrive at the most accurate conclusion possible about the character's intelligence or outsmarting capability. Some people call this *normal scaling*. Some people also call this *ontology scaling*. This system is what most people use when they analyze characters.

# Proven Ability

This is similar to narrative scaling, but has less freedom in making inferences. If the character's ability is proven, then it is valid. Statements count as long as they are trustworthy and cannot be meaningfully interpreted as a lie or a false narration. [Here's the document for it.](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1_wAML2kpScRJcUw9OxRkPzIozxVB-YOWkygvEKwVpTU/edit?usp=drivesdk)

# Stop's Scaling

Everything offscreen is **invalid**. Everything unexplained is **invalid** (you can't make inferences about the narrative that aren't explicitly given). Everything illogical is **invalid** (only logical feats are taken into account). Feats that are logical, onscreen, and explained are valid (they must meet all of the conditions).

# Methodology (Logical Scaling)

This is the successor to Stop's Scaling. Feats are judged by the process or method entirely, with the result being irrelevant. For example curing cancer or stopping world war 2 has no value if the methodology behind it cannot be analyzed. Explicit statements about the ability of the character (such as having the highest global IQ or planning 10 million years into the future) are not valid either. The main difference with Stop's Scaling is that offscreen feats are judged by their explanation rather than completely abandoned **and also** that some inferences can be made about the methodology of the feat rather than only scaling the explanation explicitly given by the story. The original document on this system lacks detail and is fairly outdated because the way people use this system has evolved over time but this system is fairly popular so you should be able to get an idea of how it works by looking at the analyses under this system.

# Pure Methodology

This is a subset of methodology scaling that is more rigorously defined. All the information from the story is split into "premises" and "connectors" where the premises includes the context and narrative elements (such as abilities possessed by the character) and the connectors are what integrates the premises (such as the reasoning and logic given by the character). Only the connectors are used to scale the character so the narrative premises do not upscale the character at all. This leaves the focus of the scaling system on aspects like complexity, creativity, logical rigour, and insightfulness. Meanwhile categories that have no methodological elements distinct from the narrative (such as pain tolerance and processing speed) are completely invalidated. I don't recommend this for beginners.

# Cosmology Scaling

This is equivalent to **narrative scaling** but with the priority being on scaling the verse itself rather than the individual characters inside the verse. I've only seen a few usages of this system in the intelligence scaling community.

# Intricatelogy

This system is a more structured version of methodology scaling (there are a few differences). [Here's the document for it if you want more detail.](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lIy4e7HBQiRGMPycfWxjcqE8a1VfJeX1h3XZmJO7jeM/edit?usp=sharing)

# RFES (Authorial Scaling)

Rational Feats Evaluation System. This system shifts from a narrative perspective to an authorial perspective. The characters are scaled based on how much intelligence the author "projects" onto them. In other words how cognitively demanding it would be for a human author to conceive the feats that the character performs. [Here's the document for it.](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18kmXIObye9cdWuRCLpziHlUVgHyIDZ8xrhhRpj8XtXw/edit?tab=t.0)

# Dual Scaling

This system combines narrative and authorial scaling. If a character scales low narratively AND in pure methodology, then they scale low in dual scaling. If they scale high in one of them but low in the other one, then they scale in the middle in dual scaling. If they scale high in both systems, then they scale high in dual scaling. In other words, this scaling system holds back characters that are either overreliant on the narrative or they don't scale high enough narratively.
