The most impressive and difficult ways to write mind games

I was recently thinking about what makes a outsmarting feat impressive, and I came to a somewhat reasonable conclusion so I wanted to share it here. Well a lot of people including me seems to have a rough idea of mind games being well written or poorly written, with all the illogical, unrealistic and bullshit allegations to many feats. Well for what I think, excluding the obvious things like "focusing more on planning and strategy than just FSIQ feats", "the feats are logical" and "the feats being implied enough to be recognized", I think there are 3 key factors that makes a outsmarting so impressive and feel well-earned, its also super hard for authors to write this way so its understandable why most don't.

1. the first I think is strictness. How strict was the situation, and what I mean by that is where the rules well defined and possible actions and settings well clear so the reader can figure out plans or strategize without having to think the author is just gonna do some random ass pull. A good example of this is of course the GOAT Akiyama with Musical Chairs, all the settings and rules were clear and covered what is going to happen in the game, so when Akiyama does something you never expected in a million years it feels impressive. A bad example of strictness would be Sora's kings game in NGNL (just a note not all of Sora's feats are like this, heck the rest are really good, I just hate kings game), the rules of "you can influence pieces" were clearly defined but the reader can't really understand as Sora just says random shit and its up to the authors to decide if it works or not, making it not that satisfying, JJK is also notorious for not being strict. This does bias FS, and RLS where audiences know what happens and fantasy setting NFS situations often lack behind in this category although series like death note are quite good.

2. The second is Replicability. If I knew the characters methods, would I be able to replicate it? I think this is the main differentiator between so-called unrealistic and realistic feats, or logical and illogical feats. For example even if I knew the psychological state of Richard Braun and knew how to speak persuasively I can never replicate what Johan did as I lack the charisma of Johan. Akiyama is another good example of this, if I had mini-Akiyama inside my head giving me advice on how to strategize and persuade people I would likely clear it as his feats are replicatable.

3. Finally its luck and Plot, this is pretty common and is not exclusive to mind games but it makes the outsmarting that much more satisfying. What I mean by Plot and Luck is that it never sides with the character you want to showcase. Akiyama constantly has many restrictions on him making his feats more cathartic. Michael Scofield is also a good example, although I've only watched season 1, like damn , every single element was against Michael, which makes Michael's clutches and outsmarting feel earned and impressive. A bad example of this is BBC sherlock where the plot and common sense actively bent towards sherlock to clutch up his terribly unlikely reasoning.

overall these factors restrict the amount of bull shit the author can pull of to clutch up a characters presented intellect, making it harder to write and more impressive to see. Awful lot of Akiyama glazing in this post? hell yeah he's the realistic Goat
