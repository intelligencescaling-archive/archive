Category Definitions - Methodology Scaling

**Methodology Scaling:** characters are scaled by the assessable logic and processes behind their feats, rather than by the narrative magnitude or outcome of the feats.

The most important aspects of Methodology Scaling are the Mental and Strategic aspects. These are primarily upscaled or downscaled by the character's thoughts and actions. However, there will naturally be overlaps between them, because thought cannot be fully separated from action.

The internal (mind) and the external (strategy) are closely related and sometimes they even become interchangeable when scaling feats.

Thus, you must keep in mind that not every category can be considered completely independent from another. However, I aimed to reduce redundancy as much as possible so that characters can be compared comprehensively in methodology scaling without the scaler having to use the same category over and over again under a different name.

-----

**—\[Mental Aspect\]—**

**Quantitative Reasoning:** number manipulation, formal logic, computation, etc.

**Qualitative Reasoning:** non-numerical inference from incomplete data

**Semantic Abstraction:** understanding of expressible conceptual information detached from concrete instances

**Syntactic Abstraction:** manipulation of symbols and representations independent of any inherent meaning

**Associative Thinking:** formation of useful links between seemingly remote information

**Systematization:** modeling interdependencies within any complex system

**Intrapersonal Understanding:** awareness and understanding of one's own emotions, thoughts, beliefs, motivations, behavior, existence, etc.

**Interpersonal Understanding:** understanding of others' emotions, thoughts, beliefs, motivations, behavior, and social interconnectedness

**Linguistic Competence:** depth, nuance, and rhetorical adaptability in verbal usage and comprehension

**Cognitive Fluidity:** capacity to shift between novel, contrasting, and versatile cognitive tasks

**Paraconsistent Thinking:** consistent reasoning despite the presence of contradictions or inconsistencies

**Prospective Orientation:** prioritization of long-term aims over short-term gains

**Creativity:** generation of novel and unconventional ideas

**Data Extraction:** identification and isolation of useful information from noisy and ambiguous contexts

**Temporal Sequencing:** arrangement and reasoning through events in causal order

**Working Memory:** capacity to hold and manipulate multiple distinct pieces of information

**Metacognitive Inhibition:** overriding default heuristics, biases, and habitual patterns of thought

**Pattern Randomization:** deliberate introduction of unpredictability within thought or action

-----

**—\[Strategic Aspect\]—**

**Multilayering:** stacking layers of intent within schemes and actions

**Preparatory Measures:** pre-planned measures taken to increase the probability of success

**Robustness:** the capacity of the scheme to handle unexpected disturbances

**Probabilistic Replicability:** likelihood of success given the identifiable sources of uncertainty

**Noise Injection:** deliberately obscuring information by introducing deceptive or vague data

**Cognitive Overloading:** the use of complexity to overwhelm the processing capacity of other entities

**Flexibility:** the capacity of the scheme to undergo modifications without major drawbacks

**Contingency Mapping:** considering multiple future pathways based on anticipated scenarios

**Iterative Refinement:** continuous calibration of schemes using feedback loops and systemic adjustments

**Internal Consistency:** ensuring that a scheme does not contain contradictions or exploitable flaws

**System Optimization:** refining entire systems for maximum efficiency

**Variable Exploitation:** leveraging overlooked objects within the system

**Aim Consideration:** considering the aims and benefits when formulating the scheme

**Contextual Rationality:** suitability of the scheme to ecologically rational contexts rather than idealized conditions

**Repercussion Shielding:** the capacity of a scheme to confine or limit consequences so that failure does not propagate beyond its immediate scope

**Equilibrium Generation:** stabilization of outcomes to prevent loss of control or to cause an impasse

**Mitigation:** reducing damage caused by the aftereffects of failure or confrontation

**Evasion:** avoiding detection, oppositional schemes, or negative consequences

**Disruption:** deliberately causing disturbances for opposing plans, systems, or tools

**Strategic Inclination:** the tendency to scheme when the opportunity or requirement presents itself
