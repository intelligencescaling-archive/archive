methodology as a valid scaling: the three most logical viewpoints

**Viewpoint 1: methodology as a complete scaling on its own.**

Just as there is PowerScaling (which scales power), there is its subset IntelligenceScaling (which scales intelligence).

Just as there is IntelligenceScaling (which scales intelligence), there is its subset MethodologyScaling (which scales methodology).

Thus under this viewpoint, methodology scaling is considered a different scaling to intelligence scaling.

Being more narrow in scope (or more restricted) does not make it less valid, just as intelligence scaling which restricts the scope of powerscaling to only intelligence is not less valid than powerscaling

This is the most intuitive viewpoint for considering methodology as a valid scaling. Simple enough? For many people, this is the best way to think of it because it's the easiest to understand and so obvious that there's no way anyone would be stupid enough to refute it while pretending that only diegetic scalings are valid or objective.

**Viewpoint 2: as an interpretation of intelligence scaling**

This is one of the more confusing viewpoints.

The broadness of the term "Intelligence Scaling" is what produces this:

Firstly, most people define intelligencescaling analogously to power scaling: how intelligent the character is (diegetic scaling of the characters' intelligence) with intelligence defined as outsmarting ability.

Secondly, it can also be defined the same way but with intelligence itself being defined more strictly to ignore crystallized capability (intelligence defined closely to "cognitive prowess").

Thirdly, intelligence scaling can also be defined with the focus of intelligence on the intellectuality displayed extradiegetically rather than within the universe of the story itself: the methodology (extradiegetic/non-diegetic scaling).

The first 2 differ because of the definition of intelligence, meanwhile the third one differs because of the ambiguity of the focus of intelligence.

This might interestingly be why Vietnamese scalers scale intelligence the way they do. You do not need to think in terms of scaling systems at all, the difference is produced because of the interpretation of the broad term "Intelligence Scaling."

Under this viewpoint, intelligence scaling cannot automatically be compared to powerscaling because power cannot be analyzed non-diegetically while intelligence can. This is why you don't have widespread powerscalers using systems analogous to methodology scaling. The psychology behind it is literally so obvious, and yet people get confused at why intelligence scaling produced scaling methods of such unintuitive forms, yet powerscalers all scale narratively/diegetically with all their systems. This also explains why the community even exists. If all people cared about was characters going up against each other unrestricted, everyone would abandon this community and do powerscaling instead. There is clearly a nostalgia or desire for intellectuality which is why some people prefer this community and dislike powerscaling. This is just an off-topic remark on the psychology behind it, which is irrelevant to the interpretation of the term "Intelligence Scaling," which is broad enough to accommodate the  interpretation as valid.

This viewpoint might be the hardest to understand though tbh.

**Viewpoint 3: scaling systems as a premise for matchups**

If a powerscaler says something like "Naruto without superpowers vs Ayanokouji in a fistfight, who would win?" are they being subjective? Are they doing something invalid by placing unnecessary restrictions? Are they asking a question they shouldn't dare to ask or going against the spirit of powerscaling? No, that would be a stupid accusation, they're clearly just using "without superpowers" as a premise for the scaling. There is nothing wrong with hypothesizing the matchup. Saying Ayanokouji would win does not mean Ayanokouji has higher power, it merely means that within the context of the specific matchup, given the premise/restriction, Ayanokouji would win. (just a random example, I haven't watched COTE or Naruto yet)

Similarly, there's no problem in using a premise for other matchups such as "Itachi Uchiha vs Chen Ran blindfolded with his hands behind his back, half his brain missing and short term memory loss in intelligence scaling" We're not saying that the result of this matchup determines who is smarter, "the restrictions" are just a premise for the matchup!

This is literally such a simple idea. Same with using something like "methodology scaling" as the premise. Nobody is saying Akiyama would outsmart omniscience in real life just because he scales higher under the given premise of the scaling system the matchup is placed under. Using an extradiegetic scaling does not mean you are automatically denying the hypothetical diegetic result.

**Extra:**

Viewpoint 3 is probably the most common that people are self-aware of. Viewpoint 1 is quite rare but I've seen it stated before. Viewpoint 2 is very unintuitive which makes it hard to understand or accept, I haven't seen anyone explicate this viewpoint in detail, even though I suspect many people actually have the same idea, it's just hard to put the idea into words.
