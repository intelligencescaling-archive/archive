good debating conduct: 15 steps

This post is just a theoretical ideal for how top tier debaters would conduct themselves

**1: Focus on the core substance of your opponent's argument rather than ignoring it to attack the side points.**

Here's an example:

Person 1: Light Yagami has never made a mistake! Ever! You think this handsome blonde character would ever make a mistake?

Person 2 (bad response): Umm actually his hair is brown. Blind ahh.

Person 3 (okay response): Just because he's handsome doesn't mean he never makes mistakes!

Person 4 (good response): he does make mistakes. Several times. Being handsome doesn't prevent the possibility of making mistakes. For example he \[insert mistakes\]. And his hair is brown btw.

**2: Don't use ambiguous reference terms unless it's clear what the term is referring to.**

Person 1 (bad): "Your explanation is dichotomized to be the other explanation if the other one doesn't work, which would lead to the one ALL the way on the other side, rather than this other one."

Person 2 (good): "Between the 4 options you presented, Light Yagami chose potato chips. Why? Because it tastes good! The remaining 3 options (avocado, brown rice, coke) clearly don't!"

(Focus on how the reference terms like "other" are used. The people reading should ideally be able to understand what you're referring to IMMEDIATELY)

**3: Be clear.**

Same as above, but applied generally.

Person 1 (bad): "The feat is economically impossible, so you'll need a lot of proof to support it happening."

Person 2 (good): "Economic *understanding* is not enough to achieve the feat on its own. You'll need more proof to support attributing the entire feat to it."

**4: Be very clear. Expand on your acronyms.**

Same as above.

Person 1 (bad): Actually your so-called PhD standard for ECREE is merely the FBI's GPA following a QRT, with an NPC and a 123, with an ILY and a YLM!

I think you get my point here. It really does not take that long to type out the full form compared to an acronym unless you're trying to hide away the reasoning from viewers of the debate. Maybe a few times here and there AFTER you have given the full form, but not if you're using an extremely obscure acronym that nobody else uses on social media.

**5: Be extremely clear. Don't substitute words with rarer alternative just because it makes you sound smarter. Only do it if it sounds more fitting.**

Person 1 (bad): What a mendacious claim you're making. It is misbegotten, spurious, inexact, and/or ERRONEOUS! You nincompoop/goofball!

Person 2 (good): Incorrect dumbass.

**6: Be unfathomably clear. Separate, add full stops, add commas. Especially for distinct ideas. Even line breaks when needed.**

Person 1 (bad): actually Light Yagami ate a potato chip and the scene shows that he took a bite out of it not what you're claiming about eating the potato chip whole which doesn't even make sense in the context where L's image in his mind is present while solving math equations and parallel processing with his left hand while pretending to be a normal kid by the way have you seen sherlock homes movie probably not actually anyways what I said about that he literally did when he was pretending to read inappropriate stuff and like this just makes it so obvious you're wrong and he really did eat the potato chip the way I said.

**7: Be concise.**

Concise does NOT mean write shortly. It just means stop yapping. For example the previous one is a lot of yap that lacks both educational and persuasive value.

If writing longer increases the persuasiveness, educational value, comprehensibility, flow, etc., then that is okay.

But if it does not add **ANYTHING**... remove it.

Again, please... concise does not mean short. It means remove the fluff that has 0 purpose other than to make your response longer.

**8: Expand!**

The opposite of concise, but it increases the educational value, persuasiveness, comprehensibility, and a whole lot of other things. So EXPAND.

Person 1 (BAD): Well actually you're wrong, all 5 of your points are wrong. Your whole understanding contradicts epistemology and philosophers have long since disproven all the claims you're making. Einstein would be rolling in his grave looking at your 5 points, which clearly contradict his theories.

Person 2 (good): Incorrect. Point 1 is wrong because if you actually read the panel, it says hello and not goodbye. Point 2 is also wrong because Light Yagami is saying it, not L. The speech bubble is literally right there connected to his head. Point 3 is also wrong. How can it completely be intuition if intuition is based on past knowledge? Point 4 is half right, but you're clearly misconstruing it because the taste of potato chips is SUBJECTIVE! Point 5 is inconsistent with your other points, you literally said he ate the potato chip in point 1, and now you're saying he didn't? Which one is it huh? Clarify.

**9: Do not bring up points you are both obviously aware of which LACK debate value.**

Person 1 (good): There is no proof that batman can levitate on the ground.

Person 2 (BAD): Bro, birds can fly, this uses the effect of air resistance and propulsion to allow them to push themselves against the shackles of fate itself, just like how humanity is bound by fate due to a deterministic casual system that reality composes of, which is how Leylin has literally done it in the past against the forces of dark Maguses who dared to attack his warship. Don't you see how there are literally birds in the sky right now? They're flying. That's how flying works. Do you not even know what flying is? Look at the way the seagull is floating as if it's not even moving. Also Chapter 17 of the comic comprehensibly and unilaterally evaluates the precendence of Batman's levitational eminence.

**10: Stop exaggerating.**

Seriously. Or I'll ontologically disintegrate you.

**11: Be nuanced.**

Nuance bro. "Light Yagami did not fail the photo test" does not mean he did everything perfectly. It doesn't contradict saying something like "he failed 2 of the 4 layers and did the first layer slightly imperfectly, but salvaged himself and succeeded in passing the overall trap."

**12: Don't bring up points that have already been refuted unless you are certain they have not been refuted sufficiently. In that case, explain why.**

Person 1: don't sit on the chair or you're going to fall.

Person 2: but what if I want to fall?

Person 1: well, that's up to you. Falling would give you brain damage and make you dumber than you already are.

Person 2: ok but who says me sitting on the chair is gonna make me fall?

Person 1: the back leg is broken and your weight will give it the final force needed to make it fall.

Person 2: but what if I want to fall?

**13: Don't waste too much time with insults and emojis.**

A smart judge with high reading comprehension doesn't need convincing from pictures (emojis) or buzzwords (insults). Make your points and be done with it. Don't rely too much on sarcasm either. Another person could just as well say the exact same thing and mean it literally, which reduces comprehensibility.

**14: Debate to come to the truth, and debate to teach, not just to win.**

Concede the points that you realize your opponent got correct if it ever comes to that. Don't stubbornly cling onto an idea after you know it is wrong just because you want to save face and maintain your reputation. Even worse if you continue clinging onto it after the debate is over because you don't want to backtrack.

**15: Losing a debate doesn't disprove your idea.**

Don't abandon an idea just because you are judged as the loser in a debate. You can still be right, while the opponent and the judge both happen to be wrong.

**Conclusion:**

It's probably not possible to follow them all perfectly because we're human. I break a few of the rules here and there too. It's just how an ideal community of debaters would conduct themselves. How top tier debaters would conduct themselves, not just a ragtag group of mere "tier 1s" who live their whole lives trying to gather win points, even if it means focusing on style rather than substance.

If it's a debate between friends or something, then you'll definitely need to follow most of the rules at least. No point in destroying your friendship and being disingenuous just to one-up them.
