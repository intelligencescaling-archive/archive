Higher dimensional > Lower dimensional NO DIFF

The title is bait, but there is a lot of truth to it.

A computer that can calculate the optimal next game-state for a particular player given a 2D snapshot of the game state (like a picture of both players or a configuration containing their positioning) in boxing requires unquantifiably more processing power than a computer that must solve chess given a 2D snapshop of the game state (positions, whose turn, etc.)

This is because boxing is more complex than chess.

Even if you make the entire boxing system discrete such as by splitting space into millimeters and time into milliseconds (despite it being far more granular in reality), and then you somehow convert the information into a 1 or 2 dimensional configuration, the preconfiguration will still be unquantifiably high and no-concept-of-diff chess in solution difficulty.

The computer doesn't even need to be omniscient or account for subatomic particles or player psychology. A solution only needs to be defined relative to the provided information (for example in chess, the provided information is the current position of all pieces, whose turn it is, and the ruleset of chess).

Even on a limbular (arms, legs, head, eyes, elbows) rather than cellular level, the calculations would still be unquantifiably higher. The reason why humans only deal with simplified models rather than perfect solutions (and boxers deal with it using muscle memory, experience, etc.), rather than thinking or reasoning or scheming is because there's no point in wasting so much computation power processing your next gamestate (position, muscle tensing, etc.) in boxing in the upcoming millisecond to an extremely granular level when you're in the middle of the fight.

It's not as if we have infinite processing speed and reaction time!

Why waste all that time trying to achieve the impossible feat of solving boxing (nigh impossible) when your body has literally been built to handle, navigate, and manage the complex 3 dimensional plane without needing to process all the variables within the plane? Why calculate all possibilities when you can get 70% of the results by simply doing \~0% of the work compared to what you would need to do in order to absolutely solve the complex dynamic system of boxing?

This even applies to chess engines like Stockfish by the way, given we haven't solved chess yet. Even chess grandmasters rely heavily on memorized games, especially in the early game, rather than raw computation. Even pattern recognition produces more results relative to the strain, as opposed to calculating every possibility.

This is why, despite chess players in general having higher cognitive output than boxers, boxing as a system itself possesses greater complexity than chess under the definition of complexity in complexity science.
