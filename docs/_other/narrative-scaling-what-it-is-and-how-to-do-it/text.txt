Narrative Scaling

I use both Narrative Scaling and Methodology Scaling.

There are people trying to force me to fit into an archetype of only using methodology scaling 🥀 even though for the entire past week or so, all I have been debating is complexity science under narrative scaling, since I've been focusing on the difference between absolute system complexity and demand in the complexity of thought.

I define Narrative Scaling as **diegetic** scaling of **outsmarting ability** (which is opposite to my definition of methodology scaling).

The purpose of Narrative Intelligence Scaling is to determine how smart the character is within the story. To determine their ***outsmarting ability*** within the constraints of the fictional world.

Narrative scaling does not mean you take every statement literally (such as Yokoya scaling to omniscience by saying "I know everything")

It means you determine how smart the character is using all the evidence that is available.

A key thing to keep in mind for Narrative Scaling is to never become overly attached to a general idea and scale overly dependently on that idea, because there are too many factors that affect whether a character is smarter beyond just a single concept.

For example, the statement "X manipulated the world" in isolation may scale higher than "X manipulated his classroom," but characters are almost never judged in isolation because there is always context that can be used to clarify these things so it isn't absolute.

There are too many factors that shift how high the statements or feats can scale. Examples of different factors:

1: The granular complexity of the verse and its characters. Is the complexity of the verse equivalent to something like Peppa Pig or the average slop xianxia TV shows made for kids where everyone can be manipulated by subpar schemes, or do they have higher individuality and resistance to manipulation like Reverend Insanity characters? OR are the characters insanely intelligent like what you find in 18LoH which makes the manipulation difficulty even more severe? This can serve as context for feats that are not detailed precisely.

2: The wider context of the character's intelligence. Suppose a character is said to be omniscient, but they display at multiple points that they are ignorant of certain things, for example they don't expect their opponent to make some highly creative move despite being called "omniscient". The statement of their omniscience becomes debunked immediately by their own ignorance (unless it isn't what it appears to be on the surface). Next, suppose a character has infinite PSI, but at one point in the story they say "Umm..." the infinite PSI has pretty much been debunked (unless a context justifies the antifeat of saying "Umm...") because saying "Umm" means you are thinking and people who have infinite PSI should not take time to think. Taking time to think is an antifeat for someone with infinite PSI. Next, suppose a character displays a level of intelligence that is quantified as "15" or something, but at a period when they were far less intelligent, they have an ambiguous feat that can be severely lowballed to a number like "2" or severely highballed to a number like "50," it makes no sense to highball or even midball the ambiguous feat since the specific feat that has less ambiguity during their period of higher intelligence already sets the standard for their higher end intellect at "15". These are some examples of how debunks can work.

Narrative scaling means you account for everything! Statements, feats, antifeats, system complexity, thought complexity, thought granularity, etc. If it can be argued that a character outsmarts diegetically (within the bounds of the fictional world), then they are smarter. A character can have a thousand feats and still scale below a character that has 1 statement proving they are able to outsmart the prior character. Outsmarting is what matters, not feat quantity or how many subjectively determined categories you take. A character can have 0 reasoning feats and still no diff a character with many reasoning feats, IN REASONING, if it is implied by their feats or statements that their faculty for reasoning is developed enough to surpass the character they are up against.

Scope and system complexity can be important for determining intelligence, as I said above. However, granularity is generally far more important. For example suppose person B controls multiple nations over the period of 5 years using basic politics and a magical notebook that can kill anyone in the world without precise predictions or accounting for all the chaotic system variables, and person A controls only a tiny isolated high school classroom, however he manipulates the classroom to a very precise level, accounting for every eye blink and finger twitch to such a precise extent that he can predict how the entire week will go based on projection of all these multitudes of variables (I haven't seen COTE yet, this is just a made up example so ignore the similarity). They may both display effective control over their respective complex systems, and the multiple nations may no diff the classroom in system complexity, but it doesn't change the fact that the classroom manipulator possesses higher complexity thinking.

Similarly, a chess player who lasts 6 hours straight in a chess match then loses may have shown only subpar effective management of the system, but they still victimize in complexity thinking compared to a random professional boxer (even though the system itself can be argued as infinitely more complex for boxing).

This is an example where Semi-effective control over a less complex system > Effective control over a more complex system because the first character uses higher complexity thinking or has a harsher time constraint, and the cognitive demand is greater.

Obviously if the boxer accounted for absolutely every variable present including the exact micrometer of the position of every limb to perfection, to the point where nothing within the complex system that is relevant to increasing his chances of winning escapes his cognition, that kind of insane processing would no diff the chess player, but we know that humans instead rely on their physique, training, muscle memory, etc., far more. We can't even assume 5 calculations for the normal human boxer given our reaction time, the dynamic adaptability of the opponent, and our cognitive limits, not to mention that infinite calculations per second is needed to account for every infinitely precise variable that can affect the outcome by an infinitely small percentage (assuming continuity matters).

Same with manipulating a classroom vs manipulating the world. The granularity/precision of control is far more important than the scope of control.

This is how Narrative Scaling (or normal scaling) should be done. Treating things like unboundedness, complexity, variable quantity, nonlinearity etc., as a range rather than as something that applies past an arbitrary subjective point makes far more sense.

By the way, to emphasize again, feat quantity is NOT that important in Narrative Scaling. It obviously matters a little because it serves as proof of your ability, but having 10 normal-human level feats in a category doesn't automatically turn you into a genius within that category.

I've noticed that the majority of feats people bring up for highly grounded and realistic characters in categories like emotional intelligence and deception are things that literally normal humans accomplish on a day to day basis in real life.
