Methodology Scaling Frequently Asked Questions answered.

Keep in mind I use the strictest version of methodology, where the method of the character's feats is scaled non-diegetically. Most people use a less strict version than what I use, so my answers may not apply to their usage of it.

**Question 1: What is Methodology Scaling?**

It is a system for scaling characters based on their intelligence/outsmarting feats, with a focus on scaling the methodology of their feats rather than their narrative magnitude or outcome.

Neither the intelligence of the character within the story NOR the in-universe reception of a feat is relevant to the scale. Instead, the feats are extracted and evaluated as a logical construct independent from the diegetic implications upon the intelligence of the character.

**Question 2: What does it mean for Pure Methodology to be a "Non-Diegetic" scaling system?**

"Diegetic" refers to elements that exist entirely within the fictional world, bounded by the narrative framing. "Non-diegetic" refers to evaluation outside of the story's bounds.

Examples:

Powerscaling: Diegetic. This scales how powerful the character is when evaluating through the lens of the story.

Conventional Intelligence Scaling (aka Narrative Scaling or "Normal" Scaling): Diegetic. This scales how smart the character is when evaluating through the lens of the story. True Omniscience > Fang Yuan no diff.

Morality Scaling: Non-Diegetic. This evaluates the virtuousness and evilness of characters based on ethical frameworks external to the story itself. Even if the author of a story calls a character "the most moral person in the world," people who do morality scaling do not immediately subscribe to the same opinion, since the evaluation criteria are external to the story.

Lookscaling: Non-Diegetic. Same as above. If the author of a manga says the manga character is the most handsome guy in all possible multiverses, but the manga art is trash, lookscalers will ignore the author statement since they mainly scale independent from the actual narrative. They scale non-diegetically.

Pure Methodology: Non-Diegetic. Same as above. Even if the author says the character is the smartest in the world, or the feat of the character implies a level of intellectual adversity in-universe that is near the peak, these implications are treated as irrelevant and discarded. Only the non-diegetic element (the methodology of the feat) is taken into consideration and evaluated.

**Question 3: Does pure methodology require a plan to work under real world physical laws?**

NO! This is a common misunderstanding. A verse that is entirely supernatural and contradicts our own universe's natural laws (like Reverend Insanity or 18LoH) can surpass a verse that is mostly built on our own universe's physics (like Dr Stone) if the feats are superior in explicit methodology. Realistic does not always mean good. Logical consistency is more important than realism. If the rules, variables, systems, mechanisms of a fictional world are defined and used in complex rigorous ways during outsmarting scenarios, that is far more impressive methodologically than simple science feats or a real-world strategy that relies on coincidences and opponents acting out of character or stupidly in order for the strategy to succeed.

**Question 4: What are some examples of useful categories?**

It can be helpful to divide methodology scaling into two primary domains. Strategic methodology and non-strategic methodology.

Strategic methodology is more heavily weighted since the methodology is more difficult to procure (non-strategic methodology relies more on knowledge which is easier to obtain than intelligence).

**Examples of subcategories of strategic methodology:** Methodological Complexity (explicit interconnected variables interacting in evaluable complex manners), Rigour Maintenance Relative to Complexity (how methodologically rigorous the strategy or feat remains as the complexity increases), Strategic Creativity (the ability to discover non-obvious paths while still remaining in established systems or rulesets), Contingency depth (the degree to which alternative branches are mapped in outsmarting scenarios as opposed to taking only a single pathway with no option available in the event a loophole is exploited by the opponent or a failure results).

**Examples of subcategories of non-strategic methodology:** Theory of mind (psychological modelling, cognitive understanding, etc.), insightfulness (depth of understanding regarding systems of varying natures such as philosophical, social, political, etc.), mentality (optimization of mindset in a way that counters getting outsmarted or facilitates higher level outsmarting capability), etc.

**Question 5: Why is Strategic Methodology weighted higher than Non-Strategic Methodology?**

Non-strategic methodological feats (like deep monologues on human nature) do not require as much intelligence to concoct. This makes it less impressive methodologically.

**Question 6: Is counterintuitiveness valued?**

YES. I already mentioned one category for it (strategic creativity). A good example of something that is counterintuitive while still being methodologically rigorous is the theory of relativity. It is highly logical when you think about it, but can be very counterintuitive and difficult to digest because humans generally think in fixed terms, since space-time relativity only becomes obvious perceptually at macro scales that we do not have the need to deal with explicitly within our daily lives. Same with quantum mechanics.

However, keep in mind that higher counterintuitiveness does not always equate to a higher scale. For example, if the counterintuitiveness results from the author pulling out nonsense to make the character look smart regardless of its logic or rigour, then the creativity does not scale high at all, since it is mere BS and author imagination rather than methodological creativity. For example, if a character somehow correctly decides that the password of an iPhone is 977838 based on someone's favourite food correlating to a quantum spin number of the element that has the same letters as what their first name starts with, and this character also uses the password to deduce that the owner of the phone will get divorced in 6 months (because (97-78)/38 = 0.5 which is equal to 6 months when multiplied by a year), such BS illogical nonsense which cannot hold up to scrutiny outside of what the author decided will succeed within that specific scenario narratively, CANNOT be validated as methodologically creative. At best, at's a 2 digit arithmetic feat.

Feats don't have to be deductive to be valid, since induction and abduction are still possible, but the logic is viewed very strictly and critically in methodology scaling, so logical leaps should not have more robust inferences that can be made if the result is ignored, otherwise it is methodologically flawed. The entire feat in the password example I gave is invalid (or possibly even an antifeat depending on how you scale).

Thus, counterintuitiveness does indeed upscale a methodology, since counterintuitive methodologies are harder to formulate, but it isn't as core to the scaling as other factors like complexity and rigour. If a character creatively does a bunch of logical leaps to reach a result that the author forcefully wants him to reach when there is no logical incentive that can be argued for why that pathway had to be taken and not others, the methodology becomes subject to heavy criticism. This is why planning merchants who "planned the entire novel" are considered frauds in methodology, even if they give a complex explanation at the end of the novel about how they controlled everything from the background. Post hoc justifications usually severely lack rigour.

**Useful analogy for methodology scaling:** if you were given the ruleset of the system the character operated within when formulating their strategy, would it be possible to eventually reach the same conclusion as the character on the method they used? If not, the methodology is most likely an asspull from the author.
