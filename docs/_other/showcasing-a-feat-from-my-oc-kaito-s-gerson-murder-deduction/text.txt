Showcasing a feat from my OC.

Context: Mrs. Violet Gerson was murdered 40 hours ago. Her children are scheduled to be interrogated within the next 8 hours. Paul Gerson was captured by Kaito (my OC) and interrogated before his death.

\### How Kaito Found Him

After obtaining permission from the police, Kaito examined the crime scene.

From the blood patterns and shoe prints, he determined that Violet had fallen down the stairs. The marks on her neck indicated that she had been strangled. A wound in her throat showed that her neck had been slashed or stabbed, and five additional wounds in her abdomen revealed that she had been stabbed five times in the stomach.

Kaito immediately noticed that there were too many actions involved in the murder. Instead of focusing on what happened, he began asking who wanted investigators to believe that this was what had happened.

He noticed that the push down the stairs was consistent with a right-handed attacker, while the stabbing pattern was more consistent with a left-handed one.

Violet's facial expression showed surprise. From this, Kaito deduced that she knew her attacker and that the attacker intentionally allowed her to see them before the attack. Violet had not been able to defend herself, and there appeared to be a mark on her finger where a ring had been forcibly removed.

Next, Kaito examined the family photographs. He noticed that they had been altered. Violet had five children. In one photograph, only four appeared. In another, only two. In the third, only one. The police report stated that the fifth child had left the family a long time ago.

However, after checking the dates of the photographs, Kaito realized that the alterations were unnecessary. The number of children shown in each photo was already correct for the time the picture had been taken. The modifications were not made to hide information—they were made to direct attention toward specific information.

At that moment, Kaito stopped asking who benefited from Violet's death and started asking who benefited from a mistaken investigation.

He searched the house and found an old children's room containing bloodstains that dated back years. In the victim's bedroom, he found a hidden whip. According to police records, the bloodstains contained three different blood types. Kaito questioned whether this evidence was genuine or fabricated.

In the kitchen, the stove was off and the pot was untouched. Two plates had been served and appeared to contain Violet's DNA, but only one of them carried her fingerprints. Blonde hairs were found in the trash, beneath one of Violet's fingernails, and on a pillow. All of the hairs belonged to the same person, but they originated from different time periods. They appeared to have been planted rather than naturally shed.

He also found cigarette butts, blood that appeared to have been partially cleaned, and discovered that jewelry, money, electronic devices, and a notebook were missing.

Kaito then investigated the inheritance.

Paul was originally set to receive 50% of the estate. However, the will had been modified twice. The most recent modification had occurred only two days before the murder and completely removed Paul's inheritance, transferring it to an unknown individual.

While interrogating Paul, Kaito casually mentioned this fact and noticed Paul unconsciously move his fingers.

That reaction caught his attention.

Kaito reviewed previous calls and inquiries regarding the will. Someone had shown great interest in the details of the first modification, but there was no evidence that anyone had investigated the second one.

That was when Kaito realized the killer did not know about the second alteration.

He then confirmed with the administrators of the will that the family had attended the second modification.

This led him to a critical conclusion.

The killer had to be someone who knew about years of family conflict, yet had been distant enough to remain unaware of the recent changes to the will. A complete stranger could not have known about the abuse within the household, while the family already knew about the second modification.

Everything pointed toward one person:

The fifth child.

Paul Gerson.

\### Profiling Paul

After identifying Paul as the prime suspect, Kaito decided to dismantle every deception present at the crime scene and build a psychological profile.

Only one hour had passed since the investigation began.

When Kaito went to capture Paul, he noticed that Paul carried his luggage effortlessly with either hand.

Immediately, Kaito connected this to the crime scene.

The push suggested a right-handed attacker.

The stab wounds suggested a left-handed attacker.

Paul was ambidextrous.

Further analysis revealed that none of the photographs were fake. The old blood groups belonged to Paul's siblings, while the most recent old bloodstains belonged to Paul himself.

Paul was the youngest sibling.

He had fled from his mother's abuse years earlier.

The stolen ring had been intended to create the possibility of a greedy female culprit, but ironically, Paul had genuinely stolen it out of greed as well.

The blonde hair belonged to Paul's eldest sister.

The cigarette butts belonged to one of his brothers.

The stolen notebook and electronic devices belonged to his other siblings.

Every piece of evidence had been selected to make all of them appear suspicious.

Kaito eventually realized something deeper.

Paul had never truly stopped thinking about his family.

Not even his mother.

He killed her because he was never able to forget her.

Despite living far away, he had preserved memories, objects, and connections to them all.

The murder scene had not been planned for forty hours.

It had likely been planned for years.

Most people who regret their actions destroy evidence.

Paul manufactured evidence.

His only real regret was that he never saw the updated will before committing the murder.

All of this was deduced and confirmed by Kaito in approximately two hours.
