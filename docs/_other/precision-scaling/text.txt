PRECISION SCALING
INTRODUCTION
This scaling system endeavors to establish a consistent and equitable methodology for comparing characters within SCD as accurately as possible. It integrates various elements from different scaling systems while discarding certain components of those systems. The aim of this system is to impartially evaluate characters across verses and their respective feats.
This scaling system is by no means intended to replace or overshadow any preexisting systems; it merely serves as an exercise for me to explain the manner in which I scale characters within SCD.
HOW IT WORKS
The premise of precision scaling is founded on the notion that an individual character's capabilities and abilities should be directly correlated to feats demonstrated both on-screen and off-screen, as well as to statements substantiated by tangible evidence.
In other words, this system excludes the use of baseless assumptions or headcanon that is only loosely supported by the material provided by the media in question. Other systems, such as Narrative and Normal Scaling, are not considered valid under this system.
Similarly to PA Scaling, both off-screen and on-screen feats are regarded as equally valid. However, in the case of off-screen feats, inferences regarding a character's thought process during said off-screen feat cannot be made without direct evidence. A simple illustration of this would be a scenario where character A states that off-screen they uncovered the method by which another character managed to perform a particular action. Unless it is explicitly stated how character A was able to figure that out, or sufficient details are provided to formulate a logical explanation, any assumptions made regarding character A's thought process off-screen are not valid. Even if said character has previously demonstrated similar feats, unless ample details are provided in a vacuum, the feat will be considered null and void.
When comparing two characters under this system, similar to other systems, point distribution is not the sole factor that determines the winner of a matchup. Since specific characters excel in different fields and categories, it would be unfair for many characters to lose due to a lack of feats in other areas or a simple loss in overall categories. This system considers the specific nature in which characters operate and accounts for any counteractions they might take against each other to more effectively achieve their goals. Consequently, this system does not support the usage of ABC scaling in any manner.

Where this differs from PA Scaling, however, is that this system can take into account the general intellect of the individuals involved in a character's feats when comparing two similar characters. This does not mean that certain characters are downgraded due to this factor. Instead, the purpose is to more accurately gauge the intelligence of two comparable characters.

Generally, when analyzing an individual character's feats, the intelligence of those involved in said feats can be considered negligible. It becomes relevant only if two feats from separate characters cannot be reasonably determined as superior to one another. In cases where no effective conclusion can be drawn, the intelligence of those involved may be considered a distinguishing factor between the two similar feats.

To reiterate, this is not intended to diminish the impressiveness of feats but is simply a method to be employed in situations where two characters' abilities are considered equal.

UNQUANTIFIABLE FEATS/STATEMENTS
Certain statements and feats that are considered unquantifiable should be discarded when scaling. Unquantifiable does not necessarily mean unrealistic; unrealistic feats and statements can still have a quantifiable value. Despite how unrealistic visualizing a planet, universe, or larger may be for a human, it still possesses a quantifiable value and provides a metric for understanding the scope of the statement. However, statements such as “smartest in the world” and “outsmarted a god” are inherently unquantifiable. If a feat or statement lacks a real method to measure any metric and is vague to the point where no real value can be derived without relying on Narrative Scaling and headcanon, it should be considered invalid. While a feat or statement may appear impressive on the surface, it still requires a basis or tangible evidence to be considered valid within this system.

Statements indicating that something has occurred or that a character has accomplished something without further elaboration fall into this category. If it is impossible to deduce a process from such statements, they would also be considered invalid.

Only in special cases can an unquantifiable feat or statement be used, primarily in contexts such as knowledge and crystallized intelligence. For instance, Ayanokoji's claim of knowing more than a person could learn in a lifetime is inherently unquantifiable. Therefore, instead of relying on this statement, we would scale Ayanokoji's knowledge based on all knowledge-related feats demonstrated in "Classroom of the Elite" by all characters, as well as general knowledge accepted among regular people.
In these situations, unquantifiable statements must be cross-referenced with quantifiable feats and widely accepted knowledge benchmarks. This ensures that the scaling remains grounded in tangible evidence and avoids relying on vague or hyperbolic claims.

STATEMENTS
Statements are often an important factor in upscaling a character's abilities, and under this system, that remains true. Statements from figures of authority and characters themselves should be considered accurate unless disproven. A general principle for such statements should also align with the established archetype and traits of the character. However, statements made by those with questionable authority and/or potential hyperbole should be regarded as secondary evidence, to be used only in conjunction with more reliable statements made by others.

Statements in this manner also need to have some degree of tangible representation in the characters' feats themselves. If a character is repeatedly shown to disprove the statement in question through their feats or other statements, the statement's viability for use should be brought into question. Note that individual instances of characters creating inconsistencies for satirical or nonsensical reasons are considered exceptions to this rule.

Another crucial aspect to understand is that, under this system, upscaling a character to match or exceed another character's specific abilities based solely on general statements is not acceptable. In many cases, a character may be stated to be smarter than another character; however, that does not mean the character is automatically upscaled in all categories above the said inferior character. Only in cases where it is explicitly stated that a character is superior to another character in all respects or in certain categories, can the feats of another character be used to upscale the superior character's categories.

ON-SCREEN
If a large majority of a feat’s important aspects are presented on-screen, it is considered under this criteria. On-screen feats should be treated as already proven and should not require additional evidence or reasoning to confirm their existence.

However, this does not mean that the feat should automatically be considered valid; it merely confirms that no proof is needed regarding the feat's existence.  In cases where the feat itself is nonsensical or falls under the aforementioned unquantifiable category, it should be considered invalid.

On-screen feats, like off-screen feats, still require some degree of explanation to be considered valid. Similar to what was stated regarding off-screen feats, a character's thought process cannot be fabricated and must have a degree of tangibility. Actions such as deductions, inductions, and abductions cannot be inferred; they must be able to be effectively scaled in a vacuum. Meaning that the processes of induction, deduction, and abduction must be explained to some degree. While blanket statements about on-screen feats that lack depth are still acceptable, they should be scaled below fully explained feats.

This system does not permit inferences to be made regarding off-screen aspects of an on-screen. Even if a feat is primarily shown on-screen, any off-screen aspects must still adhere to the established rules for off-screen feats within this system, requiring tangible evidence or statements to validate those off-screen elements. Inferences in this context are not accepted unless supported by solid evidence. Though the use of statements for off-screen aspects of a largely on-screen feat is more lenient, allowing for the scaling of these off-screen components when supported solely by credible statements.

OFF-SCREEN
Off-screen feats are where this system diverges significantly from the majority of consensus scaling within SCD. An off-screen feat is defined as one where a large majority of a character's important actions are performed off-screen.

Unlike on-screen feats, off-screen feats require validation through a logical process. If an off-screen feat contains enough general information, statements, or explanations by characters, it can be considered valid only to the extent to which it is confirmable. An off-screen feat cannot rely on inferred processes; it must be directly shown or contain sufficient evidence to be proven without inconsistencies or the need to fill in gaps with possibilities rather than definitive answers.

Off-screen feats can also be scaled based on the outcomes of those feats in specific cases. For instance, if a plan was formulated off-screen, even if the exact details are not provided, the plan can be scaled based on what is observed on-screen or the results that manifest from the plan.

Despite that fact, assumptions based on narrative, theories, and headcanon are not acceptable in any regard. Even if a character has previously displayed a similar feat on-screen, that on-screen feat cannot be used to justify the existence and process of an off-screen feat or be used to scale said off-screen feat.  The aforementioned rule regarding the on-screen results of off-screen feats is also governed by this principle. If an off-screen feat is being scaled strictly from on-screen results, using possible methods by which the feat was accomplished is not a valid approach under this system. Only what is explicitly shown should be used in scaling the off-screen feat.

ANTI-FEATS
	Anti-feats are actions displayed by a character that disprove or debuff said character's abilities or accomplishments. These often manifest as actions that contradict a character's previous actions, reveal flaws, or depict the character as less competent in a specific area than previously shown.

Under Precision Scaling, while anti-feats are still considered, those that are obviously created as a result of an obviously satirical purposes or are outright nonsensical are deemed invalid. Additionally, if the anti-feat is an outlier that is repeatedly disproven and contrasts with the character's established abilities, traits, and actions, it can also be dismissed as invalid.

The period in which the anti-feat occurred should also be taken into account. It is essential to remember that characters experience change and develop their abilities throughout a narrative. Therefore, an anti-feat that a character has evidently outgrown is not applicable within this system.

COMPLEXITY & EFFICIENCY
Complexity and efficiency are both important factors in scaling a particular feat, but there is nuance regarding these aspects. Both complexity and efficiency are valid under precision scaling, where complexity is not inherently superior to efficiency in a feat. If a feat achieves the same result in a more efficient manner, it is not necessarily superior to another feat due to efficiency alone, but it is not inferior due to a lack of complexity either.

What ultimately matters under precision scaling is the process, and whether the complexity or efficiency makes the process more effective or impressive is the deciding factor in determining a character’s effectiveness.

For example, if two characters share the same goal of logically manipulating someone to surrender to the police, and one feat, through its raw complexity, demonstrates more aspects of logical manipulation and shows a higher level of aptitude, that feat would be considered superior. However, the same holds true for the other character: if the efficiency of the manipulation is a result of the character’s aptitude for logical manipulation and displays proficiency greater than the former character’s feat, the second character would be considered to have the superior feat.

HEAD CANON & THEORIES
As previously mentioned, precision scaling does not tolerate any degree of headcanon or theories that attempt to make inferences without a proper foundation of evidence. While feats can be drawn from things that are not directly stated, all theories require a solid foundation of empirical evidence to support them. Narrative elements and vague statements are not sufficient to validate theories or headcanon; any assumptions made must be supported by reasonable and empirical evidence.
