# Introduction

Intelligence Scaling is a broad concept that can be interpreted in many ways. Here's an example split of the concept of intelligence scaling into two different forms: scaling the outsmarting ability of the character within the bounds of the story (which most people know of as normal/narrative scaling, i.e., "how smart is the character inside the story?"), and scaling the method of the character’s intellectual displays outside the bounds of the story (which most people know of as methodology scaling, i.e., "how intellectual is the method of the character's displays of intelligence outside the story?"). Both of these fall under the broad concept of intelligence scaling.

Methodology Scaling ***does not*** scale how good the character is at ***outsmarting*** within the story. What it scales is the methodology of their intelligence & outsmarting feats. The in-universe reception and implication of a feat is irrelevant to what is being scaled. Instead, the feats are extracted and evaluated as a logical construct independent from the diegetic implications upon the intelligence of the character. A character can have infinite IQ and control multiversal macrocosms while still having no evaluable methodology (or horrible methodology) and thus scale to bottom tier in methodology scaling even if they are top tier in outsmarting diegetically.

**Diegetic:** an element inside of the story bound by the constraints of the fictional world.

**Extradiegetic / Non-diegetic:** an element outside of the story viewed beyond the constraints of the narrative.

Scaling something ***within*** the bounds of the story is known as diegetic scaling.

Scaling something ***outside*** the bounds of the story is known as extradiegetic or non-diegetic scaling. The prefix “extra-” means *outside (NOT equivalent to the word extra which means "more")*.

Conventional narrative intelligence scaling is diegetic. Methodology Scaling is extradiegetic / non-diegetic.

# Examples of the difference between diegetic and non-diegetic scalings:

**Morality scaling** is mostly non-diegetic (the author/narrative’s opinion of the evilness of the character is ignored because real life moral frameworks are used for the evaluation of their actions).

**Powerscaling** is diegetic. If the story proves they destroyed a multiverse, then they scale high enough to have destroyed that multiverse.

**Looksscaling** is non-diegetic (usually people use fanarts to determine how high the appearance of the character scales rather than "the character was the handsomest in all multiverses" being said by the story making them an absolute top tier).

**Intelligence Scaling** done ***conventionally*** (normal/narrative scaling) is diegetic (how smart the character is within the story). In conventional/narrative scaling, if the character can calculate millions of years into the future, then via the implications of the story, they can automatically outsmart almost all characters.

**Intelligence Scaling** can also be interpreted non-diegetically as outlined in the introduction. In this case the intelligence that is being scaled is not the in-story outsmarting ability of the character but rather something external to the story. Methodology Scaling is an example of non-diegetic intelligence scaling.

# Methodology Scaling Definitions

**Definition 1:** Characters are scaled by the explicit methodology underlying their intelligence or outsmarting feats.

**Definition 2:** All the information from the story is split into "premises" and "logical links" where the premises include the context and narrative elements (such as abilities possessed by the character and occurrences within the story) and the logical links are what integrates the premises (such as the reasoning and logic given by the character or the causal connection between events). Only the logical links are used to scale the intelligence of the character (the narrative premises do not affect the character’s methodological scale).

**Definition 3:** Extradiegetic scaling of the methodology within displays of intelligence.

**Definition 1** is the simple definition. **Definition 2** is the detailed usage I formulated in 2025. **Definition 3** is the concise version of Definition 2. ***These are merely different wordings of the exact same concept.***

# Frequently Asked Questions

**Does methodology require a plan to work under real world physical laws?**

NO! This is a common misunderstanding. A verse that is entirely supernatural and contradicts our own universe's natural laws (like Reverend Insanity or 18LoH) can surpass a verse that is mostly built on our own universe's physics (like Dr Stone) if the feats are superior in explicit methodology. Logical consistency is more important than realism. If the rules, variables, systems, mechanisms of a fictional world are defined and used in complex rigorous ways during outsmarting scenarios, that is far more impressive methodologically than simple science feats or a real-world strategy that relies on coincidences and opponents acting out of character or stupidly in order for the strategy to succeed.

**What are some examples of useful categories?**

It can be helpful to divide methodology scaling into two primary domains. Strategic methodology and non-strategic methodology.

Strategic methodology is more heavily weighted since the methodology is more difficult to procure (non-strategic methodology relies more on knowledge which is easier to obtain than intelligence).

Examples of subcategories of strategic methodology: Methodological Complexity (explicit interconnected variables interacting in evaluable complex manners), Rigour Maintenance Relative to Complexity (how methodologically rigorous the strategy or feat remains as the complexity increases), Strategic Creativity (the ability to discover non-obvious paths while still remaining in established systems or rulesets), Contingency depth (the degree to which alternative branches are mapped in outsmarting scenarios as opposed to taking only a single pathway with no option available in the event a loophole is exploited by the opponent or a failure results).

Examples of subcategories of non-strategic methodology: Theory of mind (psychological modelling, cognitive understanding, etc.), insightfulness (depth of understanding regarding systems of varying natures such as philosophical, social, political, etc.), mentality (optimization of mindset in a way that counters getting outsmarted or facilitates higher level outsmarting capability), etc.

**Why is Strategic Methodology weighted higher than Non-Strategic Methodology?**

Non-strategic methodological feats (like deep monologues on human nature) do not require as much intelligence to concoct. Even a 14 year old average human with basic philosophy knowledge can integrate deep philosophical monologues into a story. This prevents it from scaling as high methodologically. Complex strategies are harder to formulate non-diegetically than philosophy quotes and isolated psychological analyses.

**Is counterintuitiveness valued?**

Yes. I already mentioned one category for it (strategic creativity). A good example of something that is counterintuitive while still being methodologically rigorous is the theory of relativity (just an example). It is highly logical when you think about it, but can be very counterintuitive and difficult to digest because humans generally think of space and time as fixed and linear, since space-time relativity only becomes obvious perceptually at macro scales that we do not have the need to deal with explicitly within our daily lives. Same with quantum mechanics. These are examples of counterintuitiveness increasing the impressiveness of the methodological scale.

Keep in mind that higher counterintuitiveness does not ***always*** equate to a higher scale. For example, if the counterintuitiveness results from the author pulling out nonsense to make the character look smart regardless of its logic or rigour, then the creativity does not scale high at all, since it is merely BS that the author imagines to get a character out of a situation they don’t want to think too deeply about, rather than methodological creativity. For example, if a character somehow correctly decides that the password of an iPhone is 977838 based on someone's favourite food correlating to a quantum spin number of the element that has the same letters as what their first name starts with, and this character also uses the password to deduce that the owner of the phone will get divorced in 6 months (because (97-78)/38 = 0.5 which is equal to 6 months when multiplied by a year), this string of random illogical nonsense which cannot even hold up to scrutiny outside of what the author decided will succeed within that specific scenario narratively, CANNOT be validated as methodologically creative.

Feats do not need to be deductive to be valid, since induction and abduction are still possible, but the logic is viewed very strictly and critically in methodology scaling, so logical leaps should not have more robust inferences that can be made if the result is ignored, otherwise it is methodologically flawed. The entire feat in the password example I gave is very flawed and can even be viewed as an antifeat depending on how you scale, especially if the risk is high in making that kind of assumption.

Thus, counterintuitiveness does indeed upscale a methodology, since counterintuitive methodologies are harder to formulate, but it isn't as core to the scaling as other factors like complexity and rigour. If a character creatively does a bunch of logical leaps to reach a result that the author forcefully wants him to reach when there is no logical incentive that can be argued for why that pathway had to be taken and not others, the methodology becomes subject to heavy criticism. This is why planning merchants who "planned the entire novel" are generally considered frauds in methodology, even if they give a complex explanation at the end of the novel about how they controlled everything from the background. Post hoc justifications usually do not require impressive extradiegetic methodological input and often lack rigour.

**Is Methodology Scaling “Process Scaling”?**

No. Associating it with process is a common misunderstanding, it is more accurate to associate it with method/methodology (hence the name of the scaling being ***methodology scaling***).

The in-story process is narrative/diegetic. It is within the bounds of the story. If a character has a plan perfectly predicting millions of years into the future, the process is clearly extremely complex and no diffs characters like Fang Yuan even if it only has a simple explanation. This is only the implied process within the bounds of the story/narrative though, which is outside the scope of consideration for strict methodology scaling.

Rather than the in-story process, methodology scaling scales the methodology of the feat extradiegetically (OUTSIDE the bounds of the narrative), meaning it does not actually scale the intellect of the character, but rather the extractable method of their feats.

**Since the narratively implied intelligence is not being scaled, are in-story mistakes that are not methodologically flawed also invalid?**

YES! Fang Yuan’s experience is an example of this. Multiple people claiming to be methodology scalers have argued before that Fang Yuan does not scale high, because his hundreds of years of experience is a massive advantage that forged his outsmarting capability. However, this debunk is invalid, since his narrative experience is irrelevant to the explicit methods within his feats that are being evaluated. Whether he is 500, 100, or 10 years old, you are not evaluating the methodology properly outside the bounds of the story if sliding the in-story age number up or down decides the scale of the character's feats. Fang Yuan’s age neither downscales nor upscales the methodology of the feats. Similarly, an argument like “Character A came up with XYZ strategy when he was 0.00001 years old” is an invalid argument for his methodological scale because narratively being young does not change the methodology of the feat, even if it does imply that narratively (diegetically) he is smarter.

**If the feat is methodologically flawed, do I throw the entire feat away?**

NO! A feat can have a methodological flaw in one aspect or a presumption that it relies on, while still scaling high if the rest of the feat is impressive extradiegetically. As an example, Musical Chairs by Akiyama Shinichi had many physical assumptions that are unrealistic and illogical, but the immense complexity and interconnectedness of variables still makes the overall feat scale high. Another example is that Fang Yuan’s scheme against Mo Yao required many post hoc additions from the author regarding the characteristics of different variables and the events that occurred, but the complexity and interconnectedness was still high enough to make the feat impressive methodologically.

These factors that it relies on ***reduce*** the impressiveness of the feat, but this **does not mean** that the entire feat becomes worthless.

# FSIQ (Full Scale Intelligence Quotient) Primary Invalidity

**Processing Speed (PSI):** INVALID. Processing speed as a methodological category is completely invalid because it is fully dependent on the timescale. As the timescale is decreased towards 0, the processing speed becomes arbitrarily large. If time = 0 seconds, processing speed = infinite. Therefore processing speed is completely dependent on the narrative and thus an INVALID category in methodology scaling. Scaling processing speed is only valid for scaling the in-story intelligence of the character (narrative intelligence scaling), but NOT for the extradiegetic methodology of their feats which is what methodology scaling focuses on.

**Working Memory (WMI):** The extradiegetic working memory required to understand the explicit logical structure of the character's feats with its distinct variables explicated is VALID. However, the narrative magnitude of their working memory implied story-wise from the feat is INVALID. For example, a character being said to have parallel processed a hundred homework questions and solved them all simultaneously scales lower than them explicitly being shown to solve a problem that requires a specific working memory capacity to solve. The reason why narrative magnitude is INVALID for evaluating the methodology extradiegetically is because similarly to processing speed, it can be made arbitrarily large just by changing its magnitude without any effect on the explicit logic. For example "a hundred equations ran through his mind" can be replaced with "a million" or "a trillion" or any higher number that is arbitrarily larger without any effect on the actual reasoning or logic that can be derived and analyzed outside the bounds of the story.

**Verbal Comprehension (VCI):** Only the direct verbal competence shown by the character through their explicit words are VALID. An example of this is L using Japanese grammar to trick Light while interrogating him at the cafe. Another example is Chen Ran and Pinocchio purposely stacking double negatives in their speech to avoid telling an objective lie while speaking. These are VALID feats. However, narrative verbal comprehension such as the scope of knowledge of a character or how many languages they can speak is INVALID because it can be upscaled to infinity without changing the methodological structure. For example a character knows 2 languages vs a character knows 99 languages vs a character knows infinite languages. Since it can be narratively upscaled infinitely, it does not contribute to the character's VCI in methodology scaling.

**Visual Spatial Index (VSI):** Only the spatial reasoning contributes and is VALID. The visualization itself is only narrative magnitude and thus INVALID. For example a character may be said to have visualized an apple. And then a universe. And then a multiverse, and then visualized it down to the tiniest atom. This is a completely narrative claim without any significant change to the explicit logical structure of the feat extradiegetically. Thus it is INVALID. Only the visual spatial reasoning contributes. For example, a character is told that the distance between the bottom edge of one side and top edge of the opposite side of a room is 20 meters, and that the length of the room with the prior mentioned edges at the ends is 12 meters. then using the laws of trigonometry, he concludes that he needs to build a ladder of height 16 meters if he wants to climb onto the roof. In this case, it would be VALID and scale higher than something like visualizing a multiverse. The example is a simple and unimpressive feat even in methodology scaling, since it only uses basic trigonometry and visualization, but the methodology scales higher than just being told that a character visualized the multiverse.

**Fluid Reasoning (FRI):** VALID. Not how good they are narratively said to be at reasoning, but rather their actual reasoning feats logically evaluated. The opinion of the author or character, as well as their success or failure, is irrelevant, since only the methodology is scaled. For example a character might coincidentally arrive at the correct answer because the story praises the reasoning given, but this does not automatically make the methodology of their reasoning impressive. It does not upscale the character, you could even argue it downscales them if the reasoning is bad enough and the success probability can be analyzed as low enough, with superior alternatives.

There are more subcategories, but this explanation should allow you to extrapolate the standards onto the rest of the subcats and decide for yourself whether it can be considered valid or not. I do not even use conventional categories when I scale feat methodology (I almost never use FSIQ, AC, etc.)

I prefer categories like \[ methodological complexity, rigour relative to complexity, counterintuitiveness, etc. \] since they are more suitable for methodology scaling but if you prefer to use conventional categories like FSIQ, this section helps to identify the validity (like fluid reasoning) and invalidity (like processing speed) of specific categories.

# “Methodology Scaling” Viewpoints

**Viewpoint 1: methodology as an independent scaling of its own.**

Just as there is PowerScaling (which scales power), there is its subset IntelligenceScaling (which scales intelligence).

Just as there is IntelligenceScaling (which scales intelligence), there is its subset MethodologyScaling (which scales methodology).

Thus under this viewpoint, methodology scaling is considered a different scaling to intelligence scaling.

Being more narrow in scope (or more restricted) does not make it less valid, just as intelligence scaling which restricts the scope of power scaling to only intellect is not less valid than power scaling

This is the most intuitive viewpoint for considering methodology as a valid scaling. For most people, this is the best way to think of it because it is the easiest to understand and so intuitive that there is no way anyone would be stupid enough to refute the validity of methodology scaling while pretending that only diegetic scalings are valid or “objective”.

**Viewpoint 2: as an interpretation of intelligence scaling**

This is one of the more confusing viewpoints, but this is the viewpoint that I explained at the start of the document.

The broadness of the term "Intelligence Scaling" is what produces this:

Most people define intelligence scaling analogously to power scaling: how intelligent the character is within the story (diegetic scaling of the character’s intelligence) with intelligence defined as outsmarting ability. Some people define intelligence more strictly to ignore crystallized capability (intelligence defined closely to "cognitive prowess").

However, another formulation of the term “intelligence scaling” is with the focus of intelligence on the intellectuality displayed extradiegetically rather than within the universe of the story itself.

The first two (intelligence defined as cognitive prowess vs intellectual outsmarting ability) differ because of the definition of intelligence, meanwhile the third one (extradiegetic intellectual display) differs because of the ambiguity of the focus of intelligence within the term “intelligence scaling”.

With this viewpoint, you do not need to think in terms of different scalings or scaling systems at all, the difference is produced because of the interpretation of the broad term "Intelligence Scaling."

Under this viewpoint, intelligence scaling cannot automatically be compared to powerscaling because power cannot be analyzed non-diegetically while intelligence can. This is the reason why you do not have widespread powerscalers using systems analogous to methodology scaling.

The psychology behind it makes it obvious why intelligence scaling produced scaling methods of such unintuitive forms, despite powerscalers all scaling narratively/diegetically within their systems. This also explains the psychology behind why the intelligence scaling community even exists. If all people cared about was characters going up against each other unrestricted, everyone would abandon this community and do powerscaling instead. There is clearly a nostalgia or desire for intellectuality which is why some people prefer this community and dislike powerscaling. (This is just an off-topic remark on the psychology behind it, which is irrelevant to the interpretation of the term "Intelligence Scaling," which is already broad enough to accommodate the interpretation as valid anyways.)

This viewpoint might be the hardest to understand though tbh.

**Viewpoint 3: scaling systems as a premise for matchups**

If a powerscaler says something like "Naruto without superpowers vs Ayanokouji in a fistfight, who would win?" are they doing something invalid by asking a question that is premised on one of the characters being restricted? Are they going against the spirit of powerscaling? Obviously not, they're clearly just using "without superpowers" as a premise for the scaling. There is nothing wrong with hypothesizing the matchup. Saying Ayanokouji would win does not mean Ayanokouji has higher power in powerscaling, it merely means that within the context of the specific matchup, given the premise/restriction, Ayanokouji would win in the fistfight. This is just a random example, I have not completed COTE or Naruto yet).

Similarly, there is no problem in using a premise for other matchups such as "Itachi Uchiha vs Chen Ran blindfolded with his hands behind his back and memory reset every 10 minutes in intelligence scaling" We're not saying that the result of this matchup determines who is actually smarter, "the restrictions" are just a premise for the specific matchup given!

The same applies to using something like "methodology scaling" as the premise. Nobody is saying Akiyama would outsmart omniscience in real life just because he scales higher under the given premise of the scaling system the matchup is placed under.

**These 3 viewpoints I explicated are examples of how methodology scaling can be validated logically alongside conventional diegetic intelligence scaling without being subjective.**

**Using an extradiegetic scaling does not mean that you are automatically implying the hypothetical diegetic result.**

**Claiming that a character scales higher than another character in methodology scaling does not indicate that I am claiming the character is able to outsmart the other. These are 2 separate concepts being scaled. Keep this in mind.**
