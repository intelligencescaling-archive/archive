META: Feats, Anti-Feats and Limit Feats

We know feats. We know anti-feats.

Typically, positive feats (usually just called feats) showcases a character’s abilities beyond what an average human can do.

Feats, especially if they’re done with low-mid diff, only tell us the character’s capability extends to that point, not that their ability caps off there. For that, we need Limit Feats.

In essence, a limit feat’s primary characteristic and utility is in defining the limits of a character. A limit feat can be a feat or an anti-feat. Strictly, it tells us the upper or lower bounds of a character’s abilities—what definitively is the character’s maximum ability at that point in the story. Limit feats help constrain our estimate of a character’s ceiling.

A Limit Feat defines the ranges of that character’s abilities. That means, by definition, something cannot be a Limit Feat if that character has another Feat outside of its range. What I mean should become clearer as we go along.

# LIMIT FEATS AS ANTI-FEATS

Anti-feats usually tell us more about a character’s limits than feats.

The exact definition of an anti-feat isn’t necessarily clear. Sometimes it seems like it’s when a character loses to another character. Other times it seems like an anti-feat is only considered such when a character makes a below average-human mistake.

Not all anti-feats are necessarily limit feats though.

For instance, in Musical Chairs, it is considered an anti-feat when Akiyama took Kiruma Kei’s fake letter hint about the position of the chairs. But clearly, we know this isn’t Akiyama’s limit. We’ve seen him see through much tougher deception before. So clearly this isn’t his limit.

In this case, Akiyama’s anti-feat is not informative of his true limits regarding Deception-detection. In other words, we won’t use this when forming a picture of “best form” Akiyama. However, if we saw that he fell for things like this more often, we could see this becoming a Limit Feat.

What IS an anti-feat as well as a Limit Feat is Yokoya’s Pandemic anti-feat. Yokoya hands away 300M ¥ just because he lost his cool.

Reason this is a Limit Feat is because we don’t see counter-evidence that supersedes this anti-feat.

In other words, Pandemic anti-feat reveals a vulnerability in AC that is consistent with his characterisation and is never meaningfully disproven or overcome later. (I’ve only read till Bid Poker so don’t come after me if he improves by ROT4K)

In this case, Yokoya’s anti-feat IS indeed a Limit Feat.

# LIMIT FEAT AS A FEAT

For instance, let’s say X and Y take a test with a hundred questions. It’s an extremely difficult test, with most people not even scoring 2/100. X scores 100/100, getting everything correct, while Y scores 98/100. We assume both were trying to attain the highest score for themselves.

Because of X’s perfect score, we don’t know what’s his true ceiling could be. If there were 200, 300, 1000 questions? Would he score as well? We don’t know. We could only infer via his perceived difficulty. For instance, if he achieved 100/100 with EXTREME diff (+) CGEW, then this MIGHT count as a Limit Feat.

However, with Y it is much clearer: his limit is 98/100. Scoring 98/100 definitely isn’t an anti-feat but it can still tell us about his limits.

This is an example of a Limit Feat that falls under the (positive) feat category.

When Limit Feats are Positive Feats, we expect to see a good move—just not a perfect one. We expect to see something truly impressive, just not as impressive as it could be. There’s no reason for the character to not do the improved feat, except that he or she is just not smart enough to do so.

# Perfection Scaling

Then you compare the canon scheme to your ideal one.

Essentially you need to fanfic the best possible plan.
