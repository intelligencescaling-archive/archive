Scaling Systems and their definitions

This is for those who are confused about the different scaling systems and how they work. There are a lot of misconceptions and misunderstandings going on about the scaling systems, so I will tie a comprehensive list of them and how they work.

NOTE: This is not an exhaustive nor an absolutely correct list, it is merely my opinion on how these systems work based off of what I’ve seen and understand from the people who use them and the characters where they excel in.

For those who don’t know what a scaling system is, **a scaling system (in smart character debates) is a predetermined set of rules where certain ways (or portrayals) of judging a character’s intelligence are prioritized over other potential ways where the character’s intelligence can be ascertained.** To put it simply, a scaling system is a tool scalers in SCD use to determine how exactly they are going to judge their character’s intelligence, and this can also include filtering out other ways of judging them.

Now that the main definition is out of the way, here are the scaling systems:

#1. Normal Scaling

Normal scaling is the default form of scaling and it is the most common even outside of SCD. It judges the character’s intelligence by using feats AND statements, no strings attached. Unrealistic feats are included, offscreen feats are included in this scaling as well, and the logic behind the feats are included, but they aren’t prioritized over the result. ALL reliable and concrete statements are included, however unreliable, vague statements aren’t included in this scaling.

#2. Stop Scaling

Stop scaling is a scaling system where only onscreen, logical and explained feats are included. *Everything* else does not count in this scaling. A common misconception is that stop scaling needs to have realistic feats, this is not true. While stop scaling does not count illogical or unexplained feats, this does not mean that it doesn’t include unrealistic feats. If the feat is unrealistic but it is onscreen, logical and explained then it will count.

#3. Proven Ability

Proven ability is very similar to stop scaling where the feats have to be logical, but it is much less restrictive. In proven ability, ALL feats count as long as it is logical. It doesn’t have to be explained or onscreen, it just has to have a logical method and process to it and the feat will count in this scaling. Note that in proven ability, the results of the feat (what the feat actually does) is prioritized more than the logical process of the feat.

#4. Methodology (logical) Scaling

Methodology scaling (also called logical scaling) is even more similar to Proven Ability than stop scaling is, despite methodology and stop getting mistakenly interchanged with all the time. However, the distinguishing feature between methodology and proven ability is what they prioritize. While Proven Ability prioritizes what the feat actually does, Methodology cares more about the processes behind the feat in the first place. So, methodology scaling scales the logical processes behind the feat, and it DOES NOT have to be realistic, onscreen or *fully* explained (this scaling’s nature almost requires at least some sort of explanation behind the feat).

#5. Narrative Scaling

Narrative Scaling is a scaling method that primarily only works in-verse, however it can be use cross-verse also in isolated instances. It’s a scaling that completely disregards feats and concrete statements and only looks at the narrative portrayal a character shows. This scaling mainly uses statements, but it can also use narrative implications, narrative importance, potential, etc.

#6. Cosmology Scaling

Cosmology scaling, like the name implies, is a scaling method that scales on the basis of how large the character’s cosmology is. It posits that if a character is on a dimensionally or conceptually higher plane than another (I.e. their cosmology is transcendental over another character’s cosmology) then their exploits are immeasurably more complex and therefore immeasurably more difficult to achieve, thus making the character much smarter. While feats and statements are used, they are moreso used as secondary pieces and cosmology is the primary one. Note that this scaling is practically useless when the two characters being compared have the same cosmology.

#7. Ontology Scaling

Ontology scaling is a scaling method that scales on the basis of how great the existence of a character is. Conceptually, this scaling is very similar to cosmology scaling in that both scale on the fundamental idea of transcendence over another character. The difference is that cosmology scales on the basis of a character existing on a dimensionally or conceptually higher plane than another, while ontology scales on the basis of a character having a greater existence than another character. Practically, this means that while ontology and cosmology scale virtually the same when a character is transcendent conceptually or dimensionally over another, they differ when both characters exhibit the same cosmology. In that case, if there is a transcendent character, they will have to be scaled by ontology, while cosmology is useless.

#8. Complexity Scaling

Complexity scaling is a scaling method used primarily by Japanese scalers and it scales the adversity of a character while they are performing an exploit. In other words, the higher the adversity (or the harder a feat is to replicate) the higher the character who performed it will be scaled. All feats and concrete statements are used in this scaling in order to scale the characters.

#9. Balance Scaling

A scaling method invented to ‘bridge’ the gap between unrealistic characters and semi-realistic characters. Conceptually, it is meant to balance the feats performed by unrealistic characters that are extremely potent in normal scaling but not so much in stop scaling and the feats performed by semi realistic characters that are extremely potent in stop scaling but not so much in normal. It would do this by ‘weighing’ the feats the same, as in the unrealistic character feat that is extremely potent in normal scaling would be equal to a semi realistic character feat that is extremely potent in stop scaling. This scaling is quite unknown as no one currently uses it.

This was all the scaling systems that I remembered for this post. If I got anything wrong please correct me, but I am quite confident this describes each of the scaling systems. Thanks for reading.
