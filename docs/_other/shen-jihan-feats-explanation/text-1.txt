\*\*\*LOOP DEDUCTION\*\*\*

First he thinks that looking back (during the 2nd loop) was why the count went from 0 to 1; but he discards that idea when the number did not increase to 2 after turning around at the beginning of the 3rd loop.

Due to this, he compares it with walking forward to see which was a more viable answer: Turning back and going forward are both ways to leave.

This is his exact thought process:

\> ‘When I looked back during the second loop I was suddenly thrown into the third. This means the second loop could've been seen as invalid after I looked back. Looking back in itself is a problem so that's definitely not the way to leave.’

So he eliminated the possibility of turning back while \*expecting\* the answer to be the next one. Meaning, he didn't deduce that it was correct for sure and only went on the assumption that it would be true; which is the reason why when he walks by the homeless man again and sees the sign change to negative 0 (the previous sign had 0 but the sign itself was hexagonal. The hexagon represents 6, which was the amount of times he'd be looped back in total. Shěn Jîhān sees this detail and associates it with the "Exit 8" movie \*expecting\* it to be an anomaly)

This is why when he turns back again and emerges from the opposite direction he calls the homeless man an anomaly.

When he loops back again in that instant the only thing left for him to use as a viable deductive reasoning chain was that he was stuck inside a matrix where he had to deduce the determinant, properties and identity correctly
