A new take -C- System : Tiering System for Intelligence Scaling / SCD Matchups

\-C-: Tiering System for Intelligence Scaling / SCD Matchups

The idea behind -C- is to create something similar to power-scaling tiers, such as Wall Level, Building Level, Hill Level, etc. However, instead of measuring destructive power, these tiers would determine whether two characters can even be fairly compared in an intelligence-scaling matchup in the first place.

In powerscaling, a Hill Level character at their maximum might be able to defeat a Wall Level character at their maximum (terms and conditions applied). However, in intelligence scaling, the purpose of these tiers would be different. They would simply indicate whether Character X can reasonably be matched against Character Y, or vice versa.

**The Problem With Normal Matchups**

Take two characters like Kenjaku and Akiyama.

A normal VS matchup might look something like this:

Meth: Akiyama claps Kenjaku due to XYZ reasons.

Normal: Kenjaku would destroy Akiyama, even in Liar Game 💀, due to XYZ reasons.

However, there's a flaw here: both characters operate at radically different levels, and we have no solid idea how they would perform against each other in different environments.

Kenjaku is constantly shown manipulating entire societies and carrying out schemes spanning years. Meanwhile, Akiyama is primarily shown operating within Liar Game, where he manipulates the games themselves and outsmarts his opponents.

If you put Akiyama in JJK, even if you give him knowledge of curses, it's still difficult to determine how he would perform against Kenjaku, who can utilize virtually everything at his disposal against him.

The same applies to Kenjaku. Putting him in Liar Game raises different questions. Can he adapt to the rules and systems of Liar Game? Would he know how to exploit the game's mechanics like Akiyama does? Or would he fumble because he isn't built for that level of system-based environment?

The problem isn't necessarily who is smarter. The problem is whether their respective levels of operation allow for a meaningful comparison in the first place.

This might look like I'm suggesting some kind of dimensional scaling, but not really. **Improve your reading, because you're wrong as hell if that's what you think I'm proposing.**

What I'm Actually Suggesting ,This is an intelligence-scaling framework designed to determine whether two characters' levels of operation overlap enough for them to be meaningfully compared.

I'm not saying that a Tier 2: Local-Global Systems character would automatically beat a Tier 1 character. That's not the point.

The point is that different systems of operation can function so radically differently that we can only speculate about how a character would perform in an unfamiliar environment. We might not have enough evidence to conduct a detailed, meaningful discussion about the matchup.

My suggestion is to introduce tiers, similar to powerscaling, that indicate whether characters can reasonably be matched against each other in the first place.

For example:

Tier 1.3 — Localized Outsmarting: A character who operates primarily within localized games, situations, or limited environments.

Tier 2.1 — Local-Global Systems Outsmarting: A character who operates within broader systems, manipulating interconnected structures and environments on a much larger scale.

A Tier 1.3 character can face a Tier 2.1 character. However, it would be an uphill battle in terms of establishing a fair and meaningful comparison.

How the Matchup Would Look

KENJAKU : Peak Tier 2: Local-Global Systems Outsmarting (almost Tier 2.5: Global Outsmarting), with his own distinct skill set, methods, and experience.

VS

AKIYAMA: Tier 2.5: Local-Global Systems Outsmarting, with a different skill set, methods, and experience.

Verdict: Simply an unfair comparison.

This doesn't necessarily mean Kenjaku is smarter than Akiyama in every conceivable category. It means that their levels of operation, skill sets, and demonstrated environments make a direct comparison difficult to establish fairly.

Doubts and Things to Consider

Again, a higher tier doesn't automatically mean a character is better at everything.

1. Check whether the character actually operates at that level.

First, determine whether the character is genuinely outsmarting a system at that level or merely performing below-level feats within it.

For example, in Code Geass, you could argue that everyone operates at Tier 3: Global Opposition Outsmarting simply because they're involved in large-scale conflicts. However, that doesn't mean every character actually demonstrates that level of outsmarting. Some might be better classified at Tier 2.2: Local-Global Systems Outsmarting.

Being involved in a large-scale system doesn't automatically mean you're capable of outsmarting it at that level.

2. Check the character's actual skill set and kit.

What methods does the character use? What environments are they accustomed to? What kinds of systems can they manipulate? What are their actual strengths and limitations?

A character might operate within a global system without demonstrating the specific skills required to outsmart another character in a different system.

3. Don't confuse the scale of a character's influence with their level of outsmarting.

Take Johan Liebert, for example.

Yes, there are instances where Johan operates on a country-level scale, which could potentially fall under Global Opposition Outsmarting. However, we don't necessarily know enough about how he operates between his allies, how much control he actually has over broader structures, or whether he consistently demonstrates that level of manipulation.

Therefore, rather than automatically claiming Johan is a high-level global outsmarter because he influenced multiple people or institutions across a country, I'd place him at Low Tier 3.1: Global Opposition Outsmarting, most likely, depending on how the evidence is interpreted.

The point is to verify the level instead of blindly assigning it based on the scale of the character's influence.

Final Checklist

* Before assigning a tier or confirming an intelligence-scaling matchup:
* Check whether the character genuinely operates at the proposed level.
* Check their actual skill set and kit.
* Account for radical differences between their respective systems and environments.
* Distinguish between performing within a system and actively outsmarting that system.
* Confirm and verify the character's level through evidence rather than assumptions.
* Determine whether the characters' levels of operation overlap enough to support a meaningful comparison.

Ultimately, this system isn't meant to determine who automatically wins. It's meant to determine whether a matchup is even worth debating in the first place, and whether we have enough evidence to compare the characters meaningfully.
