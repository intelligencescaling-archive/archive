showcasing different levels of scalability

# Case 1: Comprehensive SC Comparison

In this level, characters can be properly scaled, compared, and evaluated without significant differences interfering with the matchup. There is no need for stat or verse equalization, as both characters have story settings that can easily overlap for the purposes of the matchup.

In this case, both diegetic and non-diegetic scaling can be applied freely without having to account for opposing friction or excessive details. Character methodology can be compared and considered alongside modus operandi, kits, frameworks, and abilities to paint a broader picture of the characters in the matchup.

Characters can also be evaluated in terms of intelligence and outsmarting. Interpretations and theories are freely allowed because they do not significantly complicate the matchup.

**The distribution of comparisons in this type includes:**

Extensive categorical and subcategorical comparisons.

Scenario proficiency across various situations, both SCD-wise and story-setting-wise.

Comparisons of strengths, weaknesses, behavioral patterns, limitations, and the ability to overcome limitations.

**examples:**

*ayanokoji vs yuuichi/xyz*
*akiyama vs ayanokoji/xyz*

# Case 2: Contextual and Interpretive SC Comparison

In this level, character matchups must be adjusted to allow for proper scaling while minimizing bias. Scenario complexity is taken into consideration because it is related to character scaling and abilities. An opposing character may also counter a scenario through their own strengths.

However, there is still no need for verse or stat equalization. Only the settings and systems in which the characters operate require some consideration.

Both diegetic and non-diegetic scaling can be applied, although greater emphasis is placed on measurement and evaluation. Character methodology can be compared with some minor restrictions, and certain kits and abilities may be penalized. However, the characters' frameworks and modus operandi remain relevant.

This type of matchup focuses primarily on outsmarting. Interpretations and theories are heavily emphasized to provide a clearer understanding of the characters' nature and how they might approach the matchup. In this particular aspect, Case 2 is the opposite of Case 1.

**These matchups usually involve characters whose settings require contextual consideration. The distribution of comparisons includes:**

Categorical comparisons with certain categories and subcategories removed for various reasons.

An emphasis on scenario proficiency, including role-switching in both SCD and story-based scenarios.

Behavioral analysis as a significant factor in the final judgment.

examples

*patrick vs johan*

*XYZ vs red john case*

# Case 3: Equalized and Framework-Centered SC Comparison

In this level, character matchups require verse and stat equalization. Scenario complexity matters less, while abilities and modus operandi are heavily penalized and kits are significantly restricted. The framework becomes the primary focus.

Both diegetic and non-diegetic scaling can be applied, and both can provide valid viewpoints. However, methodology and category emphasis serve as the primary basis for the final judgment.

Interpretations, abilities, and theories are generally not considered. If they are allowed, they are heavily low-balled. Any speculation must remain grounded in the setting or the relevant SCD scenario.

**These matchups usually involve Smart characters from different types of settings who require equalization before they can be compared under this framework. The distribution of comparisons includes:**

Categorical comparisons that exclude many categories and subcategories.

A limited number of scenario proficiency cases.

No consideration of behavioral factors as an independent category.

**example**

*light vs lelouch*

# Case 4: Incompatible SC Comparison

In this level, a direct character-versus-character comparison is not possible because the characters' verses and settings belong to entirely different leagues. These differences significantly affect their frameworks and abilities, while their modus operandi and kits are also shaped by their respective stories.

Diegetic and non-diegetic scaling can still be applied, but doing so would produce incorrect or unreliable viewpoints because the characters' worlds fundamentally affect the scaling process itself.

Interpretations, abilities, and theories can neither be meaningfully considered nor simply dismissed, as the characters' respective worlds directly influence how their scaling and capabilities function.

**These matchups usually involve smart characters from entirely different types of settings whose stories cannot meaningfully overlap. The distribution of comparisons is therefore not applicable to the original matchup:**

Categorical comparisons cannot be established.

Scenario proficiency cannot be meaningfully evaluated.

Behavioral comparisons cannot be reliably conducted.

The only alternative is to construct a new scenario in which both characters start from scratch. Any resulting comparison would be based on that new scenario rather than on a direct evaluation of the characters in their original settings.

**example**

*baku arc wise vs kenjaku*
*friend vs fang yuan*
