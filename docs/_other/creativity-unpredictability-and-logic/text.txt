Creativity, Unpredictability and Logic

This was something that occurred to me during my "Scaling Light Properly" series and wanted to hear your opinions.

Now the basic way most people scale intelligence is to compare to characters by the use of categories and subcategories. The 16 common main categories, IQ, EQ, SQ, Adversity Capacity (which I think is pretty useless), Intelligence, Sensory, Thinking, Reasoning, Foresight, Planning, Strategy, Manipulation, Deception, Psychology, Adaptability and Judgement (decision making).

Some people also use situations to scale, such as fixed and non-fixed etc, which I think is much more important, especially after the introduction of complexity thinking and Macrocosm/Microcosm (which I still am re-reading). However there is some things that I see which make scaling unfair, which is inevitable due to bias. Such as adding in random categories that only boost a favoured character over another, gaps between difficulties, and category disputes.

Ayanokouji vs Yuuichi is a classic matchup and I personally have Yuuichi > Ayanokouji very high diff. However I do agree, accept and think it’s valid if someone has Ayanokouji > Yuuichi. However, there’s a few problems. Firstly, adding random subcategories that only favour a character. Like adding in musical intelligence, academics, knowledge application, theoretical knowledge. Firstly, these categories obviously favour Ayanokouji > Yuuichi, and instead should be replaced with more neutral categories. Like crystallised intelligence, Gardner’s theory (I personally dont use this though).

Next is the diff gaps. You’re scaling the potential possibility of an intelligent character outsmarting another intelligent character, just because Ayanokouji got way more points over Yuuichi (lets say 60-20), that doesn’t mean he low diffs Yuuichi. And vice versa. The categories show who is better at what, but it doesn’t determine the overall matchup. The diffs between characters are higher than people think, just because one is significantly lower than another, doesn’t mean they’re retarded. Hal vs Ayanokouji isnt no diff. Kanade vs Takuya isnt no diff. Light vs Fukunaga isnt no diff. Outsmarting someone who’s actually intelligent is very hard, so just because a character has one skillset that is superior to another, doesn’t mean it’s a walk in a park. Going back to my acceptance of Ayanokouji > Yuuichi, it’s only a good take if it’s reasonable, not a diff lower than mid difficulty. Actually think of the characters you’re comparing, their personality, their logic, creativity and unpredictability (I’ll explore this later). Ayanokouji’s methodical approach would be difficult against Yuuichi’s creativity and unpredictability. However, Yuuichi’s emotional manipulation would probably be useless against Ayanokouji because of how he favours no risk, logical strategies that he can implement due to his apathy.

And my last point is category disputes. Just because someone has Ayanokouji > Yuuichi in EQ, doesnt mean that automatically, Yuuichi is braindead in EQ. There are obviously categories where characters excel in more than others. And when two characters excel at the same thing, thats when subcategories can be used to determine which character should take the category. I see this a lot in ranking characters in a category. For example, Rank these characters in deception. Yuuichi, Kokichi, Light. I personally have it as Kokichi > Yuuichi > Light. But this doesn’t automatically mean Light’s deception is garbage and a random NPC can see through his lies. It just means the characters that I have over him in deception has, in my opinion, better feats in deception.

Now lets focus on what I think should also be accounted for. Creativity, Unpredictability and Logic. Firstly, logic is the gateway to creativity and unpredictability. Akiyama uses logic as a basis for his strategies, and finds innovative and creative means to create a stronger strategy. Compare this to Ayanokouji, who only focuses on logic. His strategies are often low risk, highly calculated, and barely any contingencies. If Akiyama faced off against Ayanokoji, he would definitely reason this, and tailor his strategy to exploit Ayanokouji. His logic might be on par with Ayanokouji, but his creativity is what allows him to outsmart his opponent.

The three categories are just as important as each other, which logic being more emphasised due to it allowing for innovative and unpredictable opportunities. This is the reason why i scale Yuuichi and Yumeko extremely high. They are good at all three. Yuuichi’s thought process is logical, where he finds lets say a loophole. He creatively uses this loophole, and wins. His "special method". Now lets think about Yuuichi for a second, and the situation he’s in during the all-bet arc. He is looked down upon, and seen as "disappointing" due to his act when he first boarded the cruise. He’s a disappointment because Tsukasa, and the other "experienced" tomodachi game players had expected Yuuichi to be someone invincible. They know Yuuichi is manipulative, and especially his close friends as they’ve seen time and time again that Yuuichi has won despite looking as if he was disadvantaged. Yuuichi first logically analyses his situation, and finds the loophole in the all-bet arc. Where he could use his special method, which was creative. Now, this was unpredictable. Despite everyone being cautious of him, and potentially even relieved of his humiliating lost, he still was unpredictable due to his strategy. The method he used to win the all-bet game was unpredictable. Yuuichi’s logic is low in comparison to other characters like Light Yagami or Akiyama (again, this doesn’t mean Yuuichi is bad at being logical), but his unpredictability and creativity is insanely high, allowing him to win through "illogical" means.

Yumeko is literally known throughout the whole school as being one crazy gambler. Despite this reputation, she too uses the same format as Yuuichi does. Firstly, she logically analyses the situation, then does a lot of unpredictable things as to build a creative strategy. (Or reasons other innovative methods, which allows her to be unpredictable.) Just like yuuichi, she exaggerates her unpredictability and creativity, however, due to her actual insanity, it’s much harder to win and outsmart her. She’s covered all three bases to an exceptional degree. Her willingness to gamble literally everything is unpredictable (as although students know this is her personality, they expect her to have a limit). Now, through this perspective, would someone like Light Yagami or Ayanokouji outsmart Yumeko? Ayanokouji solely relies on fool-proof strategies and logic. He isn’t unpredictable and neither is he creative enough (or at all). Light however, is extremely logical but also creative. For eg. his creative use of the death note should be considered. As although in a pure outsmarting scenario, light obviously wouldn’t have the death note, he would still be creative in his approach to outsmart Yumeko. She is great at all bases, whereas Ayanokouji and Light are only good at one or two. However, if that’s the case, how is akiyama, someone similar to light in the sense of being creative and logical, above Yumeko? Firstly, it’s still a hard result to pull off, for Akiyama to actually outsmart Yumeko. Secondly, according to the three bases listed, Akiyama is much more logical than Yumeko. Logic is the gateway to both creativity and unpredictability, and his creativity is on par with Yumeko’s. It’s to the point where it is superior than Yumeko covering all three bases exceptionally.

And this is why i scale Kanade as the smartest character besides unrealistics (also because I actually haven’t even consumed that much, but even if I did, i doubt Kanade would drop below top 3). Her logic is extremely high, intertwining perfectly with her creativity. Producing the best strategy that I have ever seen. Her strategy was so broken that only divine intervention had to stop it. Her character is extremely logical, allowing her to lead the cast through the first two trials because of her logical reasoning. Her creativity and logic in her invincible strategy made her unpredictable as the culprit. Because no-one expected her to be the culprit. Her unpredictable strategy was a result of her creative use of a loophole (her and her sister both being the culprit), because that loophole was recognised through logical means.

Another category which I personally think is underrated, which definitely upscales characters like Light, Yumeko, Kanade etc, is emotional engagement. One of the reasons why I mentioned Adversity Capacity being useless is because its categories overlap heavily with EQ categories. Self awareness, Metacognition -> ER. Stress tolerance, impulse control, discipline -> EM. Perseverance, Motivation, Determination, Willingness to act -> EE. Other than that, Emotional Engagement, allows for emotional facilitation, and lays the foundation for thinking and strategies. The motivation for a character to actually achieve their goals actually makes them more resistant to attempts to downplay their efforts (such as manipulation). For example, Kokichi managing his emotions and sacrificing himself all for the sake of his plans (which he was extremely determined to do). Or Light yagami being emotionally engaged to the point of deluding himself into being a god. Which I think should be more thought about. Now, I haven’t read/watched Monster yet however I constantly see people say "Johan would manipulate Light into writing his own name in the death note". Johan is a person (not really) who is extremely nihilistic. He would basically manipulate Light into making him believe that his goal is worthless. But because of Light Yagami’s emotional engagement within his own goals, he would be resistant to the attempted manipulation. So this strategy of johan’s would be rendered useless. Furthermore, (bare in mind this is off videos i’ve seen of johan, so basically intuition) i would believe Light would get Logic, Creativity and levels of unpredictability. EE allows for potential boost of Logic, which allows for creativity and unpredictability as mentioned before.

Other categories/ harder to counter

These three bases, also relate to harder to counter categories. These categories stem from the three bases as mentioned, and should be considered when evaluating characters. Logic categories: Which would be experience (shouldn’t really be separated as it comes under crystallised intelligence), resistances (resistance to manipulation/deceit, as explained previously), Limitations (how limited were characters in their verse. Like the restrictions of game rules in liars game or Lawliet surveilling Light). Creativity categories: Cunningness (resourcefulness in creativity and evasion. Constant evasion from strategies and plans. Differing from trap evasion). Setting up traps (self explanatory). Unpredictability categories: Taking risks (how often would the character take a risk, or would rather form a strategy which is heavily shielded and fool-proof)

Overview:

This is just a thought and isn’t something i’ve put a lot of effort in, however, I do think it’s something that should definitely be much more considered when comparing two characters in terms of intelligence

TL;DR: Logic, Creativity, Unpredictability and Emotional Engagement should be considered more whilst scaling intelligence.
