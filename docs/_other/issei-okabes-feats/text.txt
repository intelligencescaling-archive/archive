Overview of Hide.io

Hide.io is a 3D action game released on November 24, 2016. The game has surpassed 10 million installs to date, and popular YouTubers such as Mokou, Koji Seto, and Takucchi have uploaded gameplay videos of it in the past. A specific community has formed around this game, commonly referred to as the Boukaiwai, and its members are known as Boumin.
Boukaiwai is mainly divided into three platforms: X (formerly Twitter), online forums, and LINE Open Chats. Participants range from elementary school students to housewives, covering a wide age range. At the same time, clans exist within the game, and it is customary for players to include their clan name at the beginning of their nickname.

Hide.io is known as a mobile game with a low level of civility, featuring strong anarchic elements, ranking among the top five in this regard. Constant disputes occur in the in-game chat, and similarly, trolling and interpersonal conflicts are common issues on Twitter. On the forums, a constitution was introduced, and political elements were incorporated into the governance, making it an unusual community that goes beyond the typical boundaries of a game.

The main issue that was widely criticized was the presence of cheaters. This game lacked robust anti-cheat security like APEX or PUBG, and since the operation was managed by only one person, cheaters were rampant.
As a result, there were cases where five cheaters existed in a single channel, and even malicious cheats that could delete other players' data appeared. Furthermore, when searching for "Hide.io" on Google, "Hide.io cheat" appears as the third suggestion, highlighting how widespread the presence of cheats has become.

The Intruder Incident Arc

Chapter 1

(Before the Incident) In order to clear the first and second stages of his plan, Okabe needed to obtain the personal information of Boumin and conduct malware tests. He created an account on Twitter under the name "Shinsou Kakurenbo Online" and began posting niche information about Hide.io to attract the attention of Boumin. Feeling that he had gained a certain level of attention and influence, Okabe distributed an APK with a backdoor, cleverly named "β version Hide.io," to Boumin, encouraging them to download it.

(The Beginning of the Incident) One month later, after collecting a significant amount of personal information from Boumin and cheaters, Okabe put his plan into action. He created a Twitter account under the name "Intruder" and posted a roulette-style image containing the names of many well-known Boumin. The post stated that he would expose the personal information of the individuals selected by the roulette, one by one. The roulette contained over 30 Boumin, but it is believed that about half of them were people whose personal information had not actually been identified. Okabe successfully shocked the community by declaring that he had obtained the personal information of everyone in the roulette and would expose it equally. At this point, Okabe had already begun his plan to become the embodiment of fear for cheaters. His first step was to engulf the community in fear, with the goal of making more people recognize him as a symbol of terror.

(The First Victim) Five hours after the first post, Okabe announced on Twitter that "Renokun" had been selected by the roulette. He then uploaded a video to YouTube titled "The First Honorable Victim," in which he exposed Renokun's personal information. Furthermore, Okabe launched a website called "Hide.io People Control," allowing anyone to view the leaked personal information on the site. On the homepage of the site, it stated, "Destroying the toxic substance known as the Hide.io community," which was intended to manipulate all Boumin into harboring hostility toward the Intruder. While living his daily life as a high school student, Okabe produced videos exposing specific personal information in just one day, and published that information on his self-made website alongside the videos.

(The Second Victim) The next day, on the 11th, a person named Teiou became the target. In Teiou's case, a somewhat problematic photo was leaked. (Misdirection Strategy) On the following day, the 12th, a person named "Boku wa Mokuroh" became the third victim. At this point, Okabe executed the misdirection strategy. In the video, he showed the name of a high school registered on the bookmark bar and the surname "Watanabe." After the information had become somewhat well-known, he deleted the video, leading Boumin to mistakenly conclude that the Intruder attended a school with that name and had the surname "Watanabe." The background behind this strategy involved the following four objectives:

1- To distinguish between insightful individuals and those who are not
Many people were deceived by this series of misdirections, but among them were a few individuals with sharp observation skills who felt a sense of unease.
"Would someone who created such a meticulous plan really make such a basic mistake?"
"This high school is located in Chiba Prefecture. Does this mean the Intruder is trying to deliberately plant the preconceived notion that 'the culprit is a Boumin from Chiba Prefecture'?"
Okabe saw these individuals, who raised such sharp questions, as targets to be cautious of and used this misdirection as a litmus test to flush them out. Furthermore, by spreading the analyses of these insightful individuals, Okabe also aimed for a secondary effect: to make the structure of the incident even more complicated, thereby making it harder to grasp the overall picture.

2- To escalate the scale of the incident
Okabe exploited Boumin who lacked insight but had a strong sense of justice, prompting them to report to the school that a student with the surname "Watanabe" was exposing personal information online, thus involving an unrelated high school and escalating the scale of the incident. The surname "Watanabe" ranks sixth in frequency nationwide, and it would not be unusual for multiple individuals with that name to attend the same high school. Okabe turned this common surname to his advantage, ultimately attempting to use even the students enrolled at the school in his plan.

3- To give Boumin false hope
At the time, in the Boukaiwai, Boumin were in a desperate situation, watching helplessly as their comrades were exposed one after another and retirees followed suit. However, when the name of the high school attended by the Intruder was revealed, Boumin mistook it for a “weapon of retaliation” and found hope. With the posts halting after the 12th, everyone likely thought, "This commotion is over." Simply exposing people mechanically would eventually become monotonous. It is because of hope that deeper despair is created.

4- To attract the attention of Boumin to the videos uploaded afterward
Boumin united under the belief that "we should not view any videos or websites uploaded by the Intruder, but only report them. If we don't view the exposures, the act will become meaningless." However, concerned about this situation, Okabe deliberately included fake personal information in the video to exploit Boumin's curiosity about whether future uploaded videos would contain actual personal information, thus guiding them back to the exposure videos.

Furthermore, the roulette was not random; the targets were chosen intentionally by Okabe. It was revealed that the leakage of personal information occurred through the use of the APK. The "APK installation issue" was one of the core messages that Okabe wanted to convey through the Intruder Incident, and having the Rekishi Kaisetsu bot write about it was an extremely clever indirect manipulation.

Chapter 2

Okabe, wearing a hyottoko mask and a black hoodie, made a strong impression on Boumin. Okabe revealed his true identity and, after talking about his background, stated that this entire incident was the first phase of his plan, which involved three main objectives: "testing malware, increasing visibility, and enhancing the credibility of information." (In reality, a fourth element was also included—gathering and uniting the hatred of Boumin toward himself.) With all these objectives accomplished, it became clear just how high Okabe’s planning capabilities were. As part of the second phase, on May 23, Okabe launched a channel called "Ujimushi," where he posted videos distributing cheats embedded with malware. He stated that he would have script kiddies (a derogatory term for hackers with low technical skills) download the malware, and then expose the personal information of those script kiddies who had downloaded it. The cheat distribution video was uploaded on May 23, and considering that Deep Hide.io distributed the beta APK on May 3, it can be inferred that, after a 20-day test period, Okabe enhanced the malware's effectiveness from 50% to 80%. The cheat distribution video was played 812 times over the course of a month, and Okabe successfully obtained the personal information of many script kiddies. Okabe believed that the unprecedented incident—where the distributed cheat APK was actually malware—would serve as a strong deterrent to both those who distribute cheats and those who download them. Okabe feared that eliminating the cheaters would cause Boumin's hatred toward him to fade. To prevent this, he made provocative remarks in the video such as, "Thanks to your ridiculous uproar," and "You Boumin were nothing more than lab rats for my experiment." Furthermore, he declared his ultimate goal: to extract all the information from iGene (the operators of Hide.io), seize control of Hide.io, and become its supreme ruler. Upon taking power, he announced he would abolish chat, levels, clans, and even usernames. Unsurprisingly, this radical declaration—rooted in extreme egalitarianism—was met with widespread criticism. In this way, Okabe ensured that he could continue to expose and eliminate cheaters while maintaining the Boumin’s hatred toward him. At the end of the video, Okabe declared war on the cheaters by stating, "The only ones who should kill, are those who are prepared to be killed."

Final Chapter

On July 1, Okabe created new Twitter and YouTube accounts under the name O_REQUIEM. He then uploaded a video titled "The Last Sacrifice, Okaokapi." In the video, Okabe stated, "In the video I uploaded yesterday, my address and last name are visible. I want you to find it yourselves and report it to iGene, telling them to have me arrested for obstructing business." Okabe believed that by being arrested, he would become a deterrent to cheaters and trolls. At the end of the video, Okabe revealed his address and full name, took off his mask, and said, "The only ones who should kill are those who are prepared to be killed." Okabe's actions throughout this entire process were designed to create a common enemy in both the intruder and Okokapi, which unified the previously divided Boumin and forum members. At the same time, by posting fake cheat distribution videos containing malware, his ultimate goal was to deter the future posting of cheat videos and the individuals who would download them. From his appearance as the Deep Hide.io intruder on April 15th to the conclusion of the incident on July 1st, over two and a half months, Okabe manipulated the actions and emotions of both Boumin and cheaters to bring about the planned conclusion. This plan resembled a Zero Requiem scenario, and by transforming the Intruder Incident into a theatrical crime rather than a mere cybercrime, Okabe succeeded in capturing the public's attention. Lelouch concentrated all the hatred of the world upon himself, sacrificing his life as the tyrannical emperor to create a stepping stone toward the people's wish for a "kinder  world." I speculate that Okabe may have tried to transform the anarchic Boukaiwai into a "kinder world" by mimicking it.

What this event brought to the community was silence. And it was no wonder. Everyone had come to realize that they had been dancing in the palm of Okabe’s hand. Even those who harbored a deep hatred toward him found themselves confused—because they now understood that his goal had been to make them hate him. With that revelation, they no longer knew how to express their own emotions. On the other hand, as a result of this video, some people began to praise Okabe for his actions, and there were even those who said, "What Okaokapi did should be remembered in history." Indeed, the fact that Okabe "attempted to cleanse cheaters from the top down" was likely a key point. In fact, Okabe took measures to prevent the future uploading of cheat distribution videos and the downloading of such videos by using fake cheat distribution videos laced with malware. Additionally, the suppression of the harmful cheater known as "Bunimushi" was seen as beneficial to the Boumin.

After the incident ended, cheaters were rarely seen in the game, and conflicts within the community also diminished. Indeed, the case of cheaters having their personal information extracted and exposed by malware was something unprecedented in other communities. And, as Okabe had stated, the fact that those who had been at odds with each other acted and united for the same purpose was an undeniable truth. In the end, it remains unclear whether Okabe was arrested, but 10 days later, Okabe, realizing that no one had reported him to the police, temporarily revived his deleted Twitter account and tweeted, "To take responsibility for what I've done, I will turn myself in to the police." A week later, Okabe posted an image of the inventory list of confiscated items. This action seems to have been part of a backup plan (Plan B) in case no one reported him to the authorities. The fact that his real name appeared on the image strongly suggests that Okabe did indeed turn himself in. The next day, Okabe's account was deleted, and he disappeared without a trace.

1. The email address associated with the Twitter account starts with "th," and both email addresses have the same number of characters.
2. A screenshot of the list of Google accounts owned by exe includes an account named EZ and a shared number, 821.

Based on these pieces of evidence, the equation "exe = EZ" was confirmed.
Okabe directly contacted exe and, just like in the Intruder Incident, used a cheat tool to trick them into downloading malware and extracting personal information. Terrified by this exposure, exe ceased all tweets from that point forward. In this way, the conflict between Japanese Boumin and Thai Boumin came to a close with a victory for the Japanese Boumin, thanks to Okabe's efforts. After confirming that exe's activities had ceased, Okabe deleted his Twitter account. If Okabe had not appeared, the Japanese server would likely have been attacked as exe had predicted. As a result of this incident, Okabe became known as the "lethal weapon" of the Boukaiwai. By this point, he had effectively taken control of the Boukaiwai.
