Time calculation:

The dialogue after Machiya finished memorizing and reasoning adds up to around 700 words—and that’s not even counting pauses, suspense moments, or pacing adjustments. The moments leading up to the feat take up only a few panels.
Realistically, this entire sequence would take longer than the 3 minutes the murderer mentioned. But, of course, the author didn’t account for this—because who would? This is fiction.
However, this actually strengthens the interpretation that Machiya completed the feat in just a few seconds, as the sheer volume of dialogue after the fact suggests the time frame was extremely compressed.
But getting back to facts….

Determining When Machiya Started the Feat
There are different interpretations of when Machiya began his feat. However, the most conservative estimate—the one that weakens the feat the most—assumes he started right when Rascal asked him to reveal his identity.
During the feat: 180 words were spoken.

After the feat: 700+ words were spoken.

Total words in the scene: 880 words

Since the entire "trial" scene lasted 3 minutes in DeadTube’s world, that means speech occurs at 293 words per minute in that setting.
In the real world, a reasonable estimate is 140 words per minute (not even accounting for suspense pauses).
This means that time in DeadTube’s world is 2.09x faster than reality (293:140 = 2.09).

Converting the 180 words spoken during the feat to real-world time:

180 ÷ 2.09 = 86 real-world words

86 ÷ 140 = 61% of a minute (~37 seconds)

This suggests Machiya completed the feat in approximately 37 seconds, even under the most limiting assumptions.

This is further supported by the fact that Machiya subtly reacted—tilting his head—when Mai Mashiro made a key comment about the case. She stated, "I have no reason to get involved with this sort of third-rate thriller film." Machiya later used this exact statement to prove that the scenario wasn't meant to be a thriller but a horror film, which played a crucial role in solving the mystery.

Suspense Timing & Speech Pacing:
Even if we factor in suspense moments (indicated by "…"), there were 12 during the feat and 40 afterward. This aligns proportionally with the overall text distribution: 20% (180/880) of the total words occurred during the feat, while 80% (700/880) happened afterward. Similarly, the suspense marks follow a 23% (12/52) to 77% (40/52) split, further reinforcing this proportion.
When considering other suspense markers like "!!" and "?", there were 12 during the feat and 19 afterward. However, exclamation points ("!") and interrobangs ("!?") often indicate heightened urgency and faster speech compared to ellipses ("…"), which tend to slow pacing down.
Notably, exclamation-heavy dialogue was concentrated during the feat itself, meaning the speech flow would be even faster in that segment. Out of the 12 suspense markers present during the feat, 6 were either "!!" or "!?". In contrast, the later dialogue contained only 3 such markers out of 19, with the rest being simple question marks ("?").
This suggests that Machiya likely completed the feat even faster than our original estimate, as the pacing within his thought process was naturally sped up.

The only way to challenge this whole calculation would be to prove that word count is an unreliable metric or that another method would better reflect the actual time frame. While not a flawless metric, word count provides a reasonable proportional estimate for the scene’s pacing.
