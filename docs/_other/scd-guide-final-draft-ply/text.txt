* tiers are arbitrary. there are times when a character cannot be placed into a tier for various reasons, such as differences, etc.

Explanation and role of  narrative , feats and anti-feats in the scaling and matchup
Feats : Things and notable actions the character implicitly or explicitly demonstrated/accomplished.
Anti-feats : blunders by characters . two types - impediment or non impediment
Statements: Explicit claims regarding a character's intelligence, abilities, accomplishments, reputation, or capabilities.
Statements should be considered alongside their context, reliability, and consistency with the character's actual portrayal.
Methods/Processes:   "how " a character/feat achieves a result. it's about the pathology of the formulation and the steps of the feat rather than just the outcome
Narrative: The broader portrayal of a character through the story.
Narrative should be properly read and discerned rather than treated as an automatic substitute for feats. It can include recurring portrayal, characterization, comparisons, thematic framing, consistency, and the character's established role within the story.
Narrative isn't some magical excuse to scale a character higher than their feats, nor is it something that should automatically be dismissed because it isn't a direct feat.
*narrative word is literally the bogeyman for scaling/scalers for some reason. like read properly and discern
READ .THE NARRATIVE. PROPERLY.
also narrative is called story-wise.
Ability: What the character is capable of doing, demonstrated or established through feats, methods/processes, statements, narrative, etc.
ability is not necessarily the same as what a character consistently performs in every situation.
if only methods and feats are taken into account  then it's proven ability aka establish ability basically a rigorous way of judging ability expecting to to work consistently.
Outsmarting Situations:The specific circumstances in which a character's intelligence is being tested.
SCALING SYSTEM:
word of advice - don't scale any fear/character in isolation & independence  ; scale rather taking account of continuity and sequence of events. ask "is the character doing something logical to their past, character and environment or not?"
normal scaling (default system): taking everything into account, including anti-feats, feats, abilities, methods etc.
note: normal =/= narrative
non normal scaling  :
* stop scaling - only explicit, explainable feats with steps/steps that can be derived
* pure methodology - significant focus on rigour and steps
* methodology scaling - focus on the process/method of a feat, either implicit or explicit
(other methods are mostly neurodivergent or neurotic)
normal + non-normal scaling:
1. Complete Scaling Method
2. Dynamic Gauntlet system
3. any scaling that measures a character holistically

Reference Series
These series are mostly familiar within SCD on a broad and overall level. They make up a lot of the commonly used reading/scaling material in SCD. The main reference pool is Classroom of the elite, Tomodachi Game, Death Note (the big three ), Liar Game, Usogui, and Monster, while Code Geass, The Mentalist, and Danganronpa are used more occasionally or as supplementary references.
The reference pool and its series have characters across many different tiers and levels, making them useful for comparing and scaling characters against each other.
Familiarity here generally means broad knowledge of the series and its SCD-relevant material, rather than knowing every feat or interpretation in depth.
Reasons why I'm referring to TG, COTE, and DN  as the  big three are:
First, popularity and starters of SCD.
Second, all three are digestible and diegetic. COTE has digestible and diegetic hierarchy & dynamic of smart characters, TG has digestible and diegetic games & mind games, and DN has diegetic and digestible opposition and difficulty.
In other words, these series don't just have characters who are called smart. Their stories diegetically demonstrate and establish intelligence through games, conflicts, hierarchies, opposition, strategies, and interactions between characters.
This makes TG, COTE, and DN useful starting points for newer people to center themselves around when learning SCD. They provide recognizable examples of different types of intelligence and outsmarting while also giving people material that can be directly examined and scaled.
Plus, most people already have a general understanding of these series and characters, making it easier to learn by “osmosis” — you don't necessarily need to read the entire series yourself to have a basic understanding of the characters, their feats, and their general scaling.
This doesn't mean these three series are inherently superior to everything else. They're being used primarily because of their popularity, familiarity, diegetic material, and usefulness as reference points.

You can make one easily by using any 3×3 grid template in Canva and replacing the images with your own. Outcome Difficulties:

NOTE: Difficulties of an outcome are given estimations by the person scaling the characters in question.

Legend: - NEED TO DISCUSS SCENARIOS

- Difficulty estimations can find themselves in between certain difficulties, representing a middle ground between the two.
Example: No-Very Low Diff; High-Very High Diff;

- The symbols:
 (+) - Implies the difficulty could be higher than what is estimated.
 (-) - Implies the difficulty could be lower than what is estimated.

Scaling:
Will take a standard character A vs B matchup for reference:

No Diff – Almost nothing would be able to change the outcome of this match. Character A would have every winning condition and be significantly stronger than Character B in almost, if not, every aspect. Character A should still be able to guarantee victory even if they were to self-sabotage or to place themselves at a massive disadvantage.

Very Low Diff – This means that Character A’s victory would provide little challenge at all. Even their basic skill set would be enough to ensure victory. At this point, for example, even if Character A were to put himself at a massive disadvantage, that would do little to help Character B’s chances of winning given the massive overall difference in skill.

Low Diff – This means that Character A would not need to put forth their full effort to guarantee victory. This could also mean that Character A is superior to Character B is most or all facets of outsmarting. In that case Character A wouldn’t have a weakness that could even potentially allow Character B to win. Character A could ensure victory even from a disadvantageous position.

Mid Diff – This means that an average showing from Character A is enough to guarantee victory over Character B. They wouldn’t likely need to utilize their top end skills to ensure their win and Character B even with their best showing wouldn’t have a chance to win.

High Diff – Character A would need to utilize their full potential, but doing so would guarantee their victory. Character A using their full skillset and effort will ensure they come out ahead.

Very High Diff – Character A would need to use their full skillset and be fully prepared against an opponent who isn’t too far below them. However, as long as they don’t underestimate Character B, their full effort will be enough to ensure their win. It will take Character A’s best showing to ensure this. Despite this being close Character A’s efforts should manage to beat Character B.

Extreme Diff - This means that Character A’s victory would prove to be extremely difficult to guarantee, this represents the highest level of opponent they could beat more often than not. Situation variety and context are almost as relevant as they can ever be in determining the winner, as for example Character A may be superior to character B in some facets of outsmarting, but Character B might counter Character A very well. This could also be opposite where Character A counters well despite a slightly inferior skillset.

Could Go Either Way - Represents a 50/50 matchup. In this case the matchup is too close to declare an overall winner. Neither character’s full effort would be enough to guarantee victory against the other without some kind of additional circumstances. One character ever so slightly underestimating the other for example may be the difference between victory and defeat.
