# The Werewolf Game

Let's take one more example. Supposedly there is a werewolf game, and one of them figures out the other for being a healer who can bring the dead out of life. Now the person, has given up the full trust to him. Why?

Mathematically every person have created n number of pairs where n = t!/((t-r)!r!) where t is the number of players and r is the number of werewolves. So when one person dies, there is a rearrangement of order. Kill the least important person here, who's likely not to be taken and the orders remains the same giving a better chance for the werewolves.

But back to the healer, the person gives his trust because the number of players are t-1 in his mind so now he can give up and start thinking from the healer's perspective.

Natural move for werewolves in the starting is to try and make everyone believe that one of them is a healer.

# How to build a Theoritical framework

**Understanding the Mindset :** The first step is to understand the mindset, that includes personality, goals and way of operating things. For example a lot of characters uses Indirect methods, and a lot uses direct method, some uses physical presence and the others choose to remain hidden that gives us the attention lover and the minimilist approach.

**Distribution of cats :** To understand their lines of thinking is to understand what they're Good at that follows up with their losses and wins.

**Environment:** Characters like Baku, Hal are good at Fixed Situation while character like Fang Yuan is good at Non Fixed Situation. Losses should be counted yes, but if it's not their environment then it shouldn't be as heavily criticized for it.
