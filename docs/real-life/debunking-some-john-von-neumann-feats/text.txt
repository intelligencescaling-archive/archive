Debunking some John von Neumann feats

I read an article about him [here](https://www.theintrinsicperspective.com/p/john-von-neumann-shot-lightning-from), so I would like to debunk some claims about him

**8-digit division at age 6**

This stems from a misquotation. His biographer, Norman Macrae, was actually referring to the adult Johnny, and even then, he noted that the claim of multiplying 8-figure numbers was "half-wrong."

**Verbatim recall of any book**

Most stories (like his recitation of A Tale of Two Cities) involve passages he purposefully memorized to improve his English or German. It was a family habit to learn long texts by heart.

**Memorizing the telephone book**

This likely started as a joke von Neumann made himself that he knew all the numbers, he just didn't know which names they belonged to. His assistant, Paul Halmos, noted this was "undocumented and unverifiable."

**He was fluent at 8 Languages**

He was fluent in four languages as an adult: Hungarian, German, French, and English. The story that he and his father joked in Classical Greek was also denied by his family, debunking that myth and despite being labeled a child prodigy, he did not begin learning English until he was 11 years old.

**He singlehandedly invented the von Neumann architecture**

The "von Neumann architecture" was largely the work of J. Presper Eckert and John Mauchly (the builders of ENIAC). Von Neumann wrote a report (the First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC) that synthesized and formalized their ideas using "cool jargon." Because his name was the only one on the distributed report, the design became permanently linked to him.

>Johnny borrowed (we must not say plagiarize) anything from anybody, with great courtesy and aplomb. His mind was not as original as Leibniz’s or Newton’s or Einstein’s, but he sees other people’s original, though fluffy, ideas and quickly changed them in expanded detail into a form where they could be useful for scholarship and for mankind.

**Here are some anti feats about him**

\- He sucks at driving

He was so notoriously bad at driving that a specific intersection in Princeton was unofficially named “von Neumann’s Corner” because he had so many accidents there. He and his wife would buy a new car every year, usually because von Neumann had totaled the previous one.

His daughter stated that

Also Johnny’s second wife’s personal account strongly suggests he had OCD

>A drawer could not be opened unless it was pushed in and out seven times, the same with a light switch, which also had to be flipped seven times before you could let it stay.

Even though some of his feats were debunked here, he was still insanely intelligent (to the point that some Nobel Prize winners were astonish by his intellect), and I believe he had one of the highest IQs ever documented in history
