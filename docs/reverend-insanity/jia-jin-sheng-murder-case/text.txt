Before getting into the details of the murder and Fang Yuan's calculated strategy, it's essential to first cover some key background information. Understanding these aspects is crucial, as they provide the necessary context for the events that are explained in this doc. Without a clear understanding of them, it would be difficult to fully comprehend the intricacies of Fang Yuan's plan and his approach to the trial. Black Coffin Luck:
As mentioned in our Corpse Box Strategy doc, Fang Yuan got cursed by something known as Black Coffin Luck right from Chapter 1 itself, after he uses the Spring Autumn Cicada to rebirth. This curse is a variant of bad luck tied to the Luck Path, and it significantly impacts Fang Yuan's every move.
The main effect of Black Coffin Luck is that it drastically reduces Fang Yuan's success rate, making his goals harder to achieve. Also, it acts as a contextual adversity that diminishes his luck, which is already lower than his natural luck. For example, if Fang Yuan’s natural success rate for an action is 7 out of 10, Black Coffin Luck might reduce it to 5 out of 10 or even 3 out of 10. This means Fang Yuan needs to exert more effort and be extra cautious with his plans to counterbalance this reduced luck.
Do note that Black Coffin Luck is not just a temporary drawback. It's something that's with Fang Yuan for 24/7 from Chapter 1 itself, creating harder challenges and attracting more problems to him. To counter this, Fang Yuan must be extra careful and has to find ways to not only mitigate its effects but also to exploit it for his benefit.

Gu Yue Clan:
Gu Yue Clan is the clan to which Fang Yuan belongs, situated in Qing Mao Mountain alongside the Bai Clan and Xiong Clan. It is considered a middle-low tier clan in terms of power and influence within the Southern Border.
Gu Yue Clan has ongoing conflicts with both the Bai Clan and Xiong Clan, though it maintains a more cordial relationship with the Jia Clan, which provides some stability and support.
The clan is led by the clan leader, Gu Yue Bo, who oversees its operations alongside other elders of the clan who help manage its affairs.

Jia Clan:
Jia Clan is a relatively young but powerful clan in the Southern Border. While it may not be as old as the Gu Yue Clan, it is significantly stronger than it due to it being a large size clan and having more resources.
The Jia Clan operates merchant caravans that travel throughout the Southern Border, facilitating trade by buying and selling goods across the region. These caravans are crucial to the clan's wealth and influence.
One of these caravans is supervised by the Jia Clan Leader’s eldest son, Jia Fu, and his third son, Jia Jin Sheng. This particular caravan passes through Qing Mao Mountain, home to the Gu Yue Clan, once a year, creating an alliance connection between the two clans.

Flower Wine Monk:
Flower Wine Monk, a powerful demonic cultivator, was a Rank 5 Poison Path Master, known for his perverse and dangerous nature. He approached the Gu Yue Clan under mysterious circumstances, initially pretending to be interested in purchasing the rare Orchid Moon. However, his true intentions were far more sinister because he later attacked the Gu Yue Clan, leading to the death of many.
At that time, the leader of Gu Yue Clan, known as Fourth Gen Gu Yue and the clan’s elders, confronted him in battle. In a self-sacrificial act, the Fourth Generation Gu Yue gave his life to defeat the Flower Wine Monk, managing to strike a fatal blow. Though mortally wounded, the Flower Wine Monk survived and escaped, but his injuries were so severe that his death became inevitable.
Because of his hatred towards Gu Yue Clan, the Flower Wine Monk left behind an inheritance for a future successor. This legacy was meant to ensure that someone would avenge his death and take down the Gu Yue Clan in his place. One of the most significant items in his inheritance was the Liquor Worm, a vital Gu worm for low-rank cultivators.
Fang Yuan found Flower Wine Monk Inheritance and obtained the Liquor Worm as part of the Flower Wine Monk's inheritance after searching for it over several nights. Along with the Liquor Worm, Fang Yuan also acquired several other valuable Gu, including the Jade Skin Gu, White Boar Gu, and Earth Communication Ear Grass, etc.

Liquor Worm:
The Liquor Worm is a Rank 1 Gu with a distinctive, worm-like appearance. It resembles a silkworm and emits a soft, pearl-white glow from its body. The Liquor Worm, despite being just Rank 1, is highly sought after for its practical uses in cultivation.
This Gu is particularly valuable to Rank 1 Gu Masters, as it helps them refine their primeval essence and provides a slight boost to their cultivation realm, enabling them to advance by one small realm. What makes the Liquor Worm especially sought after is its safe and effective nurturing method: when used to nurture an aperture, it leaves no harmful repercussions or risks, making it far superior to other methods. However, its effects are limited to Rank 1 cultivators, offering no benefits to those at Rank 2 cultivators.
Although it is classified as a Rank 1 Gu, its value is often compared to that of a Rank 2 Gu due to the significant advantages it provides to Rank 1 cultivators.

Footprint Gu:
Footprint Gu is a unique and visually distinct Gu. It is shaped like a human foot and is about the size of a person’s palm. Its material resembles translucent yogurt, giving it a smooth and soft appearance. The surface of the Footprint Gu glows with a yellow-green luster, making it easily recognizable.
To activate the Footprint Gu, a Gu Master has to pour their Primeval Essence into it. Once infused, the Gu grows brighter before bursting into a cloud of yellow-green powder. This powder envelopes the target, revealing the last 30,000 footprints they have left behind.
Footprint Gu is an incredibly useful tool for tracking and investigation, providing precise details of the target's movements over a period of time. Therefore, it is often used while investigating crimes.

Bamboo Gentleman Gu:
Bamboo Gentleman Gu has a long, slim body resembling a bamboo tube. Its color is a rich jade green, and its surface emits a soft white glow. While phasmids of this kind are typically longer than a palm, this particular Bamboo Gentleman Gu is notably smaller, being only the size of a fingernail.
This Gu feeds on honesty, using it as its primary sustenance. When placed inside a Gu Master’s aperture, the Bamboo Gentleman Gu interacts with the primeval sea, infusing it with a gentle green glow. However, its most unique characteristic is its ability to detect lies. If the Gu Master who carries the Bamboo Gentleman Gu utters even a single lie, the Gu detects the deception and changes its body color from green to another shade, signaling dishonesty.
Due to this ability, the Bamboo Gentleman Gu is often used in investigation and interrogation, serving as an effective lie detector.

Rock Gambling Den:
In Gu World, many Gu worms exist, each with unique shapes, sizes, and effects. Every Gu worm requires specific food to survive, and without it, they can only last for a short time before dying. However, when faced with starvation, Gu worms have a survival mechanism, in which they can enter a state of hibernation by undergoing self-sealing.
For instance, the Liquor Worm, when self-sealed, forms a white cocoon around its body, curling up and falling into a sleeping state. This self-sealing allows Gu worms to preserve themselves for extended periods.
Sometimes, Gu Masters stumble upon these sealed Gu worms in the form of rocks or cocoons.
When they cut the rocks, they often find themselves in possession of highly valuable and rare Gu. Stories of such chance discoveries gave rise to the idea of the Rock Gambling Den.
These businesses capitalize on the allure of chance and fortune. Owners fill their Rock Gambling Dens with a variety of rocks sold at different price points. Most of these rocks are empty, but few contain Gu worms. Gu Masters, who want to test their luck and possibly uncover a rare Gu, purchase these rocks.

Context:
The Jia Clan is a wealthy and influential clan in the Southern Border, known as a traveling merchant powerhouse. They operate like a moving market, journeying across the region to trade with various clans.
One of their regular stops is at the Qing Mao Mountain, where they conduct business with Gu Yue Clan every year. Therefore their relationship is of mutual benefit and respect.
This particular merchant group traveling through Qing Mao Mountain this time is led by Jia Fu, a Rank 4 Gu Master and the eldest son of the Jia Clan Leader. Along with him is his half-brother Jia Jin Sheng.

However, the challenge was in the fact that Fang Yuan didn't know which rock it was in, and there were more than a thousand rocks in the gambling den. Basically, this was no different from blindly gambling, relying entirely on luck, something Fang Yuan is not good with because of Black Coffin Luck curse.
Also, it is important to note that Fang Yuan’s decision to visit the gambling den wasn’t purely for obtaining the Mudskin Toad Gu. He had two additional problems that he intended to solve with this plan.

Debunk Attempt:
Some might argue that Fang Yuan’s previous life experience with gambling and the fact that he owned and operated a large-scale gambling den in his previous life would give him a significant edge here. After all, someone with that level of expertise could supposedly increase their chances of finding the Mudskin Toad Gu, right?

Counter:
Not exactly. While it’s true that Fang Yuan’s past experience boosts his gambling skills, it doesn’t magically change the odds of the situation. The Mudskin Toad Gu is still just one Gu hidden among thousands of rocks. No amount of skill can alter the sheer statistical reality of the gamble. It’s still a game of luck at the end of the day.
What his experience helps in is giving him a sharper eye and a better understanding of the psychology and strategies behind gambling dens. This allows him to approach the situation with more caution and creativity, turning what would otherwise be a blind gamble into something slightly more calculated. But at the end of the day, the odds remain stacked against him.

Fang Yuan's Calculated Gamble:
Rock den gambling is a risky game of chance where gamblers pick one rock out of thousands, hoping to find a living Gu inside. The odds are stacked heavily against the player as there’s roughly a 1 in 10 chance of finding a Gu. Even then, most of the time, it ends up being a dead Gu, making the gamble worthless.
But Fang Yuan wasn’t gambling blindly. Using his memories from his previous life and piecing together subtle clues, he managed to find the exact counter where the person in his previous life found the rock that had Mudskin Toad Gu. He remembered that in his past life, the person who obtained this Gu found it inside golden-purple lustrous rock.
This narrowed his options significantly, but not completely. Now, Fang Yuan had to choose from a smaller pool of 20 purple-golden rocks. While this was a big improvement from thousands of rocks, it still left him with only a 1 in 20 chance of picking the correct one. Despite the reduced odds, it doesn’t change the fact that it's still a gamble.

Fang Yuan began carefully analyzing each rock, relying on his sharp instincts and knowledge to narrow down the choices. Using his hands, he assessed their weight, size, and shape, methodically eliminating options one by one.
The first rock he rejected was flat and too small to feasibly contain a Gu. It's because its size didn’t align with the space needed to hold the Mudskin Toad Gu.
The second rock he discarded was exceptionally lustrous, almost too good to ignore. However, its size was off, being about 30% smaller than what Fang Yuan remembered the correct rock to be in his previous life. This discrepancy was enough for him to rule it out.
The third rock he removed had the perfect size, but there was another issue. Given that the Mudskin Toad Gu is a frog, its fossil would likely leave an uneven shape. This rock, being too perfectly round, didn’t match the characteristics he was looking for.

Fang Yuan, in his previous life, had come across rumors about a Gu that was able to scan fossils and detect which ones contained living Gu worms inside them. However, after investigating and experimenting with these claims, Fang Yuan discovered they were nothing but baseless rumors. Even if a Gu with such a power did exist, it would likely be a closely guarded secret, known only to a select few individuals. Its existence would definitely not be common knowledge or available to the public.

Fang Yuan also understood that the terrain of Qing Mao Mountain, along with the nearby ranges, wasn’t ideal for the development of a proper gambling den. The environment and economy of the region lacked the foundation required to sustain large-scale operations.
Fang Yuan recalled from his past life that in the eastern region, gambling dens were a common sight. There, every small village had its own gambling den, and in some places, the practice had evolved even further. Large, fortified regions were established purely for the purpose of gambling.
With his sharp memory, Fang Yuan even recall specific villages that were famous for their expertise in this field. Villages like Pan Shi, Gu Mu, and Cang Jing were specialized in Rock Gambling. Fang Yuan also reasoned that, in this current time, the gambling dens in the eastern region were still operating at full capacity, drawing in an enormous number of gamblers.

Fang Yuan turned his focus back to the 20 remaining rocks. Using his deduction skills and keen intuition, he eliminated 14 of the rocks, narrowing his options even further. Now he had 6 rocks left. So, his chance of finding Mudskin Toad Gu among them was 1 in 6.
He was sure that the Mudskin Toad Gu was hibernating in one of these rocks. His assessment was based on both the structure of the rocks and the likelihood of a Gu being inside, and he was confident that there was an 80% chance of finding the Gu among them. This wasn’t just luck but rather calculated risk and careful reasoning.
