Novels Sherlock vs some criminal cases

\*Going off feats from the Adventures and the Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes A Study in Scarlet, and the Hound of Baskerville

\*Sherlock Holmes will start with base resources, having Dr. Watson and access to modern technology as would be found on Baker's Street, though he may build his influence from if he is capable of it

\*All criminals start off unaware, with their canon opponents disappearing or taking a backseat to Holmes

**The Bay Harbor Butcher - CLOSED no diff**

*Identifying the Killer -* Sherlock no diffs. With his work as a consulting detective, he would easily be able to weasel his way into Miami Metro. From there, it would take a single glance for Sherlock to zone in on Dexter from clues that would be imperceptible to anyone else such as traces of soil on his boots or microexpressions and gestures.

*Stopping the Killer/Proof of Guilt* \- Sherlock low diffs. Doakes was able to catch Dexter in the act by simply tracking him and ambushing him at his boat. Sherlock would do the same, albeit with a more foolproof strategy. The only worry would be that Dexter is very cautious and alert, so Holmes would have to be careful.

**Heisenberg** \- **CLOSED low diff (-)**

*Identifying the Killer* \- Sherlock no to low diffs. Sherlock would quickly work with the DEA, particularly Hank, who he would identify is passionate about the case. It would take little time for him to connect the dots between genius chemist Walter White and Heisenberg, and a direct meeting would seal the deal.

*Stopping the Killer/Proof of Guilt* \- Sherlock mid diffs. Sherlock would have to exercise caution due to Walt's vast resources, but he would easily be able to obtain proof of guilt, maybe through very mundane means like tailing Walt in a disguise.

**Kira - OPEN low-mid diff**

*Identifying the Killer* \- Sherlock high diffs. The primary problem would be even getting into a position where he can analyze Kira's killings, which he can't do with his meager resources at Baker Street. I believe he eventually manages to get recruited by L, where he makes many of the same deductions he does. But where Sherlock excels is up close analysis. He'd meet Light under some pretense, notice extremely small clues (perhaps Light's wrists are more stressed than one would expect from a student of his class, perhaps his fingers twitch slightly from writing so much, etc.) and he'd theorize about the nature of the murder weapon far before L ever did

*Stopping the Killer/Proof of Guilt* \- Light low to mid diffs. Unfortunately, this is where Sherlock fails. His strategies are largely basic, nothing Light couldn't handle or counteract, while Holmes himself has never matched wits with a strategist like Light. Much like L, he would eventually find himself checkmated in a position with no winning moves, especially since his identity isn't so anonymous as L.

**Jigsaw** \- **OPEN low diff**

*Identifying the Killer* \- Sherlock low to mid diffs. With some research, Sherlock would quickly go through prominent engineers who would be capable of constructing the various traps Jigsaw has made. He'd discover John Kramer and his tragic past and focus on investigating him. A direct approach would confirm his suspicions.

*Stopping the Killer/Proof of Guilt* \- Jigsaw low diffs. Holmes would likely underestimate Kramer, and this would screw him over. The minute Jigsaw knows Holmes is after him, he would know every move Holmes would ever make weeks or months in advance due to his immense ability to anticipate people's actions. While Holmes would close in on Kramer sooner than the canon cops did, Kramer would undoubtedly have back up plans in place for that, and Holmes is a dead man walking the moment Jigsaw learns of his existence.

**Hitman - OPEN no diff**

*Identifying the Killer -* 47 no diffs. I do not believe Holmes would even begin to suspect that there was a killer connected to the deaths of various individuals. There's simply no discernible pattern, as some die by accidents, some by murders where the killer is caught, others disappear, etc. He would never begin to investigate 47 since he'd have to know something connected all those deaths to begin with. Even if he did, 47 leaves no clues behind, Holmes would have the barest profile of a killer yet.

*Catching the Killer/Proof of Guilt* \- 47 no diffs.  Even granting that Holmes knows about 47 and what he is like, there is no trap he could possibly set that would catch him. Cameras would easily be detected, spies would be misled, subdued, or killed without effort, and a direct confrontation would obviously be signing his own death warrant.
