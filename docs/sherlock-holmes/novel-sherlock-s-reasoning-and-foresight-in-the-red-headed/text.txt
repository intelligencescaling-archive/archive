Novels Sherlock's reasoning + foresight in The Red-Headed League

Because there have been people underestimating Sherlock lately, I decided to make this post to demonstrate his reasoning + foresight. Some context is provided below:

**In The Red-Headed League short story, Sherlock is presented with a strange case by a guy named Mr Wilson who owns a small pawnbrokers business and has an assistant named Vincent Spaulding. This guy, Vincent Spaulding, is actually a notorious criminal named John Clay who wants to rob a bank around the corner from Wilson's pawnbrokers business.**

**He devises a scheme which I'll explain briefly here:**

**First, he creates a fake newspaper advertisement saying that there's a vacancy in a League called 'The Red-Headed League', using Mr Wilson's bright red hair to their advantage. He then proceeds to convince Wilson that it is actually legitimate (a bunch of people do actually show up in response to the advertisement) and pays him handsomely for a few hours of trivial work. All this is done by accomplices so as not to attract attention to himself.**

**After Wilson is away and not suspicious of him, Spaulding goes down to the cellars with the excuse of photography to begin digging a tunnel leading to the bank around the corner. Before this, Spaulding made sure to work efficiently to make sure Wilson wouldn't question his odd habit of diving into the cellar.**

**When they, the robbers, completed the tunnel, they proceeded to close the League offices, cutting off all ties with them.**

And now I'll explain how Sherlock saw through this with an outsider's view and managed to prevent this trap set by Spaulding (or John Clay since that's his real name):

P1: There is an advertisement for The Red-Headed League, which only allows a certain type of red-haired people to enter

P2: Mr Wilson is one of those rare people who are allowed to enter

P3: The work has very high pay for its trivial work, easily attracting Wilson's attention

P4: The work keeps Mr Wilson away from his pawnbroking business for hours

P5: The League was dissolved in a very sudden abrupt way, unlike legitimate businesses which would likely announce it to their employees

P6: There were no traces of the League or its founders

P7: The League paid Wilson quite a lot of money, making it too expensive to be just a regular prank

**C1**: The Red-Headed League, which was likely a made-up organisation, was designed to keep Mr Wilson away from his business for unknown reasons (at the moment)

P8: Spaulding came into Wilson's service at half the intended price

P9: Spaulding had many other options so this was entirely his choice

**C2**: Spaulding had some reason for working in Wilson's business

Next, Sherlock addresses the motive.

P10: There are no women in the house

**C3**: Spaulding's motive is not a mere vulgar intrigue

P11: The pawnbroking business is small

P12: In the house, there was nothing of much value that could be gained

**C4**: The motive is not related to the business or anything inside the house

We'll come back to the motive later as Sherlock dismisses this for now and moves on to the part where he actually investigates the house and business in person, meets Spaulding, and makes up his mind.

P13: Spaulding always had a habit of diving into the cellar under the pretense of photography

P14: Mr Wilson did not go down to see what he was doing because he trusted him entirely

P15: The cellar stretched out behind the house

P16: There is a bank behind the house

**C5**: The cellar, which Spaulding frequently stays in, leads close to the bank, and since Spaulding had a strong motive for working for Wilson, this could be the motive

P17: Spaulding's trousers were worn, wrinkled and stained

P18: Spaulding met Sherlock at the house's door quickly, meaning he couldn't have done anything to alter his appearance

**C6**: Spaulding was doing something that required him to be in contact with dirt for a long time and was most likely kneeling down

P19: There is some very valuable bullion (bulks of gold) in the bank from P16

P20: The bank usually doesn't have the valuable bullion

P21: Spaulding was indeed something in the cellar for many hours a day for months

P22: Photography is definitely out of question now, so Spaulding was lying when he used that as his excuse (because of **C6** and P21)

**C7**: From **C2**, **C5** and **C6**, Spaulding must be making a tunnel from the cellar to the bank to steal the bullion; they did it at this specific time because they wanted to steal the bullion not just the regular stuff at the bank

Now that Sherlock has determined how the scheme was played out, he proceeds to predict when they'll steal the stuff and set up an easy counter-trap against them

P23: The League offices were all closed

P24: Closing the League offices means that Mr Wilson returned to the house

P25: Digging the tunnel with Mr Wilson in the house is risky

**C8**: The tunnel must be completed

P26: The bullion might be removed soon since it won't remain in the bank's custody for long

P27: The tunnel must be used soon otherwise it might be discovered

P28: Saturday would be a good time as it would give Spaulding and his gang two days to escape

P29: The robbery would need to take place using the tunnel and without attracting Wilson's attention

P30: Wilson always goes to sleep during the night at set time

**C9**: Spaulding will use his tunnel to break into the bank while Wilson's asleep during the night on Saturday

Sherlock waits there on Saturday night, blocks off all the exits and finally arrests this guy and his gang at the end of the short story.

So yeah, this shows Novels Sherlock's reasoning and foresight (maybe trap evasion? I didn't describe how he trapped them), so I hope those who think Yuuichi > Sherlock to read through this and tell me how Sherlock won't be able to see through and predict Yuuichi's traps. Btw, since this comes from the short stories, this feat is actually just one of his slightly above average cases so don't go away thinking this is his best feat.
