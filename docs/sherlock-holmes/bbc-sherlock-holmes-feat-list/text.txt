BBC Sherlock Holmes feat list

William Sherlock Scott Holmes intelligence feats:

**"When you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth.” - Sherlock Holmes**

In *The Reichenbach Fall*, he faked his death using a crash mat hidden by an ambulance station that was quickly removed after he landed. A corpse provided by Molly Hooper, dressed as Sherlock, was then placed on the pavement, John wouldn’t have been able to notice the switch because one of Sherlock’s network spies, disguised as an ordinary cyclist, deliberately collided with him. This caused John to fall and momentarily lose sight of the area around Sherlock's body. This distraction allowed Sherlock to take the corpse’s place, cover himself in fake blood, and use a squash ball under his armpit to temporarily cut off circulation and stop his radial pulse, effectively convincing anyone checking for a heartbeat—including John—that he was dead. This helped him escape Moriarty’s network after Mycroft's men neutralise Moriarty's snipers as planned. Prior to this, he had deliberately let Moriarty off the hook while feeding him information through Mycroft so his future actions would become more predictable. He then accounted for the predictable actions.

This feat showcases \[PP (Planning Practicability) PC (Planning Convergence) DP (Detailed Planning) IDM (Indirect Manipulation) MM (Mass Manipulation) IM (Individual Manipulation) PM (Psychological Manipulation) ST (Strategic Thinking) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) CT (Creative Thinking) SIA (Situational Awareness) PS (Problem Solving) Foresight and LGT (Logical Thinking)\].

In *The Blind Banker*, he deduces that Van Coon who supposedly committed suicide is left-handed—based on the placement of objects in the room and the fact that the knife on the breadboard was buttered on the right side—he concludes Van Coon did not commit suicide but was instead murdered. The perpetrator had broken in, and the gun had been on the victim for protection, because it is highly unlikely that a left-handed Van Coon had shot himself in the right side of the head. He also knows that Inspector Dimmock is acting on his emotions, choosing the conclusion he likes and ignoring all evidence that doesn’t support it.

This feat showcases \[DR (Deductive Reasoning) AB (Abductive Reasoning) EU (Emotional Understanding) PER (Perceptual Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) SIA (Situational Awareness) LGT (Logical Thinking) AT (Analytical Thinking) HR (Hot Reading) FRI (Fluid Reasoning Index) LT (Linear Thinking) CT (Creative Thinking) and PS (Problem Solving)\].

In *A Study in Pink*, he reasoned that Watson was an ex-army doctor recently injured in Afghanistan or Iraq, judging from his posture, haircut, and words that he was a former soldier, and from his psychometric limp and tan face with untanned wrists that he had been injured abroad. He also inferred that Watson had a therapist because of the limp. He also reasoned Watson’s phone was an expensive gift, originally from a relative, judging by the engraving. He deduced Watson had problems with this relative—alcoholism suggested by power-aux marks, or leaving a girlfriend—because of the six-month-old gift to Clara, who was the relative’s wife, indicated by three kisses. The only thing Sherlock got wrong was assuming the giver was a brother; it was actually a sister, as “Harry” was short for Harriet.

This feat showcases \[DR (Deductive Reasoning) AR (Abductive Reasoning) PER (Perceptual Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) AT (Analytical Thinking) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) and CT (Creative Thinking)\].

In *The Sign of Three*, at John Watson’s wedding, he reasons that Major Sholto was being targeted by a delayed-action stabbing. He originally reasoned that Sholto would be the victim after Tessa mentioned John’s middle name, which revealed that the Mayfly Murderer was at the wedding since that could only be known through the wedding invitation. He then linked the killer’s employment to Sholto and considered Sholto's military history—specifically, that a unit of his recruits, including the killer's brother, had been killed in Afghanistan. This led him to identify Sholto as the intended target. He then went to confront Sholto, who had locked himself in his room. He connected this with the Bloody Guardsman case: both killers had used the same method, a locked-room murder without any force of entry, employing a blade hidden in a tight military belt. The belt prevented immediate bleeding, and the wound would only be fatal once the belt was removed. He reasoned that Sholto’s belt had been rigged in the same way. Despite this, Sholto was indifferent and about to harm himself, until Sherlock convinced him to open the door and seek medical help by appealing to their similar bond with John Watson. Additionally, he reasons the culprit: the wedding photographer, Jonathan Small, who had both the opportunity—access to adjust Sholto’s belt for photos—and the motive: revenge for his brother's death.

This feat showcases \[PER (Perceptual Reasoning) VCI (Verbal Comprehension Index) DR (Deductive Reasoning) IR (inductive Reasoning) AB (Abductive Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) LTM (Long Term Memory) EAM (Emotional Manipulation) DIM (Direct Manipulation) PS (Problem Solving) LT (Linear Thinking) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) CT (Creative Thinking) PS (Problem Solving) and FRI (Fluid Reasoning Index)\].

In *The Great Game*, he figured out a detail that proved the painting was fake while only having ten seconds, actually identifying it by the fifth second. The detail was the Van Buren Supernova in the painting, which occurred in 1858, while the painting had been made in 1640.

This feat showcases \[PSI (Processing Speed Index) DR (Deductive Reasoning) PER (Perceptual Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) AT (Analytical Thinking) LT (Linear Thinking) ST (Stress Tolerance) FRI (Fluid Reasoning Index) PS (Problem Solving) and Perseverance\].

In *His Last Vow*, after being shot by Mary in the liver, he is able to reconstruct the scene in his mind and focus on the fact that blood loss will kill him. He also scans the guns to identify which revolver she used and reasons that the bullet is still inside him because he didn’t hear the mirror behind him shatter. Realising this, he decides to fall backward to dislodge the bullet and let gravity work in his favor. He accomplishes all of this in three seconds, and while falling, he determines that, to avoid going into shock, he needs to focus on a past memory. Finally, he recognizes that he must control the pain—doing all of this to survive. He is also able to reconstruct multiple personalities in his memory, including Molly’s and Mycroft’s, guiding him and demonstrating that he emotionally understands them.

This feat showcases \[PSI (Processing Speed Index) PER (Perceptual Reasoning) AR (Abductive Reasoning) DR (Deductive Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) EU (Emotional Understanding) AT (Analytical Thinking) LT (Linear Thinking) ST (Stress Tolerance) FRI (Fluid Reasoning Index) EM (Emotional Management) PS (Problem Solving) and Perseverance\].

In *The Hounds of the Baskervilles*, while in a pub, he observes two people around him. He identifies a woman as a sentimental widow based on the way she wears her wedding ring on a chain around her neck, her cheap but sentimental jewelry, and a small West Highland Terrier named Whiskey—evidenced by the tiny hairs on her legs but not her knees and from overhearing the dog’s name on the train. He identifies the man with her as an unemployed fisherman who has turned to his widowed mother for financial help, based on the scars on his hands, the age of the scars, his uncomfortable clothing christmas gift, his location, eating habits, and motive.

This feat showcases \[AR (Abductive Reasoning) DR (Deductive Reasoning) CR (Cold Reading) PER (Perceptual Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) AT (Analytical Thinking) LT (Linear Thinking) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) and CT (Creative Thinking)\].

In *The Great Game*, he reasons that a victim found on the River Thames shoreline, dressed in a specific uniform, is Alex Woodbridge. He concludes this based on details from the victim’s uniform, his legs, watch, ticket stubs, and a ripped shirt badge, and then links it to a missing person report from the Hickman Gallery, identifying the man as Alex Woodbridge. He also determines that the murderer is the Golem Assassin, based on the killer’s modus operandi, and realises that the assassin is targeting the Vermeer painting Alex was guarding because it is a fake.

This feat showcases \[AR (Abductive Reasoning) DR (Deductive Reasoning) PER (Perceptual Reasoning) LR (Logical Reasoning) LGT (Logical Thinking) AT (Analytical Thinking) LT (Linear Thinking) NLT (Non Linear Thinking) and CT (Creative Thinking)\].
