Sherlock Interpretation

\*\*WARNING: Contains spoilers for the novel ‘Moriarty’ by Anthony Horowitz.\*\*

hello Yall, I’m the author of the blind observer short stories if you’ve read them. (more to come soon) and I thought I would bring an interpretation of the narrative as detailed in Anthony Horowit’s Moriarty novel.

So in this novel, it’s revealed that Moriarty faked his own death, leveraging it to root out an American gang led by a Mr. Deveroux that had tried to overtake his criminal empire. Moriarty had disguised himself and effectively bluffed Sherlock with a series of murder attempts to make him think he was in danger to get him to flee to the Reichanbach Falls where Moriarty could face off with him and fake his death so Deveroux would assume he was killed. This aids the rest of Moriarty’s plan, which in a nutshell consists of locating Mr. Deveroux and systematically destroying his empire, mostly by heading to America in the end of the novel.

However, there is a view that Sherlock actually knew all of this and purposely fell for Moriarty’s plan to assist him in rooting out Deveroux. It’s explained by Moriarty that Sherlock’s actions are extremely strange given his circumstances. Why would he not conceal himself within London which is his home turf? Why would he take Watson with him to make him easier to trace? Why would he fake his own death after finding out Moriarty is dead? Why would he not just at least consult Watson first if he had enemies? Technically, a lot of Moriarty’s questions here are heavily flawed and are addressed in The Final Problem by analyzing Sherlock’s misdirections and perception of Moriarty. In addition, Sherlock is heavily implied to have been surveilling Moriarty for much of the novel, even taking the room next to him to watch him. (Which he never mentions to Watson.) In fact, this directly contradicts Sherlock’s own admittance in ‘The Empty House’ that he had fled immediately. Moriarty even states himself that he believed Sherlock had his own agenda. What is especially interesting is the fact that Sherlock never, not once, confronts Moriarty. It’s also important to note that Moriarty doesn’t believe Sherlock’s explanations in ‘The Empty House’ and thinks he is lying to hide something.

The conclusion from these premises is that Sherlock had actually purposely made his own misdirections flawed in The Final Problem to lure Moriarty to the Reichanbach Falls so both of them could fake their deaths, which was his initial plan. This is especially interesting as Sherlock states that he had not thought to fake his death until after Moriarty fell, which is contradicted by this conclusion. Another detail which contradicts Sherlock’s statement is that this implied plan would require Sherlock predicting that Moriarty would predict that he would go to the Reichanbach Falls and that Moriarty would escape the trap set by him, which he did not know until after it was sprung, meaning Sherlock must have known the whole time that Moriarty was not in charge of his own gang at the moment. Because of this, it would mean that Sherlock allowed Moriarty to fake his death before faking his own so that he could survey the execution of Moriarty’s plan to capture Deveroux. The advantage on Sherlock’s end is that it gives him a significant advantage against Deveroux in case he tries to go after him and shields Moriarty in his plan, as Sherlock would generally have been the one consulted regarding him in this case. This allows much more leeway for Moriarty to execute his plan.

all in all, these contradictory elements of Watson’s narration support MoriarTy’s theory that Sherlock had his own agenda and either lied to Watson or Watson reframed the narrative to portray Sherlock better.
