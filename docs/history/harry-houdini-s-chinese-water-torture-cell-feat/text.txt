Harry Houdini CWTC feat

**"My brain is the key that sets my mind free"**

\[Chinese Water Torture Cell\] In this act, he was suspended upside down, his feet locked in stocks, and lowered into a glass tank of water. The audience believed he had to escape before running out of breath. Yet this entire performance was an illusion, engineered by him to deceive the masses. He had the mahogany boards forming the stocks designed to separate slightly when the external hasps were locked. So when his assistant inserted the keys into the locks, it triggered an internal mechanism that released the boards grip on his ankles. Once lowered into the tank, he used the tank’s walls to push himself upward, twisting and maneuvering to slip his feet free from the loosened stocks. He had the tank itself calculated to be deep enough that, when he flipped, a small pocket of air formed between the water’s surface and the top of the cell. Which allowed him to breathe momentarily while he fully opened the hinged stock boards, freeing himself completely. From there, he could climb out of the tank and present himself to the audience, maintaining the illusion. He also anticipated possible failure. having his assistants positioned beside him with an axe to break the glass and rescue him instantly if anything went wrong. He came up with this entire plan in a year. He had to imagine himself upside down, with his feet in the stocks, and predict how he could maneuver in a confined, water filled space. He calculated how to push against the tank walls to generate leverage to slip his feet free once the stocks were loosened. He had to estimate the tank depth so that, when he flipped, a small pocket of air would form, giving him enough time to breathe. He had to endure the pressure that something might go wrong. He also made sure the trick was legally protected beforehand. By staging a private one-act performance of the act in advance and then filing a copyright on the play itself. By doing this, when he later promoted the upcoming public performance, no one could legally copy the trick, because it had already been copyrighted beforehand. He also made sure that his body language revealed nothing of the planning behind the act, convincing the audience that the entire performance was completely spontaneous.
