Leonardo da Vinci feat list

This post presents a list of feats attributed to High Renaissance Italian Polymath Leonardo di ser Piero da Vinci.

He sketched the Mona Lisa to fool the viewer into thinking she was looking at them. He also fooled viewers into thinking her smile fades. He did this by sketching it at 15.4-degrees to the right side, making it go over the viewer’s shoulder so that, from a distance, the gaze appears more direct than it does up close. For the smile, he made the soft shadows around her mouth clearer when you look directly at it, making the smile appear stronger. So when you look elsewhere, those details become harder to see, causing the smile to appear faded. He did this to make viewers feel like the painting is alive.

He dissected thirty human corpses from hospital morgues at night to better understand the human body and produce more realistic drawings. Through this work, he became the first to identify that the olfactory nerve is a separate cranial nerve, showing that the brain’s nerves do not converge at the lateral or third ventricles. He did this secretly to avoid controversy. It was also taboo at the time, yet he was still willing to risk everything for research, fully aware that if he were caught, he would be imprisoned.

He sketched the Last Supper and hid a melody inside of it by aligning the horizontal lines of the tablecloth with the bread and the disciples hands, using them as secret musical notes. He also made sure the melody was a 40-second hymn to God or solemn, dirge-like requiem to match the context of the Last Supper.

He sketched the Vitruvian Man, a piece that mapped the harmonious ratios of the human body. Through the superimposed, jumping jack combination of sixteen positions in one, it illustrated the intersection of the divine and the earthly. He also used this sketch to turn the human body into a geometric system of circle and square, as well as to reflect the laws of the universe.

While working in Milan, he studied the geology of the surrounding Alps and Apennines, discovering fossilised shells and fish bones embedded in the mountain rock layers. He concluded that they were once living creatures buried on the seafloor, rather than transported by Noah’s flood like the Biblical Deluge says since they were often found in pairs and in positions suggesting they had died and been buried in place. And because the earth’s surface changes over time—which meant what was once seafloor could later be uplifted into mountains.
