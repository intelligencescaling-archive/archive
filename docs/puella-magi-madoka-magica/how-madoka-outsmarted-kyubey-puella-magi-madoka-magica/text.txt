How Madoka Outsmarted Kyubey (Analysis)

Kyubey has a major weakness: they never anticipate anything. They always assume their plans will work and that their system can’t be broken. However, there is one flaw in that system—they must grant any girl’s wish before they become a magical girl. They assumed this was fine because they target psychologically vulnerable girls, but Madoka took advantage of this rule.

Madoka already understood how Kyubey’s system worked. From tossing Sayaka’s Soul Gem, to Kyubey explaining Grief Seeds and what Incubators are, the full system was gradually revealed to her.

Based on what she learned, she pieced together that magical girls eventually turn into witches through despair. This creates a never-ending chain of energy for the Incubators: there will always be witches, there will always be magical girls, and magical girls inevitably become witches, causing new magical girls to appear. It is a cycle. Madoka chose to change that cycle by becoming a new Law of Cycles through her wish.

First, Madoka identifies the core problem: she needs to eliminate witches. But doing that would also eliminate magical girls. Instead, she realises she must prevent magical girls from becoming witches, which still erases witches without killing anyone who can be saved. However, if she phrased the wish too simply, Kyubey could twist it to only affect the present. So Madoka gets specific: she wishes to prevent all magical girls from the past, present, and future from ever becoming witches. Preventing any loopholes.

This completely breaks the system. Here are the implications: She erases Kyubey’s ultimate goal. Since witches no longer exist, Kyubey can’t generate energy through their usual method. Magical girls still retain the benefits of their wishes, allowing them to remain happy and continue saving or protecting others. If someone tried to wish for something evil, it wouldn’t work, because one must be in the process of becoming a witch to use such a wish—and since Madoka erased the possibility of becoming a witch, the process itself no longer exists; it’s not just witches that are removed from the timeline. As a result, Kyubey ends up recruiting only good-hearted individuals, which significantly decreases crime. And Madoka becomes omnipresent, omniscient, and nigh-omnipotent, allowing her to ensure that the world remains good. Also, if someone had previously made an evil wish, that wish would either be altered in the new universe or that person never made a wish at all because of the evil or despair inside of them.

And… here comes the one massive con she didn’t prevent grief. Wraiths were born after Madoka recreated the universe. They aren’t witches—which are manifestations of despair—but manifestations of grief, basically the universe’s way of replacing witches for some reason.

Though wraiths aren’t anything like witches, they are easier to deal with. They feed on emotions instead of corrupting souls. You can save someone from their wraith state without having to fight them, and the majority of wraiths aren’t actually hostile—they only target humans to fulfill survival needs. They do have fairly strong abilities, but you could counter them depending on what your wish was. Plus, Madoka is still omnipresent, omniscient, and nigh-omnipotent, so if they ever became a real problem, she could simply erase them—at least until Homura later decided to split her.

However, wraiths reintroduce the system—not through despair, but through grief. Wraiths are people consumed by immense grief. If that grief is removed, they return to normal. If you kill them, Kyubey gains energy, and you can cleanse your Soul Gem with the grief cubes if necessary.

Kyubey did not—and I repeat, did not—plan for this in any way. Wraiths weren’t their ace in the hole. No, they only came into existence after Madoka recreated the universe. Kyubey couldn’t have known what they were beforehand, unless they could see the future—which they obviously can’t.

In fact, Kyubey's actions in *Puella Magi Madoka Magica The Movie: Rebellion* prove this. He created an isolation field to trap Homura's Soul Gem and force Madoka to manifest, so they could study and potentially subvert the new system she created. They would only take such actions if it were a system they hadn’t anticipated or planned for.
