- The game starts when all groups are set. Sang Woo’s team has 3 women, 1 elderly man and only 6 average guys so they are physically weaker than most teams. The game is just a normal tug of war, a contest in which two teams pull at opposite ends of a rope until one drags the other over a central line. Sang Woo’s team gets paired up with an all men's team. As the round starts, Sang Woo’s team holds up for a bit but they quickly get overpowered and they keep getting closer to the center, everybody starts panicking. Sang Woo suggests everyone to take three steps forward and they do in a coordinated manner thanks to his leadership. After that, they start to win the game and after a while they easily win. But how did a seemingly insanely bad move win the game? Well, it all relies on physics. By stepping forward, they reduce tension in the rope momentarily. Human reaction time (~0.2s) means the opponents can’t adjust fast enough to the sudden change. The opposing team, pulling hard, loses resistance and may overextend (like pulling against a suddenly slack rope). This disrupts their balance and coordination (reducing their effective pulling force). After stepping forward, Sang Woo’s team stops and starts to go backwards. The sudden stop converts their forward momentum into a backward jerk on the rope (due to impulse, FΔt=mΔv). The opposing team now faces a dynamic load (sudden tension increase) that destabilizes them and a loss of footing as their own pull has no immediate resistance, causing them to stumble. Once the opponents are off-balance, Sang-woo’s team exploits their momentary weakness. They pull backward with full force while the opponents are still recovering, maximizing the net force in their favor. This is how they won the game. The key concepts (forces, friction, impulse, momentum) are first-year university physics, but the application (timing, coordination, psychological effects) makes it feel more advanced. This is a brilliant example of how Sang Woo’s strategy adapted to a really bad position by him coming up with a genius idea in a few seconds and also applying his knowledge while doing so.
