VOLUME 1

EVENT 1: The Plane Hijacking (Four Years Before Present)

Location: Commercial aircraft at 10,000 meters altitude

Executive Summary with Strategic Weight

Siesta demonstrates a complete strategic cycle: intelligence gathering → asset identification → pre-positioning → execution → post-conflict asset conversion. She neutralizes an Android hijacker without civilian casualties, turns the enemy into a long-term intelligence asset, and recruits an unwitting middle school boy as her future assistant. The entire operation is conducted under extreme time pressure and resource constraints, with 600 lives at stake. Critically, she achieves this while maintaining a cover that SPES does not discover her existence for years.

Pre-Conflict Intelligence & Foresight

Intelligence Gathering Scope: Siesta explicitly states: "I know everything, from the fact that you boarded this plane, planned to hijack it, and even about the people who ordered you." This reveals she had access to SPES internal communications, knew Bat's identity and his status as a betrayer, knew the specific flight and time, and knew the order that Bat die in the crash as punishment.

Strategic Value of This Intelligence:

* Allows her to board the correct flight without appearing suspicious.
* Allows her to pre-position countermeasures (the suitcase).
* Allows her to understand Bat's psychological vulnerability (fear of death).
* Allows her to plan the post-conflict disposition (Bat as asset, not corpse).
* Allows her to avoid revealing her existence to SPES prematurely.

Information Asymmetry Exploitation: Bat believes he has the advantage – he controls the cockpit, he has Android modifications, he has 600 hostages. In reality, Siesta knows everything about him while he knows nothing about her. She is a ghost to SPES at this point; he has no dossier on her. This information asymmetry is the foundation of her strategy.

Asset Identification – Kimihiko Kimizuka: Siesta selected Kimihiko before the flight. She says "Perfect, you—be my assistant" without hesitation, indicating she had already observed him, assessed his capabilities, and decided he would be useful. Why Kimihiko specifically?

Kimihiko's Profile as Assessed by Siesta:

* His "troublesome nature" (documented history of being present at crime scenes and helping resolve them) makes him both a reliable carrier (he doesn't shy away from danger) and a potential long-term partner.
* He carries a suitcase through airport security without asking questions – suggests either trustworthiness or obliviousness, both useful.
* He is young (eighth grade) and malleable – can be trained.
* He has no prior connection to SPES – he is a clean asset.
* His instinctual reactions in crises (later demonstrated when he retrieves the suitcase under fire) are useful.

Strategic Principle Illustrated: The best assets are those who do not know they are assets. Kimihiko cannot betray what he does not know.

Pre-Positioning & Logistical Orchestration

1. The Suitcase as a Trojan Horse: Siesta admits: "It was I who instructed you to bring the suitcase onto the flight." She used intermediaries (the "black suit men") to pass the suitcase to Kimihiko. The suitcase contained:

* A musket gun – an antique weapon that can still fire, likely chosen because it is easier to conceal and less traceable than modern firearms.
* The red bullet – custom-manufactured from her own blood, designed to interface with Android physiology.

Logistical Challenges Solved:

* How to get a weapon past airport security? Use an unwitting carrier who has no criminal record and no reason to be searched.
* How to ensure the weapon is on the plane without carrying it yourself? Pre-position it with someone who will be on the same flight.
* How to retrieve it at the critical moment? Instruct the carrier (through intermediaries) to stow it in the overhead compartment above his seat, then send him to get it.

2. The Red Bullet – Technical Specifications: Siesta explains: "I just shot you with the 'red bullet' that was made from my 'blood', you know? Anyone who touched this blood cannot disobey the master – your feeler can never attack me again."

Mechanism Analysis:

* The bullet is made from her blood – not just coated or containing a blood sample, but constructed from it. This suggests advanced biotechnology or Android-derived manufacturing.
* The effect is permanent and irreversible – "cannot disobey the master" indicates a form of biological reprogramming of the Android's neural/mechanical systems.
* The effect is specific to attacking her – Bat can still use his feeler for other purposes (hearing, movement), but not against her.
* The "master" is Siesta herself, not anyone who holds the bullet. The control is keyed to her biological signature.

Strategic Value of the Red Bullet:

* It neutralizes the immediate threat without killing the asset.
* It creates long-term compliance – Bat cannot later betray her.
* It provides a testable control mechanism – the fact that the feeler stops centimeters from Natsunagi's heart four years later proves its durability.
* It is a one-shot solution – no need for ongoing surveillance or restraints.

In-Flight Execution – The Deduction Game as Psychological Warfare

Phase 1: Initial Engagement

Siesta and Kimihiko are led to the cockpit. Bat is seated on the unconscious co-pilot, legs crossed, projecting confidence. He speaks Japanese despite his Northern European appearance – this is noted as unusual. Siesta immediately introduces herself and Kimihiko as "Siesta" and "Watson," claiming they "grew up together at Baker Street." This is deliberate misdirection – she is mocking Bat's expectations of a detective partnership.

Psychological Effect: Bat is expecting a serious confrontation. Instead, he gets a teenage girl making Sherlock Holmes jokes. This lowers his guard and makes him underestimate her.

Phase 2: First Deduction – The Obvious Answer

Bat demands: "Deduce the reason why I hijacked this plane. If you're correct, I'll give up on snapping the captain's neck."

Siesta immediately answers: "You hijacked the plane because you were bored to tears."

Analysis of This Answer:

* It is technically true – Bat had said earlier that he was bored enough to come to a distant country and hijack a plane.
* It is a surface-level reading that any detective could make.
* It gives Bat what he wants – a reason to "lose" the game and surrender.
* It allows Siesta to appear less threatening than she actually is.

Bat's Reaction: "Haha, hahahahahahahahaha! Amazing, it's really amazing. Impressive, really impressive. Such guts you have." He admits defeat and prepares to leave.

Phase 3: Second Deduction – The Real Reveal

Just as Bat is about to return to his seat, Siesta stops him. She reveals:

1. He was ordered to hijack the plane and die in the crash as punishment for betraying SPES.
2. He is afraid of death and does not want to die.
3. He created the deduction game as a way to surrender without losing pride.
4. He intended to use Japanese police protection to survive after the hijacking failed.

Why This Timing?

If she had revealed this immediately, Bat might have attacked sooner or simply left the cockpit to escape. By letting him believe he had won, she forced him to lower his guard completely. The reveal then triggers his attack – which is exactly what she wants, because she needs him to use his feeler so she can deploy the red bullet.

Phase 4: Provoking the Attack

Siesta says: "You retreated way too early. You admitted defeat to me and retreated. A man hijacked a plane alone in this era, a Japanese plane that's said to be impregnable, and just retreated in the face of a girl?"

Strategic Purpose of Provocation:

* Hurts Bat's pride – he cannot tolerate being seen as cowardly.
* Forces him to attack to prove he is not afraid.
* Gives her the opportunity to use the red bullet.
* Shifts the blame for the violence onto him – she is "defending herself."

Phase 5: Taking the Wound

When Bat attacks, Siesta shoves Kimihiko out of the way and takes the hit to her shoulder. "Siesta, with reddish-black liquid flowing from her shoulder."

Analysis of This Decision:

* Protects her asset (Kimihiko) – he is more useful alive than dead.
* Builds psychological debt – Kimihiko will feel obligated to her.
* Demonstrates commitment – Bat sees she is willing to bleed.
* Creates a narrative of victimhood – she was "attacked" rather than being the aggressor.
* Does not impair her ability to fight – a shoulder wound is survivable.

Phase 6: Weapon Deployment

Siesta sends Kimihiko to retrieve the suitcase. She knows Bat will destroy it – he does, shredding the suitcase with his feeler. But the musket gun falls out, and she catches it. She fires immediately, shooting Bat's feeler.

Why This Works:

* Bat's attention is on destroying the suitcase, not on catching the contents.
* The musket is an unexpected weapon – Bat may not have recognized it as a threat.
* The red bullet is designed for this exact purpose – it works on contact.

Phase 7: The "Death" Declaration

Siesta shoves the musket into Bat's mouth and says "Bam!" She then declares: "Now you will not be targeted by your colleagues. After all, you are now dead."

Strategic Function of This Declaration:

* Creates a new legal identity for Bat – "dead" to SPES.
* Makes him dependent on Japanese police for survival – he cannot return to SPES.
* Provides a justification for his imprisonment – he is "dead" and therefore no longer a citizen of any country.
* Allows Siesta to claim she did not kill him – she only "declared" him dead.

Phase 8: Post-Conflict Disposition

Siesta hands Bat over to Fuubi-san (off-page, implied) for imprisonment in the "villa" – a specialized facility for Android detainees. She ensures he is kept alive, fed, and that his hearing ability is preserved for future use.

Long-Term Asset Value of Bat:

* Superhuman hearing (100km range) – can identify individuals by heartbeat.
* Knowledge of SPES operations – can be interrogated over time.
* The red bullet ensures compliance – he cannot attack her or her allies (as proven later with Natsunagi).

Manipulation of Kimihiko – The Assistant Recruitment Strategy

Technique 1: Forced Recruitment

Siesta does not ask Kimihiko to be her assistant. She declares: "Perfect, you—be my assistant." Then she grabs his hand and drags him after the cabin attendant.

Psychological Effect: Kimihiko is given no time to refuse. By the time he could object, he is already committed. The physical act of dragging him creates a sense of being "in it together."

Technique 2: Shared Danger

Siesta brings Kimihiko into the cockpit to face a hijacker. She does not ask if he wants to come – she simply takes him.

Psychological Effect: Trauma bonding. Facing a life-threatening situation together creates a powerful emotional connection. Kimihiko cannot later claim he was not involved.

Technique 3: Sacrificial Protection

Siesta takes a wound protecting Kimihiko from Bat's attack. She could have dodged or used Kimihiko as a shield. Instead, she shoves him aside and gets hit.

Psychological Effect: Creates a debt of gratitude. Kimihiko now "owes" her his safety. He cannot abandon her without feeling guilty.

Technique 4: Partial Transparency

Siesta tells Kimihiko about SPES, Androids, the red bullet, and Bat's true motives. But she does not tell him that she used him as an unwitting courier from the beginning. She reveals this only at the very end: "It was I who instructed you to bring the suitcase onto the flight."

Psychological Effect: Builds trust through selective honesty. Kimihiko believes she is telling him everything, so he trusts her. The late revelation of the suitcase manipulation is framed as a confession, not a betrayal.

Technique 5: Ambiguous Naming

Siesta asks Kimihiko's name, then says: "I see. Can I call you 'Kimi'?" The term is ambiguous – it could be a nickname (from Kimihiko), a pronoun ("you"), or a reference to a race driver. She leaves it unclear.

Psychological Effect: Creates intimacy without commitment. Kimihiko does not know if they are friends, partners, or merely colleagues. This ambiguity allows her to define the relationship as needed.

Technique 6: The "Watson" Gambit

Siesta introduces Kimihiko as "Watson" – Sherlock Holmes's assistant. She claims they "grew up together at Baker Street."

Psychological Effect: Frames their relationship as the classic detective-assistant partnership. Kimihiko is given a role to play, and roles are easier to accept than undefined relationships.

EVENT 2: The Medusa Case (Two Years Before Present)

Location: Western-style house in a forest

Executive Summary with Strategic Weight

Siesta demonstrates environmental threat detection, sleight-of-hand deception, and pattern recognition under pressure. She identifies an airborne poison trap from three independent clues, steals the antidote during a feigned act of courtesy, neutralizes a madman without firing a lethal shot, and interprets a paralyzed girl's blinking as a conscious cry for help. The entire operation is conducted without the owner ever realizing he was being played until he collapses.

Environmental Threat Detection – The Poison Indicators

Clue 1: Closed Windows Despite Heat

The owner says the air-conditioning is malfunctioning. In summer, one would open windows. The windows are shut. Siesta immediately flags this as anomalous.

Strategic Deduction: The room contains something that must be contained – likely a gas. The owner is not opening windows because the contents of the room are dangerous.

Clue 2: Owner Not Sweating

The room is hot, but the owner is not sweating excessively. He is acclimated to the temperature. This means he spends long periods in this room. The hazard is constant, not temporary.

Strategic Deduction: The owner is immune to whatever is in the room – either through antidote or acclimation. His lack of discomfort is suspicious.

Clue 3: Mary Wearing a Respirator

Mary is in a post-coma unresponsive state. A respirator is used for breathing assistance or filtration. If the air were safe, she would not need it.

Strategic Deduction: The respirator is filtering the air. The room contains an airborne contaminant. Mary is being protected from it – or the owner is using it to keep her alive while poisoning others.

Clue 4: The "Broken AC" Excuse as Rehearsed Lie

The owner apologizes for the "malfunctioning air-conditioning" as if he has said it many times. This is a script.

Strategic Deduction: This is not a spontaneous excuse. He has used it before with other visitors. This is a pattern of behavior.

Integration of Clues: Siesta cross-references all four observations to conclude: (1) the room contains an airborne poison; (2) the owner is immune (antidote); (3) Mary is protected (respirator); (4) the owner has done this before.

The Handkerchief Gambit – Sleight of Hand as Strategic Tool

Phase 1: Creating the Distraction

Siesta stomps on Kimihiko's foot, causing him to trip and spill the drinks. She stomps twice – once to prevent him from drinking, once to cause the spill.

Strategic Purpose of the Spill:

* Prevents anyone from drinking the poisoned tea.
* Creates chaos – shattered glass, spilled liquid, apologies.
* Provides a reason to get close to the owner.
* Forces the owner to react, potentially revealing his priorities.

Phase 2: The Apology

Siesta takes out her handkerchief and begins wiping the owner's clothes, apologizing profusely for Kimihiko's "clumsiness."

Strategic Purpose of the Apology:

* Appears polite and deferential – lowers the owner's suspicion.
* Provides physical proximity to the owner.
* Occupies her hands with a "legitimate" task (wiping).
* Creates a screen for the pickpocket.

Phase 3: The Pickpocket

While wiping, Siesta steals the antidote from the owner's pocket. The handkerchief covers the motion.

Sleight of Hand Mechanics:

* The owner is focused on the spilled drinks and his ruined clothes.
* The handkerchief provides visual cover.
* Siesta's other hand is visible (holding the handkerchief) while the stealing hand is beneath it.
* The motion is natural – reaching to wipe – so it does not trigger suspicion.

Strategic Value:

* The antidote is the only thing protecting the owner from his own poison.
* Stealing it makes him vulnerable.
* He does not realize it is gone until he collapses.

Phase 4: Feigning Paralysis

After the owner leaves the room to "prepare cold drinks" (actually to let the poison take effect), Siesta pretends to be affected. She slumps over, breathes slowly, and appears incapacitated.

Strategic Purpose of Feigning:

* Lulls the owner into monologuing his confession.
* Makes him believe he has won.
* Prevents him from taking further precautions (he thinks they are already neutralized).
* Allows her to observe his behavior without him being guarded.

Phase 5: The Monologue Trap

The owner returns, sees them "paralyzed," and begins explaining his plan. He reveals that the room is filled with poison, that Mary is his "stone" daughter who needs friends, and that Siesta and Kimihiko will become her new "friends." He takes out a syringe – not to inject them, but to inject himself with the antidote.

Why the Monologue is Critical:

* Provides legal evidence of his intent.
* Confirms Siesta's deductions.
* Reveals the full scope of his crimes.
* Gives Siesta the timing for her reversal.

Phase 6: The Reversal

The owner injects himself – but the antidote is not there. He collapses. Siesta immediately handcuffs him.

Why the Reversal is Effective:

* The owner believed he was immune. His collapse is shocking.
* He has no time to react before being handcuffed.
* Siesta is revealed to have been unaffected the entire time.
* The psychological impact is devastating – he lost without ever knowing how.

Phase 7: Ventilation

Siesta shoots the musket gun to shatter a window or ceiling fixture. The poison disperses.

Why Ventilation is Necessary:

* The room is still filled with poison.
* Kimihiko is actually affected (he was not faking).
* Siesta took the antidote, but she needs fresh air to avoid secondary exposure.
* Breaking the window also provides an escape route if needed.

Pattern Recognition – Mary's Blinking as Communication

Observation: Siesta notices Mary's blinking is "rhythmic, robotic" but detects "definitely some will in there."

Analysis of the Blinking Pattern:

* Normal unconscious patients do not blink rhythmically – blinking is irregular or absent.
* Robotic pattern suggests deliberate control – she is not blinking involuntarily.
* The "will" indicates consciousness trapped inside a paralyzed body.
* Mary is trying to communicate – specifically, to expose her stepfather.

Strategic Deduction: Mary is a victim, not an accomplice. She has been aware of her stepfather's crimes and wants him stopped. Her blinking is a cry for help.

Why This Matters:

* Confirms the owner's guilt – even his daughter is trying to stop him.
* Provides a witness who can corroborate Siesta's account.
* Shifts the moral weight – Siesta is not just helping a victim; she is responding to a victim's plea.

Connection to Later Events: Kimihiko recalls this case when solving the Saikawa eye mystery. He explicitly cites Siesta's visual observation method as the template. This demonstrates that Siesta's strategic principles are durable enough to be applied by her successor after her death.

Strategic Principles Illustrated

Principle 1: Trust environmental clues over words. The owner said the AC was broken, but the closed windows, his lack of sweating, and Mary's respirator told the true story.

Principle 2: Create a distraction before taking action. The spilled drinks were not an accident – they were the cover for the pickpocket.

Principle 3: Feign vulnerability to extract a confession. Siesta pretended to be paralyzed so the owner would monologue.

Principle 4: A victim can be an asset. Mary's blinking was evidence; she became a corroborating witness.

Principle 5: Non-lethal resolution preserves options. Siesta could have killed the owner. Instead, she handcuffed him and ventilated the room, leaving him for legal justice.

The Heart Transplant Strategy (One Year Before Present – Implied)

Siesta, knowing she will die in battle against SPES, executes the most ambitious strategic act of her life: she arranges for her heart to be transplanted into Nagisa Natsunagi, a bedridden girl with no identity, no relationships, and no life purpose. The heart carries Siesta's full consciousness, memories, and will. This creates a posthumous asset that can seek out Kimihiko, continue the mission against SPES, and even serve as a vessel for Siesta to fight one last time. The strategy spans an indefinite time horizon and survives her death completely.

EVENT 1: The Revelation of the 《Tuners》 System and the "Famous Detective" Legacy

Context: After being abducted by 《Siesta》, the group watches footage of the events from one year ago. 《Siesta》 reveals that Siesta was a member of the Twelve 《Tuners》—an international organization tasked with protecting the world from existential threats. The "famous detective" was not merely a self-given title but an official role within this system. 《Siesta》 explains that the position has been vacant since Siesta's death and that Natsunagi is the most likely candidate to inherit it.

Foresight – Information Architecture and Revelation Timing

Intelligence Packaging as a Strategic Tool: Siesta did not simply leave information for her successors to find. She designed a comprehensive revelation system that would deliver information in a specific sequence at the right time to maximize psychological impact and ensure understanding. This is not a single revelation but a carefully constructed educational curriculum.
Revelation Sequence Analysis – The Three-Layer Framework:

Layer 1 (Volume 1 – The Heart Revelation):

* Natsunagi and Kimihiko learn that Natsunagi's heart came from Siesta.
* They learn about SPES and Androids as a concept.
* They begin to see Siesta as a person with a past, not just a detective.
* The emotional connection is established—Natsunagi is not just a recipient; she is a vessel for Siesta's will.

Layer 2 (Volume 2/Early Volume 3 – The Past Revelation):

* They learn about Natsunagi's past as Alicia and the circumstances of Siesta's death.
* They learn about Hel and the heart's origin as a vessel for consciousness.
* Siesta is humanized further—she had friends, she made mistakes, she had regrets.
* The group begins to form emotional bonds through shared discovery.

Layer 3 (This Revelation – The Tuners Revelation):

* They learn about the Tuners system and the Twelve protectors of humanity.
* Siesta's role is elevated from "detective who fought SPES" to "one of twelve protectors of humanity."
* The stakes are globalized—this is not a personal grudge but a world-saving mission.
* Natsunagi is positioned as the successor to this role.

Strategic Purpose of Sequential Revelation:

1. Prevents Information Overload: Each layer is processed and integrated before the next is delivered. The successors do not become overwhelmed or disoriented.
2. Builds Understanding Incrementally: Each layer provides context for the previous one. The Tuners system makes sense only after understanding SPES; Natsunagi's role makes sense only after understanding the heart.
3. Creates Emotional Investment: By the time the full scope is revealed, the successors are emotionally committed. They have already invested in relationships, shared dangers, and personal growth.
4. Allows for Testing and Adjustment: Each layer reveals how the successors respond, allowing the program to adjust if necessary. The "mistake" task is a test that Natsunagi must pass before she can fully inherit the role.

Timing of Revelation – The One-Year Delay: 《Siesta》 explains that the revelation was delayed for multiple reasons, each strategically significant:

Purpose 1 – Healing and Stabilization:

"Master Siesta had to suppress the savage personality of Hel that was asleep in Nagisa's body, and it would take a long time to stabilize her."

Natsunagi needed time to recover from the heart transplant and the suppression of Hel. The dual consciousness (Natsunagi and Hel) required careful management. Siesta spent a year within Natsunagi's heart, negotiating with Hel, preparing her for integration. Premature revelation could have destabilized Hel or caused psychological damage.

Purpose 2 – Successor Integration and Organic Bond Formation:

The four successors needed to find each other and form bonds organically. Siesta did not force them together; she created conditions for them to meet naturally:

* Natsunagi and Kimihiko met through the heart's "pull" (engineered by Siesta).
* Saikawa was brought in through the sapphire incident (orchestrated by SPES but anticipated by Siesta).
* Charl was encountered on the cruise ship (Siesta's legacy planted there).
* The group lived together, argued, shared meals, and developed genuine relationships.

Forcing them together prematurely would have created resentment or dependence. Organic bonding creates genuine loyalty.

Purpose 3 – SEED's Activation Timeline:

Siesta understood that SEED would only become active after a year of waiting. SEED had lost its best vessel candidates (Siesta and Hel) and needed time to find alternatives. Siesta timed her revelation to coincide with SEED's emergence, ensuring her successors would be ready when the threat materialized. 《Siesta》 states: "SEED shall be arriving personally to act. While SEED had not acted openly over the past year, it seemed the situation had changed recently."

Purpose 4 – Fuubi's Anticipated Actions:

Siesta anticipated that Fuubi (the 《Assassin》) would take extreme measures to stop SEED, including sacrificing Saikawa. She timed the revelation so that her successors would be strong enough to resist Fuubi's pressure. If they had learned the truth earlier, they might have been vulnerable to Fuubi's "ends justify the means" argument.

Deception – The Nature of 《Siesta》

《Siesta》's Self-Description – The Strategic Cover: "Charlotte. I shall repeat myself, I am just a robot."
The Deception's Layers

Layer 1 – The Cover: 《Siesta》 presents herself as a mere AI program, a robot built from Siesta's preserved body and memories. This is technically true but not the full truth.

Layer 2 – The Purpose of the Cover:

1. Lowers Expectations: Everyone treats her as a tool, not a person. They do not expect her to have agency, emotions, or independent will.
2. Reduces Emotional Attachment: It would be painful to treat her as Siesta and lose her again. The cover protects the successors from additional grief.

The Ritual Steps:

1. Place Natsunagi before the mirror — Creates a visual separation between Natsunagi and her reflection (Hel).
2. Remove the red ribbon — Symbolically removes Siesta's influence; allows Hel to be seen clearly.
3. Use the hand mirror to create an Infinity Mirror effect — Creates a portal-like visual; gives permission for the "other" to appear.
4. Light the candle and dim the room — Creates an atmosphere of ritual and importance; sets the emotional tone.
5. Leave Natsunagi alone with the mirror — Allows private dialogue; no witnesses to create pressure.

How the Ritual Actually Works:

Kimihiko asks: "You lied when you said the Infinity Mirror could summon Hel, didn't you?"

Why This Works Psychologically:

1. Visual Separation — Seeing Hel in the mirror (not as Natsunagi but as a separate reflection) allows Natsunagi to speak to Hel as another person.
2. Ritual Framing — The ritual gives permission to have a conversation that might otherwise be too painful or embarrassing.
3. Private Space — Natsunagi can speak freely without the group watching (initially).

Foresight – The Ten-Day Ultimatum as a Strategic Catalyst

The Ultimatum's Strategic Structure and Psychological Impact: Fuubi Kase, acting as the 《Assassin》 and speaking with the authority of the Tuner organization, gives the group ten days to prove they can defeat SEED without sacrificing Saikawa. If they fail, she will personally kill Saikawa to destroy SEED's vessel. This is not a threat but a promise—Fuubi's commitment to her mission is absolute, and her ruthlessness is well-known to the group.

Strategic Purpose of the Ultimatum across Multiple Dimensions:

1. Creates Overwhelming Urgency: The group cannot delay, cannot hesitate, and cannot waste time on internal conflicts. They must act immediately and decisively. Every day, every hour is precious. This urgency prevents the group from falling into analysis paralysis or becoming paralyzed by fear.
2. Forces Unity through Necessity: They must work together because there is no time for the luxury of individual action. Natsunagi, Kimihiko, Saikawa, and Charl must coordinate their efforts or fail together. The ultimatum eliminates the possibility of going separate ways or pursuing individual agendas.
3. Tests Resolve under Pressure: Only those truly committed to the mission and to each other will endure the pressure. The ultimatum reveals who is willing to sacrifice and who will break. It separates the committed from the merely curious.
4. Provides a Clear Framework for Action: The ten-day timeline gives structure to their actions. They can plan, execute, and evaluate within a defined window. This prevents aimless wandering and forces strategic thinking.
5. Creates a "Do or Die" Mentality: There is no retreat, no alternative, and no safety net. They must win within ten days, or Saikawa dies. This eliminates the possibility of giving up or settling for partial success.

Why the Ultimatum Was Anticipated by Siesta: Siesta knew Fuubi's nature as the 《Assassin》 intimately—they were colleagues within the Tuner system, and Siesta understood that Fuubi would prioritize mission success over individual lives, even the life of a young girl like Saikawa. By creating a situation where Fuubi would issue an ultimatum, Siesta ensured that her successors would be forced to act decisively rather than hesitate. The ultimatum is not an obstacle but a catalyst—it accelerates the timeline and sharpens the team's focus.

Siesta's Strategic Insight into Fuubi: Siesta understood that Fuubi was not evil—she was ruthless because she had seen too much suffering and believed that sacrifice was necessary. Fuubi's philosophy was "the ends justify the means," and she was willing to sacrifice individuals to save the world. Siesta's plan does not try to change Fuubi's nature; it uses Fuubi's nature as a pressure mechanism. This is a masterful use of an ally's predictable behavior as a strategic tool. Siesta knew exactly how Fuubi would react to the situation, and she designed the entire legacy plan to leverage that reaction.
