The Detective Is Already Dead Volume 4

EVENT 1: Establishing Victory Conditions — Penthouse Meeting (Early Morning)
Context: After defeating Fuubi Kase and rescuing Yui Saikawa, the group gathers in Fuubi's penthouse. Fuubi gives them a 10-day ultimatum: prove they can defeat SEED without sacrificing Saikawa, or she will kill Saikawa herself. Natsunagi responds on behalf of the group.

Feat 1: Articulating Non-Negotiable Victory Conditions

Natsunagi doesn't just accept the challenge – she immediately reframes the objective. While Fuubi operates on utilitarian logic (sacrifice one to save many), Natsunagi declares a “perfect victory” condition: no casualties, no sacrifices, everyone laughs together at the end. This demonstrates strategic foresight because defining victory shapes every subsequent decision. She also shows leadership by speaking for the group without hesitation.

Key Dialogue:

“We won't let anyone be killed, and we won't let anyone be sacrificed. We will laugh together and win together in the end. That's our goal, and our victory condition.”

SCD Categories: Strategic Framing | Leadership | Moral Reasoning
EVENT 2: First Clue Discovery — London Hotel Room (After Arrival)

Context: After landing in London, Natsunagi and Kimizuka check into a hotel. Kimizuka finds a book in the closet – the 《Bible》, which contains prophecies of future events including a monster called Medusa attacking London.

Feat 2: Recognizing the 《Bible》 from Past Memories

When Kimizuka shows her the book, Natsunagi immediately recognizes it as the 《Bible》 – the same book Hel possessed a year ago. Her expression shows recognition before Kimizuka explains. This proves her memory integration from her time as Hel is functional, allowing her to access past intelligence that would otherwise be lost.

Or maybe cause she also saw The Lost Memory and noticed that she was carrying it at the time (as hel) but still…

SCD Categories: Memory Recall | Information Recognition

EVENT 3: Flight Investigation — 10,000 Meters Above the Pacific (Flight to London)

Context: On the international flight, flight attendant Olivia announces a missing passenger – Mia Whitlock. Natsunagi and Kimizuka begin investigating. They search the restroom and passenger areas but find nothing.

Feat 3: Applying Knox’s Ten Commandments to a Real Mystery

Natsunagi recalls Knox’s Ten Commandments, a set of rules for fair-play mystery novels written in 1928. Specifically, she cites the rule that “not more than one secret room or passage is allowable.” She reverses the logic: if fiction allows only one secret room, then in this real locked-room case, there must be exactly one hiding place. This meta-cognitive leap – applying literary frameworks to reality – shows exceptional deductive flexibility.

Key Dialogue:

“Knox's Ten Commandments... I thought we would find something if we followed the same rules in solving the mystery... Not more than one secret room or passage is allowable. So what if we take this rule and reverse it? Yeah, there's one hiding place in the plane.”

SCD Categories: Deductive Reasoning | Meta-Cognitive Framework Application | Lateral Thinking

EVENT 4: First Class Revelation – Accusing Olivia (Mid-Flight)

Context: After deducing the secret room principle, Natsunagi and Kimizuka are invited to First Class by Olivia. Natsunagi directly accuses Olivia of hiding Mia Whitlock.

Feat 4: Process of Elimination Leading to Culprit Identification

Natsunagi lays out her reasoning chain: (1) She and Kimizuka searched everywhere passengers can access – no Mia. (2) Therefore Mia must be in a crew-only area (cockpit or meal cart). (3) Only a flight attendant could hide her there. (4) Olivia’s initial announcement asking for a detective is suspicious – a normal missing person case would involve police, not detectives. The conclusion: Olivia is the orchestrator.

Key Dialogue:

“You're the one who hid Mia Whitlock—Miss Olivia, no?”

SCD Categories: Process of Elimination – High | Deductive Logic – High | Suspicion Targeting

EVENT 5: Reading the Culprit’s True Motive – The Double-Bluff (Mid-Flight)

Context: Olivia points out a contradiction in Natsunagi’s accusation: “If I'm the one who caused this incident... it would be unreasonable for me to ask you to solve it.” Natsunagi answers without hesitation.

Feat 5: Identifying the Test Hypothesis and Culprit’s Hidden Desire

Natsunagi recognizes that Olivia has two conflicting motivations: duty (prevent anyone from meeting the Priestess) and personal hope (want someone worthy to meet her). The contradiction is resolved if Olivia is testing them – she wants them to succeed despite her official role. This is advanced theory of mind: understanding that people can hold opposing desires and that actions reveal the true, suppressed wish.

Key Dialogue:

“You have a mission not to allow us to meet the Priestess. But somewhere deep down, you wanted us to meet the Priestess... or maybe you're testing us with this mystery, hoping that we're worthy of meeting the Priestess.”

SCD Categories: Psychological Profiling – Very High | Double-Bluff Detection – Very High | Empathic Accuracy – High

(3) The elaborate nature of the disappearance (a locked-room mystery at 10,000 meters). She concludes that Mia Whitlock is the Priestess – the very person they came to London to find. Recognizing him despite her own appearance change and the passage of time demonstrates exceptional long-term memory and facial recognition.

EVENT 8: Churchyard Deduction – Accusing Rose Bennett (London, Day 1, Evening)

Context: After gathering information from doctors and other victims, Natsunagi and Kimizuka deduce that all Medusa victims are connected to Daisy Bennett. Natsunagi goes to the churchyard where Daisy is buried, expecting to meet Rose Bennett.

Feat 8: Building a Circumstantial Case Without Physical Evidence

When Rose arrives, Natsunagi directly accuses her. She lists the evidence: (1) all victims tormented Daisy after death, (2) all have the same poison, (3) that poison matches the gas from a case two years ago (Kimizuka and Siesta’s Medusa mansion case), (4) Rose has motive. When Rose demands proof, Natsunagi admits she has none at the moment but says searching Rose’s house would find the poison. This shows she understands that a confession is more valuable than evidence in this context.

Key Dialogue:

“Rose Bennett, you're Medusa, aren't you? ... I'm sure I'll find the poison if I search your house.”

Instead of persisting out of pride, she unties the red ribbon binding her hair and says, “Please, lend me your power.” This is peak self-awareness – knowing when to step aside for someone better suited.

My Final Remark
Nagisa Natsunagi is a high-tier hybrid intelligence type – strongest in emotional reasoning, metacognition, and pattern recognition. She lacks Kimizuka’s street-level cunning and Siesta’s long-term omni-competence, but surpasses both in self-awareness and the ability to delegate appropriately. Her word spirit ability enhances her tactical adaptability but her baseline physical frailty is a limitation.

EVENT 1: Accepting the Ace Detective Role with Resolve — Saikawa’s Birthday Party (Volume 8, Chapter 1)

Context: Three months after the SEED crisis, Natsunagi has been officially recognized as a Tuner and given the title “Ace Detective” – the role Siesta once held. At Yui Saikawa’s 15th birthday party, Charl questions whether Natsunagi truly deserves the position, given that she has no special powers (Hel is dormant) and hasn’t received any missions yet.

Feat 1: Declaring Her Own Will to Become a True Ace Detective

When Charl asks if Natsunagi is merely a “decoration” or a substitute detective, Natsunagi does not get defensive. Instead, she acknowledges that Ice Doll (the government official) may have ulterior motives for appointing her. However, she declares that she will work hard to become a real Ace Detective – not to fulfill someone else’s plan, but by her own will. She clarifies that she is not a substitute for Siesta; she is living her own life. This demonstrates metacognitive awareness of how she is being used by the Federal Government, combined with a refusal to be a passive pawn.

Key Dialogue:

“I don’t know what she was thinking behind that mask… There’s probably a meaning to why she made me an Ace Detective. But even if she has some intention or hidden agenda, I’ll work hard to become a true Ace Detective to the point where they will earnestly seek our help.” and “I’m not a substitute detective. This’ll be my life now.”

“I won’t give up. I won’t stop trying to learn more about you… just purely being able to trust each other and understand each other better, that’s the kind of relationship I want us to have.” and “But maybe not now, after all… Because it wouldn’t be fair to say it now.”

EVENT 9: Deduction – Siesta Caused Her Own Heart Anomaly (Volume 8, Chapter 3)

Context: Natsunagi and Kimizuka are called to the hospital because Siesta’s heart showed an anomaly. The Inventor Stephen Bluefield arrives to examine her – the first time he has visited in months. Natsunagi reasons that Siesta deliberately caused the anomaly to summon Stephen.

Feat 9: Inductive Reasoning from Effect to Cause

 When Kimizuka asks why Siesta would cause a disturbance in her own heart, Natsunagi reframes the question: instead of asking what caused the anomaly, ask what the result of the anomaly is. The result is that Stephen came to the hospital. Therefore, Siesta caused it to call Stephen – because she knew Natsunagi and Kimizuka needed to speak with him. This is a classic detective reverse-engineering deduction. She concludes: “That’s because Siesta is a detective who makes wishes come true.”

Key Dialogue:

“Instead, what if we interpret the occurrence of the disturbance in Siesta’s heart as the cause? What could be the result of that? Stephen Bluefield came here.”

SCD Categories: Inductive Reasoning – Very High | Reverse Engineering – High | Cause-Effect Analysis – High

EVENT 10: Questioning Stephen – Arguing for the Value of Follow-Up (Volume 8, Chapter 3)

Context: After confirming Siesta is stable, Stephen tries to leave, saying his job is only to save people, not to answer questions. Natsunagi stops him with a logical argument.

Feat 10: Persuasive Logic – The Cycle of Progress

Natsunagi argues that technology advances by witnessing what happens to the people you save and what affects the tools you create afterward. If Stephen wants to save more people in the future, he needs to pay attention to that cycle. This is not an emotional plea but a rational argument grounded in Stephen’s own values as an inventor and doctor. Stephen, impressed, agrees to answer questions while moving.

Key Dialogue:

“But don’t you think technology advances by witnessing what happened to the people you saved and what affects the tools you created afterwards? If you want to save more people with medicine and science in the future, pay more attention to that cycle.”

SCD Categories: Logical Persuasion – Very High | Value Alignment – High | Strategic Argumentation – High

EVENT 11: Understanding the Vampire Rebellion’s Scope (Volume 8, Chapter 3)

Feat 11: Identifying the Core Contradiction

After learning that the Federal Government ordered the creation of vampires and is now using Scarlet to exterminate them, Natsunagi asks: “So why is the government now making Scarlet kill his own kind?” This cuts to the heart of the contradiction. Stephen does not answer, but the question itself demonstrates Natsunagi’s ability to identify the central mystery that needs to be solved.

“So why is the government now making Scarlet kill his own kind?”

EVENT 12: Acting as Decoy – Using Her DNA to Lure Gluttony (Volume 8, Chapter 3)

Natsunagi understands that Gluttony is attracted to superior genes. She has the DNA from SEED experiments – making her valuable prey. EVENT 13: Inspiring Reloaded in the Final Battle (Volume 8, Chapter 3)

Context: Reloaded, severely injured, is determined to face Gluttony one last time. Natsunagi, who has been watching from the sidelines, shouts encouragement.

Feat 13: Inspirational Speech – Reframing Loss as Preservation

 Reloaded believes Gluttony has taken everything from her – her rival, her ability to walk, her purpose. Natsunagi tells her that Gluttony hasn’t taken anything; it hasn’t destroyed anything. She declares that Reloaded will not lose because “a magical girl will never lose to a malicious monster.” She promises to remember Reloaded’s back – to carry her story forward. This speech gives Reloaded the emotional strength to make her final leap. It demonstrates Natsunagi’s ability to reframe despair into determination.

EVENT 14: Confronting the Witch – Accepting Marley’s Request (Volume 9, Chapter 1)

Context: After graduation, Natsunagi and Kimizuka encounter the “Witch holding parasol” (Marley), who asks them to find her lost hometown. Marley has amnesia and only knows her name and a song. Natsunagi accepts the request immediately.

Feat 14: Empathy-Based Decision Making

When Kimizuka asks why she accepted so readily, Natsunagi says “Because I was just like you.” She recognizes her own past – searching for her lost memories and identity – in Marley. This is not a logical deduction but an emotional connection that drives her actions. However, it is a strategic decision as well: Marley’s case will eventually intersect with the vampire rebellion, giving Natsunagi a legitimate reason to investigate vampire-related matters that the Federal Government might otherwise restrict. She shows the ability to follow her heart while still serving the larger mission.

Key Dialogue:

“Understood. We will accept this request… Because I was just like you.”

SCD Categories: Emotional Intelligence – Very High | Client Assessment – High | Strategic Opportunism – Medium

EVENT 15: Deducing Siesta’s Hidden Meeting with Bruno (Volume 9, Chapter 2)

Context: Natsunagi travels to Hokkaido with Kimizuka to meet the Information Broker, Bruno Belmondo. She uses Siesta’s old diary (a handover document from Ice Doll) to deduce where Bruno might be. The diary shows that on May 3rd, five years ago, Siesta did nothing but leisure activities – which Natsunagi finds suspicious.

Feat 15: Deduction from Negative Evidence

Natsunagi argues that Siesta would not waste an entire day. If the diary shows no work, that means Siesta deliberately hid something – likely a secret meeting with Bruno. She cross-references this with a list of possible hideouts provided by the Men in Black and deduces that Bruno would be at the location where Siesta met him five years ago on the same date. This is a sophisticated use of negative evidence (absence of record as evidence of concealment) combined with pattern recognition and temporal reasoning.

Key Dialogue:

“There’s no way she didn’t do anything at that moment… So there must have been some movements on May 3rd five years ago. But for some reason, no records were left. For example, what if she had a secret meeting with the ‘Information Broker’ like me right now?”

SCD Categories: Negative Evidence Deduction – Very High | Temporal Pattern Recognition – High | Information Synthesis – Very High

EVENT 16: Negotiating with Bruno – Extracting Government Officials’ Location (Volume 9, Chapter 2)

Context: Natsunagi finally meets Bruno Belmondo. She knows he won’t give her direct answers about Siesta’s revival or Scarlet’s secrets. Instead, she asks for something he can provide: the current location of the Federation Government officials who hold the secrets.

Feat 16: Strategic Question Framing and Negotiation

Natsunagi understands Bruno’s limitations as an Information Broker – he maintains world balance, not personal wishes. Instead of asking for information he cannot give (“how to save Siesta”), she asks for information he can give (“where are the government officials now”). She frames it as her own decision to confront them directly. Bruno, impressed, gives her a map. This demonstrates advanced negotiation strategy: identify what the other party can and cannot give, then ask for what is possible within their constraints.

Key Dialogue:

“No, I don’t think you’ll tell me that either… So I’ll ask them directly. The people in charge of the ‘Federation Government’. Tell me where they are now.”

SCD Categories: Strategic Negotiation – Very High | Constraint Awareness – High | Information Targeting – High

EVENT 17: Confronting the Federation Government – Kicking Down Doors (Volume 9, Chapter 2)

Context: Natsunagi and Kimizuka locate the secret meeting of seven high-ranking government officials (including Ice Doll and Odin) in a mansion in Hokkaido. Kimizuka kicks the door open, and Natsunagi takes the lead in questioning.

 Natsunagi refuses to be deflected by the officials’ bureaucratic evasions. She directly asks why they are fighting Scarlet despite him being their former Tuner. EVENT 18: Deducing Scarlet’s True Bride – Marley, Not Siesta (Volume 9, Chapter 2)

Context: After Odin explains that Scarlet’s original wish was to protect a certain girl, Natsunagi has a realization.

Feat 18: Synthesizing Information to Correct a False Premise

Everyone, including Kimizuka, assumed the girl Scarlet wanted to protect was Siesta – because Scarlet called Siesta his “bride candidate.” But Natsunagi realizes the timing is wrong: Scarlet asked for protection fifteen years ago, before he even met Siesta. The girl he wanted to protect was someone else – his childhood friend, Jeanne, who was resurrected as an undead and became the amnesiac singer Marley. This deduction connects Marley’s request, the burned village, Scarlet’s contract, and the vampire rebellion into a single coherent narrative. It demonstrates the ability to re-evaluate assumptions when new evidence contradicts them.

 “I see. We misunderstood. Scarlet’s real bride is not Siesta.”

EVENT 20: Delivering the Final Truth – Playing Saikawa’s Song (Volume 9, Chapter 5)

Feat 20: Using Technology and Art to Break Through Denial

 Natsunagi understands that Scarlet’s denial is a defense mechanism – admitting Marley is a vampire would mean his fifteen years of killing his own kind were in vain. Words alone cannot break through. She uses Saikawa’s voice – the song that Marley taught her – as proof that Marley remembers her past. Final SCD Assessment for Volumes 8 & 9:

Nagisa Natsunagi continues to evolve from a passive “substitute detective” into an active, morally complex investigator. Her greatest strengths remain emotional intelligence and the ability to connect with others (whether human, vampire, or undead) on a deep level. Her willingness to sacrifice herself as a decoy and to accept imperfect outcomes shows maturity. She is now a High to Very High Tier detective – not yet Siesta’s equal in pure deductive genius, but superior in emotional persuasion and moral courage.
