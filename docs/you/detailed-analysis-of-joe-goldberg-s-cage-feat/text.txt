The Cage

The Cage is the place where Joe Goldberg holds his victims throughout the seasons of You. The cage was created by Joe's father, Ivan Mooney, who used the cage as a way of safely negotiating money.
[Stoled From You Fandom]

General Explanation
First of all, before going into the details of the cage, it is important to mention the development process of the cage. The cage we saw in the first season was designed by Mr. Mooney. This cage was not very elaborate and was really just a structure to regulate the humidity levels to prevent the books from getting moldy. However, by the second season, Joe was able to rebuild the cage in just a few days. That in itself is impressive, but it's still faithful to the original design and there are no extra improvements. By the third season, the interior design of the cage was slightly modified and Joe bought the ventilation system outright, ensuring that the materials in the room behind the oven did not deteriorate.

Developments
The important point in this process is this: This cage was never a completely rebuilt or redesigned structure; it was constantly being improved with small, but not major, changes to Mr. Mooney's cage. However, by the fourth season, things were different. As you can imagine, it's not possible to transport a huge cage by plane to France and then to England. (And there was no reason for him to transport it to France, and we never saw it being transported.) From the sketches on Joe's desk in the fourth season, we can see that the cage was redesigned by Joe. Joe had a very limited time here, maybe 2 days, maybe 3 days, because Marianne had been trapped in the box for days. Joe took her out of the box, fed her, stayed with her all day and then put her back in the box and took her back to the cage. At this stage, we now saw that the cage had changed a lot.

* First of all, the door of this cage can be opened with a digital keypad, which is programmed to send a message to Joe if a wrong password is entered.
* On the outside of the cage there is a fan coming from above, it's part of a ventilation system. Joe adapted the ventilation system from the shop above to this room. There is also a fluorescent light in the same area, which turns on during the day and off at night.
* The part that Mr. Mooney allegedly used for money transfers has an electronic button. The lid cannot be opened without pressing this button and only one lid can be opened at a time.

Crystallized Intelligence Aspect
Another impressive part is that the cage was designed without any extra equipment, just the necessary materials and a laptop. Normally, in order to design such a cage, CNC machines (for precision metal and wood processing), laser cutters, high precision sensors and data acquisition systems, oscilloscopes and logic analyzers, industrial power supplies and UPS systems, vibration and shock test equipment, EMC test equipment, etc. are required to make a very detailed analysis and to build a perfect system in practice. However, building such a system in 3 days may only be possible on paper. Joe managed to design a completely flawless cage in 3 days, despite not having any of this. Let's take a closer look at the process of building the cage.It is important to be proficient in fluid dynamics, the laws of thermodynamics and HVAC system modifications to adapt the ventilation system to the cage. Combining this environmental control setup with precise humidity regulation requires a thorough understanding of psychometrics, computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and advanced PID control algorithms. If it is a double-sided flap mechanism, it is necessary to apply the principles of Boolean logic and fail-safe system design, potentially using programmable logic controllers (PLCs) or field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) for optimum reliability and performance. The seamless integration of this subsystem with the control architecture demonstrates knowledge of systems engineering and interface design. The rapid design, prototyping and commissioning of such complex structures using only a laptop computer indicates a mastery of integrated development environments (IDEs), version control systems and possibly model-driven development. Achieving flawless performance with such minimal resources requires the use of advanced optimization techniques such as genetic algorithms or neural network-based control systems.
