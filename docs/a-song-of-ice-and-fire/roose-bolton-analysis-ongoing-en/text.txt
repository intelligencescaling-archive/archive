Chapter I: The Prologue

However, despite all his suspicions, Rickard did not know what was going on in the lands where Roose Bolton ruled. He used the tradition of the Right of the First Night, which had long been banned in Westeros (this is when the ruler of the lands on which a certain family marries has the right to spend the night with the bride before her husband does so). As you know from the plot, Roose did this quite often, but never once did he let even the slightest rumor spread and reach the Starks. In this situation, based on the emotions of Ramsey's mother, he came to the conclusion that she would do and what needed to be done to avoid unpleasant consequences.

Chapter II: The Battle of the Green Fork and the beginning of the Young Wolf's Campaign

In 299 of A.C., Lord Robb Stark marched with his army from the Twins, dividing his army into two lesser groups: he led the cavalry towards Riverrun to remove the siege from it, and sent the infantry led by Roose Bolton towards the Green Fork, where he was supposed to fight Tywin Lannister's army. Roose  understood that due to the lack of knight cavalry, an open battle was unprofitable for him. Therefore, he decided on a forced night march to try to take the enemy by surprise and attack him at dawn. However, the guard notices northerners and the Lannisters manage to leave the camp and form up in a pre-planned battle formation.
Lord Tywin's plan was as follows. The center of the army was made up of infantry and archers, reinforced by three hundred heavy cavalry, under the command of Kevan Lannister. His subordinates were also: Lord Leo Lefford, Lord Serrett and Lord Lewis Lidden. On the right flank stood four thousand heavily armed horsemen, under the command of Addam Marbrand (three quarters of the Western knights), among them were Syr Flement Brax and representatives of the houses of Crakehall and Swift. The left flank adjacent to the river, under the command of Gregor Clegane, was initially sacrificed. It consisted of the weakest and most ill-disciplined units - militias, mercenaries, free riders, three hundred highlanders of Tyrion Lannister. The only real force here was Ser Grigor's own squad. A quarter of the entire army—five thousand men -- was being withdrawn to the reserve, commanded by Lord Tywin himself. It was assumed that the left flank would be defeated by the Northerners, then the young and impulsive Robb Stark would throw all his forces into developing a breakthrough. At this moment, the reserve will counterattack and stop the enemy, and the spearmen of the center will move sideways, crowding the northerners. By this time, the right flank will have defeated those opposing it. As a result, most of the northern army will be surrounded and pressed against the river, and then destroyed.

Thanks to this maneuver, the Lannister Knights were met by the Northern infantry frontally, which thwarted the Lannister plan for a quick victory and allowed the Northerners to retreat in an organized manner.

Chapter IV: The Red Wedding

Roose was one of the main organizers of the Red Wedding along with Walder Frey. At the Red Wedding, all of Roose's strategies and plans played out, because it was only thanks to them that Robb's opponents now had a numerical advantage (3,500 Northerners against 8,500 people of Boltons and Freys). Roose and Walder positioned crossbowmen in positions from which it would be best to fire at the Northerners in the main hall, which means at least that Roose and Walder (who organized the massacre) had to visualize the area of the entire dining hall in order to find places from which the Northerners would be best exposed for their arrows. Everything was calculated perfectly, Grey Wind (Robb's direwolf) was sent to the stables, where he was shot with crossbows. The beginning of the massacre occurred at a time when all the main soldiers of the northerners were drunk and unarmed, which allowed them to be dealt with almost without loss. At the moment when Robb Stark's personal guards were able to organize themselves and try to leave the hall through the main entrance, Frey’s pre-prepared men-at-arms with axes entered there. After the murder of Catelyn and Robb Stark, the massacre continued this time towards ordinary northern warriors, who also could not organize any resistance. As a result, in one day, the army of the North under the command of Robb Stark was completely destroyed, and Robb himself and his mother Catelyn were killed.
It is worth emphasizing that it was the planned actions of Roose and Walder to lull the vigilance of the Northerners that played a big role in their inability to defend themselves. So, one of Robb's strongest warriors, GreatJohn Umber, was drunk to such an extent during the massacre that he did not even have time to arm himself and was quickly stunned.
Also, his unpredictability played a big role in the implementation of this plan, because according to the laws of Westeros, there were so-called "Laws of Hospitality", which stated that anyone who ate or drank anything under the roof of another person (in this case, a lord) automatically received protection from him. The Starks simply did not expect that Roose and Walder would simply ignore such fundamental laws for Westeros.
It is worth noting that before the Red Wedding, Ruse blackmailed Walder in every possible way to help him, mentioning that his grandchildren are Big and Small Walder Frey (originality at its finest, isn’t it?) They are in his homeland as “Pupils".
