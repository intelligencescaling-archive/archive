“Verits rubra…,” Morris whispered thoughtfully. “The red truth.”
Detective Portsman nodded.
“It has the same symbol. This is a genuine article. Speaking of which, the letter itself directly mentions Messrs. Morris and Hopkins by name. As we know, Mr. Morris and I already took up precautions to protect our connection, so whoever wrote it clearly knew of our correspondence. This seems to insinuate that the author of this letter was present at the original crime scene, or how else could they have known?”
Morris tapped his chin with a finger.
“It could theoretically be a forgery made by someone at the original crime scene, but I would personally go by that train of thought.”
Detective Portsman straightened himself and patted his grey coat.
“I must see Mr. Saxon and Mr. White again. It is possible that this is their work if your elaborate theory truly has some merit.”
Morris exhaled, emanating his condensed breath as if he had just removed a cigar from his mouth.
“You seemed to imply Mr. Barnett was very likeable?”
“Yes, his daughter stated he was a very social man who had many friends. He was divorced, but it was about ten years ago, so I doubt that has any bearing on this murder. He was obviously a savvy businessman, so we cannot rule out jealousy as a possible motive.”
“It is also within reason to suggest that he was killed partly because he worked at the same company as Mr. Saxon. It indicates a wish to align the brokerage itself with the crime.”
Detective Portsman nodded before responding.
“I agree. I would presume the man responsible for Mr. Barnett’s death worked within the brokerage with an axe to grind. I suppose I will take a visit to the brokerage again to ask a few questions,” he said as he turned to Officer Lawrence.
“I want you to take Officer Clarence to the bar and give him civilian clothing. Have him ask if anyone has left recently. Instruct him to take careful notes of any suspicious activity. We know the culprit was likely eavesdropping on your conversation and that he knew you were an undercover officer as the letter was clearly intended to be delivered to us.”
Detective Portsman rolled a metal pen through his digits while looking at the house.
“Messrs. Morris and Hopkins, I advise you to make your way to your respective residences in the event of a more direct attack.”
Morris bowed slightly and held out his arm for me to take. Strangely, I thought I might have detected a smirk beneath his normally neutral facade.
“Well then,” he said, fixing his collar which had become undone. “It appears our services are no longer required.”
I took his arm and led him from the path of the house, still seeing the officials descending on Mr. Carrow’s body, like gnats on putrid meat. What a curious case! First, we have a man killed for presumably being part of a cult, then betraying them, followed by a secondary murder of a man who worked at the same company, yet the consensus was that the crime was framed as being of a similar token to that of a cultic death. Even more odd was the third murder, which proves that the culprit is much closer to the original scene than what was initially considered and that is evidence of a wider conspiracy.
“What is your perspective on the case, Morris?”
Morris tapped his forefinger to the head of his walking stick.
“I find it odd that the perpetrator decided to needlessly give away information. It serves its purpose as a warning, but it could have been done in any number of easier, less risky ways. For example, it would have been simplicity itself to drop an anonymous package at Detective Portsman’s office with the use of a pretext, or as it had his name anyways, they would have even been able to leave it right outside the station with assurance that it would reach the person it was intended for. The manner by which it was performed suggests that they want this information to be known. If this information is meant to be known, then we are being baited if the cult is indeed real or not. However, Detective Portsman is not strictly correct with our possibilities being limited to those within the original household. If the cult does exist, then they would feel pressure from Mr. Wilson and his gang, causing them to seek an explanation. If they were intelligent enough to determine a connection between Detective Portsman and I, then the letter would be an excellent gambit to divert our attentions to the ranch of the late Mr. Saxon when in reality they may have derived the information from separate means. I am unsure what the other murder has to do with this grand scheme yet, but I am sure it will come to light soon enough.”
“I see. What about the risk of a personal attack?”
At that instant, a ray of light streaked across the lenses of Morris’s glasses, giving the impression that the eyes behind them were shining with cunning.
“I will take precautions, but I have a feeling that any attack upon us will not be of the type you may be expecting.”
“I do not understand…”
Morris stopped on the sidewalk with a hand on his walking stick and the other in a pocket, smiling at me.
“I would enjoy enlightening you, Bill, but I am afraid I may need you in the dark for a day or two. It is essential to my… ah, plot.”
I sighed.
“Must I always be in the dark?”
Morris reached out his hand to my forearm.
“I am sorry, Bill. But do you trust me?”
I thought back to our adventure with Mr. Wilson and how he was thwarted by Morris. Even if I had won the game on my own, I would likely have been implicated in crimes with the currency. Did Morris not also drag me out of the veritable quagmire of austerity once more with our confrontation with Mr. Buchanan? I was almost never told many of the details, but as he stated when I visited him near the beginning of that account, he has never led me wrong.
“Yes, I do. Possibly even with my life.”
Morris took out a box of cigarettes and offered me one before taking one himself and lighting it.
“I cannot overstate how much I value your trust, Bill. As you have seen yourself, this place is full of those who mislead and deceive, so a dependable man has incalculable worth.”
I almost laughed.
“I thank you, Morris, but from what I perceive, you are not to be excluded from those who deceive.”
Morris chuckled, almost choking.
“The difference is that I am crooked with those who are crooked. As you very well know, I hardly dupe those who do not try to hoodwink people themselves. There are, of course, exceptions, such as if I require your ignorance for the sake of a plan, but that in itself is of great assistance to me.”
“It is true that you are a very… unique man, unlike anyone I have ever met. You have had a variety of times you could have betrayed me if you really were an unsavory man, but you never have, at least in the long term.”
Morris sneered.
“I shall take that as a compliment. I only hope you trust my ability in execution.”
I broke into a throaty laugh.
“If there is anything I have complete faith in regarding you, Morris, it is certainly your ability.”
Morris paused to inhale some of the smoke.
“Thank you, Bill. I trust we shall continue this venture?”
“Affirmative,” I said as we went arm in arm back to his apartment, the morning sun unimpeded and casting its warm light upon the neighborhood, enunciating the normally gray tone of the edifices.
