CHAPTER 4: THE HAZED, CONFUSED DAYS.

I must say, the more I collect again the more I lose: to name of the absent is to perhaps lose parts of their un-essence and return them to a falsity present which comforts those still able to do that very ritual.

If the pieces left are what we remember for in their state remaining, then those of which are forgotten we are forbidden from true touch, true holding, only the emptiness of standing amongst what was, an absence: she had spent most of her youth living in fantasia and in time grew out from the throne and heritage of that imaginary kingdom.

A piece, a piece of me she un-announced; if possible, for entering and leaving the visible to the unseen, underneath and above awareness and un-awareness, to sleep once again and to awake, feeling for the unfelt and the lightness of an experience, all separated from the entity homogeneous with individual essence and without-ness. To persist where persistence is unconsidered, small and vanishing with the spirits in time solely, and in the intervening relationship of the twin dormancy's that deny and define none but the agonies and the words of the speaking un-spoken, the creatures without matter but the substance of their own, insisting upon themselves, till' upon a train station they board towards the oblivion of growing without ever growing: realizing, never learning, speaking but never singing, words and music and pieces on the board passed.

This is indeed an analysis of her character: she said on many an occasion she lived in the clouds, with the imprisonment of the body staying her of the prevailing to the mood-relief of eternal dream.

She, admittedly as growing older, accomplished privately, rarely publicly. I was unaware of anything until we had left our hometown; the fellow whom I introduce now, of good nature, fortune, otherwise extent forgetting I have, he be named Odysseus, soon was met to her, and a friendship sprouted: albeit, I always had the edge of formal, friendship intimacy, as I had known the former since youth.

Incidentally, during our stay in the city, of academt work, we met others: Nagumo, and his friend, Lobo. The two were inseparable until they drifted into some intoxications, tearing themselves apart. Alas. Of Nagumo: he decidedly was himself until he in grief transformed from he to another. Of Lobo, he be of west of rest and east of the riverrun way that the sea breezes thinning hither in, he came and goes equally like them. A fickle thing the youth: with brown curls above his eyes like Jupiter and the eyes of Apollo. Incidentally one could recall both youths as handsome, but in ways of only themselves.

I never knew much about anything, really. I secluded the in-lonely and I fell forth to other company. Night, being her cursed self and servant to the maligancy of personage, sole-removed of herself and absence required for the memories of persons, and she satisfied herself with the pursuit of the board.

We were not yet those bachelors of arts or practices: merely older youths who had graduated from childhood to the teenaged becoming.

Some of us stayed as children; most did not.

It is of constant betrayal that our senses of time, of our worlds, bodies, friends and lovers, must endure constantly: furthermore, being born in the lightness meant the ever-presence of the light of life and in that life shown itself a darkness of equal opportunity: opened and gnawing to her.

You see, Night had many names for her entity; defined and un-defined, the name of the named and the universe of the singular soul that existed without bounds, a boundary that violated itself to prevail the logic of the owner's senses, their call, title, and selfhood: Night stood before herself constant, and in that piece of game she was played by her own hand, regardless of the sole external force or perhaps many externalities.

The monotony of our days then was amplified by the growing solitude of my friend Night: for many'a'hour locked away she passed, and with the ages wines of ancestry, she was accosted by the sudden persuasions of the forefathers: whom one may surpass, or never surpass and only illusify themselves to their standards.

Night was of constant traitor to her own.

The more she had, the less she really gained: to have was to lose and to lose was to have, to gain to become and to touch of the ceaseless sightlessness, to perceive of control, of the command of ones ego and ambition, of their awareness, Night sunk into the abyss, till the abyss was her and she the abyss ever beginning; to grow was to leap in bounds for her: to wash away that childhood reality; thus the Playing King perished, in a "meadow", razed by its own ruler who had deemed ir no more, inconsequential, forgotten and for-mined of worth.

Her hands. They were the story I remember best, I start here, I wrote to them as the details returned. Those hands, thin, calloused, but gentle, capable of firmness, usually restive and placid, but still, I feared them to an extent, however, I loved them as well, her hands, in everything they were, and if I knew how to describe them more skillfully, I would say they were beautiful, as she was in whole, beautiful. They could really play the piano, her hands, force-delicately raise themselves, mix and muddle. I loved her hands simply becausethey were the hands of an angels, stolen from some fresco, which was unfit for the body it attached itself to, and I would fear her hands would fall, run away, return to where it came: I seflishly can remark that Night's hands, such as her mother Ice, and her brother Ashes, all withheld this beauty; I felt as if they were the only parts that were not isolated of this earth; as if this condemned birthright of theirs was annihilated in their hands.

For what is love to me? I loved their hands, her hands, but I did not love the persons. Only friends.

The isolation consumed her and she consumed it; in company and alone she was; a way a path to the good way of all she denied coming. Once along the way—a wayside parking—stooped a real estate of sanity, purchased by the passerby with the least, and in that she could never purchase these lying-bys, for the mind was already far too logical.

—Night, "It is a feeling. A feeling of truth, as alongside the feelings of victory, the spirit of chess, the board itself, king, queens, pawns and bishops: all asserting themselves as true: however, in the material they ask only for the perceptive deed, and for the trueness to surface, and only surpassing our own hearts and agonies, only surpassing now my own agony, wordless, is the annulment of them all, to arrive to the truth, to the loyalty of our hearts; and which grave of this loyalty is the iridescent grace of the wholeness, which bearing a human face in everything consoles us of our own conflictions; till the loss again of this annulment, till the loss of our recognitions, till the checkmate, we search for the bearing, the human face, the truth loyalty, until we cannot."

—Night, "...Ah? My friends are out; you aren't. Faded, are you Vergilius to me? Or something of different nature, of a quality and course I don't really get? The fog and mists of your entity, the fog and mists that so unknowingly obscure us far more than the endless dark which with we consume and perceive as victor over the many-faceted worlds that in perception light is weaker, ah. Fog and mists. I am far too lazy: far too timid, to accomplish much. I only accomplish what allows itself to be; or, what is and what has been, and I have made it be to existence? Is anything of the pieces, any move, of mine? Or is it another command, cold, sneer-lipped, and forgoing of our mortal vessels, which wilt and disappear, and they forevermore beyond us?"
