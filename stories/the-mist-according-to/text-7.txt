**CHAPTER 7: THIS AND NO SENSE. (Sacrificing readability and accessibility a little)**

Mist sets on the city: make haste through the fog. Unsee and unfortunate be lost to it.

The city will know of its own fog and demise whilst remaining an ever protector to its inhabitants: present and past, same as any old place, same as meadow same as hometown, different and felt odd.

Because of where opinion exists it is dominated by the majority perception of ill-reason: emotion and the trajectory of our own-ness is the corruption, and in that corruption my friend Night attempted to escape of it: alas the poet Virgil, alas I Virgilius, am a poor friend, and all companies she had held are of similar quality; and besides it, we had imagined her alone and forever of the condemned when perhaps in that very prisonrealm she had ascended of the privations of the here past here future: dynasties from her form may wander but we would never know of them because in the truth-un-here there can only be the soul and body, and instead of assuming we know without even seeing.

Sight is torture my friend I the Night, sight is what you have when you live in external means, sight of time, sight of ideas, sight of the consolations of the ever-present everything's that torment and love.

Subredia is dying: every city is, every moment it perishes as it drifts from what the previous have known it as; Subredia is alive.

If you may imagine my reader, I have felt that Night still lives, still is here, as a patient in the quiet unknown sanatorium in Italy, in Germany: she is there, she is here: she is the neverwhere, I must confess.

She is of invention: I perceived as her alien identity perceived me, and I was lost as many were to the possible relation to our world:

She spoke:

—"By this reason, the inherent partial truth of enjoying the dull, the unchanged, the arguing, the bitter and the sour, is to convince ourselves that we have been befitted of new eyes to see, but we are never seeing new but seeing from different shades."—

—"...The subject and the matter isn't necessary. A deal with my soul as the taken is inconsequential, because such a thing may not exist outside the enjoyment of the one who perceives it as possible."—

—"...A nation of silently, unknown contracted people, who are here by their own accord: and I have never been given such contract."—

—"..."

She perhaps realized that even though she had attempted today and all yesterdays, she was like all people un-immune to the agonies of tomorrows; the pieces of the world-chess game was in truth more of an imitation of the glass bead game.

Oh, welcome oh day, and welcome oh Night! That which remains of the presence and the conscious will to forever-state grows weaker; the confession I make haste to clarify is that this is the conundrum of this tragedy, and that is there is no tragedy for those who believe they have not experienced such misfortune: for when does our own perception dominate the other? When has the only delusion kept the solitary prisoner awake and breathing?

Frankly I fear that I am not myself: I recognize Night's reflection but not I, and I along the way the whole way the through-ness and intense un-dualclarity that demands of me to question, to sleep, to wake in dream, to wake in her meadow, to play King: it is plausible we, or I, have dealt upon myself the obliteration of my ownership of this state; for coming, oh Night, oh way to the small place, for the forgotten Subredia, for ever-present intertwined betrayals of our allegiances to the certainties of the general being: of the allegations towards lifes purposes perhaps we must first question and dissect the greatest conspiracy of the semi-identity: take it at this, there in I there is Night more than myself. I am Night in Faded's body, his existence?

In this place, Subredia, the constructed little place stemming from the delusions of recognition, I see, or Night saw, that in it there was the multitude of expressions and words; but what has words done to further express and explain the itself and its own purposes?

Subredia, such as Night, such as I, are all one and the same: and in this I say clearly, we are clearly the condemned race, to lesser or more extreme degrees.

Night may as well say: —"If I have realized that the inbetween of the oppression and the liberations of my own awareness multiplying, I have seen and never grasped the deeper aspects of the internal struggles, and external as well. The matter is that I have felt and seen the experiences that my consciousness denies me; I have felt pleasures and pains, I have debated and I have taken opinions: easily in my selfish apathy I may declare them all utterly meaningless, however that is the incorrect assertion; nothing is meaningless, simply because it knows itself as nothing and everything: the innert, the unmoving, they exist forever in eternity, in their moment, without the slightest hint of knowing. The mind itself is dominated by the world and the un-world, buffered by the fact there lying inside the middle is the constant reclamations of both accumulated reasons, logic, memories, and perceptions, the complete unconscious."

Night, or I Faded, or Night but in me, or I in Night—"I may note as well that in the world which feels so active yet distant, utterly ensconced in itself and all the troubles, the belief that in the semi-eternal semi-finite visible visions, either tricks of other minds, or illusions of our own, are dominated by the prospect of an inevitable doom, organic, inorganic, timely, or all."

Night—"To demand the truths from anything when that thing may stem from love, disillusionment, or one's own love is as foolish as demanding justice be birthed from rage, hate, and docility. It is with this I will make myself clear: even when alone, or in community, or in neither but the partial realization, the half dream which so many of us move life through, it is the healthiest thing for us to forget ourselves, and yet us humans cannot do such a thing: we are unhealthy beings, who attempt to find health in everything including ourselves, finding love and despair: I believe because I can, and I believe because I cannot: I think therefore my thoughts are not mine, but my past self, one whom has had them: to constantly repeat ones life, not only in material routines, perhaps breaking from them, to now repeat without knowing the constancy's tying us, we make sure to know what is and what isn't; and although I, my own identity, my own perceptions and thoughts, the betrayals of my own trusts with whom I may recall and what I can recall, what I can gain, I realize that I have been, and if it is not beyond my mind, and before the subconscious, perhaps in another, or in the irrational world, or godless world, I have still been."
