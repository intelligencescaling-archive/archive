**CHAPTER 5: DEPARTURE.**

It is necessary that there is clarification of the status and inherent nature of my position with Night Dozor. It is also of this importancy that I place the question's answer of her brother's disposition unto her; he cared deeply, but his heart was in ways a force that was turned inward, as is the ones who live far too close to the centripetal force of our world.

Furthermore, though I attempt to prevent the prevalence of my own emotions, I find it difficult not to pity the brother: as I say, though the coldness which so infected every single one of them, it was that very distance that perforated by the heart grew steadied and steadier, until there was no reason for an interaction: Night asserted upon her character that she would have the only singular form, never spreading it towards those of whom unwitting wished to benefit from that never-happening gifting.

One can say without much observation and doubt that the states of relationships to these people was drenched in the unmistakable waters of tragedy; miracle it lasted as long as it did.

When she left home for the cities on a dusty road that so similar to many other roads foretold no one that this road would be the beginning of a kind of end. The road began and through it she passed onward in journey-style, harboring an Odysseus but more of Ulysses, burnt up forth from her own sessions with inward searching that hurt, hurting and shipping, making and passing, all and again. If you have ever lived in a world that does not regard itself as a world, but only by those aware enough of its history, then that world does not owe you anything but a name: a child owes their mothers and fathers their lives, but a child such as Night, already so grown and forever incased in the stretches of harmony with the visible and the invisible, the coldness, the way that be of Byzantine ships in the Bosphorus: all ships passing through that ancient strate pay respect to what they pass upon, and so shall we, so did she, as she felt that in leaving there came upon the being a fine-ity: once in the darkness of a midst-ever-state, a chronic accumulation of loneliness and the ever-present presences of realities and perceptions, of the beauty of her hometown, of her memory, of her and her name, of Night, she in sorts boarded a Byzantine Ship, greek and Orthodox, ventured somewhere, converted and ascended to the Papal States Forgery of her mimicry state that which consoled her unconsolable entity, that denying and unfeeling felt everything so sorely she was incompatible with this ever final-stepping world.

She who alone and in company is thoroughly oppressed by her thoughts and circumstances, and in agony and in feeling knows that to be in joy is to savor that energy that flees of us itself so readily, so readily a way of life and becoming a lonesome aspect which in seeking we preoccupy the mind and soul to journey for: and for joy itself being a composite of the people we love but do not know why, for the places, for Playing King, for fields, for days and making the days feel longer than they ever were: in leaving this very special place does for a moment she realize that the special place is not a "here" but merely a "A", a little swath of land throughout time and space's grip that holds especial means to the wordless and nameless spirits: and in being hurt by that very cruel world, in leaving behind that aspect for future prospects, prophetic and self-speaking, Night not only came unto the lands that inbetween the I and the "Am," (as with all statements with the self, she realized that she was "Am", thinking and feeling, sour and cold, apathetic and withdrawn, but still am, am here, I here), she is not given to the pieces of Chess, but more of, as a player of the game, the game comes to her and she transmutes the moves from there to here: transmuting, figuring out, figuring the way of mind to real.

For when did action hold itself accountable from the consequence of severing the inherent conserving mind's ability to separate all functions, bodily or spiritual, from the meaning of the immutable and beautiful, lonely and rowdy world?

How can anyone understand the game and the  affliction to winning the inconsequential thing? How are we given so much that there amongst us there are those who are solely different by means of solitude, a race ever tortured by themselves, by those around? When does such a classification from us to that race occur? For, in there and forevermore, striking and struggling, stands themself and upon declaring there the victorious groups: groups without forms, groups all in the head alone.

Suffering to her was something I never understood properly: she suffered in a many that was different yet close enough I was nervous to attempt to grasp it, frightened by perhaps the offense of trying to see in the eyes of someone who was apparently inflicted; her brother Ashes may have been affected by that very fear himself.

The nature of her own identity was of being, of being of words, of being of minds and courses and ships, never the same but compelling the movements through "sameness" that the disgruntled and distancing spirit seemed same enough.

Vanishing nobles without heads and rearing their children with heads makes them equally headless; a baby rising without limbs and only the resemblance; in seeing these things, the consequent entity of reason chooses to be overwhelmed firstly by conclusion of emotion arising.

So in leaving, so in returning, never being the same, never being good or bad, but being.

The spirit, Night's spirit, is incapable of being tethered to us in ways we can see; in destruction or resurrection, she climbed the mountain of dark and of choice to find a great array of peaks, similar and shaded, umbral and narrow, yet leading all the same to the distant non-light that still glowed nonetheless; to the creature of this race condemned a light will shine through, even illusory.
