FANFIC OF THE SUB RETURNS: THE MIST ACCORDING TO, PT. 1

# CHAPTER 1: A DREAM

I will write this; it feels as if my first memory of my good friend started with a dream.

I would not like to be of notice to the reader: in fact, it would be best to state quickly and sternly that however this preface may be narrated I wish not to present myself in the foreground foremost, as it before stands this written dedication to my friend, Night Dozor.

My name is Faded Vergilius T. G.

If this was to ever escape the impregnable but beleaguered fortress of my chest of words, I would be displeased and answer only to the questions I deem befitting of the necessary acquisitions of knowledge from whoever finds it.

However, upon a gaze and glance of my words I am thoroughly ashamed at my failures and inability to properly comprehend nor explain what transpirations and their effects unto us and host of persons, which because of it I without realizing am an unabl-ing essence to the truth: I must write as earnestly as I can, and therefore find I must annihilate all pretenses of goodness and kindness I have to my companions past and passing beyond me, a left behind virtue here to remember what has been.

And of the oath of allegiance to the truth and loyalty I had sworn internally an age ago, now it be-foes me and sways me against with arms and legs to whip my hazardous heart and weaponized pen: of the events, and of time, I must ask without an agency of explanation to my reader, expect to befall the dreams of my own, as I shall not attempt an accurate explanation, but instead explain in what way fits and clarifies itself.

As with that being said, I confess: I am no good writer, nor friend. I am merely a person, and I seek for an understanding in my memories: I can say that of all my friends, I am the fool who comes and collects joy.

I will start from where I can first ascertain myself as a competent liar: it started with a dream. Night and I were friends from a small town in Subredia, bound together only by that fact alone. I concede I find no other reason we remained in touch.

I write carefully, as before I lay my words consider well my thoughts will: plainly Night was of good and leaping skill with the board: chess! However throughout those early days, my departed companion would never touch that aspect; she did not like chess.

Night and I were followed by Doggo: he was never close to either of us, but in later days became a sort of guide; a consolation. Whenever we went out to play he followed: whenever we did not return home after dark watching the stars, Doggo would appear and withhold us from that, until someone did find us all, in trio, and return us home. Needless to say, I do deem he as a friend, albeit far. One step away from a farewell.

Furthermore, Night and her brother Ashes, were separable with ease: far apart and drifting distantly every Christmas. He went hunting for foxes far too often, she would complain, rarely; it was not of her character to complain.

What can be known is that her mind was so clearly without form; indefinite. A fractured system, wholly meaningless pieces filling it up. For all she possibly knew, she inhabited an endless array of puzzle pieces that did not choose to fit, nor fit because it must. She was indeed never-befit of her own awareness, I will confess, and continue again, as she had entirely devoted herself to appeasing the various views she would rotate: the mind, as with chess, became a simultaneous shelter and a burden; it was a curse for her to be of bearing to the unbearable, inconsistent lightness of being which so clearly chose itself over reason and logic.

It is human to entirely devote oneself to this life entirely; to be of a piece, and of a perception; perhaps a love and care, a friendship. Like a game of chess we push our pawns and watch the structures fall; if we study all the theory of life we will still suffer: for what reasons do we feel torment? Unknown. Night had spoken to me of the matter many times: inconclusively.

And, if she had indeed existed as I remember, as I perceived, she had been an object that knew it was an object with superior qualities; she had been given form through space, time, and in the between of present and past—my mind, or, as I can say, a human mind, and her mind as well: for, in terms of realization, reality, as with our present emotions, are contained and imprisoned within our self, and is entirely unlimited in capacity, stretching from the expansive ranges of the universe, to the very idiotic things that define themselves as clearly human action.

Some would say she was an apathetic individual with only herself and her reason: her board later, and her sleep.

I would beg to politely differ and decline them of their view which uninformed is doubtless a view I understand.

She was never inclined to open her heart: for in doing so she would relinquish the gates of her heart as well, and sorely become more of a human that felt and could be perceived as one. She would seldom declare a name, using nicknames, and accepted care without returning it.

She had accepted my friendship, but not the identity that I so cultivated. I was T. G. Always T. G. Not Faded: Faded was not to her tastes, but T. G was. A modern name you need, she said one day to me while we strolled down a creek, a dirty frog in my hand. I wondered subconscious a many if she had ever told a truth in her life relating upon a subject she had affected with great indifference.

I vaguely recall she would Play King in her house or in her "meadow,", a dignified position and expressed, a breaking of her usual tone, and I would be not a jester now but perhaps a good herald, my young legs carrying me from here to there, wherever, searching for fabled things, as childhood does, as freedom of mind allows. The "meadow" is where she stayed for many centuries, growing old there and reverting to the current self which in tradition seldom seemed to alter in habit or form: those reversions appeared when she had to go home. She Played King until I believe she had lived into a different Night; not after youth ended, and not before. Her throne was grass: Doggo came like a beggar and was in expulsion a foreign emissary, as she put it. Her "meadow" could be anywhere: she was, indeed then, her own King, with her own little kingdom, and her mind and thoughts and all her time as her loyal subjects.

A great devotion to her cultivating her powers: all about her there was only nothing to an eye passing casual; only in depth could one see the leaping forth from here to there in peering to her.

Life, and thus experience, are constant in loving, and are unable to be divorced from time as well; our minds, as with my writing, as with my memory, is an incomprehensible vagueness which nobody is privy to a full understanding: even in context, even in a life spent a'side, we can only ever know what we may, and be left wondering if we had seen well and known well, just enough to enjoy our recollections and forget the savageries of one's own mind.
