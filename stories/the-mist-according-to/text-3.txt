FANFIC OF THE SUB: The Mist According To, Pt. 3

CHAPTER 3: NIGHT'S WAY

I do apologize for the bloated after-words of the previous chapters I have accustomed myself to: this shall be a concise and short reprieve chapter: the previous chapter was a lengthy discussion; much too say, and much too figure out how to say to begin with.

I will now detail what I have always called, "Night's Way."

For a long time, Night was an afflicted child. Rises her moon; looking through her own room's window, she witnessed a different twilight, an umbral undercurrent that portal-esque sent her through the scapes of dream and awake, and awaking she found herself regretful of that wake; a path a length a singing ever-voice of choice untaken, road unleapt, too many holes she said but the holes were a shallow puddle: she would've been a pianist if Ashes wasn't so skilled at chess.

Speaking of he her brother Ashes, he was an un-present force in any of the family's lives. Oddly enough, although he was never there he was acknowledged as an existent force un-illusory, illuminating by way his various things and makings in his left-behinds by the annual morning vanishing: for some doubt, Night was a prodigy learner of being without being, and eventually it would become difficult to even discern if she was ever there or not.

The kind of loneliness and utter loyalty to others whilst displacing ones own self was a family trait: a family of that seldom race condemned; not so often when the two casks of wine-bottle produced and revealed, Night being herself would venture and drink despite an age unfit (not yet).

Night, in her own words, said in retrospect of her childhood: "She has never participated with others; never had been brought to participate. If she did care at all, she didn't know herself. Nothing had been done besides her own interests. She was tailored into a suit she wore too well—a suit of a devoid intertia, a suit of withdrawal, a suit of disquiet silence."

A childhood is only a childhood if the child is to become: Night never became, she always "had been", and constant, for her repulsions, her desires (or lack of it), her language, they all remained utterly the same; only in a peering of deeper matter and manner was any deviation from the production-line similarity. A manner of contradictions was she; silence was always disquiet, her absence was a presence; her freedom not hers perhaps, and her control was a freedom in other names.

I confess't, if we, in farther long-stretched days, fell into wrestling matches, play-fighting, it shalt be seen and presumed she was not an instigator of them; only I would apprehend her fleeting un-change and bring about childhood, or a minute aspect to her which holdable now and grasping to it she did.

There was a care: it was gone before anyone in overcoming her apathy founded their hands to find it.

Ashes would bring her foxes: she loved them, admittedly, whilst he found them sport and a despise-able creature.

Most of them brought were expectedly deceased. She threw them out: so she told me, but I do recall a burial a ceremony gathering for that fox form, formerly offensive now a placated corpse as all others beneath the earth shall be.

Whatever childhood this was, she had steady arranged the ways of her, and her self separated; peak and past constant, a performance of virtuoso and of no-word, a none and everything: she had hemmed in the little ego death that comes with the angst-rage of growth, but in doing so she found that the metaphorical term and expression was unloyal and unseated rapid: forth and onward she trapped and escaped of she it did.

It felt as though my friend Night lived within two spectrums of anchored certainty; a side being her and the other being everything else she buried or tossed out. Not for fear of what others spoke, but for an anxiety for the things she absorbed: she was obsessed with her own character un-sullied; and in doing so fell through the cave of Sun-Night revolutions that inside she still stood outside and in perception felt above invisible worlds and cities: a hometown, with the people, with the culture miniature, is a glass city.

A glass city: invisible city-scapes; fashioned out of thin and nameless walls see-through, we understand them as glass and unseen: yet acknowledge their presence and the way we may exist with, perceive with, and experience them.

Night, in her way, was a glass city.
