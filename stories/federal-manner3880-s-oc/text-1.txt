||OC SCALING||

"Your task is to add these numbers and provide me with the correct sum within 5 minutes."

"Add them?"

"Start."

My brows furrowed slightly, "What happens to me if I can't do it?"

The man looked at me with a sharp, cold gaze before placing the timer on top of my desk and quietly leaving the room, "Finish before the timer reduces to zero."

He didn't answer my question, but...the way he looked at me reminded me of how *she* used to.

My lips pressed together as I took a deep breath to calm my beating heart.

He'll kick me out if I fail won't he?

I exhaled.

I definitely can't let that happen.

Glancing over to the timer on my desk, I saw 4 minutes and the number 30.

The larger number kept getting smaller which must mean that my time was running out.

Turning my attention back to the numbers on the paper, my eyes kept trailing down the paper on the table.

It had the number 1 at the top and 100 at the bottom.

I guess all the numbers in between were what would usually be there since none of them had more than 2 numbers attached outside of the 100.

I know that putting something with something else means I will end up getting more of that something so that should be what "adding" means.

Finding the sum would mean getting a bigger number after combining all of of these right?

Imagining it as sticks, if I add 1 and 2 I get 3. Add 3 and 4 I get 7.

I continued adding the numbers in my head but soon realized that doing it that way will get confusing the more I add and will take a long time to write out too...

I frowned at the paper while tapping the white pencil I took up from the table on it again and again.

The soft clack of the pencil met my ears everytime I tapped it.

This continued until 4 minutes turned to 3.

I'm running out of time.

If adding the normal way gets confusing the higher I go, what if I added the biggest and smallest numbers together?

I stopped tapping and crossed my arms while leaning back in the seat.

It...could work. Probably.

Alright so, adding 1 and 100 gives me 101.

99 and 2 gives me 10...1 too.

It's the same with 98 and 3.

A smile slowly made its way up my face.

Without wasting another moment, I began writing out the sum of the numbers I had gotten but after reaching the third one, my smile dimmed and I frowned before stopping completely.

I forgot to consider one thing.

The numbers are not as complicated to add as before since they all apparently come up to 101 but it's still going to take too long to write out.

It'll take up the whole page to add up all of those numbers and I'm not so sure if I'll be able to find the sum before I run out of space to use...

I glanced at the timer again 3 minutes and 10.

I watched as the 10 dropped to 9 then 8 and even seven before closing my eyes and thinking up another way to deal with this problem.

Alright...So if all the numbers from 1 to 100, when added from smallest to biggest, come up to 101 then I don't have to worry about how to deal with that and should just focus on writing all of this short enough not to take up too much space.

I stared at the number table for another minute and something ended up catching my attention.

When I add from smallest to biggest I'll end up with 50 and 51 as the last two numbers to add together.

But when I count forward and backward from 50, I end up with exactly 50 both times.

It wasn't the same when I counted from 51.

I ended up getting 49 and 51 after counting. It was the same was for 48. I got 52 and 48 as my answer after counting them too.

I tilted my head with a frown, "50 and 50 is 100...There are 50 smaller numbers and 50 larger numbers which means..."

My eyes slowly widened in realization.

"It could work..."

If I put the 50 and 50 next to each other I'll get the same answer as if I added 101 fifty times without actually having to work out anything at all.

I took up my pen and began writing out the sum with under a minute of time left.

I wrote: All numbers added from biggest and smallest give 101. 50 is the middle of 100. Adding 101 fifty times gives 5050 as the sum. If I put the 50 and 50 next to each other I'll get the same answer as if I added 101 fifty times without actually having to work out anything at all.

But since adding 50 fifty times comes up to 2500 and doing it again leads to 5000. I have to another 50 for it to lead to the same answer as 101 added 50 times.

So my way of doing it is adding 50 * 50's to 51 * 50's. If I put it together like this: 50* (50 + 51) = 5050 it looks more readable.

Fixing it up some more I get 50 * 101 = 5050

As soon as I placed down my pen, the timer dinged and the man made his way over from the now open door.

He looked a little annoyed so I guess he must've been watching me through the glass screen somehow and saw that I just barely finished on time. It might be something else, but that's the only answer I can come up with that makes sense right now.

The man took up my paper and glanced over it. He scrunched his nose.

"Who taught you arithmetic?"

Arithmetic?

Confused, I asked, "What's that?"

The man froze in place and then looked at me with narrowed eyes, "Do you know what multiplication is?" His voice was frosty and demanding.

It felt like if I didn't answer truthfully I'd die.

I looked away from his eyes, "No sir..." My reply sounded uncertain, but it was the truth.

"Then, who taught you to add?"

"I..." I pursed my lips and lowered my head while fiddling with the pencil on the desk, "No one...sir."

"Is that so."

His cold voice creeped into my ears and my chest began pounding. I could feel his eyes staring into the top of my head like a hungry owl watching its food.

"Did I..." I began to speak in a low and shaky tone, "Did I do it wrong, sir?"

"Wrong?" My heart sank.

"On the contrary your answer was flawless." He turned around and began walking away, "Good work Montague. You'll receive another test in an hour's time."

(He is 5 years old. He's lived in the slums since birth so he never got any teaching before this especially since his mother despised his very existence. Everything he knows is from experience [he didn't know that putting more than 1 things together was called addition until this specific scene. He didn't know what a second was. He assigned the word "minute" to the 4 and 3 and not "second" to the numbers after it since he could only go off of what the man said.]

He doesn't know what a bracket is. He just made that separation between the numbers because it made it more readable like he noted. He added the asterisks purely to show that the fifty on the outside interacts with the numbers on the inside separately. He doesn't even know that the asterisk is an asterisk, he just uses it as a stand in for his version of multiplication [which he also didn't know])
