#"CHAPTER SOMETHING": THE DREAM PRINCE: PT. 2

Useful sat down in a landscape that befit a detached, detailed portrait, took with him a bag of sand, tea, and items of no name but only purpose. He observed them, questioned them, and finally decided they meant nothing of substance nor value; value defined itself by the consequences wrought from them, themselves having the only power and reason to be of value, give itself value, or by, wretched powers beyond them, take their value, assert new values, and destroy or create new selves to be manifest in the heart and mind; by act of tyranny, injustice, to injure the other self by means of assumption, assuring oneself of the truthfulness of reality, was not only the greatest sense to build the world upon, and by doing so view it with a critically flawed eye, an eye that pleased one's mind itself, banished the fear of the unknown, because not caring for the unknown meant it did not exist outside the scope of the mind.

For Useful, he reasoned not only was the world around him either a cruel lie, non-existent from the scopes of the mind naked and scant before the reasonable vocations of others that he found unstable; either that, or reality was not a lie and only the singular step between life and death, waiting for death by accomplishing the tasks of motion, and pondering whether, on death's door, you followed your tasks properly; for Useful, he neither truly cared for what he tasked himself with, nor cared for what would occur between, only what happened did he actually consider potent to his future and deniable views on such futures; sensibly he was a man of little action beyond his own perceived ambitions; but Xamot questioned his ambitions and always sought to prove to Useful that the pursuit of wealth was not an original vow, but one that would overtake the mind to question worth by material that only rotted with the death imminent and irresistible; however, Useful had known of this and resorted to assuming it false, he denied it fully, wished never to see it, wageres his own  self on such ambition, and, if he decided to view it as Xamot did, he would as such become a nothing, search yet again for another purpose, and then collapse into the sea of folly, folly then turning to obsessions; obsessions over what his mere purpose was beyond becoming wealthy, why he wanted to be wealthy, not because he pitied others out of spite, nor was it the detached reasoning that sprouted from the learned man's desire to own oneself and better others through silent indifference, no, no, Useful simply thought wealth was equal to a greater person, and the greater the person the more person they were in every aspect.

But, aside this, Useful had his own doubts; he was a doubtful man, doubted reality, was insecure of it, doubted every certain essence such as morality and the birthright of man (the birthright of man is what we assume to be our most natural pieces, virtue, kindness, or we are born innatelt evil), to this doubt it held much merit to his eyes, history told him of innumerable attempts to become the only person to have an equal truth that was of such great value that it rivaled the dichotomy of life, death, and ascend it to eternal questions such as living, what is living; this Useful still doubted, because he found no reason to use his eyes to see it, understand it, and finally reason it.

Useful now banned his own life; as the townsfolk did when he murdered another person; now he wondered, "who have I killed?", and feels the pain that only comes from mercy; mercy killings? No, how twisted, but Useful decides to deny it. Who have I killed? I have forgotten, Useful thinks and knows, but denies effectively. To deny is to be certain of facts as malleable as truth; to be in denial of certain facts, repress them, and take them different than what occurred in actual truth, is to embody an extremely difficult power; to deny is power, to deny is folly, to deny is the only way Useful can convince himself that he is the most sane in any room, and the only person he cannot deny is the thoughts, untamable, of Night; she has no reason to deny any aspect of reality for she has no parts of reality to deny; she has not lived within it; only those who suffer in earnest, those who feel pain, those who let themselves weep, can know if they have truly acquired a heart respectable in social circles.

"Night." Useful muttered. Like some god he waved his hand and wished to a red, malicious but capable "genie" on his shoulder, some Faustian deal, and thus the two minds in sleep and in twilight of the between of consciousness and the entering of surrealistic dream visions combined into one; she had come to his dream; and to her, he had come knocking on her dreams, asking if she wanted to come with him, until the clever "genie" formed up a fake world for both to stand evenly and, for minds so strangely allike yet containing hundreds of contradictions, reason together and understand each other.

Night! Night sat down before Useful in the dreamscape, and observed his regal robes of stardust and celestial bodies, constellations, his red cape embroidered with Capricorns and all sorts of imagery; she wore a plainness that was indescribable; Useful found himself outmatched by her naturalness  although he thoroughly decked himself in regalia befitting of a great monarch; she outshined any attempt, however; the primary beauty is not of material but of genuine-ness, and she was overflowing that very concept. This genuine-ness was banished from Useful's existence; he wasn't exactly there at all, only a man without organs, massive eyes, hidden behind the eyelids, thus removing any possibility of a true self; and even now he wears clothes that are not his; there is no aspect of desirable nature to him, nor the appearance of honorable being, he is simply a detached person until he cannot detach himself any longer, than feels the human state so distantly he believes himself detached.

"Good evening," Useful said at last.

"Are we both...here?" She asked. Useful understood her query; "this is our dream."

"I won't question it," she said.

"Do you enjoy being here?" He asked, not softly, never tenderly, but still in a manner that eased her senses.

"I have not yet felt this, uh, strangeness, so I can't tell," she said. He only nodded; "I see."

"Night; you toil over telling me a truth I am ready to hear," he suddenly said.

"Oh? I do?"

"I believe so. I see it."

"You assume it,"

"Does it matter? Whether I see it, assume it, it is all based on a truth permanent to your character," he said.

"Ah. Justly put I guess?" She answered.

"Not justly put; truthfully put. It is a permanent aspect. You view me in such a way,"

"I view you in pieces," Night said, leaning forward. She was bold for someone locked in a room; cursed. No, not bold. Simplt inquisitive; her eyes were convicted to curiosity long buried by her circumstances.

"...I see." Useful said.

"I think I should clarify! I view you in pieces because I haven't known every piece of you at all; your person is a puzzle!" She said.

"Once I discover an aspect of you, I wonder on it until I can understand it."

"No permanence is truly ours; we see that reality is full of things, and one presupposes that either that thing is good, evil, detestable or praise worthy; then there are the things we cannot decide whether they fit any of it, and that is when we reject them, or they reject themselves," She finished.

"You...try to understand me?" Useful said, somewhat shocked, maybe aghast.

"Yes. I think you're trying to understand me, as well," Night answered, inquisitively.

"Yes, yes. You're a bird without wings, that's all," Useful said.

She seemed to flinch at this for only a second.

"Without wings or perhaps my wings haven't come to their full length yet?" She sighed. "I'd like to know what my wings would look like; you say I have no wings but I have confidence they are there."

"False confidence is a killer. You dream of having wings, having the world beyond you, and now all you do is talk to me in a dreamscape," he said.

"Dreamscape or not, I'd like to feel any world," she said.

"Folly."

"To you."

"Yeah, put it in such a way."

"Dreams, dreams. How impotent; without dreams we would not know despair," Useful said, "you are trapped in a hell, dreaming of the heights of heaven, and I am an aspect of hell brought to you, in your home, by some ignorant Noble, and all you see of me is that I am that aspect of hell, so that is all I will ever be to you," he shook his head and would begin to speak again yet refrained; to speak at such lengths sounded pitiful, and to even hear such speeches he realized that he made himself only a greater instigator.

"You assume! I have no...assumptions of your character," Night retorted.

"Really? You despise me as all others do, for a crime I have no knowledge of! I did not kill anyone," Useful said. He stood up from his seat.

"A crime, yes. It's only...innate! If someone you are made to perceive as violent comes, what will you do? Watch for them of course; be wary of them,"

"That has merit. But I have done nothing of any sorts; I am too detached from this world to even harm,"

"You say that as if you were to re-assure yourself," Night said. Useful felt no anger, only growing doubt; she was in a sense correct. He hated how she would dissect his answers.

"I have a theory about all this; truth is only just another thing that we can bend to what we want; the real truth is never there until it is revealed to be more bitter than any sweet lie; I fly, like a bird, not because I have great wings, but because I have no feet, and my wings are being withered by other birds pecking at them and even myself," Useful said.

Night nodded. She frowned, then shook her head, for a moment she thought, only by thinking at length finally speaking, "To have wings or no wings at all, which is better?"

"It is up to circumstance," Useful said.

"Not really; we make do with what we have, and when we have nothing we make do with what we believe we have," Night answered, tapping her chin.

"If we have nothing we believe we have something? We never have nothing at all; we only have the abscences of things that should be there, and then we decide it isn't there; our perceptions of what things are, and what things aren't, can be the most important aspect of how we see existence. For me, I see what I wish to see; I control what I want to see; I am a Prince of Dreams, and my dreams are rested under my control now, as I have decided to see myself as a ruler of them," Useful said.

"You'd rather remain ignorant?" Night answered.

"No. It's nor ignorance; it's my own choice to see things in a way that is different," Useful said.

"You see, Night, I see you, but I don't see you; it's all a lie, an instinct, and you are instinct," Useful continued, almost confessing to himself now.

Night's face fell somewhat; "I'm an instinct?"

"Yes."

"This wish for freedom of yours is instinct; everything is. There is no true freedom because we must contain ourselves lest we become depraved," Useful said. "False freedom is, by my sight, the way; because false freedom may as well be as good of a freedom as any freedom,"

"No. That, that isn't true by any means. I will be free; even if I don't know freedom I'll pursue it; I'll always try, and I'll always know I'll try to immerse myself, lose myself, to the things that to others are meaningless; the smallness of things, I don't care what they are, I'll immerse my soul until I've lost it so thoroughly in the everyday I become free," Night said.

Frustated with his wording, his inability to prove the superior quality of his sight, he snapped his fingers and asked the clever "genie" to make Night gone; and when she had mysteriously been whisked away to her room, he heard not a sound of protest (perhaps he did but denied it), then, once he had done so, he regretted it so strangely, he felt pity, he felt wronged, and then he whisked himself towards her locked door, and opened the cosmic knob, stepped unto and upon an infinite stairway.

"Night! Come, return, I am ready for the bitterness," he said, qouting a book.

No answer.

"Hello?"

No answer.

Then, the writhing hands returned, enigmatic ghasts of ghoulishly mysterious quality, skin so smooth and so stretched it filled the entire eyesight's view; imagine a house who's walls are too large, and everytime you imagine the walls they are larger than imagination. These hands then moved to attack, but he swatted them away, with a sword of money; but the money was not real and the sword quickly disappeared after thrice swung harshly.

They started screaming once again; now, instead of saying he killed a person, they screeched of his murder of Night; how? How? He had done nothing! Nothing at all!

No.

"Tell me; am I an abyss incarnate? No. Is it fate that I am a criminal? No. I will fight fate if I must," Useful said. Then, in an attempt to bolster this, he added, almost blindly to what he was saying, "I did not kill **him**! I DID NOT KILL HER! IT WAS THE CURSE."

Then, moments passed, and he presently thought; "Am I the curse? Am I the thing that shouldn't be a thing? No. I fear no thing; there is no things beyond me; I am neither superior nor inferior to any aspects because with certainty such aspects cannot exist, and until I realize them true I am the inbetween that invites criticism but has no answer because such answers would contradict one's very worldview; I view myself as myself until my view can be changed; it has been changed in the eyes of others; but not I! Not I! I refuse everything; and for a moment now I could hear Night say, I could hear Xamot say, 'You coward, you think you are enough to deny all that is because you have such a natural inability to feel all that is, that all that is is yourself and only your dreams; only you, you, and your future; never the present; never contentment, never understanding only seeing,' then I believe as I would hear such words I would yell, as I try to do now; Xamot! I have not wronged you; Night! I do not believe I have, because you are wronged by all things."

"Night! What do you want of me? What do you want to know? The murder? I did not do it. It has been nothing but a misunderstanding. I have not killed him; he was killed by others their inability to comprehend him; how foolish, such folly. He, he the name I forget; because if I remember I would regret it; all I can do is deny it all. Yes I admit it true, I deny this facts, for the betterment of myself and to prove my own points; everything must be how I see them because what can I do if they are not; and in them being the wrong facts would ruin me; my sight akin to only blindness, my body reforged beneath the wings of a Golden Phoenix, thus free; in death all things that we believe are annihilated intensely, we are children of life until we become children of death; and the thousands of natueal deaths and twilights of consciousness passing by us at the seconds I speak in a world of dream do I will myself to act upon no conviction but my own; I do not care, I shouldn't; I am at war with myself, that war whether to feel, the merit of feeling, the merit of genuineness, the merit of the soul, the failure of my ambitions, and my pursuit of my ambitions; wealth, such a great desire that poisons the mind and body, such as I, and now I shall not wish for wealth but only see it."

"I want something I have no name for because that name would destroy my perceptions that support my views that create my stable values; so many things I have no names for; but I strive onwards; Night! Night! Why does your name matter heavily? You weigh your soul to be of superior importance and I believe that correct; a soul within a room, and that room should've been transformed by such a soul; and I would come to ruin it, perhaps another wishing ruin upon you, because you have wronged them in some past that should be forgotten but they are connected to it too intrinsically to forget; forgetting is bliss, denial is a shield, and I am all of the above to become a glorious specimen of the organless man; I feel that you, Night, should feel for me in any way, I care not for what way it is; I only want to have genuineness have a perception of me; all other views are tainted, twisted, by others."

"Night. If I could kill that sorceress I would; not because I truly care for you, but because it would create the illusion I do."

"I think I'd like to create my own worlds because this world isn't evil; it isn't cruel; it's just as it is, and what it is is that the world is rather too large for all these dreams; perhaps too large for your dream, Night; that's why by natural malevolence it traps you inside of a mansion, and with a man with no name as your guardian, only an empty title; like a Capricorn, the essence of a something, and a nothing. The Noble, a capricorn. What's the difference now?"

"..."

"Even the man who has lived through one hundred years of solitude will have known better things then I; I am but the removed."

"Happiness is only simply a temporary gift; and when we feel such happiness we try to preserve it, and for some they never have happiness, and they never know it because they believe they are unable to feel it, for reasons of society, for the self, or for both; happiness is the demonic and the angelic force with which some build their lives around until they run out of happiness, and once that has become of them they are rotted, then from rotted to void; and for those who never feel happiness or know it, either by never weeping in earnest, nor for never trying to feel happiness, they are immortal because they can never die; never living constitutes to inmortality."

Then Useful collapsed into an abyss, his robes flittering above and turning to various creatures that ate him; then he was truly detached from reality, and even his dreamworld, and became—

The heat of the sun bathed him so harshly he felt that it would burn him.

Then, as he opened his eyes, his aching eyes, and lifted his aching body, he felt the grass unreal, but still accepted the illusion; the faintly outlined forms of money thus flew above him, until they morphed to birds then finally solidified to trees, giving shade.

Distantly he heard some form of humming; but that was all. Yet, entranced by the sound, he followed it, hunted it, and then was bewitched by dreamlike eyes that haunted him; quietly he waited for reason to rise, but it never did.

He kept following the sound.

Eventually he stumbled upon it; it was Night, somehow, surrounded by mangled birds; their wings only clear, every other aspect wrong or not there at all; what was this scene?

Silently he stepped forward and attempted to appease his earlier arguments, until, after toiling with his ego, and himself, he gave them away temporarily, and finally found footing, the strength, to approach her, now more humbly, for the betterment of his own mind; and as he did he scared off the mangled, dream birds, but not her, she was too busy staring at them; unnoticed in fact, he was, and only when he did raise a hand to touch a fleeing bird she noticed the presence of the "stranger."

He didn't say anything, only tapping the bird ever so lightly, and thus righting it; the body became clear, the things gone was now there, and by his touch he revealed what a bird actually looked like to a person who had only read them; only observed them from great distances; to a person who was not only curious, but had no ability to satisfy it by means of imprisonment of her self.

Again he touched another bird, revealing it.

He wondered why he was doing this; why? Why? Why did he care for such things? Was it to satisfy one bit of Night's curiosity? What was this strangeness, the feeling so pervasive but unnamed? He kept revealing birds, and only when he finally revealed the last of the flock he understood nothing but this; that he was selfishly trying to help himself. He didn't care whether Night cared, it was to see if he could still find a solace in something beyond Night; to remove his parasitic fascination with her, and to detach himself from the final shred of reality he could not deny; he was failing!

Night had not said a word, and Useful neither; the two had nothing to say.

Finally, Night raised his arm and the birds, now normalized, flew to his outstretched arm.

"Bird man." Night murmured.

"Bird man?" Useful looked at her, now speaking in a way he found he was inclined to; as if instinct had now controlled him to be more tender.

"Yes..." she said, plainly, guardedly.

"Bird man." Useful turned his head, looking at the birds on his raised arm, on his shoulders.

"How childish." He added.

"It's the truth," Night said.

"You act like you know me,"

"It's easy to know someone when they are all muddled; you can easily make assumptions about them and half the time you're right," Night said.

"How...what an odd idea," Useful answered.

"I know."

There was a silence. The dream forest grew more ethereal, veiled in the gentlest of any light.

"Do you want me to apologize?" Useful said, awkwardly, rehearsed.

"No. I don't think you can; you're not a very...sincere person. Not genuine." Night said, plainly.

"You into bore me like a good spike," Useful said, inching his face back from a curious bird.

"Ah."

"I'll find something," Useful said.

"You will?" Night said, surprised.

"I suppose." Useful said.

"You...I'll take it, though," Night said.

"Lazy, I'm not lazy," Useful said.

"No." Night would've added something but relented.

"You still hate me, don't you?" Useful said.

"Partly, I'll be honest," Night said.

"You are a very honest person, Night," Useful said.

"I just don't hate you; I want to know you. You intrigue me." Night said.

"Odd." Useful muttered. Than, he said, "I...I see."

There was another pause.

"Do you want some birds from the bird man?" Useful said, humorously now; how odd! He suddenly felt swallowed by the enigmatic spirit of something.

Night remained still, before, finally, she only nodded.

He gently lowered his bird covered arm, and beckoned one to perch itself upon Night's shoulder.
