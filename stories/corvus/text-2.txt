FANFIC OF THE SUB: Ignis Fatuus, Part Two

#CHAPTER SOMETHING: THE RUNAWAY

...The first thing notable was not the rush of water on the knees, nor the cold air beset upon, but, instead, the dark; the night impenetrable, for, portant vision obscured by the hands of tendril-like blackness there was unspeakable pain still lingering, the fall had most likely inflicted damage deeper and potent than expected; unrealized wounds festered outwards, but, the Runaway lifted her feet and stumbled onwards, gripping the edges of her cloak, hands sutured to it in ways as if a life giving aspect was present in the cloak inanimate but supposedly life-harboring and life-protecting.

Her feet were sore; sore and bloody, sore and bruised.

She had followed the light of a candle; outlining another form, another being: thr Maw had hidden them from her, and, curiously, despairingly, the Runaway donned that title once she fled from her captor's house; for what seemed eternity she had ran across wherever her feet would take her, attempting to seek out that light, candle, candlelight, gentle light, soothing light; for in searching for that mysterious light she had forgotten temporarily the living city's eyes following, its inhabitants stirring.

The candle! The candle, spotted from a small cut through the tall wall: she had seen it, a short but potent moment; she had caught glimpses of unearthly corpeal body made manifest from that light; hence the stubbornness to find it----a drive consuming and unrelenting----still, thought lingered in her mind that the captor had let her go, on purpose...

Her captor? She had never seen that thing's face; simply a being existing to ascertain into itself qualities of hunting and seeking: such a being had been in presence for her whole time here in the Maw; in fact, she felt sure the Maw had a piece of it's flesh and concrete into being manifested; obsessed with itself and collecting things, people foreign to the Maw's mind; a being who perceived and perceived falsely but was convinced in the perception.

For simple terms; her captor was what she called the Fake Reader.

She kept running across a thin beam----unfortunately, an invisible force struck it, and caused it to break; expectedly she fell, bracing herself upon impact as she rolled across slippery roofs and walls slant and curved into strange forms, the eyes on the walls closing, pupils dilated and small, then widening once they realized what exactly hurtled down unto them; still, falling couldn't be stopped. And so she fell.

Of course, she came to, and continued to stumble in the direction of that thing, that glimpse of light, in the hopes it lead somewhere...

...

Corvus had always worn that mask; the crow mask. He had that thing ever since he could walk, thus meaning memory was insufficient as to prove where he even got it, but still, the mask was an actual object possibly originating from an unknown dream: linked to the dream about that vivid hall? Perhaps. He had seen it somewhere, but where? He didn't remember much of his earlier life, nor the circumstances he met Gullet at all.

All he had was his pouch of sands and his mask. Hence the name; Corvus.

For----a distant but evidently sudden crash interrupted his thoughts.

"...Did you hear that?" Corvus said, securing his pouch to his feathers; ah, hear now of the strange clothes upon him, a bird like person, and a person bird like, what's a difference between such things? Bird cloak thingy----excellent title skills from Corvus, of course----he considered himself neither an expert but still permitted himself an ego.

Gullet had adjusted their hood, white bangs afore eyes and hiding them well; their oar had adjusted the head to a makeshift lamp, a candle head.

"...What?" Gullet turned their head, wearily. Corvus stretched his head out, looking around, but Gullet smacked him back down.

"Not here. We stay low."

"Ah. Right. Sorry. I really heard something, though."

Silence except the rain.

The ambience of the city appeared to be that of the sea; it was as if the entire form was drowning, groaning beneath the weight of it's ever expanding self; Corvus noted how each hallway seemed to shift and tremble, threatening to change.

As for the people...Corvus had found Gullet had drawn up some notes; ineligible because he couldn't read. Right: only Gullet can read, and they have to tell me what they've written; what a shame...

*Shame.*

The buildings loomed and curved inwards, watching them.

"...We'll be quick, yes?" Corvus said, adjusting his bird feather shawl. Curiously he reached out a bony hand as to examine some rubble; the rubble moved, and Gullet gave it a savage smack with their oar-stick-candle thing (Corvus wondered what to call it)

..."If you're scared, just hold my shoulder." Gullet said.

"I'm fine...thanks, though." Corvus murmured, looking back.

...

A moment passed.

"We're being watched." He said, suddenly.

"...What?"

"Watched. There's someone, or, someones, like lots of people, watching us."

"...Just hold my shoulder and you'll be fine. Besides, you have enough sand to shield us, right?"

"...Barely any. The water dampened most of it." Corvus said, paranoid.

Gullet merely shook their head, continuing to descend deeper into the impossibly shaped and distorted city; eachx surface was an amalgamation of various materials, organic and in-organic, chemical or even spiritual.

"I see."

"...When do we reach the Train?"

"I'm not sure."

"...I swear we're being watched----"

"You're being paranoid." Gullet said, continuing their strides.

"No, it's just that----" Corvus stiffened, before he hurriedly grabbed Gullet's shoulder.

"Gullet, snuff out that candle." Corvus said, looking around quicker. His hand reached for the pouch of sands, but relented: he had few, and save he must for times in sure of danger.

"..." Gullet quietly snuffed out the candle-head, their stick returning to an oar shape.

...

There was a great silence.

Gullet silently tugged at Corvus's side, before interlocking their hands with his and dragging him away from that spot: move! Right, of course, we must keep moving, Corvus reminded himself through the haze of anxiety.

...

"C'mon," Gullet said, not so sternly now.

"...I----" Corvus's eye picked up on some strange form shifting about in shadow: he saw and knew, but knew not it to be true.

Then whatever thing from the shadows came hurtling forward----huge. Maybe? Corvus hadn't the time to think----

"Corvus," Gullet's hand shook his shoulder, snapping him from rapturously tight thoughts. "What is it?" They said, pale eyes focused unto his mask; as to where eyes should be; his eyes, but unable to meet the others eyes.

"...I----I thought...never mind." Corvus muttered, looking back.

"...Don't get too scared. I'm here." Gullet said, reassuringly stern. "We'll be able to pass through this place, don't worry. I promise I'll get us through."

Corvus sighed, adjusting his mask and walking alongside them.

...

The two walked in silence.
