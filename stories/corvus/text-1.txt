#CHAPTER SOMETHING: CORVUS AND GULLET DESCENDS INTO MAW

(I don't know how to use these dashes ----)

...And it came to the attention of no particular person that a bird had fallen from the sky, and, having landed into the oblivion, was asleep till' no more and gaining into itself consciousness once more inhabited into being a body; the bird! A bird! He slowly came to, the rain seemingly drowning everything: a city of rain.

A bird! A bird!----enough of this bird business.

He sat up, reaching for his affections; a mask, crooked and beaked, as a crow and a packet of sand----collected visions, of many things: a memento.

Strangely the world did not move, nor did the rain! It was as if, the rain had moved and not moved, and time abd flowed and un-flowed, for, in the time and timeless apathy incorporating upon the vast and umbral city there appeared to be no right or left, no up nor down, just a "here", and "there."

The bird saw an endless hallway; with thousands of doors stretching along...he felt at home but also uneasy; had he seen this place before? No, impossible. But still, he felt as if he lived within this place, and had already lived there as long as memory allowed.

"Odd."

Without a word forthcoming did the bird named Corvus lift his packet, his pouch of sands, and thus dutifully nullified his own subconscious from entering into his own present sense of awareness...

...

He awoke finally after piercing through a troubled dream to find that his body had fully transformed into that of a person once again; the dichotomy of dream and physical being unable to transmute the body's mind from both worlds was constant: but, he still had the mask, the crow mask, on his face.

The rowing lulled him into alertness.

Rowing, rowing, rowing...sea? Where was he again? Right...

"Gullet?" He called out; the answer was taken by wind and sea, and thus repeating question did now answer come satisfactory.

"Yes?" The pale youth looked at Corvus's face from under their hood; hood of night, hood of...hood of what? Never mind. Their face was clearly visible beneath the ossuary-like surface and depth of that garbled and intensely dim water, unearthly light not showing but imagined: for Corvus, he had to imagine the features as to be sure he wasn't in some dream again.

The youth shifted, barefeet against the boat's planks; here to there moving again to right the trembling ship----trembling by means of natural energy now in apparitions invading the stability of that wooden thing!

"Corvus----DontKnowMyself----" Gullet asked, wearily rowing onwards. "Are you awake?"

"...Are we near landfall?"

"I think so."

"...I should get ready, then."

"..."

The small wooden boat served its purpose true but faulty; weakened by sea and wave and creature underneath and unseen, the boat was wracked as if organic by ailments; an assortment of patched holes and barnacles.

Corvus sat up, raising his hand for the lantern. Gullet smacked his hand back down, shaking their head.

"**No**. No lights. The Maw eats it up."

"...This isn't a good idea," Corvus muttered, nursing his hand. He covered himself from an ocean spray----black water, illuminated by the floating anomalies; the buoyant lights, of course! What else, what else...

"We need to pass through the Maw if we want to reach Home."

"..."

"Hey." Gullet said, sighing. "Never mind."

"...I'm sure we've reached as far as we can on this boat." Said Corvus with a hushed tone; he taised his pouch of sands, and reached inside of it to begin the entry into the Maw. "...Drop the oar, Gullet."

"I'll keep it handy," Gullet answered, finally raising the immense thing from the water; the oar could shrink and grow depending on what exactly was happening.

"Before we go," Corvus said, his hand stopping, sand slowing falling out. "Remember your name. It will, uh, help with the process."

"I'm aware." Gullet said.

"So...your name?..."

"...I have to say it?"

"Yea. Just, bear with me."

"...Gullet----FederalManner." The youth grumbled, idly leaning on the now shrunken oar.

"Good, good."

Then, Corvus dropped the sand, and it engulfed the two of them. However, the moment it did, an almost ethereal essence was nearly upon them; like an entity invisible about to grab one of them, but, soon, was forced back by the whirling gold of sand.

...

...With the spiraling hedges of impossibly constructed buildings, rising so high as to obscure even the limits of integrity, within them the shining lights of bulbs many. The Maw! The Maw! An organic city; a shell of concrete and brick and steel; with pipes and clotheslines and emptiness, inhabited by entities in multitude, the rain ever falling; organic city, in-organic organic-ness, meat in the street but cleverly disguised as another gray brick.

And as the mist became an imperceptible fog, difficult to see through, and strewn between it and in it the shrouded and scorched, seemingly discarded figures, standing; somehow strangely tall and linked to buildings around----standing or more of being used as energy sources? Fading into obscurity and fading into more than nothing, simply fusing into the city, or, worse, twitching until body granted, could move.

An eternally growing city; an infinite city, dominated in the center by the manifested eye in the sky; eyes here, eyes there, some closed some blinking, a labyrinth of watchers, labyrinth of darkness, labyrinth into yet more labyrinths; towers and skyscrapers archaic, creatures reanimated.

Luckily the two landed on the shoreline of the city.

First to get up? Gullet, of course. They prodded at Corvus's side with their oar.

Next, the crow boy lifted his head up, shook it free of sand, and dusted himself.

"...Right."

"Right." Gullet said, curtly. They turned their back on him and quietly started to move towards the towers shrouded by fog in the distance.

"..."
