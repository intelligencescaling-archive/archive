Monopoly Game & Stock Market Battle Strategy Guide
Document Source: 18LOH PART 1 Update Frequency: Automatic (Every 5 minutes)
I. Game Structure & Objectives
1. Platform Dynamics
* Setup: Nine players stand on nine individual, floating 2\text{ m}^2 platforms directly above an abyss.
* Life-or-Death Mechanism: Each platform’s physical existence is tied directly to the operational survival of the player's assigned company. If a company goes bankrupt, its corresponding platform disintegrates, causing the player to fall into the abyss to their death.
2. Player Roles & Victory Conditions
* Roles: Each player operates a randomly assigned corporate legal entity (numbered Company 1 through Company 9).
* Objective: Accumulate financial assets, protect corporate liquidity to prevent bankruptcy, and systematically eliminate opposing players (specifically the anonymous enemy force designated as “Phú Hào” / The Tycoon) through deliberate economic attacks and market manipulation.
II. Production Mechanism (Supply Chain)
The game enforces a closed, circular supply chain structure that strictly mandates inter-player transactions to achieve operational efficiency.
       [ Company A ] ---> Sells Product A ---> [ Company B ]
           ^                                       |
           |                                       v
     Sells Product H                        Sells Product B
           |                                       |
           +--- [ Company H ] <--- ... <--- [ Company C ]

1. Product Distribution
* Product Classification: There are 9 distinct product types: A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, M.
* Chain Structure: A given company’s output serves as the mandatory raw material input for the next company in the sequence (e.g., Company A sells Product A to Company B, which processes it into Product B and sells to Company C).
* Assignment Distinction: A player’s Company Number is decoupled from their assigned Product Type. Corporate entity numbers are assigned at game start, whereas product designation is selected separately (e.g., Player 1 owns Company 1, but does not necessarily produce Product A).

III. Stock Market Mechanics
The stock market serves as the primary instrument for corporate takeover and opponent elimination.
1. Valuation Baseline
* Shares Issued: 1,000,000\text{ shares} per company at an initial evaluation price of 10\text{ points / share}.
* Total Valuation: 10,000,000\text{ points}.
2. Price Volatility Drivers
* Depreciation Vectors: Mass sell-offs by corporate owners, mass employee layoffs, or unhandled random environmental events.
* Appreciation Vectors: Expansion of production scale, workforce recruitment, and capital expenditure on new machinery.
3. Forced Listing Mechanics (Mandatory Liquidation)
If a legal entity's shareholding percentage rests exactly on a round decimal threshold (100%, 80%, 60%, 40%), the system automatically forces 20% of total shares onto the open market for public acquisition.
Tactical Adjustment: Legal owners can repurchase micro-lots of equity to maintain an odd float percentage (e.g., holding 81% instead of 80%) to temporarily suppress the automated liquidation trigger.
4. Corporate Control Thresholds
* Majority Control (\mathbf{>51\%}): Any shareholder or coalition holding over 51% of total equity possesses the legal authority to convene a Shareholders' Meeting. This body can dictate strategic directions—such as forcing production expansion or withholding dividend distributions—overriding the legal owner's individual intent.
IV. Core Survival Mechanics & Hostile Strategies
1. Market Inflation & Expansion Pressure
* Cost Inflation: Operating overhead (employee wages and maintenance costs) escalates continuously each cycle due to employee seniority and mechanical wear-and-tear.
* Capital Requirements: To expand operations (hiring additional personnel and acquiring advanced machinery costing 8,000,000\text{ points}), companies require massive liquidity infusions.
2. Equity Dilution
* Secondary Offerings: Substantial production expansion requires issuing additional equity (e.g., issuing 1,000,000 new shares).
* Dilution Risk: If existing shareholders lack capital to purchase their pro-rata share of newly issued equity, their ownership percentage is halved (e.g., from 51% to 25.5%), triggering an immediate loss of corporate control.
3. Random Event Mechanics
* Event Frequency: An environmental disaster, labor strike, or public scandal triggers every 5 minutes.
* Resolution Time: Requires a dedicated cash expenditure within 1 minute. Failure to resolve results in immediate share price depreciation and severe inventory degradation.
