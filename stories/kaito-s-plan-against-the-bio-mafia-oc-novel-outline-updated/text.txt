I took the time to update the plan. That was something written around two months ago, so I decided to explain the situation more clearly and fix some things. I’ll directly copy the description from the previous version.

Well, I'm trying to write a novel (not literally an SCD novel, but there are characters that could fit into that category), and I want to see if the first outline I've written, along with its explanation, is understandable. Does it make sense? Is it possible to replicate it? Also, based on this alone, what category would “Kaito” fall into?

Brief explanations: The civilization is advanced, and things like materials that prevent fire from reaching a house exist.

Kaito belonged to the mafia (he was blackmailed with his sister’s life) and after a fatal mistake, he was sent to work as a prostitute—and no, he isn’t gay.

Without further ado, here it is:

Kaito, after 8 months of being forced into servitude, learns that in one week they will decide to sell his sister. He snaps and decides to end everything before the deadline.

He analyzes the situation. He will need something capable of drawing the attention of both the authorities and the public while he handles the rest, but he must not be recognized by civilians so he cannot be linked to the upcoming fire. During his free time, he buys tape recorders. He notices missing person posters from grieving families and goes to a mass grave where the mafia sends its dead. He pulls out the most recent corpses, matching them with the missing person posters: people who disappeared because of the mafia and were dumped there, but who had not yet officially been declared dead. Using an anchor, he drags the bodies out, loads them into his truck, and stores them in his house.

Before everything begins, he enters the headquarters every day for an entire week, walking through the building under the excuse of delivering what he collects from the brothel, while secretly observing locations, information, camera angles, and security patterns. He goes to the mafia boss to deliver his report while carefully examining the office. He notices a special painting belonging to the boss: a hidden Morse code. He studies it for a few seconds and leaves. On his way out, he subtly observes the camera overseeing the data center and later buys a remote controller matching the same model used for the security cameras.

The next day, he arrives with one of the managers standing behind the counter to deliver payment. While talking to him, his hands remain inside his pockets. Hidden within, he is programming the remote to synchronize with the building’s cameras as a secondary controller. Some money “accidentally” falls to the floor, and while the manager bends down to pick it up, Kaito swaps the original controller with the secondary one. Afterward, he says goodbye and leaves.

Soon after, the cameras begin experiencing intermittent failures: black screens, random shutdowns, delayed feeds, and corrupted signals.

That same day, a small clash occurs with the rival mafia, orchestrated by Kaito himself. He leaves insulting messages across territory protected by the rival mafia while marking them with the insignia of his own organization. Kaito becomes “accidentally” trapped in the crossfire and turns on a recorder. He disarms one of the soldiers and points a gun at him. The soldier screams, only to be shot moments later. Kaito examines the corpse and finds the man’s phone, protected by facial recognition. Using the dead man’s face, he unlocks it, changes the settings so the screen never locks again, connects it to power so it won’t die, turns off that recorder, and activates others while dealing with more members of the rival mafia in what appears to be self-defense.

On the third day, at the brothel, Kaito mentally reviews the weekly meeting schedules he has carefully written down in his notebook. He is obsessive with details like these and always knew he would not remain there forever. He memorized everything for a moment exactly like this.

He waits patiently, and when the pimp leaves to check on something, Kaito slips into the office. He searches the room and finds a safe, but he does not know the password. He stores that information away and investigates the room further until he discovers a hidden note left behind in case the password was forgotten. Using the rival mafia soldier’s phone and the pimp’s email account, he fabricates conversations about selling information in exchange for money. The messages are sent both from the soldier’s phone and the pimp’s accounts. Once finished, he clears all opened applications and leaves everything exactly where it was before returning to work.

After a week of moving through headquarters and memorizing different paths, Kaito begins the next phase. First, he researches abandoned houses and selects several of them. He enters each one, seeing them as the perfect opportunity to keep law enforcement focused on the mafia. He thoroughly inspects every building until he confirms no one is inside, then leaves and returns with his truck. He distributes the corpses throughout the houses and places the tape recorders on their chests, increasing the volume. Each recorder loops different screams for each body.

He “accidentally” drops one of the mafia insignias and leaves, concluding that this distraction will buy him enough time with extremely high probability.

Next, he visits the populated houses surrounding the abandoned ones. Knowing he cannot be recognized in any way, he approaches homeless people and offers them high payments if they sell fireproof materials to the homes he points out. Some refuse; others accept out of desperation. They only see him wearing a cap, glasses, a mafia insignia, the fake name “John Brown,” and using a deep, artificial voice.

He buys matches from one store and gasoline from another.

Several hours later, after a series of transactions, the homeless workers inform him that they successfully sold and installed the materials. Kaito thanks them and pays them as promised.

He drives to the abandoned buildings and spreads gasoline throughout the area. From a distance, he lights a match and throws it into the fuel. The fire erupts violently, growing larger as he escapes. He stops at a nearby store, buys a disposable phone, and while the flames continue spreading, he calls the firefighters pretending to sound terrified and concerned. He gives them the address, claiming he can hear people screaming inside. Once the call ends, he smashes the phone to avoid future tracking.

In Kaito’s mind, the goal is simple: force the authorities to discover that the burned victims were missing persons connected to the Bio Mafia, while simultaneously ensuring the mafia cannot defend itself during the chaos surrounding the fire. With that accomplished, he calmly returns to the brothel to continue his routine.

Roughly twenty minutes later, Kaito, now disguised as a woman, enters the pimp’s office and begins seducing him. For months he had noticed the pimp’s hidden sexual uncertainty. The mere memory disgusts him, but now that information becomes useful, lowering the man’s guard.

Then Kaito suddenly pulls a pistol from his pocket, the grip wrapped in cloth, and points it at the pimp’s head.

During his week inside headquarters, Kaito noticed the use of special security cards required to access different areas. He begins shouting hysterically and aggressively, demanding to know where the pimp keeps his access card. Terrified, unable to think clearly under the threat of immediate death, the pimp reveals the location. Kaito orders him to retrieve it personally and hand it over.

The pimp rises at gunpoint and opens the office safe. The moment he hands over the card, Kaito shoots him in the side of the head. He stores the weapon away and disposes of the cloth.

He changes into the pimp’s clothes, wears a cap and glasses, and prepares to access the mafia’s central information network. Then he calls the mafia boss, perfectly imitating the pimp’s voice, claiming he has discovered critical information regarding the southern mafia and requesting an urgent meeting.

Kaito arrives at headquarters while the burning houses are nearly extinguished.

He perfectly imitates the pimp’s mannerisms while keeping his face hidden from the cameras. Multiple cameras watch him as he walks with his hands in his pockets. Suddenly, one camera shuts off.

Using the real camera controller hidden in his pocket, he delays the signal across the entire building. Then he disables five cameras in sequence: one far from the target area, another near it, another farther away, one at the entrance, and finally one directly overlooking the location he intends to enter.

As personnel are dispatched to repair the cameras, Kaito uses the pimp’s access card to enter the information sector.

However, once inside, someone recognizes him.

Kaito has no choice. He draws his pistol and shoots the man. After checking the body, he realizes the victim was not actually part of the mafia, but an innocent worker.

Kaito remains silent for several seconds.

Then he stands up and continues the mission.

At the central monitor, he remembers the painting. He translates the Morse code into numbers and uses it as the password. He gains access to the encrypted files. The contents are difficult to understand, but he does not care about the meaning itself. He copies everything onto a USB drive and leaves.

By the time the cameras reactivate, Kaito is already gone.

The mafia becomes suspicious and calls the pimp. The phone rings unanswered. They track it down and discover the pimp’s corpse. Nearby lies a burned body dressed in the same clothes Kaito wore while disguised as a woman, its face completely destroyed. The fingerprints match Kaito’s, his hair is present at the scene, and the phone contains the calls to the southern mafia and the leaked information.

Kaito is officially presumed dead.

The firefighters finish extinguishing the blaze and begin connecting the pieces together.

Meanwhile, several miles away inside his truck, Kaito contacts the rival mafia and claims to possess secret information about the Bio Mafia. Looking at the encrypted files on his phone, he recreates the same encryption style using a mixture of fabricated and real information. He sends them authentic data regarding the central headquarters along with fake intelligence claiming that the northern Bio Mafia plans to attack the southern branch within hours. Real and false information are blended together so seamlessly that distinguishing them becomes impossible. In exchange, he demands money.

When they ask for his identity, he gives the pimp’s name.

Kaito’s reasoning is simple:

The hatred between both mafias had already reached critical levels. Tensions were so high that whoever made the first move would inevitably trigger war. Suspicion had already intensified because of the earlier conflict Kaito orchestrated. All he needed now was the final drop that overflowed the glass: someone greedy enough to betray their own organization while possessing access to sensitive information.

He profiled the pimp for months and learned to imitate him perfectly.

Now, with the police already alert due to the fire, the murdered civilians, and the planted evidence linking the Bio Mafia to everything, any violent clash between the two organizations would inevitably draw law enforcement straight to them.

When the southern mafia investigates further, they discover the pimp died from a gunshot wound. They conclude the northern mafia discovered his betrayal and executed him, therefore confirming the leaked information must be genuine. There is no longer any need to pay the informant.

Meanwhile, the northern mafia reviews the phone messages and concludes the pimp uncovered information about the south and attempted to leak it to them.

Each side now perceives the pimp as a double agent working for the other.

When the police investigate the burned buildings, they determine the fire was intentional. They discover the corpses belonged to people murdered by the mafia and realize the blaze itself was merely a distraction. They find the northern mafia insignia among the evidence.

At the same time, the southern mafia launches a rapid assault against the north before they themselves can be attacked. The northern mafia retaliates intelligently, having already transformed the headquarters into a trap-filled fortress.

As the leaders attempt to escape, they discover all communication lines are dead.

The camera failures were merely a decoy for the real sabotage: delayed signals and disabled communications.

They try escaping by car and helicopter, only to realize every vehicle is empty of gasoline.

When Kaito infiltrated headquarters with the pimp’s access card, he did not only steal information. He also drained the fuel from every possible vehicle and sabotaged the building’s cables before escaping through the parking exit.

The mafias attempt to flee through alternate routes but are intercepted by police.

There is no escape.

The southern and northern mafias collide against each other while the police storm the central headquarters, realizing the scale of what happened: a three-sided collision between two criminal organizations and law enforcement itself.

Meanwhile, Kaito simply disappears.
