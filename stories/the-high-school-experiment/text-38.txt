However, unfortunately for Innō, I had the edge. I happened to have studied tea ceremony and Omotenashi.
I wonder how this match will turn out.
I silently considered the outcome of the match as we walked to the mall. Sure, I had the edge, but I had no idea what was up Innō’s sleeve, so I had to perform exceptionally well to avoid losing.
At any rate, we began as soon as we entered the mall. Well, I started just before we entered.
I opened the door for everyone with a warm grin.
“What a gentleman,” Yoshida teased.

I couldn’t blame her for being shocked, considering how I acted during dates.
I was mostly boring and just simply went with the flow, though that was because I didn’t know what to do, well, at least for the first date; for the second, I had to be silent to learn about Aiya. However, now I completely changed my entire personality—it was a personality makeover.

“So, are we following the schedule that we had originally had, Aiya?”
“Hmm? Yeah, why not?”

“Yup.”
“Yeah.”
“Sure.”

I sent the message I prepared beforehand to Miyamura, and he read it, of course, understanding what I meant.
I told him not to react to what I said because it would be weird otherwise.
After reading it, he gave a slight nod.
While I told Aiya not to tell Yoshida that it was because of my favour with Aiya that she met Innō, I told Innō no such thing, so it’s best if he doesn’t intervene for now.
I mean, I don’t want Yoshida to know that I was the one who orchestrated her dating Innō.
“Well, that’s unexpected,” Yoshida said, her cheeks slightly reddened as she continued. “Innō texted Aiya that he wanted to meet me, and he asked me out—let’s just say it was love at first sight.”
“Wow, so romantic,” Aiya commented.
It’s good that Aiya is unreactive to the lies, but it really makes you wonder—
“What about you?” Yoshida asked, breaking my train of thought.
“Huh?”
Doesn’t she already know how we
“How did you and Aiya-chan start dating?”

“Oh…” Yoshida was sorely disappointed, but what did she expect?
I mean, did she expect us to meet after I saved Aiya from a life-and-death situation, or did she think Aiya was a long-lost childhood friend who just happened to have fallen in love with me and was looking for me?
Does she think this is a rom-com?
Who knows what that girl is thinking?
Anyway, we arrived at the cinema, and it was now time to select a movie to watch.
“What movie are you guys interested in?” I asked the whole group; however, it was focused on the girls.
“Let’s watch an action movie,” Innō said, beaming with excitement.
I looked at him with a stare that screamed, “Really?” I was totally flabbergasted at what he said.
Dude, you’re supposed to let the girls choose. Did you forget about the competition?
I considered telling him my thoughts, but eventually decided against it.
“…I think the girls should choose,” I said finally.
“Huh?” Innō realised his mistake. “Y-Yeah, you’re right.”
“Really? We should?” Yoshida asked.
“How bout’ that one over there?” She pointed at a romance film. “What do you think, Aiya-chan?”
“I’m ok with it.”
Innō had an expression that showed his disinterest, but he didn’t say anything.
He really is giving his all, huh?
“That means it’s settled,” I went over and paid for all of us.
“Nakamura-kun?” Innō was shocked, and so was everyone else.
“Yeah, why’d you pay for all of us?” Even Aiya was surprised at what I did.
“Don’t worry about it. I mean, after all, a gentleman pays for people, doesn’t he?” I grinned.
That was so corny!!!
I could tell that was what everyone was thinking based on how they looked at me.
“Ahem,” I faked a cough. “Let’s go inside, guys.”
Anyway, we walked in and went to our seats—I sat close to the front at the far left with Aiya beside me. Yoshida was beside her, and Innō was beside her—we all sat close to each other.
Throughout all this, Innō was unusually quiet—rather, he had been quiet the entire day; it seemed he really was giving his all.
Anyway, as we settled in, we started to make some small talk about school, but it dwindled when the movie started.
It had a basic premise—a girl who wanted freedom from her parents.
Such a thing wasn’t uncommon among young adults and was rather prevalent among Japanese individuals. However, what was different about her was when she met the supposed love of her life.
They met in a very novel manner. She needed to use the restroom in school, but had no toilet paper. A boy, a new transfer, walked past her, silently handing her some with his number engraved on it.
A lot happened between them—mostly inconveniences that turned into good coincidences—but the short and long of it is that they managed to get together, eloped to another country, and were living happily ever after.
Despite the plot not being impressive and the dialogue being less than acceptable, I quietly enjoyed the movie.
“We have no popcorn?!” Yoshida said a little too loudly, so it warranted stares.
“Oh, right! My bad, I forgot,” I slapped my forehead, feigning forgetfulness.
You see, I didn’t wanna buy popcorn because I wanted to save most of the money I got today, so I acted as if I had forgotten, but Yoshida remembered, so I had to get some, or else I wouldn’t be a gentleman.
“Don’t worry, I’ll go buy some.”
“Should we send the money now or after?” Aiya asked.
“Oh, don’t worry, it’s fine.”
“Are you sure? You already—”
“Really, it’s fine,” I cut her off.
“Well, if you’re sure. So, uh, can I join you?” Aiya asked shyly.
“Nah, it’s fine.”
“You sure? We could go together.”
While that would probably be amazing, as I want a little alone time with just us, I need to be a gentleman.
“Nah, I’m fine.”
I promptly went to get the popcorn, though I must say that I was surprised Innō didn’t offer to follow.
It must be because he’s totally focused on the film
Innō looked quite engrossed in the film despite his expression earlier.
His expression put a slight smile on my face as I left the cinema. Anyway, I headed straight to the snack bar. On arrival, I noticed a rather short line.
Well, most would get popcorn before the movie.
In said line, I noticed that a certain someone was directly in front of me.
Well, at first I wasn’t sure it was her because she wore a large brown coat with a black hat, a black face mask, and sunglasses—basically, she dressed like someone trying too hard in a spy movie.
That… Is her, right...?
Anyway, just to be safe, I didn’t say a word and just kept quiet until she bought popcorn. I also bought mine (that is, four; one for each of us) and left quietly.
Of course, that last part wasn’t just going to be so peaceful and uneventful as I would like.
Good.
“What an unpleasant surprise,” she growled with disdain as she removed her sunglasses and facemask in an attempt to show me her glare.
Seems like she caught me.
“Hey, Hayashi,” I grinned awkwardly.
“Ugh, what is it?”
“Hey! You’re the one who came to meet me!”
…Not that I could say that, though.
“Uh, why are you dressed like a criminal?”
“None of your business, and you know what? I won’t let this spoil my day. I’m leaving.”
On a normal occasion, I would have let her go, but this time was different—I wanted to know something.
That’s why I allowed her to catch me.
“Hayashi, how do you know me?” I emphasised ‘me’ intentionally.
“You don’t know?”
“I wouldn’t ask if I did.”
“Well, if it isn’t important to you, then there’s no point wasting my time,” she stormed off—she was clearly displeased, but why?
What had I done to warrant this? I racked my brain for answers, but nothing seemed to click at all.
Well, it seemed this answer wouldn’t be something I would get now. At any rate, I had to just make do with the lacklustre excuse for a plan I just came up with to avoid this girl.
“Do you know there?”
She turned back at me and stared blankly before answering. “But of course, Eiya-kun.”
Her voice was sweet, but the words behind it hurt more than even poison.
My expression changed immediately, as if I had been stabbed by a knife.
How, how, how. She isn’t from there; I’m sure of it. I remember every single person, but she doesn’t ring a bell.
Calm down; let’s hear what she wants.
I regained my composure as quickly as possible, but the damage was already done.
It’s been a while since I lost my composure like that.
“…What are your demands?”
“Relax,” she grinned. It seemed innocent, but it was far from it. “I want just one thing. Help me get into the student council.”
“…”
“Do you refuse?”
“No,” I sighed. “What a drag.”
“Don’t care what you say as long as it’s done.”
“Ok, but if I do that, my secret is safe, right?”
“Sure, and I might not have a means of proving that, but you have to believe me blindly, right?”
“...I suppose,” I managed to utter.
After she left, I saw her being swarmed by some random people I hadn’t seen before.
“Hayashi-chan!”
“Is that you?!”
“It’s her!”
“Huh?” Hayashi was completely bewildered but accepted their presence. “Hey, what’s up?”
She was clearly repulsed when they approached her, yet she was happy to see them now.
I wasn’t even exactly sure if she was acting.
“Oh, it’s nothing, we just wanted to thank you for that—”
I honestly didn’t bother listening to them and headed back to the cinema.
Though it seemed that Hayashi had racked up a huge fanbase—you see, since coming here, Hayashi had been acting like a goodwill agent, solving any and all conflict near her.
Of course, I had only seen this once during the class debacle, but I had heard so many stories from my classmates while eavesdropping, of course.
She helped with both big and small cases.
She’s like a celebrity! Good, then this will work.
I stopped in my tracks before pulling out my phone and stopping the recording. It was why I lost my composure like that.
While it wasn’t a real agreement, it would work on Hayashi.
As you can tell, she was a celebrity, but not all celebrities cared about their image; Hayashi did.
Have proof? Yeah, but barely.
On the first day, when she acted harshly towards me, she checked if people were there—she wanted to protect her social image.
And that’s why I didn’t make this deal before now, because I wanted her image to grow.
Perfect.
Another thing I happened to notice is that she knows nothing about there.
She just wanted leverage, but unfortunately, I saw through that.
If she really knew, then she would have said something about it or would have made a better deal.
Well, either way it works in my favour, though her being in the student council might really help me.
I sighed and returned to the cinema to continue the film. The second part was unlike the first.
While the first started grim, it became somewhat happy-go-lucky; however, for the second part, it was the other way around.
The protagonist had to become manipulative and exploitative so she and her lover could hide from their parents.
She lied and cheated more and more people. Until she eventually got tired of her lifestyle and committed double suicide.
Yoshida, Aiya, and Innō cried when it ended; it seemed it really was a grim ending.
Well, not only they, but almost everyone who left the cinema felt sad—including me, but that was to be expected; it was the normal reaction.
Why’s everyone so sad? The movie isn’t that grim; rather than feeling sad, it feels rather nostalgic, in a way.
Or perhaps self-reflective?

Maybe I should have, considering I already told several lies today.
