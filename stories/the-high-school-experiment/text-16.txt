Chapter 3.1: I Hate Parties
Is this really the same school I’ve been going to for weeks?
The west side of Elysia was unlike the centre; instead of school buildings and recreational places, there were only places for recreation.
Well, that was my assertion based on what I could see from left to right—there was a place for anyone who wanted to have fun.
It looked nothing like what you would expect to find in a school.
While we had places like this in the general school area, this area was solely for fun.
Weird.
“Whoa!” I exclaimed. “Is this place only just for fun?”
“Yup, it’s a place of fun for the students, by students.”
By students, huh?
“This school really pulled no stops.”
“You haven’t seen anything yet,” Kagura retorted. “It’s a shame I can’t show you around; maybe another time?”
“Uh, sure, I’d like to see more.”
The West Side of Elysia was a place of recreation, but it wasn’t only for fun—I noticed a few hotels, resorts, and beauty spas, as well as a few convenience stores.
This seemed really weird to me for several reasons—most of the things here were already in school, but here it was amplified.
There was also the problem of the resorts—they shouldn’t be there. We lived in school, so what was the point of hotels and resorts?
Was it just for leisure or something else?
I wanted to ask Kagura, but she looked too engrossed in her phone, so I just kept silent.
In about five minutes or so, Kagura and I arrived at the karaoke box—it was apparently called Big Echo, or so the massive neon signboard said.
“Ready to go in?”
“We’re already here, aren’t we?”
“Yup, so no turning back.”
“Not like you gave me a choice.”
“Mhm,” she nodded with a wide grin.
Not even trynna deny it, huh?
We walked in, and on entrance, all I could do was stare like a kid in a candy store. I was in awe of the place.
It had luminescent lighting with air conditioning that was a little too cold, though I wasn’t one to particularly hate the cold.
Also, the place was quite dark for an entrance; in fact, if not for luminescent lighting, it would be pitch black.
Anyway, we were guided to the front desk.
“Good evening. How many people?”
“Actually, I’m going to my friend’s room.”
She gave the details, and we were led to the room. The hallway was well illuminated, unlike the entrance.
As we were walking in silence, being led by an employee, Kagura nudged me.
“Now, before I forget, here’s my acc. It’s Kagura-chan.”
“Ok.”
I added the account as we were heading to the room, and then as we entered, we heard a loud sound.
“…destined for love!!!”
That was the first sound I heard on entering the room. It seemed as though everyone was immersed in singing.
The room was just like the entrance when it came to lighting, though it occasionally flickered and moved around, flashing different colours.
The room was also way colder than the entrance. Anyway, I managed to move through with no one noticing as the girls focused on Kagura.
I wasn’t avoiding them because I didn’t want friends; instead, I was avoiding them because making friends with people as energetic as that would be too much of a chore—it would also be awkward to butt in as they were singing.
So I decided to keep to myself and pass the time by using my phone. Anyway, as I moved through, I noticed an open seat in the corner and immediately went to it.
I brought out my phone and went on YouTube, but then I was suddenly approached by a girl.
“Hey, my name is Yumeko.”
These words were spoken by a girl with long dark brown hair that got darker the higher it went—it was to the point that the hair that emerged from her skull looked darker than black itself.
She also had glistening green eyes that seemed like they’d pull me in.
She wore a big sweater and some headphones, though she kept them around her neck.
She looked like someone who kept to herself and didn’t have many friends. She also had an unusual presence.
She’s a loner!
“Hey, I’m Nakamura,” I beamed as I offered my hand with a wide grin—it was a good opportunity to make a friend with another loner.
“I’m surprised to see someone like you here,” she ignored my hand and kept talking, but I didn’t mind.
Someone like me? Is she referring to someone who is a loner?
Did I misunderstand, and she’s not also a loner? Shit, then I must look weird; I must look very weird.
I–
“Earth to Nakamura.”
She cut me off from my existential crisis from not having friends, though it was premature of me since today was just my second week.
Except people make friends in just a few days, so it seemed I was just trying to cope.
“Oh, sorry about that. I was lost in thought.”
“Like I was saying, I’m surprised to see someone like you here.”
“...Do I look too much like a loner?”
“You do,” she said apathetically.
Way to rip off the band-aid.
 “…but that’s not all,” she continued.
“Then what else?”
“You’re like a corpse.”
“A corpse?”
“No… perhaps a robot? Yeah, that fits. Your personality—it feels fake.”
What’s this girl talking about?
“I don’t know how to react to that.”
“Of course, you wouldn’t. A robot can’t understand complex stuff like that.”
I don’t think that’s how it works, but alright.
“Hey, that’s kinda hurtful.”
“By the way, I’m a fortune teller; that’s why I can tell.”
“Don’t you need a crystal ball for that?”I joked.
“To be frank, we fortune tellers cold read rather than ‘read into your destiny’."
“But aren’t you too young to be a fortune teller?”
Yeah, her story wasn’t really adding up, as most fortune tellers were old women. At least, according to what I read, I had never seen one before.
“Not all fortune tellers are old women; however, that being said, I’m kind of an exception,” she kept quiet and looked at me.
Maybe she wants me to be impressed?
“Ahem,” she faked a cough. “My family runs a fortune-telling store; because of some issues, I’m the only one who can take over.”
“Oh. So, what do you see in my future?”
“Didn’t I say we don’t actually see your future?”
“But you tell people’s futures, and they turn out correct. You probably predict it based on their actions, right? Anyway, do mine.”
“Well, to be honest, I see great tribulation in your future.”
“Hmm,” I nodded.
“You’re not surprised?”
“And here I thought you read me? Can’t you guess why I’m not surprised?”
“It’s not always perfect…”
“So basically, you know nothing about me except that makes no sense, so how and why did you read a stranger?” I asked with an unreadable expression.
Fortune tellers asked questions or gave open-ended answers so they would know more about their clients and give more educated answers.
While they used cold reading, that was hardly enough, especially in cases involving someone’s future that needed some pre-established information.
Yet Yumeko said she could see my future. Of course, her reading was vague, but still, it was just enough to make me interested.
Very interested.
“Do you know about me?” I asked slowly, forcing a smile.
You see, I was trying to unnerve Yumeko here, so I tried to appear as creepy as possible by making my smile look uncanny.
I gave a wide forced grin with Tsurime eyes (or squinting eyes), and it seemed my smile did unnerve her because Yumeko had a nervous sweat.
“No, also your voice is colder.”
Seems like she doesn’t want to mention my weird smile.
“Yeah, I noticed,” I avoided the subject about the information she knew about me and changed it to something else. “Anyway, what do you see in my past?”
“You’re not going to ask how I know?”
“I have a faint idea, so no need.”
“...About your past—there was some hardship.”
“That’s way too vague; doesn’t everyone experience some kind of hardship?”
“Yours was more intense.”
“I see.”
“...And don’t worry, this info won’t be spread.”
“Why is that? It’s not like I’m forcing you, and it’s not like anyone is actively looking for it.”
“Actually, someone wanted to make sure of something. Her name is Hayashi Nanase.”
The girl from the train.
“She’s the one who gave you info about me, right?”
I was surprised Yumeiko released Hayashi’s name so easily—Yumeiko could be lying, but I abandoned that thought and came to the conclusion that it was probably true, though I wondered why she did so easily.
Maybe she just broke under pressure—but what pressure?
“…Well, I’ll be on my way.”
The girl left silently, almost like a ghost. She didn’t even seem like she existed; however, I knew she was real. She had to be, or else how would I do this? It was obvious; I had no other way, wasn’t it?
I wondered if she followed me here, though I didn’t notice her at all, so I threw that thought away. It was probably a coincidence that she met me here.
So then Hayashi knows me, huh. Then I need to meet her soon before anything spreads.
…
No, not really. Perhaps I should leave it as is. Yeah, that works better.
As the excitement from the singing faded, the room's energy shifted.
It was subtle at first—their laughter turning sharper, glances lingering longer. And then, all at once, they turned to me.
“Kagura-chan, who’s the little kid you brought with you?”
They all laughed simultaneously; perhaps today was not my day. Yup, it wasn’t my day at all.
“So cutie, what’s your name?”
“N-Nakamura. Nakamura Eiya,” I stuttered.
“So cute!!!”
I’ll spare you the details—you can probably guess what happened to my face. And yes, it was all of them.
Eventually, it died down, and someone proposed an idea while I applied ice to my face.
“Now for the main event of the night—strip or drink.”
“Huh?”
Drink? We’re high schoolers, so how’d they get alcohol? Why even try it?
It’s clearly against the rules. Don’t tell me that’s the reason they came here.
Nah, I’m probably overthinking it; maybe it’s just juice or something.
I silently doubted they would bring actual alcohol until it was dropped on the table. I read the contents of the bottle, and it had 20% alcohol.
20%!!!!
“The rules are that we’ll play truth or dare; however, if you don’t do it, you’ll either drink alcohol or strip.”
“So Nakamura, wanna play?”
“Uh, nope.”
“Sorry, but no is not an answer.”
Shit, do I really have to agree?
I mean, the person talking to me was beautiful. She had chestnut hair and was endowed with quite a bountiful bosom.
And yeah, I could probably see her naked if I played hard enough.
However, that was probably a ruse; I could tell.
I doubted they’d actually strip in front of me—they’d probably just bend the rules to their advantage, and I’d end up in a difficult situation, or worse, I’d be drunk.
And I really wanted to avoid that.
“Just play one round,” she glared.
“Yeah, just play.”
“Yeah, I wanna see what will happen.”
Some girls were interested in seeing me play, but with the possibility of getting drunk being in front of me, I vehemently denied it.
I really can’t have that happening.
“Nakamura, just play, ‘kay?” The girl held my shoulder, smiling menacingly. Her voice wasn’t suggestive; it sounded like a threat.
It seems like normal peer pressure, but if I reject, they’ll… Oh, I see; this is what they call bullying.
I always blamed the bullied, but I really can’t do anything. I wish I could just disappear into the chair or something.
Please, someone, save me.
I silently bowed my head. I was about to accept my fate when—
“Guys, I don’t think Nakamura wants to play, so don’t force him,” Kagura voiced.
“Why? It’s not really a big deal or anything. I mean, don’t tell me you don’t want to see these,” she caressed her chest, trying to arouse me.
I would love to… If not, this was a trap.
“This is supposed to be fun, not forceful,” Kagura didn’t back down. “What if he’s not comfortable showing skin?”
Kagura really is amazing; she’s doing this for my sake.
“He’s a man; men do this stuff all the time. Moreover, that will only happen if he loses.”
“Well, it’s still his choice.”
“Why are you on his side?”
The voice came from the same instigator who started the cafeteria fight. This time, it wasn’t really to instigate, but it was astonishing that she was here.
So she’s here too, huh? Well, she seems popular.
Despite that, I couldn’t find out who it was.
“He is my classmate,” Kagura answered.
“This is... You know what? Let’s settle this with a drinking contest,” the girl didn’t back down.
“Yeah, a drink-off,” Kagura wouldn’t back down either.
“Kagura-chan will win!”
“Yeah, she’s so kind!”
Talk about a landslide; everyone’s on Kagura’s side. This is an attestation to her popularity.
But why must everything lead to alcohol?
“I don’t think that’s healthy.”
This time, I had had enough of this and tried to stop this behaviour, but it only made them angrier.
“Shut up. I’m doing this for your sake,” Kagura glared.
“Yeah, shut up,” the other girl glared.
“Yeah, who does he think he is?”
“He thinks he’s hot shit just because Kagura is helping him; she’s just being nice, dumbass.”
Yeah, who do I think I am? I should just leave them be.
I left them, then went to a lone couch, and then went to sleep.
Heugh
I sighed internally.
I hate parties.
