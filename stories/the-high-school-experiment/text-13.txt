Chapter 2: Why I Hate School.
Ahh, school.
A place where children are moulded into adults.
A place where discipline and patience are taught and instilled.
A place where you learn social skills and make friends.
A place where mental and academic growth is fostered.
A place that I had grown to loathe.
To think I had only been at this school for a week, yet I already hated school—talk about a horrible first impression.
Well, second impression, but it was basically still the same thing to me.
Of course, it was not for the reasons you would expect. I didn’t hate the concept of school in and of itself, that is, the concept of learning alongside your peers.
What I hated was that I had not made a friend and, because of that, had been forced to live a lonely life.
Naturally, you’d ask, “What about Hiro?”
Yeah, he was my friend, but the thing was, I couldn’t seem to find his account name or even his social media accounts.
I tried his name, surname, parents’ names, and many other things, but I still couldn’t get it.
Maybe I hadn’t tried enough, or was I just unable to? Was it a sign of a lack of intelligence? I couldn’t tell which it was.
Anyway, I’m sure that you’ll ask another question.
“Why not meet him in person?”
But unfortunately, Hiro was always preoccupied with girls. It was to the point where he could be called a ‘chick magnet’.
I was more than sure that they knew I was trying to get close to Hiro, because they shielded him indefinitely.
Not that I could see him anywhere besides the hallway and the cafeteria, considering I didn’t know his class and didn’t have any other friend to ask what class he was in.
All in all, I had no way to reach my best friend, so the only option was heading back to square one.
At any rate, I started to get the hang of this school and even began to enjoy my time here, well, except for the fact that there was an issue with Class Beta, and I couldn’t do anything about it.
I could only hope that someone as wonderful as Ito would do something, especially in contrast to me, since I had nothing I could do.
Though it was less of a hope and more of an educated guess of his actions because, after all, he didn’t tell the class about the condition of Class Beta.
It was also probably up to Kagura. However, she didn’t really seem to fit the bill, though. Well, that was what I thought, at least.
At any rate, a week at school didn’t reduce the tension in my class; in fact, it made it worse, and I remained a wary spectator.
Alas, I remained in my life of spectating. However, that only applied to me when I was in class. You see, spectating wasn’t the only thing I did—I also managed to pick up an interest in something.
In fact, I was in my room trying to figure out that new hobby.
“I guess it’s now or never, huh? I mean, I’ve cooked every day till now since resumption, but that was just Tamago Kake Gohan—the only thing I know how to cook,” I sighed.
I gulped and tried to make something different; it was a self-made recipe, though nothing special—steamed rice and a special sauce (spices with carrots and onions) with fried fish, except it didn’t go according to plan.
I forgot to add oil when frying, and the stove was on high, so the fish almost burnt down the dorm room.
“Shit!”
There wasn’t any fire safety equipment in my room, so I called the dormitory staff through the landline.
“Hello—uegh,” I coughed.
“He—are you coughing?”
“Yeah. So, there’s like a fire, and I need—”
“Don’t worry, I’ll be there in seconds, so please remain calm.”
The woman on the phone wasn’t lying; she was there in less than a minute, and she put out the fire in a very calm manner with an indifferent expression.
Whoa, she’s so calm. Is this a frequent occurrence? I mean, for some people, this is the first time away from home, so perhaps burning down your dorm room is rather frequent, or at least almost burning it.
Well, either that or she’s just good at handling these kinds of situations.
“Why didn't you use your fire extinguisher?” she asked in a matter-of-fact tone.
“I looked for it and didn’t find it.”
“Heugh,” she sighed. “Please try to cook with caution next time. I’ll call maintenance to bring a fire extinguisher into this room. Also, your room will be renovated as you’re in class tomorrow. So bear with us for today.”
I’m surprised this didn’t cost Rp; actually, I’m thankful.
“No problem and thank you; I really appreciate it,” I bowed deeply.
“No biggie, it’s my job; moreover, it’s our fault that you didn’t get a fire extinguisher.”
After a while, I got a fire extinguisher, and then, after lamenting for a while longer, I accepted my lack of culinary genius and went to bed.
The next day went as usual; I woke up, took a bath, and dried my hair with a towel before putting on my uniform—though this time I made the interesting decision of trying to make my hair look as good as possible.
Maybe if I look better, people will approach me.
As I combed my hair, I paid extra attention to my features. You see, I had the same average plain black hair that everyone had, just like my mom.
It was in a round, mashed hairstyle—the same hairstyle I’ve had since birth. It was the same hairstyle we all had. They said it was the most optimal.
Anyway, I made sure I had no eye bags by constantly washing my face, and when I was done with that, I brushed the tips of my eyelashes with my finger, though I mistakenly touched my eye.
“Oww!”
I looked teary-eyed, and my eyes grew a bit redder. Luckily, I already had crimson-red eyes, so it was all good.
Yes, I was trying to brag about my eye colour. Did it work? Are you enthralled? … Y-You’re not? Meh. You lose some, you win some, I suppose.
At any rate, after that was done, I headed to the grocery store to buy a sandwich and a soda—a coffee-flavoured one this time.
“That’ll be 1000 Rp,” the salesperson said.
“Ok, thanks,” I paid and promptly left.
I bought breakfast because of what happened yesterday, though after everything had been fixed, I would switch back to my usual breakfast.
Hopefully, I won’t burn that too.
That thought changed my expression to a wry smile, which I carried to class before switching to a better expression. Then I continued my daily observation until something unexpectedly happened.
“You know what, screw this,” Innō swore.
He was being antagonised by people, as usual; however, this time he fought back—of course, verbally.
“Don’t even try to act innocent.”
“Innocent? It isn’t my fault that you all are spineless cowards.”
“Yeah, freakin’ buncha bitches,” Inoue joined in.
“Bitches?! You literally lack self-restraint.”
“Better than being a doormat.”
This was very clearly going to happen. Innō got insulted every day, so he eventually lost his mind—well, not that he lost his mind; I suppose it’s more accurate to say he lost his cool.
You might be angered by such a response, saying, “But wasn’t Innō at fault?” Yes, he was. In fact, one could say this wouldn’t have happened if not for him.
That didn’t mean that he had to be antagonised daily. When it first occurred, I was cutting my classmates some slack, but now this was just too much.
One could even say Innō’s not even in the wrong due to this being a reasonable reaction. I mean, after all, he’s human.
However, I couldn’t help but feel it’s part of Class Beta’s plan.
Who is the person who planned this?
A mastermind? Nah, that sounds way too outlandish. I mean, this is just high school; why would there be a ‘mastermind’?
It’s not like some weirdo is trying to play pretend, right?
“A doormat, right? At least the only thing I’m good at isn’t volleyball. In fact, you’re not even good at that, Mr “On and Off”. You’re stacking everything on a sport, not even on your studies. Your parents must…”
“Shut the hell up. Talk again, and I’ll kill you.”
“Like hell you’ll—”
In that moment, Innō lost his cool and pushed his table. He headed towards the student insulting him with the intent to swing at said student; it was going to be a devastating punch.
Baam!!!
Luckily, Ito caught that punch in the nick of time.
This guy, he really is like a saviour.
Every time Innō got insulted, he always de-escalated the situation. Always making the situation better.
“Guys, calm down,” he said with the same smile he had when the situation was escalating, like he didn’t mind stepping in, though his smile faltered slightly. Maybe he realised he couldn’t keep this up forever.
“Everyone…” Ito took a deep breath and then continued; he had an unreadable expression. “I have an announcement, and I need your opinion.”
What? Is he going to tell the class what happened in the faculty room?
Well, at least there’s some development.
“I withheld some info when I came back from the faculty room; in fact, I didn’t say anything, and I’m sure everyone avoided asking because of the tension. That is even the reason why I withheld info in the first place. But I must tell you all now,” he took another deep breath and looked wary, though he spoke again.
“We lost the case; we attacked first, and they had video footage.”
It was an obvious outcome that wasn’t what anyone cared about; they only cared about the punishment.
For this reason, everyone was silent before the start of classes—a time when high schoolers were normally overly energetic, even though it was early in the morning.
“The student council is supposed to be involved, though I managed to propose a compromise.”
Everyone shook at the word “student council”, though they calmed down when they heard “compromise”.
Some even became cheerful, while the few observant ones noticed that Ito wasn’t smiling.
He was not even seeming remotely happy; he was rather unhappy and discontent.
“So what’s the compromise? Is it Rp? You don’t look happy, so it’s quite concerning,” Suzuki pointed out.
“You’re right. I’m not happy, and no, it isn’t Rp. It’s much worse. It’s the suspension of everyone in class.”
“...!”
There was a silent panic that erupted—it rendered everyone speechless. Even those who knew, like Kagura and I, were rendered speechless when we faced the reality of the situation again.
“F-For how long?” someone managed to mutter.
“Indefinitely; for as long as they want.”
“So, they want to expel an entire class at once?” Someone murmured.
Huh? How do those things align together? Though we would get suspended, that doesn’t mean we will get expelled.
At most, our attendance would get messed up, and we would miss some tests, but those things wouldn’t lead to expulsion.
Especially in a school where student autonomy mattered more than anything.
So I couldn’t help but be confused, though I didn’t ask Suzuki because I was focused on what happened in front of me.
“Ito-kun, what do we do?”
“Yeah, aren’t we basically screwed?”
“Yeah, what do we do?”
“Ito-kun, answer us, dude!”
“Ito…?”
Ito probably didn’t have a plan, or maybe he did; I couldn’t say for sure.
I mean, I hoped he did, but if he didn't, he couldn’t just create one in the heat of the moment, especially now when the pressure was on him.
Nonetheless, he had to say something to reassure everyone, because for some reason, everyone in class trusted him.
Including me, of course—whether that was warranted or not wasn’t important; what was important was what would happen next.
“I’ll handle this,” he said, his voice steady with a grin on his face. “Don’t worry, this case is as good as done; I just didn’t want you to be in the dark. I’ll convince Class Beta to leave us alone, even if I have to pay for it. I’ll convince them even if it’s the last thing I do, so trust in me and don’t doubt!”
“…Ito-kun, you’re our hero!”
“Yeah, it’s cuz it’s Ito.”
“That’s Ito for you.”
And don’t doubt, huh? Maybe this guy really is as amazing as people say he is.
Everyone slowly began to believe in Ito; he managed to convince everyone to believe in him completely.
Of course, he was lying; we were screwed. I mean, a solution wasn’t presented anywhere, but because it was Ito who said it, everyone agreed with it. I was also moved by his words—so moved that I was fully convinced he had a plan.
He definitely has a plan. Considering he used the experience in the faculty room to prove it was a planned action, he must have something up his sleeve.
After he finished his words of power, we had homeroom and went on to have class.
The day brushed by as I waited to see what Ito would do, so when the day ended, you could say my curiosity got the better of me and kind of took over.
“So that’s everyone, right?”
Ito created a gang of people to help him as he planned to beg Class Beta. Their group was about 6.
Consisting of Ito, Kagura, and 3 girls who always stuck to Ito and were friends of Kagura. With my interest piqued, I settled on joining them.
I took a deep breath, stood up from my seat, and quickly gathered my stuff in my bag. Well, before that, I cut a piece of paper.
Now, let’s see what happens.
“Leaving already?” Suzuki asked.
“I wouldn’t exactly say that,” I replied with a slight smile before heading to the front door of the class, where Ito and the rest stood.
“Can I join you guys?” I asked when I arrived.
