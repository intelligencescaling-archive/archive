Chapter 1: My New Life.
Ding, ding, ding!!!
“Ughhh,” I groaned in bed as I—
Déjà vu, huh?
I picked up my phone from the nightstand and turned off my alarm. When I looked at the time, it was 8:15 AM.
“Shit!”
Today was my first real day at Elysian High, and I was already cutting it close.
I was lucky that I set an alarm for 8:15 just in case there was a day I woke up late.
Who knew it would be my first day on campus?
In a rush, I took a bath and put on my uniform. I also hurriedly prepared Tamago Kake Gohan, the only meal I actually knew how to cook, scarfing it down despite the rice being steaming hot.
“Hot, hot, hot!”
After getting ready, I went on my way to school. I quickly ran to the school area.
I really need to work on my time management skills. Well, I’m sure I’ll just get a slap on the wrist—I mean, today is basically my first day.
My first day at Tokyo Metropolitan Elysian High School.
I grinned at my thought as I finally arrived at the school gate, and unsurprisingly, I was the only one who was late.
I brought out my phone just to find that it was already 8:35 AM. Which meant that I was already 35 minutes late.
“Heugh,” I sighed. “This is what I get for watching YouTube all night.”
However, something was weird. Despite my being late, no one stood at the gate of the school to punish me.
It was weird since we were Japanese, and punctuality is one of our core principles.
It seems there’s no one to punish me. Maybe because today is my first day?
No, I highly doubt this amount of leniency would come from such a school, or maybe it’s because of the promotion of student autonomy?
This school had several weird rules, one of which was the absolute promotion of student autonomy, allowing students to do whatever they saw fit.
However, anti-bullying rules were set in place, so if caught bullying someone, then the person would be instantly expelled.
Nevertheless, the school gave students freedom with no apparent punishments.
It seems like I won’t get punished.
After entering school, I went to my class but—
The back door was locked, meaning I had to pass through the front one, but Jackson-sensei was in the middle of homeroom.
Heugh.
I sighed internally, exasperated at the predicament. I could leave the class entirely; however, if caught, explaining myself would be quite an issue, especially with the cameras.
I took a deep breath and walked in, but—
She said nothing about me walking in. It was like I hadn’t done anything. As I walked to my seat, there was no response.
I couldn’t grasp why this happened. Sure, I get not punishing me—as I was a new student, and free will was promoted here—but not addressing me was not normal at all.
I silently wondered how absolute the student autonomy really was.
Well, if no one’s going to say anything, then I won’t either.
“Now I’m sure all of you received the message about applying to a club. Since it’s a new term, those without clubs and those who want to change clubs can both get into new clubs.”
Wait, what? There are clubs?
“I have to join a club this term, considering the benefits I missed last term,” someone mused.
Benefits?
I was about to check my phone for said message when—
“Yeah, I really can’t wait to apply for the literary club. What club are you applying for?”
“I don’t have any in mind, though I might join you, considering it’s free boobs.”
“Exactly, for a second I thought you weren’t my brother.”
“Like, who doesn’t love a bookworm? They’re the freakiest.”
“Yeah, and this club is full of ‘em.”
The duo pervs—or so I started to call them—were at it this early in the day. It was my first day (second, actually), and all they could think about was girls.
I understand, but tone it down, dude. Also, I don’t think your theory on literary girls is accurate.
Though I have no experience, so I can’t be sure. I mean, after all, I’m still Mr I’ve-Never-Had-A-Girlfriend.
I brought out my phone and saw the message about the club application.
I sighed in exasperation because I didn’t notice it yesterday, and then put my phone back into my pocket.
I could still apply today, especially with those “benefits”, but I value my freedom way too much for that.
“With that out of the way, I will take my leave,” Jackson-sensei strode out quickly.
She really hates being in this class, huh?
With her gone, I sat silently waiting for my first class as I looked outside the window to admire the clouds.
Until I heard a pleasant voice beckon my attention.
“Nakamura-kun.”
It was my neighbour, Suzuki Aiya. She was the gyaru who sat beside me, well, to the right of me, but you get what I mean.
“Yeah, what’s up?”
“Why’d you come late?”
“I woke up late, that’s all.”
“Oh, really? I thought you just wanted to skip class.”
“I-I would never.”
“Sure,” she answered while showing apparent scepticism. “Anyway, do you wanna—”
Before she could finish, a teacher walked in; we stood up to greet him and then had class.
I looked at her to continue, but she just brought her books for class; she then wrote something on paper and handed it to me.
It read, “I’ll tell you later.”
I nodded and was also about to bring out my books, then I remembered that I was a new student, meaning I didn’t have any books.
Shit, I forgot to meet Jackson-sensei for my books. Well, no one told me, so it isn’t exactly my fault—who am I kidding? It’s totally my fault.
“Uh, sir,” I said, standing up from my seat, trying to talk to the teacher.
“Yes, young man.”
“Um, I’m actually a new student, so I don’t actually have any books.”
“...You didn’t get them before now?” The teacher looked slightly exasperated.
“Yeah,” I replied, scratching the back of my head with a nervous smile.
“...Feel free to leave the class—you can get your books from the homeroom teacher; she should be teaching a class right about now.”
“Thank you, sir,” I ran out of class.
As I left the class, I conveniently remembered that I forgot to ask the teacher where Jackson-sensei was.
It felt weird going back to ask (as I would look like an absolute dumbass), so I simply headed to the class closest to mine—Class Delta. On arrival, I knocked.
“Come in,” a male voice answered.
“Sorry to intrude,” I said, entering Class Delta. The class’s interior mirrored mine, though there were a lower number of students who dressed like people from my class.
Still, the difference was hardly noticeable—it dropped from 49 students who dressed casually in Class Epsilon to 42 students in Class Delta who dressed casually.
“Are you in this class?” a middle-aged man in his 30s asked.
“Uh, no. I’m actually looking for Jackson-sensei.”
“Oh. She’s in Class Gamma.”
“Thank you, sir,” I bowed.
I ran over to Class Gamma and rushed in without knocking on the door.
“—And that’s how… Hmm?” Jackson-sensei stopped her teaching to address me. “What are you doing here, Nakamura-kun?”
“Uh, I forgot to collect my books,” I said, looking around the area.
The classroom was, of course, similar to my class but had a more mature vibe to it, and the students appeared quite attentive. Also, there were a lower number of people who dressed casually—only 15 students.
I kept looking around until I met her gaze, and she gave a slight smirk.
Huh?
“I see,” she replied. “Wait for me in the halls; I’ll get your books for you.”
“Ok, ma’am,” I quickly followed her instructions and waited outside my class.
She smirked? But why?
I couldn’t quite think of why she smirked; I didn’t do anything particularly funny or entertaining.
I hate to say it, but maybe she was just appalled at my stupidity or something like that. If that’s the case, then that’s just pretty sad. Hopefully, I’m wrong, and there’s some other reason.
I mean, the smirk could just be my imagination.
“Yeah, let’s just go with option two,” I said aloud.
“What are you talking about?” Jackson-sensei asked, her face stoic.
“Uh, nothing,” I nervously backed away.
“Well, alright then. Anyway, here are your books,” she handed them to me. “Now, don’t go barging into classes again—it messes with a teacher’s flow and can also affect you.”
Jackson-sensei made an effort to emphasise the word “affect” before promptly going back to her class.
Wonder what she meant by that. It’s probably just that I’ll get punished.
Yeah, that was probably right. Anyway, I carried my books and headed to class. This time, I used the back door.
I made sure to sneak quietly to my desk without making much noise.
Tip-toe, tip-toe, tip-toe.
Just like my inner monologue implied, I quickly tried to tiptoe and successfully pulled without a soul noticing me.
“Safe,” I quietly exclaimed as I arrived at my seat.
“I see you’re back, young man,” my teacher said. “Can I assume that this will be the end of your disturbance to my class?”
His sarcastic, condescending tone made quite a number of people giggle.
“Yes,” I squeezed out quickly as I brought out a jotter, due to my realisation that my notes were a term behind.
That means I didn’t even have to leave the class in the first place. I could have just got the books after class!
After bringing it out, I quietly began taking notes while glancing at Suzuki’s note.
I also glanced at Suzuki as she wrote—she was really cute, but that’s not what really stuck with me.
What I noticed was her expression whenever she met my gaze—she had an expression that basically showed that she really wanted to ask something important, and to be honest, I was really curious about what she wanted to ask.
