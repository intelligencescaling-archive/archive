Chapter 11.3: I Must Do It
What the hell is this? Why did ‘she’ have to come here today? I thought she got expelled.
I realise this much is obvious, but I was very stressed. The stress made me start biting my newly done nails. I’m sure that if Aiko had seen me, she would have screamed, considering how much they cost.
When did I pick up such a habit?
You see, I just started doing something like that recently—I never did such a thing, especially in there; I could never even consider it. The instructors would see it as a sign of weakness.
And weakness was an unforgivable sin, especially when it created a stark difference between Nakamura Eiya and us.
So why was I openly showing my weakness?
Perhaps it is from the stress of being with Nakamura Eiya?
I couldn’t tell for sure. All I could do was stare at the stuff around my room.
My room was pink-themed—everything from my bedsheets to even something as minimal as my toothbrush was pink. It had to be—that was who Suzuki Aiya was, after all.
Anyway, looking around helped me focus on what was more important: Tsubasa Yui.
Yui, of all people. Why now? After everything she did last term.
Argh!!!
…I need to calm down—she hasn’t done anything yet.
I paced around my room for what felt like hours, replaying my first term at this school in my mind.
The final exam resulted in Yui’s expulsion—that was perfect and helped with my own plans, yet it seemed they had been derailed because I had just seen her today.
It was frankly surprising that I hadn’t heard anything about her still being in school. It was like something had been done.
Then again, I suppose it was awfully foolish for me to believe some like us would be expelled so quickly.
But how? I made sure, didn’t I? How did she blindside me? Definitely, she didn’t do it by herself, so how?
Did the chairman do—
Clank, Clank, Clank.
A knock at the door finally broke the spiral of my thoughts. I sighed, reverting back to Suzuki Aiya before going to open the door.
As I opened the door with a warm smile, I noticed Kagurazaka Akane at my door.
“Akane? What are you doing here?” I was shocked to see my closest friend arrive at my door.
Most people thought Aiko and Yua were my closest friends, but they were easily surpassed by Kagurazaka Akane.
…Not that I openly showed it, though. It was an intentional measure—no one would see what was close to me.
“Aiya, we need to talk,” Akane had an unfamiliar expression, one that was barely seen at school. She looked angry—rather, she was exasperated.
“…” I silently considered why she was angry and what she was angry at.
“I assume that I can come in?” She broke my train of thought.
“Yeah, sure.”
Akane entered my living room and sat on the carpet because the couch had lots of books—ranging from The Art of War to The Prince.
“So, coffee or tea?”
“Do you have cocoa? I’d prefer that.”
“Uh, sure.”
I didn’t think she would choose another option.
Luckily, I happened to have a hot cocoa mix, so I made what I had after boiling some water. After I finished, I put it on a tray and brought it to Akane. Her expression lightened up slightly as she saw it.
Well, hot cocoa was her favourite beverage.
“Let’s cut to the chase. Why are you here?” I plopped down beside her, getting straight to the point.
Akane took a large sip of her hot beverage; it seemed she enjoyed the cocoa very much. After she finished her sip, she spoke.
“I’m here to tell you to lay off Nakamura,” Akane said, glaring.
“Sorry, but I can’t; he’s my boyfriend.”
“Oh, is that all?” Akane rolled her eyes. “So, you can’t just break up with him?”
“…Akane, I have a question.”
“What?”
“You’re the one who took a picture of us and posted it online so everyone could know we were dating.”
Akane probably came along during that double date—Nah, she sent someone from the class to come along.
Meaning when someone talked about us on her first date, she probably deduced from that that we were dating and sent someone to see if we would come Saturday.
The person couldn’t follow us, of course, because I would obviously notice, and so would Nakamura.
So instead, they just noticed along the way, and she made the story more true because people like Aiko, Yua, and Matsumoto affirmed it.
How discreet.
“That isn’t a question.”
“I’ll take that as a yes—that implies that you were stalking us on one of our dates, right?”
“No, people around took pictures, and I happened to get one from one of my friends because they were talking to me about it to see if you were really dating Nakamura-kun.”
Of course, she wasn’t stalking us, as I would have noticed; I just wanted her to go on a tangent about how she got it.
So that’s how you got it. Well, I thought as much.
What she said was the most probable way to get it, so it was within my expectations, but it was possible I was wrong, so I had to be sure.
“Of course you knew that, right, Aiya?” Akane noticed my thought process—her red eyes meeting my blue ones.
Hmm? She noticed?
“Who knows? Anyway, what do you want?”
“I’m sure I’ve already stated what I wanted.”
“…Eiya isn’t someone suitable for this school, so just mind your business.”
“Why are you doing this to him?”
“You remember when I told you about my childhood and where I grew up.”
“How could I forget? It’s not really something particularly fun to hear.”
“And yet you protect Eiya?”
“I-I’m not protecting him,” Akane moved closer to the left of the couch—her back was touching the armrest. “Y-Y-You know that, don’t you?”
I had a wide grin sprawled in my mind; of course, I had nothing but a calm look of quiet disappointment.
“So, you’ll choose his side over mine. Is that what you’re implying?” I moved closer with a blank expression.
This should be enough. She’ll probably take my side.
“No, I’m not against you.”
Good, because I’m no–
“But I’m also not going to turn a blind eye to this.”
Hmm? What’s this? What’s going on?
I calmly assessed what my best friend was doing; she was indeed against me, but I expected that, so I guilt-tripped her, yet she easily shook it off.
It wasn’t that much guilt-tripping; after all, I wouldn’t try that hard for someone I cared for, but it was surprising that she was this adamant about Nakamura Eiya.
He was a new student, and Akane wasn’t a saint, so then why the unwavering concentration on him?
…Redemption, huh?
I planned to relent, but I wouldn’t let such a selfish wish exist, especially when I also had a goal.
I apologise, Akane, but no, I won’t relent just like you.
“Akane, as you know, I grew up in fear, or rather in worship, of one man. A supposed god, a perfect human…and a weapon—a devil, if you will,” my voice quivered slightly.
“Nakamura-kun, right?”
“Yes, he was that great evil that haunted me… And now he’s my boyfriend.”
I had to be as vague as possible because she might have been recording this conversation, though that was unlikely, but there was always a chance.
However, as a contingency, I made sure that I emphasised Nakamura, so in case this conversation was leaked, he would go down too.
“I won’t let you just do whatever you want,” Akane’s voice turned cold.
“You are welcome to try. Might not win, though.”
“Doesn’t matter, I have no choice but to stop you.”
“Heugh,” I sighed. I really didn’t want to fight my friend, so I had to end this right now. “You’re acting like that wasn’t your plan already.”
Akane silently continued to sip the hot cocoa till she finished; she drank it like it was an ice-cold drink.
She really likes hot cocoa, huh.
“Can’t you just give up trying to play a hero?”
Akane’s fingers tightened around the empty cup, her knuckles whitening.
“I will be taking my leave,” Akane said as she stood up to leave.
“Akane, wait—”
“No, I won’t. I won’t let anyone get expelled again,” Kagura said as she shut the door.
“I’m sorry, but I’m expelling Nakamura Eiya even if it’s the last thing I do,” I said to no one in particular, as Akane had already left.
The only thing I could do was stare at the floor. I held back quite a lot just now. I could have done a number of things to make sure that Akane didn’t affect my plan.
Even my supposed attempt at manipulation was half-assed at best.
I-I-I was…soft.
…I didn’t hate it because it meant I wouldn’t be that far from rehabilitation, but I was still afraid because it meant that I would be able to play a coward’s game forever.
“After all, I’m also a coward too.”
