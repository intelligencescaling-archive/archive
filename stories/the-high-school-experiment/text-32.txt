Chapter 9: A Confrontation
Despite what I said, I still had no plan. I suppose I was just trying to believe that I was actually doing something.
I tried to contact the number, but… Well, you can imagine trying to contact a number that didn’t exist. It wasn’t even saved on my call log.
“So, the only thing I can do is confront her, huh?”
The issue was that the person only had Kagura’s voice—there was no actual proof that it was Kagura.
“Perhaps I should tell Suzuki,” I checked my phone and saw that it was past 10, so it would have been abnormal for me to call Suzuki at that time.
“So, confronting it is,” I sighed and went to take a bath before going to sleep.
I wonder how this will turn out.
The next day, I came to class early to confront Kagura. She must have gone to great lengths to do what she did; to be found so easily would shock her and disorient her—that is, if the culprit was even her.
As for why I came early, there are two reasons.
I. She is a vice president, so out of pure assumption, she should come early to clean the class. I mean, Ito didn’t come early, so I assumed she’d come early—I couldn’t really tell because I also came quite late or just barely on time. I couldn’t figure out why Ito didn’t come early to help, though.
II. But in the case that I am wrong and Kagura didn’t come early to clean the class, at least I would be the first person to intercept her when she comes, though that is if she weren’t with friends.
But did I need to do this much?
I could just ask her like normal, as a friend, but she would most likely lie because why go through the trouble of hiding your identity just to reveal it?
Besides, I didn’t have any evidence to point any fingers, so she could easily evade me.
At any rate, the walk to class was quiet, I suppose. It was early, so no one was there yet; it was just me and my thoughts.
This is the best plan I can come up with, so I highly doubt it will turn out my way. Well, maybe luck is on my side, and this ends up working.
Oh, who am I kidding? This will probably blow up in my face.
As I arrived at school, I promptly switched into my indoor shoes and approached my classroom. When I arrived at class, I saw Ito cleaning alone.
Huh? What’s he doing here?
My plan was going off the rails—I couldn’t help but keep a wry smile.
So, what now? Should I head to the library? No, how will I see her when she arrives? Should I wait in class…? Wait, if I go in, then I’ll have to help, won’t I?
The thought made me wanna stay put. I honestly didn’t like cleaning, and waking up early today was pretty annoying.
But since I’m here already, I might as well help a friend out.
I let out a sigh while cleaning my tired eyes as I entered my classroom.
“Yo!” I waved as I entered the class.
“Oh, hey Nakamura-kun, you’re early today. Don’t suppose there’s a special occasion?” He grinned at me.
“Not really. Need a hand?”
“Nah, don’t worry, Kagura-chan is on her way. But thanks anyway.”
“No, don’t worry, it’s also my class after all.”
“Don’t worry; today is my and Kagura-chan’s cleaning day.”
Ah, forgot about that.
I suppose it was less forgetfulness and more ignorance, but because I hadn’t experienced my cleaning day, as two people were given a day. I hadn’t been selected yet, so I entirely ignored its existence.
Or that’s what I think it is. I mean, I don’t know how they arrange the cleaning in high school.
“I am worried; just let me help.”
“Well, if you insist, then okay.”
This works in my favor; if I’ve already reduced the workload, Kagura won’t need to do much, and we can get straight to business.
Moreover, I could also help a friend out.
“So, what should I do?”
“Uh, just arrange the chairs. I’ve pretty much done everything else.”
“Huh? But isn’t Kagura supposed to help you do some work?”
“Well…”
His expression told me everything; Kagura was being tardy. I couldn’t be surprised. Kagura always had somewhere to go, so expecting her to wake up early would be impossible.
Ito probably understood that more than I did since they had been classmates longer than I had.
“I see,” I nodded, completely understanding what he meant.
There was silence as I arranged the chairs, though I couldn’t quite call it awkward; it was just silence. In a short time, Ito had finished cleaning the board and started helping me.
After we were done, he let out a sigh of relief and then sat at his desk.
“Eughh,” he stretched while sitting.
I sat on the seat beside him, basically hinting that we should talk.
“So Nakamura-kun, how is your experience at this school? Besides the Class Beta debacle, of course,” he joked.
“Frankly, it’s underwhelming,” I sighed.
“Eh?”
“No, don’t get me wrong. I love being free and all, but the school system feels too easy.”
“The best way to get the right answer is not to ask a question but to say the wrong answer.” This is a law—originally an internet law, it was popularized by Steven McGeady, a former Intel executive, and he credited it to Ward Cunningham, the creator of the first wiki, WikiWikiWeb.
Though it mostly applies to an internet setting, it can work in this kind of setting, though it has a chance of not working on some people.
I was doing this because I was getting curious about the school, especially about what Kagura possibly did.
“Easy? Isn’t that way too much of an understatement? Even if you're leaving out the Class Beta debacle, there are other things.”
“How so? As far as I’ve seen, this school is way too lenient.”
“You do know that a student was expelled from this very class.”
“That could have been for a number of things.”
“It was for breaking a rule in the exam we had last term,” Ito said, looking at the ground.
“I see, but wasn’t that just his fault…” I noticed I had struck a nerve.
Seems like this is a touchy subject.
“My bad for speaking out of line,” I bowed.
“No, it’s not that. I mean, I would go on but…”
“No, feel free.”
“Well, I…” Ito was conflicted.
“Hey, you don’t have to, but if it’s that serious, then don’t you think I should know? Y’know, so I don’t make the same mistake.”
“...Yeah, you’re probably right,” he took a breath and continued.
“Kobayashi was a kind boy, though a little bit perverted, so some girls disliked him,” he paused momentarily before continuing.
“During the exam, he went against one of the rules that prohibited telling information to people, and that led to…” Ito just stared at the ground for a while before he finally continued.
“Worst of all, I’m the one who told him to do it. I thought he’d blame me for his expulsion. However, Shin-kun just said I should tell everyone that he’s throwing in the towel early,” he grimaced slightly.
Not to say that Ito lied, but I highly doubted Ito was the one who made Kobayashi Shin get expelled. Yeah, I know he said it was just a mistake, but no, I didn’t believe it was even that.
At any rate, it seemed he was the one who took the blame.
He must have really hurt him, but judging from the way he phrased it, I doubt they were close. Probably along the lines of the relationship we had now. That amount of a saviour complex was too much for Ito to carry; if he weren’t careful, it could really end up bad.
But back to how Kobayashi got expelled. It seemed breaking one of the rules could get me expelled.
It sounded way too strict, but it was possible that it just applied to exams.
Well, someone like me will have no problem following the rules.
After all, it was all just perspective—even now, as I analysed this, I brought my own prejudice and bias into it. Based on what I had seen from Ito, I came to a conclusion that could even be wrong.
“Thanks for telling me it looked hard to do,” I said remorsefully.
“Yeah. Thanks to him, we barely passed through.”
“But what kind of exam was that?”
“Ah—forget it,” Ito said.
He won’t tell me that, huh?
“I AM HERE!!!” Kagura slid into the class. “Ito-kun, do you know that reference?”
“Uh, no,” he replied quickly, trying to cover up his saddened mood.
“Sorry for being late. I’ll come earlier next time. Also, hey, Nakamura-kun.”
“No problem,” Ito replied. “Though I’m already done.”
“Hey, uh, Kagura, can we talk?” I asked.
“Ok, let’s talk.”
She brought a chair to me and spun it around.
“It’s about Suzuki—I have girl issues,” I laughed nervously, scratching the back of my head.
If I say it like that, then it’s more than a good reason for Ito to not be interested.
He doesn’t seem like the kind of guy that's interested in other people's business.
“Uh sure, let’s not talk here,” Kagura said plainly.
“Do you have any other place where we could talk?"
“Yeah, follow me.”
She was silent on the walk to the place she was taking me to; she was probably thinking about what to say.
…Can’t use that as evidence that she’s the one who did it, though.
Anyway, she took me to a secluded place with a bench and sat me down beside her.
She probably thought I was lost because of her phone call, and I contacted her for her opinion, but I had a plan in mind.
“Sit,” she grinned.
“Alright,” I sat down, and she sat down after me. “So, Kagura, what I wanted to say was that I…got a weird call yesterday.”
“Weird call? What kind? Don’t tell me a ghost called you,” she snickered.
“No, not that kind of weird call.”
“Then what kind?”
“It had no caller ID."
“T-That’s not possi–”
“I saw it, please trust me,” my eyes wavered at hers.
“...” She silently nodded slowly before speaking. “Alright, I trust you.”
“The person told me to break up with Suzuki or…”
“Or what?”
“Or I would get expelled.”
“What do you plan to do then? Is there a way to track the person?”
“No, I tried. I just started dating Suzuki yesterday, yet… Do I have to leave Suzuki just to not get expelled? But that’s not fair,” I looked down, shaking a bit at my words.
Matsumoto once said I looked delicate; I suppose I looked more delicate than ever because I was about to cry because someone threatened me to break up with my girlfriend.
Yeah, I definitely was a poor, helpless boy.
“It must have been hard,” Kagura hugged me. “And you must have been especially scared when they called at 10 PM, but don’t worry, I’m here.”
“...Kagura?”
“Hmm?”
“I, uh, never mentioned the time.”
Gotcha.
