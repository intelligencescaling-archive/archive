Chapter 0.3: The Marvellous Cafeteria
We had to line up in a queue to order food. There was a TV that displayed the menu, similar to a fast-food restaurant.
However, before joining the line, you picked your order by selecting the name of your meal on a screen; after getting a meal ticket, you’d join a queue and pay.
I picked omurice stuffed with pork and some vegetables, then got my meal ticket. I was to join a queue, and there were three options laid before me, but all of them were full.
“Heugh,” I let out a slight sigh and went to a line. I was silently watching my line reduce in size until—
“Just accept my cash!”
A student in the queue next to me seemed to have forgotten his phone in class and wanted to just pay in cash, but the cashier denied his request—of course, the school had already explained this.
Probably a new transfer like me.
“Sorry, but we don’t accept that.”
“Come on, please! I can’t go back to get my phone; I left it in class, plus the line is too long.”
“Sorry, sir, but—”
“Could I pay for the two of us?” I butted into both the conversation and the line itself.
“Yes, that’s possible, but…” The cashier replied.
“I mean, you literally just said that it’s possible, so can’t I?”
“Well, if you’re sure, then it’s fine.”
“Thanks, man. I-I promise to pay it back soon,” the student said.
“Don’t worry about it.”
I used my phone to pay by snapping a code and inputting the amount.
Our meals cost 8,000 points each. The problem was, I had no idea if that was expensive or not, but I guessed it was.
After paying, I left to go table-hunting.
“Nice!” I exclaimed after the student left earshot. Helping that random student made me get my food earlier; it wasn’t that I helped him just because of that, though, but...
“I wish I had asked for his name, though. Meh. Anyway, time to get a table.”
You would think that in such a big cafeteria, there would be more than enough tables.
Ordinarily, there would be, but as I could see, most tables were occupied because everyone just sat with their friend group.
Rather, it was the “popular” type that was mostly in the cafeteria; I supposed that those who were less popular would eat in class or alone.
This appeared to be an apparent social divide that I wasn’t aware of.
Well, there was nothing to be done now, so I went on to look for a table.
I went over to the table closest to me. They appeared to be just first years, though they were not from my class.
You could tell the difference between first, second, and third years from their shirts; first years wore white, second years wore maroon, and third years wore black.
Their blazers and trousers changed accordingly, though both second- and third-years wore brown blazers with a touch of white and brown trousers.
No matter how hard I looked, I couldn’t see any upperclassmen.
Maybe there is a cafeteria for each grade level?
“Can I sit here?” I asked the people at the table I approached.
“Umm, no,” a girl answered. “Try another table.”
I sighed and then went to another table that was close by. This one had 4 open chairs.
“Can I sit here?”
“Uhh... someone’s coming?” A boy replied nervously, mistakenly phrasing it as a question.
From their faces, I could tell they were lying, but as I said earlier, there was an apparent social divide. I was about to just carry my food to class when I heard a familiar voice.
“It seems you’re a bit of trouble, huh, E-i-y-a-kyun.”
The voice carried a casual tone dripping with condescension.
Seems like that guy really is here.
I turned in anticipation to see that voice. It was undeniably Hiro, my best friend.
He looked a bit different from when I last saw him—he had his trademark Prussian blue hair and freckles, but now he had ear piercings, about 3 on each ear, and also wore his uniform casually.
I was shocked, but let out a soft smile just because I was happy to see Hiro, because he was one of the reasons I came to EHS in the first place.
You see, being with him was a future goal I wanted to achieve.
“Seems like it. Care to help?”
“That depends. What does helping entail?”
“Oi, Hiro.”
“Yeah?”
“Don’t tell me you can’t figure such a simple thing out. If that’s the case, I might have to resort to calling you by your old nickname. Muno-kun,” I had a wide smile that perfectly reflected my malicious intent. “I’m sure everyone would love to call you that~.”
“Muno” means “incompetent”.
You see, Muno-kun was a nickname I came up with for Hiro when we were kids because he was generally bad at, well, everything.
He acted like an idiot, though he eventually became popular among the ladies and abandoned such a nickname.
That being said, he had a bit of a complex about it, and that was why I mentioned it—it was a punishment for his unwanted sarcasm.
“Hey, don’t be like that,” he patted my back nervously. “I was joking.”
“Relax, I’m kidding, dude. Just take me to your table and stop being a jackass.”
“Fine. Follow me.”
Hiro led me, and I followed silently. The walk to his table quickly grew awkward—well, for me at least.
I didn’t know what to say. You see, making fun of your friend, or rather teasing them, was way easier than having a normal conversation.
“Uh, Hiro, what’s up?” I managed to mutter something as we came close to Hiro’s table.
“Just school stuff, I guess. This school’s a bit different from other schools.”
“O-Oh, is that right?”
The conversation quickly turned stale, but luckily, we were already at Hiro’s table.
His table had an abnormal number of girls on it. In fact, the only boys at the table were Hiro and me.
Well, Hiro was always like that, so no shock there.
“So, Eiya, what do you think of this school?” Hiro spoke the moment we sat down.
And at the same moment Hiro said my name, the girls’ conversation came to an abrupt halt.
One of them shot me a glance sharp enough to cut steel, her fingers tightening around her drink.
Another whispered something to her friend, who gave a barely concealed smirk in my direction.
It didn’t take a genius to figure out what they were thinking: “Stay away from our blue-haired king.”
Not that I had any interest, of course, but naturally they didn’t know that.
Also, it was quite annoying that they didn’t notice my presence when I arrived, but did when Hiro mentioned my name.
That being said, I didn’t do anything about that and simply answered Hiro’s question—albeit a little weirdly.
“It’s... immaculate,” I said nervously. It had been some time since I last saw Hiro, so I was feeling a bit nervous and anxious.
“Dude, why’d you say it like that? Ha ha ha ha,” Hiro began laughing while pointing at me. “Don’t tell me you're nervous.”
“I-I’m not, idiot.”
Guess even after all these years, Hiro is still Hiro.
“Anyway,” Hiro said after calming down a bit. “How's Auntie?”
“Oh, my mom? She’s fine; she was actually against me coming to EHS, but I convinced her.”
To be honest, I don’t know how she’s doing.
“You had a fight with her? I would love to see the calm Eiya get flustered. So, why did you pick my school? Don’t tell me it’s because you missed me.”
Spot-on deduction, Hiro, but I’ll vehemently deny it.
“Don’t flatter yourself. I chose it because it's the best school in the world.”
“I think that the second part is a stretch; I’d say it’s just the best in East Asia, and also, you forgot the part where you say, ‘I miss you, Hiro’,” he blew kisses, trying to mock me.
“Miss you, my ass. I’d rather drink bleach.”
“Why don’t you? I mean, it would be so much better if you killed yourself,” one of the girls mumbled.
Hey, that’s a bit too much, isn’t it? Come on, it was just a joke.
Hiro didn’t hear it, but I think if I told him what she said, she might actually make me drink bleach.
With such harsh words said to me, I focused on my meal, finally allowing the girls to converse with Hiro.
Their conversations were mostly about ‘girl drama’, so I paid no mind to it, though I constantly heard the name ‘Takamine Hoshiko’.
I silently wondered who that was, but didn’t have the confidence to ask.
Occasionally, Hiro added me to the conversation, but I barely added input—simply saying “Yeah” or “Mhm”.
I was sure he was trying to make me not feel left out; Hiro was a good person after all—but the thing was that the more I talked, the more the girls seemed to hate me, and unfortunately, Hiro didn’t notice.
Not really a shock, though, since Hiro is basically a golden retriever.
His behaviour and enthusiasm were amazing and would normally make me feel at ease, but the girls still glared at me with extreme murderous intent and immediately made me tense up.
In any case, the food was good, I guess. Nothing really stood out to me from it, but it wasn’t bad in any way—rather, it excelled at everything to the point that no part was better than the other.
I suppose one could say it’s pretty good.
Anyway, after I finished my food, I immediately stood up to leave.
“Y-Y-You’re done?” Hiro said, appearing shocked as I took barely 3 minutes.
“Yeah, dude.”
“Wow, which one are you? A food fighter or a mukbanger?” Hiro said, still staring at me in awe.
I stared at him silently as a result of his cringeworthy joke.
How am I even supposed to reply to that?
As for the food, well… To be honest, I rushed through my food as fast as possible. Between the death glares and someone telling me to off myself, I figured it was best to leave.
“Are you sure you want to leave? L-Let’s talk for a while. I mean, I hope no one made you leave,” he urged before looking at the girls to see if anyone was forcing me to leave—not that they would actively show that side to him or anything.
“Nah, no one made me leave.”
I knew for a fact that if I didn’t lie, my peaceful school life would turn into a living hell.
“I see. Well, if you really wanna go, then I can’t stop you. Sucks that I can't give you a tour, though,” he sighed.
Of course, I wanted nothing more than to say I was down for a tour, but the girls gave me a look that made me keep my mouth shut.
“Anyway, give me a text if you ever get lost, kay?"
That’s unlikely considering the death glares from the girls beside you.
I mean, what if they steal your phone and lead me to the middle of the Bermuda Triangle?
“Sure. See you later, man.”
“Yeah. Later, dude.”
With that done, my reunion with my childhood friend/best friend ended. Now, it was time to look for other friends.
