Chapter 5.0: Who Am I?
“Just answer this, Nakamura. Who are you?”
Ugh!!!!
Why is he asking this already? He was probably told something about me; if not, he wouldn’t have come to this conclusion yet.
Did he learn it from Hayashi? No, she probably won’t do this yet either, especially after she just sent Yumeiko.
It’s probably because of Jackson-sensei. I suspected she might know something since she’s my homeroom teacher.
That woman is too dangerous; I need to meet her very soon.
She might know my past, and that will affect my normal life, but what does she want?
I’m sure she at least told Ito what her goal was, though not all of this is from Jackson-sensei.
People don’t just believe anything they hear—meaning he must have had some sort of preconceived notion.
He seems like he even managed to figure out my plans; maybe I can probe him later.
No, no, no, what am I talking about?
I absolutely cannot do that. I mean, after all, I’m just an ordinary student, so the only way I can react is by doing nothing.
“Are you not going to answer that, too?” Ito got impatient; I had spent over 2 minutes thinking.
“…Who am I? Well, I don’t know either; maybe I’m a shadow, a memory, or a phantom,” I lied.
“What does that even mean? You just said a whole lot of nothing.”
“Well, I am just in my first year of high school, so there’s no way I’ve figured myself out already.”
Of course, that was a lie, and I was sure even he didn’t believe it for a second.
Even though I might not understand everyone, I can say without a doubt, I completely understand myself—no mistakes there.
“That’s true, but—” he said.
“Anyway, I’m someone who’s not important, so don’t pay much attention to what I do. Well, see ya.”
“Wait—” he urged.
“Ito, this conversation is over. Stop dragging it out to see if you can get more information,” I walked off because I had no intention of continuing the conversation; after all, I would gain nothing.
I left him there, and when the next day rolled around, Ito told the class about what happened—leaving my parts out, of course.
However, that was expected. I doubted he would gain anything from ratting me out; moreover, there was his saviour complex.
“…So that’s what happened,” Ito said.
“That’s Ito-kun for you, always dependable.”
“Yeah, never doubted him.”
“Hey, Innō, you should learn a thing or two.”
“Grr…” Innō growled at anyone who dared to reprimand him, but quickly calmed down.
I sat quietly until my neighbour spoke to me about something.
“Didn’t you also do the case with Ito? I didn’t hear about you, like at all; even those that didn’t do much, I heard about them, but why not you?” Suzuki asked.
“Oh, well, the thing is, I was more of an observer.”
“Uh huh… Well, it’s fine, I guess.”
“You guess?”
“I just expected you to do something.”
“Like what?”
“...I don’t know; anything would have been fine.”
“So you’re discontent that I did nothing.”
“Yeah.”
“I’m not really good at that stuff, so I think it’s best that I did nothing rather than arbitrarily messing stuff up.”
“...I see.”
No snarky comeback, huh?
I supposed that was sufficient for her, so I silently nodded and faced Ito.
With Ito drawing the curtain on the case, the day went uneventfully. And it would have stayed that way, but unfortunately, as I was about to leave for the dormitory, I was interrupted.
{Nakamura Eiya, you’re called to your homeroom teacher’s faculty room.}
I heard a voice through the microphone; it sounded similar to Jackson-sensei’s voice.
The voice caused a bit of chatter in the class as most were confused. Rightfully so. Only Ito looked at me with knowing eyes.
“Huh? Why’s she calling you?” Suzuki asked.
“Dunno, maybe it’s related to the case?”
“Then why isn’t she asking Ito?”
“He’s always pretty busy, so she probably wanted someone with an open schedule.”
“Is that right?”
What’s with the interrogation? I need to remove myself quickly before I slip up.
I had a nervous sweat as I stood up to leave.
“Well, anyway, see ya tomorrow, Suzuki,” I quickly added.
“Yeah, see ya.”
With that, I hastily moved through my class’s back door and headed to Jackson-sensei’s faculty room.
I was expecting this development, though not today. I expected she would reach out more inconspicuously, especially since Ito confronted me just yesterday.
Damn, she’s way too impatient. Anyway, today I need to shut down this operation—for good.
The thought instinctively made me sigh.
“Seems like I’ve picked up quite the habit since coming here. This damn sighing every time things turn… tedious.”
At any rate, I arrived at the door to her faculty room in a few minutes.
At the door, I took a deep breath and entered the faculty room. On entering, I saw Jackson-sensei in her kitchenette, making something.
The faculty room was big enough to house a kitchenette and a parlour; it was more of a lounge. It was the perfect place to laze around, as it even lacked cameras.
With this place, you can just slack off and avoid classes… or threaten someone.
“Do you know how to cook?” She emerged from the kitchenette in an apron.
She held a knife while wearing an apron that hugged her cleavage nicely. *
Cute. I’ve got to burn this into my memory.
“Uh… Not really.”
“Is that so? You can’t cook? In that case, why don’t you eat this meal I just made?”
“Uhh…”
“Just eat it,” she grinned. “I sometimes cook for fun and end up throwing the food, so it would be nice if you could just eat some.”
Well, this is an unexpected development, and also I am pretty hungry, so I suppose I can have some.
I nodded before sitting on a couch close to me, and she sat opposite me on another couch. She then put the dish on the table and slid it to me.
“I can eat it all?”
She nodded in affirmation, hands on her chin, grinning from ear to ear.
“Are you sure?”
“Yeah, like I said, it would have gone to waste,” she smiled.
This feels like a trap, but… It looks so damn appetising. I feel like a lamb walking to a slaughterhouse just because it saw lots of grass.
I really wanted just to stand up and leave, but what if this wasn’t a trap? I mean, what was the guarantee? After all, I was just speculating, right?
Yeah, I was, wasn’t I? Meaning this was well within my rights, as I was just giving Jackson-sensei the benefit of the doubt.
…At any rate, it was still a choice. One that made me gulp as my stomach rumbled as if to tell me, “Go for it, Eiya!”
And well, I listened to it, and I took a bite and—
It tasted amazing, nothing like I’d tasted before—a totally revolutionary meal.
“Do you like it?”
“Yeah, it’s so—”
“Now, to cut to the chase, I want you to help the class,” she cut me off.
Of course.
And this is why you should always, and I mean always, despise the free lunch, literally. I can’t believe I fell for it.
Damn bitch took the fact that I haven’t eaten since lunch to trick me.
“You know why I called you here, right?” She continued.
“Nope,” I took another bite. “This is really delicious, you know.”
“You like the dish, right? Then don’t lie.”
“I respect authority, so I will never lie to a teacher,” I lied.
“Yeah, right. You’re lying through your teeth.”
“It’s the truth.”
I continued eating till I finished the meal while Jackson-sensei watched me with a glare.
Why are you glaring? You literally are the one who offered me food, and I am pretty hungry. Wait, she didn’t poison this, did she?
…
…No, she wouldn’t. I would know.
Also, I think it’s time I took action.
After finishing my meal, I dusted myself and spoke with a sly grin.
“Thanks, so are we done?” I asked.
“Why intentionally fail your entrance exams?”
“…”
“If you don’t answer, I will frame you for a crime.”
“A crime? Which one?”
“A crime that even a child such as yourself can face adequate punishment for.”
There were many such crimes, like murder and arson, but those took immense preparation to do.
Not to mention hiding the evidence and even the amount of effort needed to frame someone for it, so it also had to be something easily believable.
It’s probably only one thing—
“Rape, right?” I guessed with a smile.
“…Just talk; my patience grows thin.”
“…Heugh,” I sighed, “...Why do all this for a useless person?”
“Useless? Do you still try to deny it? I know of your abilities.”
“Well, I doubt that. If you did, you would know how useless I am and wouldn’t even involve me in anything.”
I suppose you wouldn’t know what it means to be useless, or rather, defective.
“To be defective or useless is to be unwilling to work,” or so he said.
I let out another sigh, then moved forward to grab her collar while maintaining an unreadable expression.
“H-How did you move so fast? I didn’t even see you move an inch; did you even move?”
That’s an overestimation; I did move. Though maybe I overdid it. 0.5 seconds might be too much for her.
“…”
“Can’t you just attempt to help the class? Just try to help them reach Class Alpha, to make them surpass it even further. Or at least tell me what your plan in this case was,” she looked away as she shivered a bit.
She’s more polite and a bit shy. What happened? All I did was grab her collar; she isn’t acting the same at all. This isn’t the Jackson-sensei I know. What changed?
I mean, after all, I’m a student, and holding your teacher like this can lead to unforeseen consequences.
“Fine, I’ll indulge you; I’ll tell you my plan.”
“Really? Please go on.”
There was no use pretending; in fact, I felt like indulging her a little bit.
“I provoked Shimizu after figuring out that he values intelligence way too much and sees us in Class Epsilon as beneath him mentally—he has disdain for when people under him mock him.”
“Yes, I noticed this when you and Ito came for the case on the first day. So how does Arai give up?” She asked, regaining some of her composure.
“Relax, I’m getting there. After provoking Shimizu, I went to the dorms and did some thinking, and then I realised something.”
“What?”
“I had always thought it was Class Beta’s mastermind that planned this, but it was actually just Arai.”
“How did you know?”
“When you think about it, it makes a whole lotta sense. The plan was too sloppy, especially for someone leading a class like Class Beta, with 50 students who were quite intelligent.”
“Yeah, but that isn’t all, right?”
“Yes, it isn’t. After I figured out that this was most likely Arai’s plan, I made sure Shimizu would know our plan—or rather, Ito’s plan—by making Ito tell me in Class Beta’s pathway.”
“How did you know Shimizu would come there? What if he didn’t—”
“He wouldn’t do that; I knew he would use his class pathway because he probably wouldn’t like dealing with us in Class Epsilon or even any other classes below him.”
“How did you know such a place existed? This is still your second week?”
You’re glossing over the fact that it’s in the school map.
“A little birdie told me.”
“Just say it, please. Can’t you at least tell me that?” She pleaded.
…What is going on?
“Fine, since you begged, I will.”
Her expression darkened but quickly lightened as she remembered that I was still holding her shirt collar.
“I checked the school map,” I lied.
Of course, I’ve already memorised the map; I did it when I reached the dorms on the first day, but she shouldn’t know that.
“So, then you provoked Arai, too.”
“Yes, however, for the entire day I had to not meet with Ito.”
“Huh? Why? Wait, were you being spied on?”
“Exactly, so you can be smart,” I snickered. “Anyway, it was Shimizu who was spying on me.”
“How’d you know?”
“I just guessed as much; he would have liked to have seen if there were altercations.”
“But how would you know that Shimizu would come by the time he did?”
“Huh? Oh, I just guessed as much—I mean, perhaps he had a strategy meeting or something because of what I did, so he would wake up earlier than usual. Anyway, I have decided that I will help the class.”
“Huh? Really?”
“That is, if you tell me how you realised I held back my intelligence. The real reason.”
It would be a real pain in the ass if she learned it from Hayashi because I would have to go against my plan.
“I, uh, heard from some co-workers.”
“That’s a lie,” I doubted she would say the real answer immediately.
“It was from a co-worker, I swear.”
“You’re not lying, right?”
“Yes, I’m not.”
Of course, just saying that you’re not lying doesn’t mean you’re not, but there was no point in just outright doubting her.
Moreover, it explained why the dorm knew who I was; she probably heard my name from a co-worker, too.
I couldn’t confirm if this was a lie, but many things pointed toward it being true, and honestly, I needed it to be true.
She probably isn’t lying; if she learnt it from Hayashi, she would ask more questions and know how useless I am.
But this Hayashi girl—just who is she? I can’t remember her, no matter how long I think, so that means we haven’t met before this school, but then how does she know me?
Did they send her?
No, what am I saying? They can’t even if they want to.
“Hmm… I suppose that’s enough,” I let go of her shirt collar and sat on my seat in a relaxed posture, spreading my legs and hands as I looked at the ceiling.
“Nakamura Eiya,” her voice went back to normal.
“Yes?”
“How dare you hold your teacher like—”
I suddenly glared at her, and she went mute. I couldn’t think of why she acted so strangely; it made no sense.
Why would a teacher be afraid of a student? One who was even a first-year student and didn’t have any violent tendencies and even looked quite innocent—and dare I say, non-threatening.
“I’ll be on my way; also, do not try to dig into me, and just let me live peacefully.”
“If I do that, then can you help surpass Class Alpha?”
“Hmm? Sure, we will surely surpass Class Alpha. Trust me.”
Of course, that depends on what you define as surpassing. I mean, I don’t really care after all; however, what does she mean by surpassing?
Maybe she means academically or athletically.
But it’s not like you will gain anything, or is there a hidden benefit that I haven’t considered?
“Nakamura!”
“Hmm? What?”
“Oh, uh,” she shyly said.
She looks really cute like this; I can’t believe she’s the same robotic teacher who teaches my class.
“Thank you for working for the class—” she continued.
“Hmm? I think I’m confused by your words. When did I say I would work for the class?”
“But you just said you’ll help the class.”
“I will help, not work for the class. Don’t worry if you don’t get it.”
I meant that I would follow someone’s plan, but I wouldn’t make one myself—that I would perform a role given to me, but I wouldn’t create one for myself.
Basically, I meant that I wouldn’t do what I did with Class Beta; I wouldn’t act on my own. However, that help would only come if I felt like it, and I won’t be forced to do it.
I’m not going to work for anyone ever again. I suppose that’s wishful thinking, but I’m fine with that fantasy.
I left the room and walked back to the dormitory with a slight grin; of course, I donned said grin after I left Jackson-sensei’s office.
I donned it for one simple reason—I managed to fool Jackson-sensei.
I’m sure now she’ll think I’m just some cocky student with his head far up his ass and that I’m not actually that intelligent, though she still sees me as a usable instrument.
With that being said, she really is quite the actress.
During our conversation, there were only two instances when Jackson-sensei was actually scared; those were when I first grabbed her collar, and when I glared at her—though she quickly rubbed it off.
She faked her fear through her conversation to try to figure out my true personality. While I won a battle, there was still a war that I lost—the fight for my peace.
I suppose there’s nothing to be done.
With everything done, I sent a message to him—to Yamada.
[Me: Thanks again.]
[Yama: … Just don’t ask for anything again, and delete these messages.]
[Me: For sure.]
Ahh, right. An explanation is needed, it seems. Basically, I lied about guessing that Shimizu would come at the time he did.
Yamada was the person I helped on my first day here, so when I saw him involved in the case, I knew I could use that to my advantage.
It’s honestly still shocking that he was convinced to help his class after one day, but that proved he was easily convinced, so that was also to my advantage.
Anyway, when we went to Class Beta, I dropped my account name on my desk, saying I was the one who saved him.
He texted me subsequently after thanking me for what I did on the first day, and I reminded him about how he said he would owe me a favour.
Pretty underhanded of me, not gonna lie.
At any rate, for said favour, I said that after Shimizu came back from meeting me, he should convince them to hold a strategy meeting on what to do, and he should do his best to prolong that meeting by at least 30 minutes or at least until it’s past 10 PM.
Arai probably already held this strategy meeting without Yamada having to intervene, but where he had to intervene was in length.
Well, I suppose the details don’t matter cuz I managed to luck out.
Yes, I completely and utterly lucked out—because a lot of things could have gone wrong. What if Yamda reported me to Arai? What if he ignored me and did nothing? What if my plan completely failed?
Yeah, all of those things could have happened, yet they didn’t. I know, I know; it wasn’t particularly due to my ability; it was because a certain lady smiled at me—lady luck, that is. So yes, I was very lucky.
Anyway, after texting him and putting my phone away, I was lost in reverie; the situation I was in, frankly, made me a little angry.
Especially after all I had done to get this so-called peace. Now I find out that even here, peace is nonexistent.
So even here I can’t have peace, huh?
I suppose I should have seen that coming. No, it’s still too premature to complain.
It’s still the second week of school. I can’t give up, but wouldn’t a normal person give up on something hard?
But if I give up, I won’t be normal, but I must have the qualities of a normal person to be normal.
Shit, what a paradox.
Anyway, I learned something substantial today—Jackson-sensei is hell-bent on surpassing Class Alpha; I wonder why.
Anyway, from now on, I will just have to be more normal, even boring; I don’t care as long as I can just be normal.
And fade into the shadow.
