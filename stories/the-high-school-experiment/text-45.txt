Chapter 12.1: In Bowling, Winners Take All.
Ring, ring, ring.
Normally, I would quickly wake up and rush so I could head to school or something of the sort. However, today I slowly woke up and turned off the alarm.
“...I set it too early, didn’t I?”
You see, I woke up early, around 6 am, for my special outing that would happen today—Ahh, by the way, it’s Saturday.
Anyway, I had a date that was supposed to happen yesterday that was postponed due to Aiya meeting her childhood friend. Afterwards, she called to remind me.
I got ready quite quickly; after 15 minutes, I was ready for the date, since I had ample time, as the date was at noon.
So I waited for a while, but by 11:30 I left. In fact, I woke up early for no reason, but it allowed me to just laze around; however…
I should have done what I did last time.
As I was turning around a corner so I could use the elevator, I bumped into Innō.
“I’m sorry. Innō?”
I was surprised to see him, though it wasn’t impossible to see him. The school had 3 dorms, one for each year. That meant we were staying where this year’s second years stayed.
It also meant it wasn’t impossible to see your classmates and friends.
Also, we had met before, hadn’t we?
“N-Nakamura? Hey dude, what’s up?”
“Oh, nothing much.”
“Ok, it’s been a while, huh?”
“Yeah.”
Since we both started dating someone, we became quite distant—well, not like we were close in the first place. I mean, he was closer friends with Inoue anyway, so maybe that relationship stayed the same.
“Anyway, what are you up to? A date?”
“Yeah, what about you?”
“Same. Do you think it’s another double date?”
“Probably.”
Aiya was really adamant that we had the date by 12 pm and made sure to say it was just a normal date, so I suspected she was lying.
“Should we go together and surprise them?”
“Nah, let’s go separately and act surprised.”
“Alright. Well, see you later.”
“Later, dude.”
I rushed over to my date and ended up using the bus to save time.
In retrospect, I should have left my room immediately after I was done.
Luckily, in this school, you had 3 free bus fares per month, and I had never used the bus, so I didn’t have to pay. By the time I got there, Aiya was already in the venue—the bowling alley.
“Hey.”
“Eiya!” She ran and hugged me—no, she jumped on me.
“H-hey. I presume you’re happy to see me.”
“Of course, why wouldn’t I be?”
I smiled at her words; they reflected her true emotions, as they were clear to see on her face. Damn, it really was amazing to be loved.
“Are you going to let me go?” I joked.
“You should be happy that your girlfriend is happy to see you,” she pouted, hugging me harder.
“I-I am,” I was getting flustered as I could see her bra.
“Wait, don't tell me you’re shy?” she said with a devious look on her face.
“I-I’m not,” I dropped her quickly so I wouldn’t blush.
“Anyway, let’s go in,” I said, trying to change the subject.
“Fine, but I’ll have you know I’m a professional bowler.”
“Don’t worry, I’ll give you a run for your money.”
We went into the bowling alley; it had an automatic door—the sliding kind.
It had really bright lighting; it was probably made to boost focus. It also had good air conditioning, even better than the ones in class—it was like the ones in the arcade. It happened to be cleaner than I expected.
It was also less crowded than I expected, and even fewer people played. It had about 10 lanes, and only 2 lanes were in use. The other people were just at the snack bar.
We went to pay for the services, then we went to play the game. But before we started, I noticed Aiya pouting silently. What could I have done wrong?
Oh, right, I didn’t compliment her outfit.
On most of our dates, we wore the school uniform, so I barely ever complimented her, and she didn’t really mind; however, today she looked extra cute and wanted a compliment.
She didn’t want this when Yoshida was here, though, so what changed? Hmm?
“You look cute.”
“Huh?”
“I said you look really cute, and not just cute but drop-dead gorgeous.”
“O-Oh, thanks,” she looked down shyly.
Wait a minute, she was angry that day; what if this was one of the reasons she was angry? I didn’t particularly notice it, though.
…Maybe I’m just overthinking it.
We headed to a lane so we could get started with our bowling match. Aiya wore a black blouse paired with a black tennis skirt and tights.
She was starting, so she went to get the ball. She tied her hair in a bun to avoid getting it in her face.
I went to research bowling, so I’m sure I would do at least better than average.
The problem is, I have no idea how good Aiya is.
But that also isn’t a big deal. The thing about bowling is that it isn’t a sport that allows for external interference—that is, to put it simply, the more pins knocked down, the higher the possibility of you winning.
Now let’s see how good Aiya is.
She assumed a bowling stance and threw the ball; her form was elegant, even more elegant than some professionals.
As the ball rolled down the lane, it knocked over six pins with ease.
She knocked down 6 out of 7 of the pins.
“Wow!” I exclaimed.
“I’m good, right?”
“You’re better than good; you’re amazing.”
“N-Nakamura-kun?!” A voice behind me said my name; it was Innō, pretending to see me for the first time today.
I gave him a wink to signify a good job.
I was wondering when you’d come.
“Innō? What are you doing here?” I followed through with my act too.
“I could ask you the same thing.”
“Oh, Innō-kun and I were just out for a date,” Yoshida said, emerging from behind him.
“What a coincidence. I can’t believe you also wanted to go on a date to the bowling alley,” Aiya said, supposedly surprised to see her best friend.
“Like for real,” Yoshida replied. “Well, since we’re both here, let’s play a game.”
“What kind of game?” Aiya replied.
“It’s more of a competition.”
“Go on.”
“Between our boyfriends, let’s see who can get higher scores, best of 3.”
I seriously don’t want to do it. I don’t want to compete with Innō; well, I won’t need to if they both say no.
I threw a look at Aiya that said, “Please say no.” She smirked with a menacing look.
“Yeah, let’s do it,” Aiya agreed.
That bitch! It’s fine as long as Innō doesn’t—
“I’m down,” Miyamura also agreed.
You idiot!!! Because of you damn demons, I’m now basically forced to play the game with Miyamura; might as well just throw in the towel.
“Also, by the way, the loser treats everyone to lunch at that new restaurant.”
Ok, I’ve got to win; I don’t want to waste money at that new restaurant.
Innō looked athletic, but this game was not like football and basketball, which required a certain level of athleticism.
Bowling was a bit different; it needed grip, speed control, and good stance/footwork.
Since it doesn’t need expert skills, I can replicate something good enough to just get a little higher than him.
As we stepped onto the assigned lane, I adjusted my grip on the blue-and-black bowling ball I took—I didn’t pick it for a specific reason.
Innō carried a red ball, probably to match his red hair.
“I’ll show what Hiroshi is made of.”
“Hiroshi?”
Who’s that? Is that his nickname? Maybe that’s his first name.
“You don’t know? That’s my first name.”
“Oh. By the way, I want to go first.”
“Sure, go for it.”
“Eiya, win it for both of us!!!” Aiya cheered.
“Innō!! Win!!!” Yoshida cheered.
It’s kind of unfair that the girls get dinner no matter who wins.
Well, it doesn’t matter now; all that matters is that I win without standing out. However, it seems students around are paying attention.
I hate these conditions; I can only win by one point, or at least that’s my plan.
I want to go first, so it isn’t obvious that I’m just getting a point above him.
I lined up my shot. I stared down the lane, taking a deep breath before stepping forward.
I used the athletic stance, the most textbook, basic stance—I wasn’t a professional, so I only knew 3 stances, and this was the most efficient one.
I toppled 5 out of 7 pines.
I think that’s good enough. It was neither impressive nor underwhelming.
“Nakamura-kun, chill.”
“Huh?”
“Your expression—you look so serious. Like a dead fish—no, like a statue.”
“…I don’t know how to reply to that.”
“Just relax; I’m still gonna win.”
I was instinctively reacting to this, but that was the only way I knew to deal with stress.
Relax, it’s just a bowling match.
Next was Miyamura’s turn. He used the pendulum stance, which involved the bowler starting with a relaxed arm position to prepare for a smooth pendulum swing.
His ball knocked down all the pins, a perfect strike. He turned back, giving me a playful shrug.
“One down.”
“…”
It seems I should change my form; I’ll follow the stance of a famous bowler, Parker Bohn III.
I skimmed through his book—Bowling: How to Master the Game—as I finished preparing for my date with Aiya.
The stance that he used is known as the stroker stance.
My throw was a strike.
Innō threw his; he also got a strike.
“If I get a strike, then I win,” Miyamura said, his tone more serious now.
“…”
“Don’t sweat it; I’ll order something cheap this time.”
Somehow, I highly doubt that.
“Don’t give up, Eiya!!!”
“Yeah, you got this!!!”
“Beat that red-haired punk!!!”
Everyone in the bowling alley was watching, and random people started cheering my name.
“Hey, I’m not the villain.”
Actually, you are—trying to steal my Rp again, though I suppose your girl is more to blame, but whatever.
I prepared my stance, trying to calm my mind. Most people at this point would give in to the pressure, but I was not one for that, especially because Rp was involved.
I threw my ball; I got a perfect score.
I have to reinforce my win; I need to do something right now. I’ll have to cheat.
“Innō.”
“Yeah.”
“When you lose, I hope you’ll pay up.”
“Sorry to disappoint you, but it’s you who’s losing.”
“I suppose you’re right, but as a loser, don’t I deserve a consolation prize?”
“Like hell you do.”
Hey, weren’t you the one who collected a consolation prize last time?
“So you won’t agree?”
“Tell me first what prize you want.”
“Don’t worry, you’ll figure it out.”
With that, I had laid the seeds, so now I was going to display a lacklustre plan I came up with on the fly.
Really hope that this much is enough.
Since I was done, I went to where Yoshida and Aiya were. We started conversing before he finished bowling. I made sure we were out of his range, so he wouldn’t know we were talking about how he’d win.
“I might lose; I hope you guys take it easy on my wallet,” I pleaded.
“Don’t worry, I’ll help you pay.” Aiya offered.
“Really. You’re an angel.”
“Unfortunately, you can’t do that, Aiya-chan. It won’t be a fair bet anymore.”
“O-Oh.”
“Yeah, how ‘bout this, Nakamura? We’ll only order a certain amount of food.”
“I don’t want to limit the food you order, but ok. Why not have just three rounds?” I said.
“Fine with me.”
“Same.”
“Thanks to both of you, the bank will remain unbroken.”
Yoshida and Aiya started laughing. I glanced at Innō, then mouthed the words, ‘It will be a threesome.’
He had a complicated expression before subtly switching to anger; he was probably trying to figure out what just happened.
…Huh.
Anyway, Innō threw his ball in a fit of rage, forgetting his form; it was a gutter ball. He came back and pulled me to the restroom.
“What the hell?” Innō glared.
“Dude, what?” I feigned confusion.
“Don’t play dumb. You said you’ll do it.”
“Do what?”
“You said you’ll have a threesome with Yoshida and Aiya.”
“Eh?”
“Huh?”
“Uh, dude, just chillax; I think you misunderstood what I said.”
“Huh? What about the threesome you said?”
“What threesome?”
“You said you’ll have a threesome.”
“I said we agreed on only having three rounds at the restaurant.”
“R-R-Really?” Innō stopped to think, unsure of what I actually said.
“Yeah.”
“I think the pressure was getting to you.”
With this, my subpar gaslighting skills would be completely on display. You see, the Japanese for those words weren’t exactly the same, but seeing me with his girls saying ‘three rounds’ could connote a threesome.
Please work.
“Oh, sorry, man. I guess I was stressed,” he let me go.
After my explanation, Innō and I left the restroom. Innō repeatedly apologised, but I told him it was ok; I mean, after all, it was a lie, so I didn’t really mind.
I followed such a plan only because I knew who Innō was, and the plan wasn’t that impressive, to be honest.
It was Innō who had a lacklustre amount of self-control—that time during the class debacle, he had the ability to not brawl anywhere, which was good to avoid any lasting damage.
But he lacked the ability to push continuous insults away; after all, he was the first to hit someone during the class debacle. And despite being in a case, he was about to hit someone again just because of repeated insults.
I wasn’t sure how much he valued Yoshida, though, but perhaps his competitive side was more than enough. He was an athlete after all, and was supposedly a good one, so I took my chances.
And they worked.
