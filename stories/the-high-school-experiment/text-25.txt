Chapter 7.1: How To Help Innō
The walk to the dorms was peaceful; the gentle, cool winds and wonderful scenery reminded me of why I liked this school.
Reminds me of the time we went to Iceland.
“No rain today, too, huh?”
This month supposedly had a lot of rain, but I only experienced it on my second day in this school, and never again after that day.
“Not that I’m complaining, though.”
I didn’t like a particular type of weather but preferred generally cold areas, as they were somewhat… nostalgic.
Before I knew it, I was on the elevator to my room, and in just 3 minutes, I was at the door to my room, which I promptly opened.
As I entered and closed the door behind me, I let out a sigh of relief.
“Heugh!”
After all that happened today, I had finally reached my room. I was kinda hungry, but unfortunately, I didn’t have the funds to buy anything.
Cooking it is, huh?
This was just the start of my second week, and I couldn’t count on getting Rp anytime soon, so I had to cook again.
I usually cooked noodles at this time; the only thing I knew how to cook besides Tamago Kake Gohan, but those two were basically eaten every single day, so I wanted to make something new.
The thought of doing such a thing reminded me of what happened the last time I tried to experiment. That being said, this time was different; last time, I didn’t use oil.
However, realising something as simple as that wouldn’t really affect much, so I wanted to use Jackson-sensei’s food as a reference—in the sauce that Jackson-sensei made at her office, I noticed some mistakes.
She added thyme, curry, soy sauce, and aonori, but too much of the last was added, and there was no extra spice, and the food ended up savoury, though lacking something—that essential zing.
After mentally fixing the mistakes I found, I started cooking.
I made the rice, then added soy sauce, curry powder, black pepper, and thyme, stirring well.
I then flaked the fried fish and mixed it in gently, not rushing any step. I finished with a sprinkle of aonori on top.
Then I added a fried egg on top, and I successfully made curry fried rice with fish.
It looked good and smelt good, but I was afraid it would taste bad.
“Let’s have a taste,” I slowly picked up my spoon and put it in my mouth.
…
..
.
“It’s so…yummy!!!” I rubbed my cheek because of how good it tasted. It tasted so amazing that I finished the entire bowl alone.
“Damn, I’m thankful I saw the names of ingredients on the menu of the cafeteria.”
I noticed the names of the ingredients of the meals I ate, then googled them. Unfortunately, Google didn’t tell me how exactly it tasted, so I used Jackson-sensei’s meal as a reference.
Though what she made was basic curry rice, I learnt from both of our mistakes when it came to cooking and made a dish devoid of any of them.
With this now, I could make myself meals every day and not have to fight for a cafeteria table.
“Yes, with this I can survive with 10,000 Rp.”
Oh, right, I suppose it’s time I explain.
Yeah, I know that you’re probably wondering why I couldn’t cook for shit.
Before you think I’m some buffoon or something, it wasn’t my fault I didn’t know how to cook; rather, it was because I never had to cook for myself. In fact, I had never approached a kitchen ever in my life, well, till now.
Even now, I managed with what I knew how to cook, and that was Tamage Kako Gohan and noodles. I knew how to cook them because the former was taught to me, and as for the latter, instructions are written on the packet.
“Now, for Innō, well, I can create a strategy to handle that in the morning.”
I took a shower and went to bed, but didn’t feel tired, so I just looked at the ceiling before I went on my phone out of sheer boredom. I didn’t exactly feel like watching a video, so I instead went to check the accounts I had.
I had 19 of them; 1 was an emergency number for accidents, and if there was something that needed to be fixed.
15 of them were the accounts of the homeroom teachers—even the ones for upperclassmen (these 16 are pre-installed)—and the remaining three were Kagura, Innō, and someone else.
It was Kisaragi. I forgot to text him because of the debacle with Class Beta.
[Me: Hey, sorry I haven’t texted you before now; I got really busy.]
[Me: I’m really sorry.]
[Me: Uh, are you gonna answer?]
He didn’t answer or view any of my texts; perhaps he didn’t see them? That was possible, but I had the impression that he was purposely ignoring my texts.
To get my mind off that, I went over to YouTube to watch some anime recommendations.
I was able to do this because of the free Wi-Fi around the school.
You see, several internet lines spanned through Elysia (what Kagura used to refer to the entire island as a whole), well, at least on the west side and in EHS.
These lines were always active and were supposedly free, with there not being a limit on the amount of internet being used.
“This school really is just too amazing,” I said, finishing my fifth video. “Well, that’s enough for tonight.”
I put my phone on the nightstand and turned off the lights before promptly going to bed.
The next day rolled over, and I used the normal pathway to school, but something unexpected happened—
Ito came and walked beside me, grinning from ear to ear. I tried to increase my pace, and he caught up. I thought of running, but there was no way I could run faster than him.
“Uh, Ito, what are you doing?”’
“Oh, I’m just walking with you.”
“Why?”
“What’s wrong with me walking with a friend?”
Is this a form of probi—
I blushed slightly at his words; I couldn’t fathom why an ikemen like him would think of me as a friend.
Why’s an ikemen walking with a loner like me? Wait, is this my… popular arc?!
“Nakamura-kun, are you okay? Your face is red; perhaps you’re feeling feverish?”
“Oh no, it’s just—”
A girl bumped into me, and I almost lost my balance and fell on my face; luckily, I caught myself with my hands.
“Hey! What’s the big idea?!”
“Ito-kun~!”
The girl who bumped into me didn’t acknowledge me and berated Ito.
This scene made Ito wear a complicated expression; he was about to admonish her for what she did to me, but before he could, some girls swarmed him to the point I couldn’t even see his face. It seemed he had a fan club like that of Hiro.
He’s like the main character of a romance animanga.
As I watched in awe (and envy), the girls noticed me and gave me a look that said, “What are you still doing here?”
It wasn’t as hostile as the girls on Hiro’s lunch table, given that some were my classmates, but it was hostile enough to make me leave.
I arrived at class and, due to having nothing to do, fell into a slumber; it was a habit I developed at this school. I stayed asleep until I was awoken by my beloved neighbour to my right, Suzuki Aiya.
“Hey,” she said.
“Oh, hey,” I replied.
Because of how frequently people have woken me up, I have gotten so used to it that I wasn’t even surprised when I woke up and just rolled with it.
Though I need to do something about my sleeping habits.
“You sleep way too much; do you have narcolepsy?” she joked.
“I hope not,” I laughed lightly.
“Well, anyway, do you have a club?”
“Nope, missed my opportunity on the first day.”
“For real? Well, it just so happens that a certain friend of mine wants to start one. Do you wanna join?”
“Well, no but…”
Wait a minute; I didn’t think about it before, but Suzuki’s popular, right?
That means she’ll know people who might be suitable for Innō, and since she’s joining the club, those cute girls will join too.
Let me ask just to make sure.
“Yeah, I’ll join, but only if there are lots of cute girls!”
It took a moment for me to understand what I just said; almost everyone looked at me. The pervert duo looked at me as if I were one of their own.
“Fu fu fu,” Suzuki giggled. “I don’t know if there will be lots of cute girls, but girls will be there. Actually, I want you to join because to make a club, you need at least one male member.”
“No other boy wants to join?”
“Well, they do, but that friend of mine said they just want to join because of me.”
“Oh, I see, but why me?”
“Figure that out yourself,” she grinned slightly. “Besides, I’m sure you don’t wanna tell me why you want to know if there are cute girls either, right?” she grinned.
“Y-Yeah, anyway, where’s the meeting area?” I responded nervously.
“Come here, let me show you on the map.”
It was in a building close to this one; the meeting area was on the second floor in the third room on the right.
“Also, can someone join alongside me?”
“Hmmm,” she put a finger on her cheek as she thought. “Fine, but who’s the person?”
“It’s Innō.”
“I see, but can I ask why?”
“Uh, it’s a secret.”
“So that’s how you wanna do it. Hmph,” she pouted.
Cute.
But didn’t we just agree we both have something we don’t wanna say, so why’s she angry?
I wanted to ask that, but classes had already started, so we couldn’t talk.
During break, I ate the meal I had made; it was the same as the one I had made yesterday. I ate it silently in class as everyone went to eat in the cafeteria.
Since I was alone in class, I called Innō and briefed him about how I got him into the club.
“You did what?!” he screamed through the phone.
Are you trying to blow up my eardrums?
“Why are you shouting?”
“I’m shouting cuz you put me in a club full of randos—I’m already in a club.”
“Randos?”
“Anyway, I’m not going there.”
“What if there are—”
“Not going.”
What do I do to convince him?
I couldn’t come up with anything to convince Innō—I had forgotten that he was in the volleyball club.
However, I never planned for Innō to stay in the club. I just wanted him to come today and see the girls, so I could find someone suitable and execute my strategy.
“Innō, can’t you just come for today?”
“No, I have—”
“Suzuki is going to be there,” I cut him off before he finished.
“I’m coming.”
Suzuki was a very cute girl, and I noticed Innō was fond of her because even on my first day, they were talking.
He didn’t confess to her, so maybe he didn’t like her romantically, but I was willing to bet he would come because of her.
After I ended my call with Innō, I went back to eating in silence until Kagura suddenly came to class.
“Nakamura? You cook?” she asked.
“It’s a hobby I picked up. Just trying to save money, y’know.”
“Oh, you must be running short on money.”
“Well…” I laughed nervously.
I’m not sure you’re supposed to tell a girl your financial situation, especially when it’s not in the best situation, but…
“Anyway, it’s odd to find you alone. I thought you had many friends,” I added.
“Uh, sometimes I like to be alone.”
That was odd, at least to me. Kagura was very popular; she had many friends from different classes and was quite extroverted.
The thought of someone like that seeking solitude was surprising, but maybe it was just that she wanted some time to think.
Well, a loner could never really understand the mind of a social butterfly, right?
Anyway, we sat quietly until some people from other classes came over to meet Kagura.
“Kagura-chan,” a friend hugged her. “Where were you? Oh~.”
They noticed my presence and started to deduce some things in their heads.
“Is he your boyfriend?”
“No,” Kagura said in a matter-of-fact tone.
“Wow, you didn’t even blush or think about it. Well, I suppose someone as bright as you wouldn’t date someone gloomy,” another friend said.
Hey! I’m not gloomy, am I? Tell them, Kagura, tell them that I’m not gloomy.
“Yeah, that’s right,” Kagura replied, looking at me, then suddenly turning back to her friends. “Wait, no, don’t say it like that?”
“Say what? You’re the one who agreed to it,” her friends retorted.
“Uh, um, Nakamura-kun, I didn’t mean it like that. Promise,” Kagura said, her face full of remorse. “I just said that because I don’t really see you with any friends.”
It felt like she sent an arrow to my chest.
“That’s…” I replied, my expression a bit sad because none of my friends hung out with me. It hurt even more because she was also one of said friends.
“No, don’t get sad. I’m sure someone like you can make friends, especially with your cute face. I mean, I’m your friend, so I’m sure you’ll get more. A cutie pie like you is bound to make more friends."
Damn, another arrow.
My expression darkened at her words—I suppose no man wanted to be called ‘cute’; it was sort of taboo in a way.
In fact, even obviously cute children got rather heated when called cute.
Anyway, Kagura happened to notice that I was disconcerted and tried to retract her comment.
“Uh, no, I mean, uh, you’re not that bad. You are ranked 41st among the ikemen in our class.”
41st? Damn, another arrow.
“Huh? No, I meant—”
“Chop,” one of Kagura’s friends gave Kagura a karate chop on her head.
“Huh? What was that for?”
“You’re overcomplicating things; there wasn’t a point in mentioning that list.”
“I-I was just trying to help—”
“Shut up,” she said sternly. “So Nakamura-kun, was it? As you can see, she’s an airhead, so bear with her. Don’t worry about your rating because, on the cute guys’ ratings, you’re number 3 in the whole grade,” she gave me a thumbs up.
“Are you sure?” I was sceptical of her words. “You don’t even know my name.”
“I’m not exactly that good with names, but I’m great with faces.”
…Is that so?
That wasn’t particularly a weird ability, as humans often remembered faces more than names, especially names attached to someone especially important or striking.
Wait, that means my face didn’t even leave an impact to the point of her remembering my name.
“I see, but you do know that your words don’t really make me feel better, like at all,” I sighed.
“I guess not, huh. Well, most boys don’t like being called cute.”
“Yeah. Also, I’m a little curious: who made the ratings?”
“The girls, of course~,” she grinned, like it was just a normal thing to say.
“I see, so is there a female rating?”
“...”
There was an uncomfortable silence, and their expression changed to one of disdain before they all left without saying a word.
Though I suppose it had to do with me saying something about a girl’s rating.
I really shouldn’t have asked that, huh.
After they left, the class was silent once more, and the day went on peacefully without me knowing what I did. Yeah, I knew I shouldn’t have asked, but why?
Well, I must say that Kagura did have lots of friends because almost every few days, I saw her with a new group of people.
In fact, today was a good example; the people in class today weren’t the same people that she was with when she went to the party. They also happened not to be my classmates either.
Seems like she’s got an infinite well of friends, huh?
Well, I suppose that’s what came with being so socially adroit. I mean, no matter what group she was with, she knew how to act accordingly.
It was amazing that she could do this despite being an airhead, though I suppose natural intelligence wasn’t exactly related to social intelligence; some even say it dampened it.
Not that I would know anything about social groups since I’m especially socially inept.
Anyway, after school ended, I went to the club area. It looked quite abandoned, with a water leak in which algae began to grow, and overgrown grass.
“This is the place, right?”
Suzuki had shown me earlier, but this place really made me doubt my own memory because what was I even looking at?
It looked too unsightly for an elite high school; nevertheless, I went to the second floor and the third room on the right, like Suzuki said.
Brmm!
I was walking over to the classroom when I heard a sudden sound, so I dashed to the club room, but as I entered, I was shocked.
“Oww…” A girl sat on the floor, holding her head and wincing.
It seemed she had fallen and was still in pain, though she appeared uninjured.
She had long, dark green hair with matching eyelashes and nails—she was quite pretty. She was probably a lower-class student—that is, a member of Class Delta or Epsilon—and had lavender eyes. She also seemed to be dressed rather casually.
Oh, right, there was something I forgot to mention: that girl was in her underwear. It was pink underwear.
“Cute.”
