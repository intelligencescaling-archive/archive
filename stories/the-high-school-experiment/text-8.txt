Chapter 1.1: Cafeteria Debacle
After the fourth period, the bell for break rang. Everyone was leaving with their friends, and I, still being friendless, was left alone—no one even approached me.
However, Ito looked at me for a while and was about to call me, but he was surrounded by friends—specifically girls—so he couldn’t do anything.
He happened to still see me, so I gave him an intense stare, trying to send a message.
Invite me to come with you! Please!
He already wanted to call me, so this was sort of my way of saying, “Yes, I want to come with you.” Unfortunately, he didn’t get the message and left shortly after.
“Heugh.”
I sighed at my predicament, though I suppose there was nothing to be done
I guess I’ll just head out on my own again. Luckily, there’s always the trustworthy and always reliable Hiro.
I was in my seat arranging my books (well, they weren’t really used) as I was leaving for lunch until—
“Nakamura-kun.”
“Yeah?”
“Wanna have lunch together? That’s what I wanted to ask in the morning,” Suzuki said casually.
So that’s what she wanted to ask? Also, how can she say it so casually? Like it’s nothing.
Come on, how could I say no? I definitely can’t say no. I will say—
“No,” I managed to squeeze out.
“Huh? Why?”
“The thing is, my best friend and I have lunch together.”
I would have still said yes in a heartbeat—sorry, not sorry, Hiro—but I happened to watch a video on YouTube that said bros before hoes.
Not that I’m implying Suzuki is a hoe, but…
“Why do you look like you’re thinking something rude?” she asked.
What kind of Godly intuition is that?
“Trust me, I’m not… Not like I have the gall to,” I said that last part under my breath.
Why would I mess with a gyaru? Do I look like a madman? I hope I don’t.
“Wait, don’t tell me you’re shy,” Suzuki snickered.
“What? N-No, I’m not.”
“Really? Fine, then say it distinctly.”
“Uh, well…”
“So, Nakamura-kun, are you shy? Yes or no.”
More scared than shy.
“N-No, I really am not!”
“I totally heard you; you said yes, didn’t you? Nakamura-kun is shy~,” she smirked.
“Uh—Um, I, uh—fine then, just forget what I said.”
Not that I even said yes to begin with.
“Nope. I won’t,” she grinned.
“Heugh,” I sighed.
“Anyway, that’s enough teasing. I mean, I don’t want you to try to change seats. Also, if you ever change your mind about lunch, head over to my table, okay? See ya.”
Suzuki left for the cafeteria, swaying her hips left and right in a rhythmic motion, seductive actually.
I was looking with my tongue out like a happy dog, but stopped myself because people were staring.
Just how many times am I gonna embarrass myself?
I stood up to leave; however, on entering the cafeteria, all I could see were long lines.
Come on, man.
After getting a meal ticket, I got in a long line and just managed to get a meal, though I got a cheaper lunch set than last time.
It cost 5,000 Rp; even though the price difference between this and the last one was quite small—being just 3,000 Rp—I was at least saving money.
However, even when I got a meal and went to Hiro’s table, something unexpected happened.
The table was full of girls and had no free tables. How could it be so full in less than 5 minutes?
The girls at the table looked pleased with themselves, most being unable to hold their laughter.
They are literally snickering at my expense, so I don’t think it’s presumptuous of me to deem them the suspects.
I silently accepted defeat and left—I could see Hiro’s face of disappointment directed at the girls, but even he was powerless in the face of these girls.
He couldn’t just chase them away, though I still would have preferred for him to do something, but I suppose it was fine—after all, he was always such a good person.
Though aren’t those girls a bit too overzealous?
Anyway, I tried walking around looking for Suzuki’s table, all to no avail, but of course I kept looking around because I didn’t particularly enjoy standing and eating.
And here I thought I could depend on ‘the trustworthy and always reliable Hiro.’
I should have listened to Suzuki and gone to her table.
“Hey, why the hell are you cutting?!”
“Why would I wait for you to get your lunch first?”
“Huh? What kind of BS is that?”
“Hey, are you trying to complain? You’re supposed to just say yes and go along with it. It seems you lot have forgotten your place.”
I might be wrong since I haven’t seen everyone yet, but it seems students from my class are fighting people from another class.
Wait a minute. That boy from the opposing class—he’s the one I helped yesterday.
“Our place? Our place?! Whether you’re in Class Beta or not, that doesn’t mean you can’t cut the line,” said the representative for the people from my class who were in the line.
“Even students from Class Gamma and Delta are quiet, so why are you angry?”
“What’s that supposed to mean?!”
“I don’t have the energy to speak to an idiot like you, so can you quiet down?”
“This is—”
Before he could finish, his friend, Innō, or so Suzuki said yesterday, came and held him back as if to say it was not worth it.
In fact, my classmates were on the verge of giving up when a certain demeaning voice intervened.
“So, you’ll let them boss you around like that, huh? Maybe you really are an idiot. Well, they are in Class Epsilon, so no shock there.”
A lone voice spoke—not commanding, not demanding, just enough to throw fuel at the fire.
Those words made those around laugh—Wiagt, maybe it was better to say that the laughter spread around like a virus. It started with one person at different tables.
Almost like it was planned.
The students from my class stood in silence for a while until Innō, still trying to calm himself down, spoke up.
…Looks like it’s about to go down.
“Screw this shit,” Innō swore, punching the Class Beta student who talked to his friend.
That was enough, huh? Instigation, that is.
It’s a dangerous thing, a form of gaslighting, or rather, gaslighting in its truest form.
If you’re in a calm or normal state of mind, it’s useless, but if you’re even a little agitated, then it’s like matches that start a forest fire.
To a viewer not paying attention, this is completely random; however, it was planned.
Whoever did that probably did it intentionally.
I know what you’re thinking: just one word did it? Is Innō really that temperamental? Or is it the laughter? Hasn’t Innō been in Class Epsilon for a while? Is he a child?
No, I’d argue he was not.
Normally, just one word cannot really affect someone, especially someone who has been in a position for more than 2 months, but Innō was already angry and was just trying to be the bigger person.
And no one wants to feel small when trying to be the bigger person—especially when the one who is making you feel small is technically better than you.
You see, if it were done by one of our classmates, then Innō could probably overlook it; however, it was done by someone who is supposedly way better than us.
Because of this, it sprawled into a full-on fight, though it was mostly one-sided. Punches and kicks were thrown on each side, or so it seemed.
On closer inspection, it wasn’t just one-sided; it was a total beatdown. Class Beta didn’t attack back or even try to block.
It wasn’t like they were capable of handling it because they didn’t complain, as their faces were severely bruised from being a punching bag, so they seemed to have some physical conditioning.
It was almost like Class Beta was allowing themselves unless…
Is this part of some plan? Though unlikely, everything now feels too easy, almost intentional; however, I can’t conclude anything yet.
All I can do is wait and see.
After some time, my classmates stopped to catch their breath.
“Switching to violence? Typical for Class Epsilon. I mean, after all, you are a bunch of idiots,” a student from Class Beta managed to speak, his mouth severely bruised.
“It seems your brain needs more rattling. Just keep quiet, or you might end up in the hospital,” Innō’s friend voiced his exasperation.
“I will—”
“You won’t be doing anything of the sort. If you guys continue, then I will call a teacher,” a student between them said with a calm expression.
The student from Class Beta was cut short by a student, one not from my class and not from Class Beta.
It was Hayashi, the girl from the train—a student from Class Alpha.
Why is she intervening? I suppose it’s because of the fight, but is that her only reason, or is there another one?
No, there probably isn’t an ulterior motive, or it isn’t yet visible; perhaps it’s because she wants to apply for the student council.
Anyway, the fight created by Class Beta is very clearly a ploy, but why…?
After Hayashi spoke confidently, both Class Beta and Class Epsilon left quietly.
At any rate, the constant thinking made me lose my appetite and leave my food on the table. I went back to class, though on the way, I saw a vending machine that had a QR scanner.
Since I didn’t get lunch and would most likely get grumpy later, I got a canned drink. It was called Mitsuya Cider.
I stood by the wall and cracked open the drink; I was curious about how it tasted.
Gulp, gulp, gulp.
“Eugh!”
I took a deep sigh.
“Damn, it’s way too sweet.”
Anyway, you gotta love the point system of this school, or so your average high schooler would say.
Its structure makes it way too easy to spend, which should be counterproductive considering it's government-owned.
If every student got 100,000 Rp as I did, then they would be spending 75,000,000 yen for just expenditures, not to mention living expenses and other miscellaneous expenses.
This school is—
“Heugh,” I sighed, throwing my can in the trash can.
I don’t know what they are planning, and I probably won’t be able to find out, so it’s better if I just let it go.
After all, it's best to leave the exceptional cases to the exceptional and the average cases to the average.
“Damn, I’m already getting hungry. I should have just eaten my meal in class, shouldn’t I?”
