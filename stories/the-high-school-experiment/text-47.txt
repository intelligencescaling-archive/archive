Chapter 12.3: The Last Date.
Rustle, rustle, rustle.
Uh huh. Yeah, that’s right. Yup. It was definitely morning.
You see, I moved around on my bed, unable to get back to sleep. Basically, I was tired of sleeping, but also didn’t want to wake up.
As for the reason why I wasn’t tired despite it just being 7 AM, it was because I slept quite early yesterday, around 6 PM to be exact.
Despite being clearly awake, I kept turning around as I silently soaked up the cold air conditioning when I heard my alarm.
Ring, ring, ring!!!
My alarm blasted at me from my nightstand. Normally, I would be quick to wake up because of school, but today was a day of relaxation—it was the end of the weekend, Sunday.
Ring, ring, ring!!!
That being said, I didn’t exactly like the loud noise that rang in my ears, so I had to turn off my alarm.
“Ugh,” I slammed the alarm clock.
After ‘waking up’, I sat up, looking towards my window—moving my blinds—in an attempt to see the sun.
You see, my room probably had one of the best views in the morning (I assumed so), not that I noticed; I hardly ever looked at it, but it was a Sunday ritual I did since coming to this school.
At first, it was a mistake.
I accidentally set my alarm to wake me up at 7 AM (I still make that mistake to this day because I forget to remove the alarm on weekends), forgot to close my window the night before, and ended up seeing the view in the morning.
It was… special.
Probably.
I had never seen anything like that before, so of course I was speechless—it was like a certain bright light that woke me up every day.
Anyway, it became my Sunday ritual, albeit waking by 7 AM was still quite annoying. At any rate, after seeing the sun, I tried to assess what I was going to do today.
As you know, today was Sunday, and unlike other Sundays, I couldn’t laze around like a sloth. I had to go on a date today. I hadn’t prepared anything special to wear, nor did I really feel like going out.
“So, I’m free till Aiya calls me, huh?”
My day was probably going to be spent just lazing around. I knew I was going on a date, but I really didn’t have the energy to put any effort into it.
I was all tuckered out. That being said, since it was Aiya, I was willing to go the extra mile just for her. However, moving from my bed was still way too annoying for me to do.
Alas, I managed to push myself and move to the bathroom.
“FOR LOVE!!!”
I immediately cringed at my words, but still, I went to take a bath and brush my teeth. As I dried my hair, I thought of what I should do before my date with Aiya.
Wait, how could I plan what to do without even knowing when I would have the date?
“Should I just call and find out when? I mean, it’s normally in the morning or in the early afternoon, but even she has to rest at some point, so when? Yeah, I should call… Nah, I shouldn’t.”
The right thing to do was probably to wait until like 10 AM to find out when the date would occur, but waiting for 3 hours for an answer honestly felt impossible.
Texting her and waiting for an answer was probably the better alternative, as it gave her ample time to think and respond, but it turned this into a waiting game.
…What should I do? Hold up, aren’t I overthinking this way too much? What’s wrong with calling her to find out now? I make it seem like I’m doing something bad. I mean, she was the one who wanted the date, so there’s nothing wrong with my finding out when I should come.
In the end, due to my curiosity, I ended up calling Aiya.
“Yo.”
“Hey.”
From her voice, I could instantly tell that Aiya was just waking up—it was more than obvious when her words always bounced rather than moved through the air.
I silently wondered if I was too curious; maybe I should have just waited till a little later.
“What’s up? So, uh, I called to know when we’re having the date.”
“And you couldn’t just text that?”
“Well… I guess I was just, uh…”
I needed to come up with an excuse quickly. I didn’t want to seem like an insensitive piece of shit, so I rattled my brain for an answer quickly.
Nice!
“I just wanted to hear your voice, y’know,” I replied, barely skipping a beat.
“Huh? Uh, well, you should have said so,” I couldn’t be sure, but I had a feeling Aiya blushed at my words—seems like the plan worked.
“Though I must say those words feel a tad bit insincere, Eiya-kun,” she emphasised my name. Her face behind the phone was scary, probably.
Aiya’s words made my heart skip a beat, and not in the good way. I was scared shitless, so I told the truth.
“Uh, well…. I’m sorry, I was just impatient.”
“Eugh,” she sighed. “I knew it was too good to be true.”
“I’m sorry,” I bowed, although she couldn’t see me.
“It’s fine, “ I stood up at those words. “So, is 5 PM okay with you?”
“Yeah, that’s fine.”
“Alright, I’ll see you then.”
With that done, I was free to enjoy my day peacefully. I wasn’t planning on going anywhere today (well, not until 5 PM), so I was going to live leisurely.
“So, till 5 pm I’m free, huh? What should I do? Should I just laze around or…”
I had a full day to myself and couldn’t quite choose what to do, so instead I chose to spoil myself a bit. I mean, I deserved it, especially after achieving my goal.
At first, I planned to use today to learn about the places not on the map or to get the full map, but then I changed my mind—I was going to enjoy myself to the fullest.
So, how do I spoil myself?
Many thoughts came to my head—I could order food from a store, go on a walk, or just get ice cream.
“Or I could just do everything,” I grinned.
After dressing up casually, I went to a restaurant at the mall, ordering a 5-course meal.
“That’ll be 23, 000 Rp.”
“Alright,” I gave a wide smile to the waiter.
Normally, I would have been alarmed at the price, but today I paid everything peacefully and tried to finish the meal—of course, I couldn’t and took the remaining food home for dinner.
After using the restroom and resting for a bit, I went for a leisurely walk around the school area. I noticed some people around.
Maybe they came to study with their study groups? But then wouldn’t they just go to the library?
Well, I couldn’t be sure, and it wasn’t like I was friends with anyone outside my class, so I couldn’t just ask. After walking around enough, I went back to the convenience store near the dorms to finally get ice cream.
It was the same flavour as yesterday.
“So good,” I held my cheek, savouring the flavour. “Should I go get some mo—”
Ring, ring, ring.
“Ugh! It’s time already?”
I set a timer for 4:35 PM so I could remember my date with Aiya. After noticing the alarm, I ran to the bus station and came to the amusement park around 5 pm.
On arrival, I waited outside for her. I had a feeling she would be arriving soon—I was right; I found her just getting off a bus.
She probably used this day to rest so she would be free during the evening.
As she came down from the bus, I noticed Aiya was engrossed in her phone; she appeared to be texting someone while muttering something.
“Yo,” I said, walking over to her.
“Hey Eiya, how are you doing?” she grinned. She didn’t seem the slightest bit surprised that I came up to her suddenly.
“I’m good. What about you?”
“Oh, me? I’m super excited.”
“So, what was the urgent thing you wanted to talk about?”
“Let’s finish the date first.”
Don’t tell me she just wanted to have a date and needed a reason to get me here.
“Heugh,” I sighed because that was a very strong possibility.
I suppose it’s fine because I don’t think I’ll ever get tired of seeing Aiya.
The amusement park was alive with energy. Bright lights blinked rhythmically, music drifted through the cool evening air, and the excited chatter of students and even some staff blended into an almost magical atmosphere.
As we stopped at the gates, I glanced over at Aiya. She was already grinning, her eyes darting from one ride to the next like a kid in a candy store.
“This place is huge,” she said, brushing a strand of her hair out of her face. “Where should we start?”
“You’re the one who wanted to come; you decide,” I shrugged.
“How about…the rollercoaster?” She grinned.
“Sure.”
The line for the rollercoaster snaked around metal barriers, the sounds of the ride roaring overhead. Aiya’s excitement was contagious, even if I wasn’t particularly moved by the sight of the towering steel tracks.
“You’re not nervous, right?” She teased, leaning in with a sly smile.
“Why would I be?” I replied calmly.
“Good,” she said. “Because this is going to be intense.”
When it was finally our turn, she practically dragged me into the front seat. The safety bar clicked into place, and the ride began its slow ascent.
Aiya leaned forward, her hands gripping the bar, her pink hair in the wind. “Look at this view!!!”
“Yeah,” I said, glancing down. The height didn’t faze me—I haven’t really been afraid for a long time, since I was seven.
Or so I would like it to be. I was just desensitised to this basic stuff—my parents were strict after all.
As the rollercoaster plunged, I let out a carefully timed yell, just enough to blend in with the screams around us. Aiya’s laughter rang out beside me, pure and unrestrained, as the ride twisted and turned through loops and drops.
When we finally stumbled off, she turned to me, her eyes shining. “That was incredible! You screamed so loud!”
“Did I now? I have no memory of that,” I said, raising an eyebrow.
“Don’t even try to deny it,” she said, poking my arm. “I heard you.”
I smirked; it seemed I had fooled her.
After the rollercoaster, Aiya insisted on grabbing snacks. We found a food stall selling churros and hot chocolate, the smell of cinnamon and sugar wafting through the air.
“Here,” Aiya said, handing me a churro. “Don’t just stand there, try it.”
I really like trying all this new food. Before now, I had only seen them eat this. They were kind enough to tell me the name of the snack—churros.
I took a bite, the warm sweetness filling my mouth. “Not bad.”
“Not bad?” She repeated, her eyes narrowing. “It’s amazing, admit it.”
She must really love churros. It’s good, but I would still prefer ice cream, preferably a popsicle, to this.
I guess they were wrong about one thing: Churros aren’t the best snacks in the world.
Well, it was probably hyperbole, but still, I wonder how many things they were wrong about.
“Alright, it’s good,” I said flatly.
She laughed, taking a sip of her hot chocolate. “Relax, you’re too serious.”
“I hear you.”
Next up was the haunted house, a dimly lit maze filled with actors dressed as ghosts, zombies, and various other horrors.
“Are you sure you’re ready for this?” Aiya asked, her tone playful.
“Are you?” I countered, holding the door for her.
The first scare came almost immediately—a bloodied zombie lurching out from the shadows.
Aiya screamed, clutching my arm, but her laughter followed just as quickly.
“That was so fake!” she said, though her grip on my sleeve didn’t loosen.
“Pretty obvious,” I agreed, though I kept my expression neutral.
I don’t really like horror, though it’s not necessarily because it’s scary.
I just don’t like the idea of intentionally making myself scared; it’s kinda stupid. And when I actually get scared, what do I gain? A higher blood pressure? A scrambled amygdala?
It really is so… stupid.
As we moved through the maze, Aiya alternated between screaming at jump scares and laughing at herself for being startled.
I played along, flinching when appropriate, though nothing in the house was remotely frightening.
By the end, Aiya was grinning from ear to ear. “That was so much fun!”
“Glad you enjoyed it.”
She raised an eyebrow. “You didn’t get scared at all, did you?”
“Of course I did,” I said, keeping my voice light.
“Liar.”
It seems I have been caught, though you can’t expect me to be scared by such low-effort scares!
In fact, no one here was remotely scared of anything aside from the rides, and that was because, no matter how sturdy they were, they could fail, so it was not due to the ride itself.
Well, it was called an amusement park, not a “fear park”.
Anyway, the night was winding down when we reached the Ferris wheel, its colourful lights reflecting off the nearby water.
Before coming here, I didn’t think I would be able to do any of this—it feels so surreal.
It seems I’ve reached my goal; I have friends and even a girlfriend who loves me. I can even have fun.
My thoughts brought a grand smile to my face as I looked around the amusement park.
Why was I happy?
It wasn’t obvious? My goal had been achieved! In a few short weeks, it had been achieved. Nakamura Eiya was happy; yes, Nakamura Eiya was happy.
My smile faltered at those words.
My goal has been achieved, but…
I still feel the same way.
I have not grown at all; everything is still the same. I constantly have to delude myself just to even feel normal.
I’m sure if I give it my all, I can become normal, but would that be normalcy? No, it won’t. It would just be me acting, nothing more.
“To be normal is to act as you were made,” I read those lines in a book—it was an engineer talking to the robot he had made, trying to explain that authenticity equals normality.
That being yourself will eventually lead to normalcy. However, just like the robot cannot be itself because that person is not someone who can be considered a normal human, I also can’t be myself.
Honestly speaking, I don’t understand why I can’t do it.
Is it that I haven’t tried enough?
I have!
Am I unable to change?
Yes!
Am I still tied to there?
…Yes!
So, then why can’t I change? Why…? …Well, it’s me we’re talking about.
Despite what I do, I’m still that boy, that person. I cannot leave that cage—I suppose I’m stuck in that bastard institute.
I’m still the same—the same person from 6 years ago—the same Nakamura Eiya.
Despite everything, I’m still—
“This is the perfect way to end the night,” Aiya stopped my internal monologue as we climbed into one of the cabins.
The wheel began its slow ascent, lifting us above the park. From the top, the entire amusement park stretched out below us, a dazzling mix of lights and motion.
Aiya leaned forward; her hands pressed against the glass. “It’s beautiful.”
“Yeah,” I said, though I was watching her, not the view.
Regardless of how I feel, I still have to focus on Aiya now. I have a feeling she still has something to tell me.
“Right?” she said, leaning back in her seat, noticing I was looking at her and not the view. “W-W-Wait, you were talking about me?”
Aiya blushed at the realisation, her cheeks as red as raspberries.
I nodded, indicating that she was right.
“So, why’d you want to go on a date today?”
“Oh, that,” her expression changed. “I want to break up,” she looked outside the window.
…
..
.
Eh?
“Eh?”
The sudden shift made it seem like time had stopped. I couldn’t understand why. What did I do wrong?
No, that wasn’t the right question. I mean, I did a lot of stuff wrong, but didn’t we get through it?
Or did I mess up something?
What? Isn’t that way too sudden?
“Why?”
“You’re too boring, and—” Her voice was cold—too cold. Then, just as she turned away, she paused for a moment. “Never mind.”
I tried to rationalise what was going on, as I couldn’t even comprehend it.
Let’s calm down a bit. Why’s Aiya acting like this? Did I piss her off?
Maybe I did because, to be honest, I’ve never heard her talk this way. She always sounds playful, so the juxtaposition brings a shock; it almost sounds deceptive.
“Elaborate.”
“You act like a textbook that you won’t allow me to read.”
…Her words mirror my real personality.
Hey, Nakamura Eiya, should I… just let this one go? Have I been too inconsiderate of this girl’s feelings, with Ai—with Suzuki’s feelings?
“…”
“Moreover, I don’t want to be in a relationship with a person like you.”
I see… Well, maybe we can remain friends after this, so I can one day change my ways.
“…”
“In conclusion, you’re completely and utterly bland.”
In an instant, my relationship with Aiya ended—ironic, how our relationship started and ended with the word “bland”. We sat in uncomfortable silence as the Ferris wheel completed its slow descent.
For a moment, the world felt distant, the noise of the park muted by the height.
Aiya silently left when the Ferris wheel came down, before she was out of earshot; I spoke.
“See you tomorrow, Suzuki.”
“…”
Now that we weren’t dating, I didn’t want to call her by her surname, so people wouldn’t misunderstand and think that we were dating anymore.
The walk back to the dorms was peaceful. I enjoyed the cool breeze.
On my way back, I saw a classmate—that same purple-haired girl I saw outside the window on the first day—in the bushes. Her name was Yumemiya Aikawa, and she also sat in front of me.
I went to see what was up. The thing is, if I were caught, I couldn’t really explain what I was doing.
Well, I was doing this because I saw Aiya texting and muttering to someone before our date.
I didn’t wanna ask because it could have been weird, but with how we broke up, it may have been a friend she was talking to—perhaps it was this Yumemiya.
Who knows? At any rate, I was curious.
“Aiya has done her part. What’s left is to get my class’s student records.”
Student record?
I was slightly rattled, but my mind immediately shifted to who the person she was on the phone with was.
I wonder who she’s talking to, I mean. I doubt she’s talking to herself.
At any rate, at this distance, I should be able to hear the person she’s speaking to.
“Good work; I trust you’ll be able to handle everything,” an unknown voice replied. The voice was like that of a chipmunk—ahh, the person Yumememiya was speaking to was using some kind of voice changer.
This person is very cautious, but she’s literally in the bushes, so who’ll eavesdrop? I mean, I am, but that’s a coincidence.
“Yup, trust in me,” Yumemiya giggled.
Well, they seem close.
She was about to leave, so I went to my room. I spent some time at night thinking about what I had witnessed.
Well, I wasn’t sad or anything. I was just surprised as well, though I planned to get an answer out of Aiya the next day—that is, about why she broke up with me.
I walked to school the same way I always did, but today was a little different when I walked into class—there was no Ito or Aiya, just me.
However, on my way to school, my classmates gave me nasty stares. Almost like I had done something unforgivable, like stealing a girl’s underwear.
It felt honestly weird. What was going on?
I didn’t really put any mind to it and headed to class. When I arrived in class, the stares intensified, with murmurs going around.
What in the hell is actually going on?
Despite the stares, I managed to walk to my seat. On reaching my seat, I noticed that Suzuki was crying, so I went to try to calm her down until—
Yoshida slapped my hand away before I could touch Aiya.
“How dare you try to touch her after what you did?”
What I did? What is she talking about?
The murmur in the class only grew more and more until Ito walked up to me. He had his head down as he slowly approached, and when he did reach me, his eyes looked wary as he gulped before speaking.
Why’s he so nervous?
His nervousness also made me nervous, and of course, the cherry on the cake was the way he addressed me without an honorific.
“Nakamura…”
“...Yeah?
“...Did you try to rape Aiya?”
What?
