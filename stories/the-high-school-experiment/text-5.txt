Chapter 0.4: Who Is This Guy?
I left the cafeteria with a clear goal—to make some friends. The question was how?
I was planning on finding people to meet, but there was no clear way to do that—no one was in the hallway, and heading back to the cafeteria would be weird.
Maybe it’s where I’m searching that’s the problem.
I tried taking a stroll around school, but there was barely anyone, and most were already in a conversation.
“Heugh,” I sat down on a bench. “Talk about a hard first day; well, at least I got to see the campus a bit.”
I went around the school and found a library, a few special buildings, and an administrative block.
“That took me well over 10 minutes, so I think it’s safe to say that the school grounds are quite big.”
It was then I felt it. I felt it coming—it was a storm. A very big storm. That being said, there was a way to alleviate this storm. Yes, there was a solution. And it could only be solved in one way.
“I need to use the restroom.”
I clutched my stomach and ran to the nearest restroom, using the map on the phone to guide me.
And… I promptly used it.
“Perhaps rushing through coffee with a bagel isn’t the best way to start a day,” I retorted as I flushed the toilet. “Especially if you eat omurice next.”
I didn’t get a good look at the toilet when I rushed in, but I could tell it was quite pristine for a public bathroom.
Though internally rating a toilet feels a bit weird.
I headed out of the toilet, feeling satisfied, when I felt a vibration from my pocket.
Hmm…?
I pulled out my phone and saw that I received a message that all transfer students should go to the school hall for a school orientation.
There was no reason not to, nor did I want to, so I headed there right away.
Getting there wasn’t really hard because I had the map of the school.
The school hall was quite a distance from the first-year school block, so a bunch of students walked together.
How is everyone already so chummy?! This is like, so not fair.
As I silently lamented my lack of friends, I noticed the girl from the train—she wasn’t alone, though; she came with two girls.
Wow, so she made friends already! She’s a fast worker.
On entering the hall, I went toward the front row, and while looking for a seat, I saw the blonde boy from the train.
“Hey,” I said, sitting beside the blonde-haired boy. “This seat taken?”
“No,” He looked up from his phone. “Oh, hey, you’re from that speech at the train station, right? Well, everyone here heard the speech, but y’know what I mean, right? Anyway, n-n-nice to meet you.”
He’s fumbling his words? Wait, don’t tell me he’s a loner like me? Nice.
I had an internal sigh of relief at his words because I was actually nervous to talk to him.
After all, he looked like an ikemen with his dyed blonde hair and his nose and ear piercings—2 helix piercings on both ears—along with his 3 silver necklaces and 5 rings on each arm, but perhaps he was just trying to become more extroverted.
Well, he totally looked the part.
“Also, no offence, but what’s your name? Sorry that I didn’t ask before now.”
“Nah, no problem. It’s Nakamura Eiya; what’s yours?”
“I’m Kisaragi Renjiro.”
“Oh, ok. Anyway, do you think that man who addressed us in the morning is going to do the orientation?”
“I hope so. I’ve been thinking about buying a mirror, and he looks like the perfect one.”
“Mirror? I thought it was the sun.”
“Pff ha ha.”
“Ha ha ha.”
We laughed quietly before an awkward silence settled in. Kisaragi went back to his phone to avoid the awkwardness.
It seems like neither of us is a conversationalist—well, that’s what you get when you’re both loners.
I also played a bit with my phone, but it was drier than the Sahara Desert.
“Heugh.”
Anyway, after some silence, a figure entered the room. I didn’t pay much attention to it as I patiently waited for the principal to address us, but instead of the principal stepping up, a male student walked to the stage.
As he walked, his presence filled the room as we looked at him because of his striking features:
Violet hair that was darker at its root but gradually became brighter at its tip, dyed slightly with a bluish hue, alongside peach-coloured eyes.
He had fair skin that looked natural, and his hair also appeared natural despite its weird tint.
He also looked like a Gaikokujin as well as an ikemen; it wasn’t an understatement to compare his looks to a model or a J-pop star.
“Such a cutie!”
“He’s so hot!”
“Is he a model or what?!”
He knew this and walked rather slowly and deliberately to emphasise his features.
He dressed rather casually despite wearing his entire uniform, but there was something that stood out.
He wore a maroon shirt, a brown blazer with white accents, and brown trousers.
He’s an upperclassman.
He walked up to the podium with a confident look on his face before he began to speak.
“Hello, everyone. I am Kaneko Katsu, and I’m the president of the student council. I was given this position for a sole reason; does anyone know what it is?”
There was some silence till a lone girl raised her hand to answer; she was subsequently given a mic.
“Is it populari—”
“No, next person,” he cut her off before she was done.
Another person was given a mic just a moment later.
“Maybe it’s influen—”
“No, next person.”
Another was promptly given a mic.
“Is it because you were vice-presi—”
“Next person.”
This pattern continued for a while. People mentioned just about everything—going from physical and mental to even religious attributes.
“This guy is so full of himself.”
“Like for real.”
“Yeah, we get we don’t know what made you president, not like we care anyway.”
“Like, look at him; he’s probably having the time of his life. I won’t be surprised if he says the answer is luck.”
What is this guy getting at?
Judging from the comments here and there, people were getting more and more agitated, though he completely ignored it, just keeping a sly grin.
“It seems no one can answer. I expected more from this batch; when I asked the first term’s first years, they answered quite quickly. To be honest, I’m really disappointed.”
“...”No one had an answer; almost all the known options were exhausted, and most people were past the point of caring.
Honestly, I was too, and expected this segment of the speech to end when he realised that his stunt wasn’t exactly fun; well, that was until a voice spoke.
“Pardon me, but,” the girl from the train spoke up. “Can I try?”
“Please do.”
“...Is it merit?”
There was a silence after she spoke. The silence was deafening as Satou stared at her with a grin that slowly grew wider and wider.
“That is correct. Merit or value is the most important thing here; never forget that. I understood that, so I made myself the most valuable person on campus—I excelled in all sectors, whether physical, social, mental, or academic, so when the election came, it wasn’t a surprise that I won by a landslide. You see, people only support those with promise, and those who show promise don’t do it by accident—they have a goal, a reason.
I’m sure you are all here for one reason or another; you want something—to be frank, I don’t really care. However, what I do care about are students who have value.
Fight with your life for what you came here for—because when all else fails, your goals are all you have.
But notwithstanding, you are aware of the school you’re in; it is a special school. Therefore, the only conclusion is that to exist here, you must become special,” his voice was calm, yet it cut through us even sharper than any knife.
Some people thought he was egotistic, some thought he was having fun at their expense, some thought he was a weirdo, and others thought he was just passing the time; however, at this moment, no one made a side comment but courted him with their unbridled attention.
Even someone as lackadaisical as I was was listening to the point where I felt somewhat motivated.
Really impressive!
“Now, why am I saying this? Why am I motivating you all with my words? It’s simple. I would like to have a successor, someone who will come to claim my position. This applies to you all, even though you’re in your first year. I want you all to try to surpass me, but to do that, you must, of course, join the student council,” he said. “I advise you all to try to apply, as there is no special qualification for candidacy except for not joining any other club activities.
However, if you even have a second thought, don’t have enough courage, or have a weak self-image, I suggest you don’t attempt to join us.
Besides the fact that you won’t be selected, I don’t want any naïve fool who thinks they have merit to come and do nothing, sullying the school’s image in the process.
We, as the student council, have very important roles in this school—we make and interpret rules. Meaning that there’s a lot of external pressure from the school, so I’m sure you can guess why such a person cannot survive in that environment.
Now, my beloved kouhai, come and show me.
Show you have enough value, enough merit. Make me respect you. Now, thank you for your time,” he said, spreading his hands out with a smile.
His speech was strategic yet adaptive; it was most likely not planned, and if it was planned, it was more impressive.
“This guy… He must have wanted to put us on an emotional rollercoaster.”
Well said. That guy really is something.
His speech moved in such a way that a normal one couldn’t. He drove all of us on a rollercoaster—from making us agitated to making us inspired.
Truly impressive, Kaneko.
As the student council president left, a huge number of people followed, probably motivated by his words, though not everyone was as impressed.
“I’m sure he got voted for cuz he’s eye candy.”
“You don’t get to look that good and talk about value.”
In a way, these people were right; Kaneko probably had an easier time winning the election because he was particularly eye-catching, but even then, your looks would only take you so far.
Well, it was only people who were not that attractive to begin with that had that sentiment. Then again, those that genuinely didn’t care would also not give much mind to Kaneko’s speech.
But that was expected, and I had a feeling that Kaneko knew that; he probably didn’t plan to sway everyone but to convince further those who had large ambitions.
Well, that’s just what I think, at least.
Anyway, as he was leaving, I heard a girl talking behind me—the girl was a friend of the girl from the train and was talking to her.
“Hayashi-chan, why don’t you join the student council?”
So, the girl from the train’s name is Hayashi, huh?
“I’m not cut out for it. You heard what he said. If you don’t have enough courage, you shouldn’t join.”
“Don’t listen to him; he probably just wants to scare off slackers.”
“Umm… I don’t know. Do you really think I have what it takes?”
“Yes, I mean, you were the only one able to answer his question.”
“That was a fluke, but… Maybe I’ll give it a shot. No promises, though,” she chuckled.
“I’m sure you will get in,” her friend convinced her.
“Alright then, I’ll go meet Kaneko-senpai.”
Hayashi stood up with renewed resolve and went to meet Kaneko along with her friends.
So, she’s that easy to convince. I expected some more back and forth. Also, why convince someone who doesn’t want the job?
I know that she was the only one to answer the question, but as the student council president said, the first years from the first term got it way faster.
I’m sure there are other intelligent people, so her competition will be immense, though maybe she’s more capable than she seems.
After the event ended, Kisaragi stood up to leave, and I followed suit.
But as I left, someone seated at the front with a maroon shirt looked at me with prying eyes as if watching my every move.
Was he looking at me? Nah, what will he want to do with me anyway? Also, it’s just now I noticed that Kaneko is just in his second year!
After we left the hall, Kisaragi abruptly stopped walking and then turned around to look at me.
Huh? What happened?
After some seconds of silence, Kisaragi spoke up.
“So do you want my number?”
“Uhh… Sure.”
Number?
“Sorry, I meant account name. This system is kinda weird sometimes,” he laughed nervously. “Ahem. Anyway, if you want it, then the name’s Kizzy.”
“Uh, sure, I’ll add you right now.”
I hurriedly searched for his username in an attempt to add him.
This is weird and awkward… I’m sure if I were Hiro, this would be a breeze.
I really need to talk to people more.
“Got it,” I showed him.
“Yeah. So see you later.”
I wonder, why’d he just tell me his account name? Maybe he also doesn’t have friends, but does this make us friends?
Guess not. Anyway, what kind of nickname is Kizzy?
At any rate, I got my first account name.
