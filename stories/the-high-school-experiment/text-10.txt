Chapter 1.3: Narcissists Suck
Although I was rather annoyed with my predicament of having to stay for swim class, the class went on without a hitch.
After we finished the required drills, I sat silently, still fuming about being there as I watched my classmates play in the pool. You see, since we technically finished class, we could do whatever we wanted.
Wait a damn minute? Doesn’t that mean …?!!!!
It was in my annoyance that I reached a moment of clarity—until I didn’t have to be there.
Can’t believe I just forgot I have free will.
Heugh! What a drag. I’ve been here for what? Five minutes? Talk about wasted time—well, in my defence, sometimes I forget I can actually just do whatever I want.
I’m sure from my words alone you can imagine how my parents were. I wasn’t maltreated or anything, but… sometimes my thinking can be considered a bit weird.
…Yeah, I suppose that kind of way of thinking can be considered weird.
Anyway, time to go to class. I mean, it’s not like I have anything that I’m doing here.
I was about to leave for class, but then I turned to look at the pool for a while and saw some smiles, and I kinda wanted to see what was going on.
At first, I wasn’t interested, but my curiosity sat me down to watch. I eventually found out that some guys wanted to do a race.
Not gonna do anything in class since my phone’s still pretty dry, so maybe watching this can alleviate some boredom.
Well, that was my plan at least; however, something unexpected happened.
“Hey, Nakamura-kun, wanna join in?” Ito asked.
“Huh? Uh, sure.”
He used the ‘-kun’ honorific instead of ‘-san’; he’s becoming more friendly towards me!
Yes, this is a chance to make friends; I shouldn’t throw it away!
Ito brought me into the competition; it was an attestation to his goodwill, and despite the situation before now, he was surprisingly calm.
It was surprising, but I just humbly headed to the area where the boys were.
“Ito, get ready to lose,” Innō stated proudly.
“Are you sure you’ll win?” Ito asked, his face calm.
“Sure as ever.”
“Nah, I’d win,” Inoue butted in.
“So you do read manga?” Another contestant retorted.
The other contestants were bantering among themselves; however, one of them was just sitting by himself.
He had light brown hair that almost covered his eyes, had a silver ring on his right hand, and had a brown pendant.
He also appeared to be mixed—his appearance was somewhat similar to a European’s, so perhaps that’s what one of his parents was. I wasn’t sure because I didn’t ask, but he probably had mixed roots.
I mean, his first name was Amadeus.
Anyway, I walked over to him because there was something I wanted to learn about before starting.
“Yo.”
“…”
“Uh, hello?”
“…Oh, were you speaking to me?”
No, I was talking to my mom… Heugh.
“Yeah, I have a question,” I ignored the impolite thought in my head.
“Sorry about that. Your name’s Nakamura Eiya, right? I’m sure you heard my name during class, but mine’s Arakawa Amadeus. About my silence, well, I wasn’t really paying attention because I was lost in thought. Sorry again,” Arakawa politely replied.
Damn, now I feel like a jackass for my earlier thoughts.
“If you don’t mind me asking, what were you thinking about?”
“Oh, nothing much. Just how extremely exuberant this situation is.”
“Oh, really? Why?”
“Oh, it wasn’t obvious?” Arakawa was shocked at my question. “It’s because my graciousness is performing in it.”
Hmm…?
“So, the situation is good because you’re in it?”
“Precisely. Want me to tell you more?” He seemed genuinely excited to continue.
I completely take back what I said—this guy is so self-obsessed it’s no wonder he didn’t hear me.
“Yeah, go on,” I said sarcastically.
“I am the most perfect in the world, and because of such, my presence is so wonderful that it is a miracle that I grace you with it. Aren’t you euphoric just by speaking to me?”
Does this guy not know what sarcasm is?
“Yeah,” I lied. “But can I ask you a question?”
“Hmm. Oh, I suppose I may allow it; do go on.”
“What do you think is the average swimming time?”
“It’s 20 seconds,” he answered without missing a beat.
“You sure?”
“Positive.”
“Well, if you’re sure.”
“Now back to me…”
He immersed me in all of how perfect he is, telling me about how perfect his daily schedule is and how he lives his life. He made sure to mention how everything he did was “paramount”.
It continued until—
“I’m sure you all know the rules, but I’ll go over them again because of Nakamura-kun. This is a swim race where the winner—that is, the person with the fastest swim time—gets 10,000 Rp, while the last two must pay 5,000 Rp each. Now, since everyone is ready, let’s begin.”
A male student from my class was the announcer; everyone in class was paying attention.
My saviour!
At last, I could leave that egotistical prick, who just went on and on and on and on.
I was sure that if not for this interruption by my saviour, Arakawa would have gone on forever.
However, I didn’t hear about the losers paying before now, this badly. I really don’t want to lose any Rp.
This is why you should listen to the terms and conditions before doing anything.
Heugh.
I sighed internally because I was quite bad at swimming—so bad that I would have to push myself just to get an average score.
Why’d I join again?
We lined up horizontally, ready to start. The people participating were Ito, Inoue, Innō, Takashiro, Kurosawa, Arakawa, and I—the last three were names I had just learnt during swim class.
“We’re starting in 3, 2, 1—Go!!!”
We dived right into the pool, though I started late as I was lost in thought about something.
With the race started, I put all my mind into it. I was swimming at quite a reasonable pace, focusing on stamina rather than speed, but then I noticed something strange.
…Huh?
I didn’t mind it, though, and just continued swimming. I kept up until Innō passed me, and then so did Inoue, then Ito, and then Arakawa.
I ended up finishing the race in fifth, the position just above the penalty. A completely average position.
I think that was good enough, about 20 seconds.
However, Innō, Inoue, and Ito tied, so the race had to be a tiebreaker. I sat on the sidelines to watch them do their race.
So Arakawa didn’t make it, huh?
In the second race, Innō dusted the opposition, or should I say drowned them, considering it was in water?
Innō won the entire event, getting 10,000 Rp; I just sat back admiring his athleticism.
Wow, this guy’s amazing. He’s the thorough definition of a jock, though I expected Inoue to win, considering he’s muscular.
After the race, everyone went to play in the pool, as we still had time left; I remained on the bench.
“Now pay up.”
“L-Let’s talk about this.”
“Nope,” Innō put Takashiro and Kurosawa in a headlock with one hand each.
“Dude!”
Everyone laughed as Innō ‘joked’ with his friends, though I was sure no one would want to be in their position.
After they paid, everyone went back to what they were doing before, and the boys continued to play, though Arakawa was out of sight.
“Dude, my muscles are way bigger.”
“Your arm is a literal twig.”
“Look at my abs; abs matter more.”
“Just cuz you’ve got a rock for a stomach doesn’t make you better.”
“You’re one to talk.”
They were arguing about who had bigger muscles. However, that wasn’t what I was focusing on; I watched intently at a certain gender—the female gender.
While ogling is wrong, there were times a man couldn't help himself.
This was one of those times.
Wow, their busts are amazing, especially Suzuki’s. Damn, I should be down there with them, shouldn’t I?
Yeah, I should go there—
“Nakamura-kun~” a female voice called.
“Huh? Hello.”
A group of girls came up to me; the person who spoke was someone whose name I didn’t know.
“Hey, so uh—don’t worry. Guys, let’s just go.”
One of the girls left visibly disappointed, but the question was, ‘at what?’
“Uh, sorry about that; we just, uh, forgot why we came. Yeah?”
“Yeah, sorry for wasting your time.”
The two others, being more polite, apologised and left.
Huh? What the hell just happened?
The girls left just as soon as they came.
“Did you see his face?”
“Yeah, he looks like my little brother. I can’t believe I almost wanted to get his acc. name.”
“Like for real. Damn, why’s he so well-built with such a cute face? He makes me feel like asking out a baby. I didn’t have the gall to ask for his account; I might look like a creep.”
It seemed the girls came to meet me because my body looked well-defined, but they got discouraged when they saw my face.
“Heugh.”
Body shaming at its finest.
“Baby-face-kun,” Arakawa came from nowhere.
“Huh?”
“Like the nickname? I didn’t want it to be imposing yet too disrespectful. Anyway, I find you interesting; you can now feel honoured,” he grinned widely.
“Uh…” I stared at him with a confused look.
“I’m saying that I know what you did in the race.”
“I’m confused,” I said so outright since it seemed he couldn’t tell from my expression—well, he was kind of obnoxious.
“You obviously rigged your scores; anyone with eyes can tell that with ease.”
“Why would I intentionally rig my own scores?”
“To appear average.”
“Why would anyone do that?”
“I don’t care enough to listen to your lies. I’ll just tell you what you did.”
“I see,” I said. “Fine, then just humour me and sit down.”
“But of course, someone as perfect as me shouldn’t exert energy when he doesn’t need to.”
“…”
Must everything you say be obnoxious?
He sat down beside me with a confident smile as I still stared at him.
“So, you asked me the average swimming time because you wanted to fake your scores, right?”
“Nope.”
“You didn’t want the average time, but rather, you wanted the time that would seem average—and you asked me specifically because I was by myself and seemed distant.”
“Not at all.”
“After that, you were also thinking about how to reduce your speed; that’s why you started late. Moreover, you didn’t join that barbaric showcase of muscle because you wanted to appear average.”
“No, not really.”
I answered each question nonchalantly, but I could tell Arakawa didn’t believe a single word, though nothing I said was a lie; he was actually wrong—very, very wrong indeed.
Ring, ring, ring.
“Seems like it’s time for class. Well, I cannot have late attendance, so I’ll see you later, liar-boy.”
“…Will you tell anyone this?”
“No, why would I concern myself with such matters? I am way above all that.”
“Then why tell me all this?”
“It’s simple; so simple that I’m surprised you don’t know the answer.”
“How about you humour me again, and just tell me the answer?”
“It’s for you to remember that I’m above you,” he said, walking away still with a smile. “Can’t have you thinking you’re above me.”
“If that’s so, why did you fail intentionally?”
“…” His smile faltered.
Perfect.
I noticed something during the race; Arakawa was also holding back because he was constantly fluctuating between slow and fast, like he wanted to stay within a range.
I couldn’t understand why someone so obviously egotistical would hold back. It seemed unthinkable to me.
“You think I would overexert myself for this? As long as I don’t lose, then it’s ok. Goodbye, liar-boy.”
Those were his last words before leaving for class. I stared at his back till he left before turning away.
Meh, perhaps I did it too early.
Also, what’s with the stupid nicknames? Ugh!
“Heugh,” I let out a sigh before heading to the locker room to change promptly and heading over to class.
After that, the day went in a flash, and it was finally time to go back to the dorms, and I could rest—or so I wanted.
Well, it was inevitable that this would happen. I’m sure you guessed it, but Ito brought up the case again, this time asking a question that required the total attention of all my classmates because, of course, he did.
