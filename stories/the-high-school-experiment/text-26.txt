Aiya-chan? Oh, she must be referring to Suzuki, as Aiya is her first name.

This girl really has a bad habit of cutting people off mid-sentence when she’s angry. You can’t understand because you never let me finish.
“I don’t need to understand anything; you don’t care about calligraphy and just came because of Aiya-chan,” she glared.
What’s going on?
“…” I kept quiet, staring blankly at Matsumoto.
“I’m sure you probably think calligraphy is dumb, right? I’m not surprised; even my friends say it’s useless and boring. So feel free. Say it.”
This girl is just coming to conclusions—no, she’s reiterating what people have told her.
I could tell what was going on. Matsumoto was constantly shot down by her friends and those around her, so she tried to keep anyone out before they could do that to her again.
“…” I kept the same expression.
“Why aren’t you saying anything?” She asked, almost about to cry, her face down.
…Seems like this really got to her. I can’t believe it was to the point of tears.
“…I don’t think it’s dumb.”
“Huh?” She looked up.
“I said I don’t think calligraphy is dumb.”
“Are you—No, that’s a lie to trick me, isn’t it? I’m appalled that you think such a thing would work on me,” her words and expression didn’t line up; it looked like she wanted to believe me but had to reject me.
The door to her heart was open; all I had to do was step in. I was lucky it ended up like this; if not, I would have had to use a more roundabout method.
Yeah, I was lucky she was an emotional girl—it was honestly ironic how she was very delicate yet called me delicate when we first met.
“You’re wrong, Matsumoto. Calligraphy is a wonderful art that I adore. Although I’ve never tried it before, I really respect it,” I spoke the truth; there were no lies in my words.
Even before coming here, I always adored it and wanted to attempt it, even just once.
But I was unable to because of the circumstances surrounding how I lived my life.
Such distractions were shunned, but I still wanted to try it despite that, and that was why I expressed my true emotions to Matsumoto—I wanted them to reach her.
“You’re just saying that so I won’t get hurt,” she didn’t seem to see those emotions.
I have to be more proactive.
“If you don’t want to leave, then I’ll leave,” she turned to leave until—
I caught her hand, holding it gently yet firmly enough for her not to leave. When she stopped trying to leave, I let go of her hand. I didn’t need to force her to stay; my words would reach her, probably.
You’re trying to close the door to your heart; I’ll forcefully pry it open.
“I really do want to experience calligraphy, and I do not find it boring or uninteresting; I came here because of Suzuki, but… Well, I suppose you won’t just believe empty words, so let my emotions reach you; let them show my expressed desire for calligraphy. So, Matsumoto, will you help me out? All I want is to be welcomed aboard,” I said, looking at her lavender eyes.
They shook at my words because she wanted someone to say that—maybe not those exact words, but I was sure it was something similar.
Though it was precisely because I knew she wanted those words that I said them—it was the only way I knew she would allow me to stay here.
Hmm…
I was not fond of the manner in which this was solved.
“…Only if you really focus on it.”
“Sure, as long as you’re diligent enough to teach me,” I grinned warmly; it was a true grin from my heart.
“…Is that so? Then welcome aboard,” she grinned, stretching out her hand. “I mean, you do look like someone interested in calligraphy.”
“But of course I do.”
I shook it, keeping a kind grin. I did so as I understood what happened to her; everyone discouraged her for loving something, adoring it.
While no such thing had happened to me, at least not to that intensity or at all, in fact, I could sympathise.
“So why’d you join in the first place?” she asked. “You wanted to say something about Suzuki but stopped midway.”
So now you remember.
“Oh, right. Well, I was bored, and I lost my chance to join a club on the first day,” I lied.
Well, I can’t exactly tell her my reason, can I?
“Oh. Well, as you can tell, this isn’t exactly your standard club.”
“Yeah, why are we way out here?”
The building we were in was a 7-minute walk from the school block, unlike the other clubs that were in the club building, which was barely a 3-minute walk away.
“We would normally have a standardised building, but my club lacked male members and couldn’t be considered a real club, so I wasn’t given a real clubroom—actually, we are kind of an illegal club.”
“Illegal club?”
“Well, we aren’t recognised by the school as a real club, but I know we will when we get male members—I mean, you’re here after all.”
Yeah…except I’m just here to find Miyamura a girlfriend. Though I suppose it wouldn’t hurt to stay here for a while.
“I guess that makes sense. Also, Matsumoto, I know you’re passionate about calligraphy, but why make a club? Is it really worth the effort?”
“To be honest, I was surprised there were no calligraphy clubs; everyone thought of it as inconsequential and didn’t bother to make one, and unfortunately, clubs were solely made by students.”
“I see.”
Most Japanese students were taught calligraphy at a young age in schools, but due to the art being a time-consuming and tedious exercise, some people wanted to avoid it.
Not to add that it wasn’t as important as other clubs, and it was a rather archaic practice; moreover, it wasn’t as though everyone disliked it, but since they could do as they liked without parental or external intervention, people opted to avoid it, as the art of calligraphy was something from a more traditional era.
That being said, it wasn’t to say that Matsumoto and I were the only students who enjoyed calligraphy; there probably were others, but they weren’t as proactive as her, as calligraphy was something normally done by introverted or closeted individuals.
Well, I knew for a fact that those people existed because they would be the ones attending the club today, though perhaps those were just Matsumoto’s friends trying to support her.
Perhaps they’re making up for laughing at their friend’s conviction.
“So I thought, why not make one?” Matsumoto grinned; she was happy to reminisce on such thoughts. “I wanted to make one in the first term but…”
“But?”
“Anyway, thanks for joining. I really appreciate it,” she sat beside me, changing the subject.
She suddenly changed the subject, avoiding it altogether. Though I didn’t think much of it and didn’t rather mind it.
“Yeah, no problem.”
She’s really close; I can smell her perfume. It has a citrus scent and is quite an amazing brew.
“So Nakamura-kun,” she began, her voice low. “...do you wanna try practicing callig—”
“Yuno-chan!”
A girl ran towards Matsumoto and hugged her. The other club members finally arrived.
The people who came were Suzuki, Innō, and some other people from our class whose names I didn’t know.
With everyone gathered, I sat beside Innō to fill him in, though before now I had told him by text about the club.
“So did you find anyone you like?” I asked, in a tone like that of a whisper.
“Uh, what happened to your mouth?”
“Don’t worry about it.”
“Sure. Anyway, I already know everyone here,” he said. “And I don’t particularly like anyone.”
“Huh?”
“Are you surprised?”
“Well, I thought you said as long as a girl is cute, she’s alright.”
“That only applies to people outside our class; everyone knows that.”
Unfortunately, I didn’t know that, and I was sure many didn’t either; only Miyamura might have known what he was talking about.
“Why is that?”
“Dude, you’re going to see her every day until graduation. If you’re dating her, then you have to date her for real. No takebacks,” he lectured.
His logic made no sense; for one, there was no way you could hide from someone in this school, even if you weren’t classmates, at least not forever.
Also, what he said sounded a bit weird; he said until graduation, as if implying we don’t change classes.
Did he mean it another way? Or did I mishear?
He said his words quite too quickly, so I couldn’t be sure, as the words ‘sotsugyō made’ (until graduation) and ‘sotsugyō made in’ (by graduation or before graduation, but the latter in this case) sounded quite similar.
I was about to ask when—
Matsumoto came to the podium to say something.
“Thanks for coming—this is the calligraphy club; today is just for introduction, so feel free to do whatever,” she grinned.
She said we could do whatever, so we did, and almost everyone broke into a conversation or played around with their phones.
I sat silently. I didn’t have any games on my phone, and I didn’t have anyone to talk to—Innō had already started to talk with girls; he left me to revel in my solitude.
What an amazing friend!
I sighed, exasperated by his actions.
Dude, at least try to include me in a conversation.
I sat there until—
“Nakamura-kun~.”
“Huh?”
“Come over here.”
Matsumoto and her friends were calling me over to sit with them.
I joined them subsequently, as I had nothing else to do. I sat amidst them as they scoped me like I was an alien from another planet.
“So, Nakamura, how are ya?” a girl asked. She was a gyaru like Suzuki; well, she looked the part—dyed hair, long socks, short skirt, painted nails, and her first buttons on her shirt were open. Of course, we couldn’t forget the casual language.
“Oh, I’m fine,” I replied.
“Hmm? What happened to your mouth?”
Matsumoto and I shivered at her words but kept calm expressions. If they knew what I did, I would be skinned alive, so I kept a calm expression; it also seemed Matsumoto didn’t want to tell them what I did.
“Uh, don’t worry about it.”
“Kay’,” the girl replied. “So, what do you think of Yuno-chan over here?”
“Hey, l-leave me out of this,” she said.
“What do I think of her?” I asked. “Well, we’re friends, at least that’s what I think.”
“Is that all?”
“Well, yeah.”
“Hmm. Is that so?” She looked suspiciously at me. “Then it seems like she was telling the truth. Well, that’s all we wanna ask.”
I left and went back to where I was sitting. I knew that talking with gyarus would drain me—in fact, only one gyaru didn’t drain me of energy: Suzuki.
I was about to search for her with my eyes, but didn’t need to because of what happened next.
“Aiya-chan! Why?!”
A lone voice shouted, and while the classroom wasn’t exactly quiet, that voice was still an annoyance.
The voice sported lemon-yellow hair and bright green eyes; her dressing was on the casual side, but she dressed more like me than the gyarus do. The only difference was that she tied a sweater around her waist.
Her figure was on the athletic side; it was something that expressed her intention to maintain a good figure, but her face was the complete opposite, being very cute and delicate.
On the plus side, I happened to know her name through the countless interactions at my desk.
“Ugh, she’s so loud.”
“She’s even like that in malls.”
Some people were exasperated, including me. But as the murmurs grew louder, Suzuki spoke to Yoshida.
“Hey, Aiko-chan, chill, you’re making too much noise,” Suzuki said.
“Huh? Oh, sorry, guys,” Yoshida apologised.
Everyone subsequently forgave her; it seemed she was familiar with most of them.
Wait a minute. Cute, loud, and even obnoxious—she’s the perfect fit for Innō.
She seems to be friends with Suzuki, so I should probably go meet Suzuki.
I walked to where Suzuki and Yoshida were, then sat beside Suzuki, though she didn’t notice as she was still playing a mobile game with Yoshida.
“Yo,” I said.
“Huh? Nakamura?” Suzuki was shocked as she didn’t see me come.
“Surprised to see me?” I asked.
“Yes. Actually, I think this is the second time you’ve ever come to talk to me; it’s always the other way around.”
“Really?”
I hadn’t noticed that I seldom initiated a conversation with her—in fact, the only time I actively went to meet her was when I needed her notes, and even in that case, she first met me.
“Y-Yeah,” she blushed.
Suzuki blushed? I didn’t think gyarus could do that—especially she, who teases me.
“Oh, Nakamura-kun, right?” Yoshida looked up from her phone. “What’s with your voice?”
How many times have I heard that question? I suppose it’s not their fault; I sound like I got dropkicked.
Funnily enough, I did get dropkicked.
“I messed up my mouth.”

“I was just kinda bored, y’know,” I said. “Anyway, what game are you guys playing?”
“Battleground 3,” she said, going back to her phone; she gave up on the conversation.
“Oh…”
I’ve never heard of that before, though it must be fairly popular among my peers.
“Well, to be honest, I’ve never heard of it.”
“Is that right?” Yoshida smirked, looking at me suspiciously. “You’re telling me you’ve never heard of Battleground 3?”
“Yeah?”
“Cut the shit. What are you trying to do? Tryna flirt with Aiya-chan?”
“Uh—”
“Don’t lie, I know what you’re trying to do. You slit—”
“Hey, Aiko, leave him alone,” Suzuki cut her off.
“But…”
“Don’t worry, Nakamura-kun isn’t like that.”
“W-Well, if you’re sure,” Yoshida quickly backed down at Suzuki’s words.
Though we had a rough start, we eventually diverged into a conversation—mostly small talk, occasionally bringing up things about school.
“I actually happen to enjoy math class,” Yoshida retorted while still playing her game.
“You enjoy being taught by a robotic dictator?” I was referring to my one and only homeroom teacher.
“Yeah, she’s endearing. I’m sure she’s just doing that to motivate us.”
“Nah,” Suzuki added. “Probably just hates our class.”
“Who knows?” I said.
Our conversation continued for a little while, with our conversation ending when Suzuki and Yoshida started talking about Battlegrounds 3, with Suzuki joining her in a match, and me staring blankly at them before Matsumoto came up to the podium again.
“Would you look at the time? It’s already 5 PM. Well, if anyone wants to leave, they can,” she said. “Also, thanks again for coming today—I’ll pass a register, so please fill in your name and Acc. for identification.”
The register was passed around, and I filled in my name and acc.; of course, Innō didn’t do so because he still had his volleyball club.
So the school also uses our account name for identification? I don’t know if I should be weirded out or impressed.
With that, most people stood up to leave—I was one of them until...
Suzuki held my hand. “I thought you would ask, but do you want my acc.?”
I was surprised at her question. The answer was obvious, was it not? Of course, I wanted it. What boy wouldn’t want a cute girl’s number?
In fact, I already planned on getting it, though I planned to get it from Innō.
“Y-Yeah.”
“The name is Aiya-chan.”
“W-Well, s-see you later,” I stammered. I still couldn’t believe a cute girl like her would ask if I wanted her account name—of course I would.
Though I wondered why she gave it to me now; after all, we were neighbours—rather, seatmates.
Anyway, with that done and Innō subsequently leaving, I left the room and headed for the bathroom as fast as possible. It was because I needed to text Suzuki.
[Me: Hey, it’s Nakamura.]
To be honest, I was anxious. I needed a favour from her, and I wasn’t sure she would answer soon. Luckily, she did.

Well, not anything.
[Aiya-chan: I’ll hold you to your word, kay? Well, see you later.]

[Me: So, no one caught your fancy today, right?]

This guy really is something, huh?
[Me: Alright, I will, but only if you let me try one last time.]
