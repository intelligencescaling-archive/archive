Chapter 10: Kagurazaka Akane’s Soliloquy
I regretted that day and hated that I couldn’t seem to forget it, regardless of what I did. Of course, I was referring to the day Kobayashi got expelled. Everything spiralled out of control because of a choice I made, one I couldn’t take back.
I was the one who convinced him to break the rules. At the time, it seemed harmless, perhaps even necessary.
After all, who would have thought they’d actually expel him? And even if they did, wasn’t it for the best? Kobayashi was a known pervert, a distraction to everyone in class.
His presence made some of the girls uncomfortable, and I convinced myself that getting rid of him would benefit us all.
Or at least, that’s what I told myself back then.
But deep down, I knew the truth. I didn’t act for the good of others—I acted for myself.
I remember how Ito-kun blamed himself, carrying the guilt as if it were his burden to bear.
He genuinely believed it was his words that persuaded Kobayashi to cheat during the exam. But the truth is, Kobayashi wouldn’t have probably listened to anyone except me.
That’s just how he was—someone who could only be swayed by a woman’s attention, and unfortunately, he was completely starved of that. I was the only exception to this, of course. I knew this, and I used it against him.
And now? Now I live with the consequences. However, if Kobayashi hadn’t shared those answers, we wouldn’t have passed.
We were unprepared—desperate. How would we know that the school was serious about the rules?
But that doesn’t make what I did right. I like to tell myself I had no choice, but is that really true?
The worst part is how I handled it afterwards. When the dust settled, I didn’t admit what I had done. I didn’t own up to my role in Kobayashi’s downfall.
Instead, I let everyone think I was the one who tried to help him.
I crafted a story—a lie—that painted me as the hero who stood by him, as someone who had tried to save him from himself.
And they believed me. They showered me with praise and admiration. It’s disgusting, really.
But it’s the only thing I’ve ever been good at: maintaining appearances, playing a role, keeping everyone’s eyes on the version of me that I want them to see.
On the other hand, Ito-kun dared to confess his part, even if it wasn’t entirely true. That good-natured fool, he was such a lovable idiot. I mean, his mind didn’t even consider what I did, or perhaps it did, but he betrayed the thought.
After all, he bore the brunt of the blame without hesitation. Meanwhile, I basked in the undeserved fame and goodwill that followed. Not that people distrusted Ito, but for a while, he noticed a decline in Rp.
Damn, when I think about this, I really can’t deny that I’m kind of an asshole.
You know, I thought coming to this school would change me; well, it seems like we’re at a loss, huh. Anyway, I thought I’d leave my old self behind—the one who relied on manipulation and appearances to get by.
But it turns out, the problem isn’t the school. It’s me, Kagurazaka. I’m the one who needs to change.
But can I?
Am I even capable of being someone better, someone honest?
And now there’s Nakamura Eiya. I don’t know what to do about him. He’s become entangled with Aiya, and I can’t shake the feeling that this will end badly.
I don’t trust her; well, less her and more how she’ll handle him. She’s up to something—something that could ruin him.
I know for a fact that she wants to have him expelled, but why?
What could she gain from it?
The questions gnaw at me, but I know better than to get too close. Not while Kaneko Katsu is involved.
Our deal with Kaneko-senpai keeps me tethered and restricted. Until Monday, my hands are tied. I’ve been avoiding Nakamura-kun, staying out of his way, hoping he can hold on just a little longer.
After that Monday, I’ll be free to act. I just hope it’s not too late. I’ve made so many mistakes already—can I afford to make another?
For now, I’ll keep my distance. But the guilt and the fear are suffocating.
Every time I see Nakamura, I feel the weight of my inaction. I wonder if he knows, if he suspects anything—after all, he is an asset.
All I can do is wait. Wait and hope that this time, I’ll find the courage to do the right thing.
Yeah, right.
