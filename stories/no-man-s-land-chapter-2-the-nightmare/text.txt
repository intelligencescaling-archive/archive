He goes where he pleases, doing as he wishes, finding a new reason to keep living. Does he have a family? No—he lives alone, of course. And in this vast solitude, he manages to find a measure of comfort, something that brings him satisfaction

“I am, first and foremost, a machine for observing errors. For every problem, there is a solution. That is my philosophy”

Casinos. The nameless man always found them fascinating. The mechanics of profiting from games designed to favor the house—and the solutions casinos employed—naturally drew him in. It was his calling to devise and refine strategies that could turn the tables. They called him "Nightmare," for he shattered the dreams of casino managers. Eventually, however, reality catches up with everyone, exposing their weaknesses. Unless... you become a master of logic

"It’s time... to finish this game!"

It was February 28, 2026. A group of men arrived at the Allstars Casino in Rio de Janeiro. There were at least eight of them, yet within hours, they would make enough money to drive the casino into bankruptcy. Most were down-and-out gamblers seeking one last shot at a perfect turnaround, led by the master of betting himself. The plan was simple: win as much money as possible, as quickly as possible, before the casino even realized what was happening. The team focused on the games where specific techniques could give them the edge over the house: blackjack and poker

In blackjack, it is possible to overcome the casino's edge by using a number based algorithm (-1, +1, 0) combined with a flawless strategy for every scenario. While difficult to master and potentially detectable by casino staff, the group had managed to remain discreet up to that point. They won over 80 thousand dollars playing the game

Poker also involves a form of optimal strategy, where certain card pairings are statistically superior to others. Reading one's opponents is also crucial for survival in the game. In this instance, since they could not extract money directly from the casino, they devised a strategy to damage the casino's stock value

They invited casino stakeholders who held significant equity in the establishment to play poker. Ultimately, they won a total of 800,000 from these individuals. To cover their losses, the stakeholders liquidated a large portion of their holdings, specifically, their “buy” orders. With the “buy” positions eliminated, the "sell" positions gained strength. The gamblers anticipated that investors would recognize the unfolding situation and realize the casino's stock price was bound to fall as a result. Consequently, they used the money from their short positions to generate an even greater profit

Market sentiment regarding the stock shifted decisively toward selling, causing the share price to plummet. The gamblers used their 800,000 in poker winnings to go all-in on short-selling, amplifying the effect and further draining the value of the casino's stock. Before this tactic, Allstars Casino held 43,750 shares valued at 32 dollars each, immediately afterwards, the price dropped to 18 dollars per share, a loss of 600k dollars, with the total stock value falling from 1.4 million to 780k. And that amount was destined to fall even further after the final game

All these events had unfolded in little more than an hour, and now only one game remained to complete the plan. It was a recent trend that had originated in international casinos, with each venue featuring its own signature game. The final game was…

And naturally, we make the player wear this blindfold so they can't cheat. Make no mistake: that blindfold blocks out all light; nothing gets through.”

“That’s the standard casino modus operandi: profiting from chance. This type of game is actually quite appealing; if you really look at it, the odds are better than in any lottery. They charged me 5,000; if the loss-to-win ratio holds steady, they only need to collect 4,600 reais from the 215 losers to cover the 1 million prize for the winner. “Very astute, Mr. Castro, truly astute. In the end, the house always wins, and the decision always rests with the bettor. However, we can reach a logical conclusion that narrows down the available choices, creating a limited set of patterns. Round Six

“Professor, I’m sorry, Professor! I’m sacrificing myself for you!”

“Player Felipe has been disqualified for attempting to aid an enemy team,” a voice announced

“Felipe…” The professor noticed the objects that had been tossed over the wall. “They’re… coins… Team 1’s coins, from their containers! We have most of them now. All the counterfeit coins are in our hands. Each team starts with at least 50 of each type… we received 18 gold, 8 silver, and 35 bronze. That means they have 32 gold, 42 silver, and 15 bronze. They’re bound to use high-value coins, I have to counterattack with low-value ones!”

He pressed his ear against the wall again, now anticipating a specific pattern

“I see… there was a pause between the sounds of the last few coins. There was no hesitation in placing them, which means it’s a simple pattern. What confirms this is that the coins were placed one after another in a line, unlike the first coin which was followed by four coins arranged around it. And with so many coins placed, it has to be this pattern…”

So… I’ll create a pattern using 41 counterfeit coins and 40 bronze coins. I’ll rack up over 200 points with that!

Round 6 Results

Team 1: 284 points
Team 2: 121 points
Team 1 extra points: 163 points

“What? How is that possible? There aren’t enough coins on Team 1’s board to yield that many points, yet they were counted anyway. How can this be?” He stared intently at the board and the table, trying to find some logic behind it all. “Ah… so that’s it…”

“Boss… do we still have a chance to win this game?”

“Yes, yes. Trust me! Look! The table has recessed areas that form a 9×9 grid, just like the pattern on the board. It’s possible that… coins can be placed below the board level, that part of the table might count as part of the board, even if the coins aren’t visible from above! And it looks like coins can be placed in the same grid square, either on top or underneath.”

“This is my last chance to win!”

Round Seven

“Disqualified!”

 “Team 2 broke the rules by making an illegal move, so the entire team has been disqualified. Team 1 established a pattern worth 284 points. In total, after deducting Team 2’s points, Team 1 gains an extra 525 points. That comes to 3 thousand and 150 dollars!”

“The winners are…”
“Team 1”

“Huh? Wait... but... hey, what mistake did I make? Did I mess something up? What does this mean!? Explain yourselves!”

“Actually, there is no mistake, my good sir!” Pierre appeared suddenly. “You would have realized that if you had paid closer attention to the rules…”

“What do you mean by that!?”

“What he means is…” Castro’s presence felt like a dark mist behind Luís. “You made a huge mistake. You created a problem, but there is always a perfect solution, a man's worst nightmare”

“You... who are you, anyway?”

“Just a guy who likes to wander around, making curious facts my specialty”

“What did you do!? Answer me!” Alvarez tried to lunge at him, but Cas remained motionless.

“I think I could have beaten you without it, but I wanted to dominate this match to teach you a lesson. Let me tell you... about my perfect strategy”

“During the third round, I discovered that your student was your lackey. I also figured out the trick of listening through the walls. At the start of the fourth round, I realized it was possible to use the coins on the other side of the board. I discovered this when I touched the board and noticed a 0.3 cm difference between certain sections of the grid lines. I realized then that the ‘lines’ were actually made of wood connecting the top and bottom surfaces of the board. That made sense, given that the referee had instructed us not to place coins outside the boundary lines. Since there were lines on the underside as well, the same rule had to apply, proving that coins could be placed underneath too”

“So, it wasn’t just the 9x9 pattern on the table that reinforced that idea…”

“Of course not.”

“So, my plan went like this: I deceived your disciple by telling him I suspected others of being the traitor. I spoke to each person individually, creating the impression that I was investigating alongside them. In reality, I assigned the others the task of listening at the wall on the opposite side. To pull this off, I planted the idea that the traitor would take the coins and pass them to the other side. I did all this to get my team to sit opposite me, with their backs to the wall. The traitor, so fixated on the board and the investigation, failed to notice that a different team member was taking turns listening at the wall. For the plan to work, I had to keep you and your student from realizing I had uncovered the trick”

“And so, in the fourth round, I tossed a coin against the wall so you would discover the trick. That way, you’d become reliant on it, abandoning your unpredictable adaptability and I could manipulate your board using the sounds I produced.”

“In rounds 4 and 5, I had to minimize both my bonus points and yours, to foster the impression that your adaptation was working. I knew that if I scored too many points using that tactic in round 4, you’d figure out I was using the ‘wall’ strategy. My score would have dropped after that. By keeping it under wraps, I managed to manipulate your board to create a wider point gap in round 6. That was the key... waiting to pull off a major turnaround in rounds 6 and 7 was the best move. Keeping that secret from you in round 5 was tricky, given the significant lead I’d already built. But there were several reasons why you wouldn’t suspect me.”

“Could... could you tell me what they were?”

“Oh, sure!”

 “1) You never imagined I’d lose on purpose just to hide the ‘wall’ tactic. 2) Even though your score was lower than in round 3, it was still quite close. 3) Even with all the information you had, there was still a chance you could misinterpret parts of the pattern. All these factors combined to keep that possibility off your radar. First, I created my pattern. You listened to it and used a counter-strategy. Then, I simply tweaked it in a plausible way so the scoring would end up the way it did”

“At the start of the sixth round, I left the containers in my original spot. When I revealed the true traitor, he recalled the explanation I’d given earlier regarding the traitor’s objective. To make it even more convincing, I even acted worried when I glanced at the containers behind Felipe. He then took that objective on himself, moving the coins to his side... or so you thought...”

“But I had your coins!”

“Then... how did my final board end up with 41 gold coins? And why was that specific number of coins given to you?”

“Tell me what you did!”

“Alright... remember what the referee said about placing other items on the board squares?”

“‘Anything other than our coins won’t be considered during the analysis.” So what? What does that have to do with anything?”

“Look here, in my hand...” He held up some bronze and silver coins. “These are the coins I used on the upper part of the board during the sixth round; let’s call them “ordinary coins”. They aren’t Gameland coins, they’re pocket change the players had on them when they arrived. They’re indistinguishable from the game coins, yet they aren’t counted during the analysis phase”

“Our coins have an internal chip for identification. Aside from that, they’re absolutely identical” Pierre added.

“No… that’s it. I get it… that’s why the silver coins at the top of the board weren’t overlapping my bronze ones. Logically, there would have been a swap; you had to avoid that swap to keep the illusion up”

“Exactly. In reality, I had nine gold coins to complete a 41-coin pattern. Of the eighteen gold coins you received, nine were ordinary ones. But to you, it only looked like I could form a limited pattern of gold and silver coins. In the sixth round, I used a pattern of 41 gold and 40 silver coins. I chose those specific coins for the top of the board because the pattern you thought I had, combined with those ordinary coins, would yield the same score as the 41 and 40 pattern. I did it that way so you’d think the top coins were placed in the same squares as the bottom ones. But you can’t have two coins in the same square”

“Which rule forbids that? That is unfair!”

“It isn’t. The referee calculated the maximum and minimum possible scores. Curiously, he noted that the maximum score is 284 points. In other words, if a single square could hold two coins at once, the value would be much higher”

“It was a trap… all this time, it was a trap!”

“Wait, does that mean you also foresaw that Luís would discover the bottom section of the board before the game ended?” Pierre asked.

“Yes. I had enough information about him to be certain of that.” Caster looked directly at the professor. “I managed to trick you into a fatal error in your reasoning. And so, no matter which pattern you chose, you were doomed to fail!”

“Fine! I admit defeat! You played really well. I didn't know people like that existed…”

“Well... don't feel too bad. You're more entertaining than some of the institutions I've taken down. That’s enough for me. Maybe these games are better than I’d imagined”

“Oh! There are games out there harder than this one, with even more formidable opponents!” Pierre replied.

“That is good. I want to satisfy my appetite for challenges, to push the boundaries of my consciousness.”

 And so, the game came to an end, with each player on Team 1 walking away with 1600 dollars, including the 800 dollars Team 2 players used to enter the game. Caster used the extra money to repay the 600 dollars he owed his client. He also kept 550 dollars for himself. The remaining 2 thousand dollars was distributed among the players on Team 2, allowing them to recover 50% of the money they had lost. Did Caster feel the team had proven their worth against him?  Perhaps he attributed the gesture to a twinge of pity for the players. But in the end, either way, life goes on just the same.
