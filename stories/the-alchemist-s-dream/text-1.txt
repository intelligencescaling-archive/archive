# The Alchemist's Dream.

# EPISODE 1: The Alchemist's Wake.

~~————————————————————————————————————————~~

Harboring themselves unto air; taken up, the wind as vehicle. The energy shifted: something had changed.

...

After an unsettling dream of a Cicada hanging over a tower, Consistent Ear awoke to find his back was throbbingly sore;  he did not remember what he did before he slept: he had eaten cheese and had gotten a dream of the Kafkaesque....beyond what he had seen in that dream: the dream, for all it was, now dominated his waking mind. He had awoken from an illusion that, to his knowledge, had entirely encompassed everything in an instant. Not because he had nothing, but it was because of how he was: he knew that there were many faces around him, but all were diverted into their own individual thoughts. He felt that this dream, of the tower, with the Cicada, had been shared: it was not a personal experience.

He brushed it off as an unusual dream, nothing else.

Standing up was easy. His back's ailment weakened, but remained.

The windows were arching: weakly light let itself in through curtains pulled carelessly. Although these windows were of the size and shape of Witch's Towers, they assumed themselves in the city as more of an imitation done in the methods of carpenters from approximately twenty to thirty years ago, when it was prevalent to imitate designs: it still is, but not so clearly, and not for the inanimate, but instead it had proliferated the methods of concealing one's imitation of another.

Daguerrotypes hung from portraits, pinned into the wall. Black and white smudges of faces not particular. They were passed by. But they were still faces: they were hard to distinguish, but they were, and all around faces are as if they are daguerreotype, until one pulls away the black and white from it, revealing many colors: still, to the passer to another, any face is as smudged and as indistinguishable as these rudimentary pictures.

Taking his robe and tie, he adjusted himself before a silver mirror and dashed out without eating proper breakfast; while seemingly befitting of his age to be this reckless about dietary habits, he needed to see something. He took with him his specialized lenses, not the usual: they were ridiculously round, thin, and multi-layered, with particularly strong refraction and the ability to fully map out movements from the energized bursts of Gu.

...

The city of Southern Puddingwater stood as a backdrop to the rear-line of the general Greentoastian Empire, as it was situated near the contested zones, and finally it was established with the key intention of being the junction between inner cities and frontier cities, hence the artificial elevation: the city itself beyond these traits shared the usual senses of architecture found in the southern quadrants, that of specifically nimble roads, golden spires, and a point of interest with murals and large windows.

...

Consistent Ear weaved quickly through crowds of city folk, travelers, merchants of the higher degrees, cultivators from lower schools, and the odd wizard or two.

While most of the wizards and witches took themselves as superior to the general populace of Cultivation, there had been a unity between both methods of power: it had been an industrial force that integrated both sides into one, and caused the blooming of the times.

It seemed the industrial-alchemical revolution had become almost representative of the day, there was the sense that no one had come with it: instead, all had been taken with the sheer force of the change, as if all had been made to undergo a metamorphosis, and were constantly surrounded by such change, leaving only simplicity as almost deified by the old, and the weary, and the young: otherwise, they made there way through this revolution, and adapted as they could. All were alone; all were in the best of company; all were speakers of the time, and most likely not because they understood it, but because they simply lived in the time, and supposed, presumed, and finally trifled with the present, calling upon the "future" and the "past" to redeem themselves from the monotony between change, and the aftermaths of change.

The overall fashion senses of all countries had been assimilated by the "modern" look, they retained distinctions: cultivators were recognized not by the stove pipe hats, but instead by some traditional folk hat: referred to as the Cultivator's Fez, it was embroidered with the insignias of sects, and finally emblazoned the cultivator's home clan. Anyhow, wizards and witches shared the archaic pointed hats, or had "modernized" and took an array of top hats and mitres. Witches still did prefer their original hats: entirely conical, with a rigid point, and a small rim.

They shared robes nonetheless. While some protested or used traditional clothing, generally the robes had become usual: still these robes were appropriated by all sides, filling them with embroidered patterns, geometrial shapes, and finally a wild sense of color.

Both sides were called Alchemists by now: the distinctions had faded, and were fading. Whether one was of the clans of the Great Stepping Ones, or instead had originated from a respectable tower, for example the Tower Of Coss, they were all "alchemists". Still they prided themselves as wizards and cultivators, but both, when employed by the Greentoastian Empire, fell into such singular classification.

...

Passing through was not without difficulty but he was difficult and made his way to where he wanted to be, via sneaking and a few parts of leaping over a stairwell perishing: a tower in the easternmost section of the city, by the rest of the clergy-spires and the great Cathedral. Adjusting his lens, he put them on. He stared through them: the prescription for these lenses was at least a bit lower than what was recommended by the physician.

Judging by the general change of wind, and the trajectory of residual energy that quickly dissipated in the air, leaving small sparkles of infused light with the highly sensitive and concentrated GM(Gu-Mark, the overall effect in which the latent Gu that comes with the general machinery, a waste byproduct flung into the atmosphere, is forcibly stored by air or becomes a kind of renegade air molecule itself. This means that when the particles of an opposing system is introduced into the atmosphere, even by a discharge a millionth of the quantity as the latent GH in that pocket of atmosphere, the two will react against each other, which starts a chain reaction which causes the introduced Gu to dissipate in the deluded GH, causing infused light to spark, causing the birth of miniature Gu which is absorved by the GH, until the entire cycle is burnt out in mere seconds).

Consistent Ear craned his head to the sky, searching for what he expected to see. The lenses confirmed everything. He began to lean out of the tower's window, and through the clouds he began to see it. They were aptly dubbed by most as , "the Provokers Of The Heavens," and most commonly, "The Alchemist's Ship": officially they were Air-Dreadnoughts. The Air-Dreadnoughts slowly potruded through, their armaments soon coming into view: their engines bolstered by elegant alterations, causing the usually obtuse forms to become in appearance a more appealing bulge with polygons of light steel and huge bolts. They were painted white, and were blessed clearly by the mass of cheering below, who only saw the facades, obscured by the sun's early position. He, however, was seeing them in their full: the broadside consisted of several cannonades, which were built with inhouse equipment to keep their ballistic Gu alive, hence the odd, almost miniscule tower on each barrel, which potruded with various flags, draperies, and heraldry hanging upon them. They were surrounded by floating swords, upon them cultivators, acting as vanguard, while steel broomsticks flew closer to the massive hulls, un-ridden.

Overall, there were more than ten Air-Dreadnoughts: a sight, and a significant military presence. Why they were here was not his concern. He only wanted to see something as magnificent as they were in flight.

Witnessing these industrial giants floating, soaring amongst birds, he knew what he wanted more than ever: he would build them, not because he wished to fly, for he had his broomstick: but because he wanted others to know how to fly, even if they had no means. Flight is for all who see the sky and seek it.

The sun dimmed: something had changed, but all was unnoticed. Day to day things passed insignificantly and even the sun itself became an occurrence that it was regulated as all other things, and now even in dimming, clearly ill, it was unnmoved because we did not see it as movable, and remained ever the same, even as it slowly began to alter: metamorphosis.

After they had passed by the tower, he scaled down slowly and popped out in the foyer, elaborated by windows stained. It was not yet service.

Knocking over a small candle stand, he adjusted it before rushing out. It faded the moment he left, followed by a sporadic dismissal of a few more candles. The cold was cold, and warmed again as a priest entered.

...

Watching from below in a cove beneath the easternmost tower, with a telescope pointed at the sky, a young man noted the positions of the Air-Dreadnoughts.

Altruistic5 singled out a particularly large ship as most likely the flagship of the flotilla. While not excited, he knew what to say at least to AncientEnd, No\_Strawberry, and ConversationSea: they had been correct in assuming something of military interest had occurred.

He signaled for JohnKonami to send a telegram to the center tower: the Tower Of Arnhorst, really, or more of the Tower Of Crystalized Intelligence, one of the two in the entire continent: the other being the Tower Of Fluid Intelligence. John followed the directive, cutting the odd pieces of wood that had suddenly fallen, before heading out. LightningBench would be pleased with the information, but as of now he was in business: a small bookshop keeper.

Taking a bulbous suitcase, and a cuirassier's helmet on his head, Altruistic5 headed for an unassuming shop that led deep into the bowels of the city: the Witches Tower had long since been buried, and while most assumed it entirely removed, it had merely adapted to its surroundings. Most witches by now knew how to properly maintain Gu with arcana, the most popular method being a variant of playing cards infused with health-sigils and automatic-time refinement arcana.

(What this precisely means is that witches can automate the process of growing Gu, using a repeating system of healing and allowing the Gu to bind with the witch's personal sense of time to idly grow the Gu).

He stooped down to pass through small tunnels, winding. A subterranean city beneath, dug and built around the buried Witches Tower. He was a tall man, but was wiry, so it wasn't much of an issue that he had to pass through such tunnels.

His monocle flashed when a passing train rumbled overhead, suspended by quivering steel and organic fibers, forcibly digging into the earth, only moments later for that very same wounds to be repaired by an automated Heal Gu infused with earth arcana: the whine of the turning gears and compression of thousands of arcana by overworked gears filling the tunnel. Again, something was out of place, for the sound followed the train a second too late. But he paid no mind.

How loud. It reminded him of the summer cicadas.

...

Wrapped in a headscarf of a particular cultivation clan, No\_Strawberry yanked open a round door to find AncientEnd consulting with ConversationSea. The two had been debating for hours.

"Oi! You two! Altruistic's here, now you can settle your damn problem and see what he says!" No\_Strawberry said, sharply. He shut the door, before walking down the tunnel to meet with Altruistic.

"...I believe he is annoyed," ConversationSea said flatly. He stood up and dusted his robes, before opening the door with care. "Poor boy."

AncientEnd nodded in agreement. She followed ConversationSea's movements, before sighing and sitting up: or more of she leapt up, in a subdued fashion.

"Well, for all I know," she started as she walked out of the room, not closing the door. "He couldn't have heard anything. The door is thick. And we were not speaking in particularly urgent language! C'mon, he merely presumes."

"He heard everything," ConversationSea said with a sardonic expression. He rubbed his face with his hands before looking at a cup of untouched tea on the table: he looked like shit in the reflection.

"Lord," he muttered, as AncientEnd grabbed her pointed hat, ate some indiscriminately large slice of omelette, yanked her robe from a robe rack, and dashed out to find Altruistic5.

"I look like I'm divorced," he said absentmindedly, watching the young witch speed off: down the tunnels. He ate the rest of the omelette and slowly made his way out, dressed in a fine suit with his wizard robes hung over his shoulders.
