The Alchemist's Dream: Fanfic Of The Sub Ep. 2

# The Alchemist's Dream.

# EPISODE 2: Cicada.

~~————————————————————————————————————————~~

A young man, dressed in the attire of a cultivator: a red hanfu with a bowler hat and a black prinz-nes with golden chains attached to the miniscule edges, stalked through a hallway of an underground section of the medical branch of the cathedral. He held up a Gu Lantern, peering into the darkness.

This was not a hallway: rather something imitating a hallway. Dark\_Synapse breathed in slowly, before advancing forward. His hands gripped at the thick air: slimy air, almost flesh-like. It twitched back.

Somewhere in here, Mr\_Disintegrator had vanished.

Dark\_Synapse was enveloped by flickering shadows as he entered the bowels of the dark.

Approximately two dozen feet above him, there was Consistent Ear, running out of the tower. The tower itself, called the Tower NightDozor, was also a church, and one of higher prestige than any other local parish.

...

\*Crack\*

A firecracker in the main square, over the din of summer cicadas. NoMarionBerry, a youth with a strong grin, ran through the celebrations, clutching some mock sword.

Looking both ways as not to be run down by a celebration goer, he stooped into a small door and crawled out into an extension of one of the tunnels. A man with a cuirassier's helmet and a sturdy suitcase in hand passed by without a word, clearly engrossed in his own thoughts.

While NoMarionBerry usually headed home immediately, he felt intrigued. Not intrigued. Drawn. Someone was pushing him and he didn't know who. It was as if he had entered a dream, and the dream knew itself as a dream.

He shrugged: I can still make it home by afternoon. Creepy-Ebb-3026 won't say much, if I bring back some food. He absentmindedly ran a hand through his pockets: three coins. Enough for noodles and a side of pasta.

The "Gu-Town", the specific, almost ghetto-like part of the city of Puddingwater, housed the population of cultivators of the more disgruntled sects. Although in it was cultivators no different than the others,  it should be noted that it was common knowledge that some practitioners of cultivation, and in extension Gu, were unequal. There were layers to equality.

Thelightmaster7 adjusted the sleeves of his changsan before donning a simple white trenchcoat. Looking out, he checked the obsolete clock on his wall: an arcana powered infusion with the older steam obsessions. The night had been uneasy. He took a homburg and put it on his head, before heading out of the dingy apartment complex, furnished with old fire marks and indiscriminate chips of drywall. The landlord, a man named The\_sillyone, usually cleaned up before heading to one of the many churches dotting the city. Today he was not here. He hadn't left for church either, as his friend, the young attendant who lived and worked in the apartment, CommissionJumpy3220 informed Thelightmaster that he left his coat and his headwrap at home.

"I thank you for your time," Thelightmaster said. He asked CommissionJumpy for the heavy suitcase he had lent a week prior, and the latter gave it to him without complaint, clearly happy to be rid of the cumbersome, highly secured thing. She asked whether Thelightmaster was looking for anything else, and he quietly declined the request.

...

His breath smelled of the usual smoker's strivings. He had quit cigarettes years ago, but tobacco ran as an old loverms bite in him. Thelightmaster slowly descended out of the apartment, almost knocking over an inconspicuously small vase.

He shrugged, putting it back, adjusting, then moving again. A bug on a window, a beetle, crawled idly. He nodded in acknowledgment to a passing woman, who muttered a quick "hullo" to him absentmindedly, and was hurriedly rushing up the stairs.

The stairwell, each level, looked, smelled, and now almost was the previous. The same vase. Same bug.

Reaching the third floor, he looked both ways: up, down, then slowly pushed open a maintenance door.

There was no beetle by the window. Only a cicada, or the exoskeleton discarded. Left, unusual: like The\_sillyone's coat.

The hallways smelled of wet dog.

There lived not a single dog in the entire complex.

Most, if not all had been removed by a prior sweep of inspectors from the local health committee. However, there was a surplus of bugs: beetles and the odd cicada specifically. They had retreated from the trees and foliage into crevices and dirty laundry.

...

AndyTeasey, having been informed about a possible disappearance near the easternmost tower, had entered during the slow arrivals of parishoners and the clergy. He had to squeeze through the crowd, and had to talk to the Father to be allowed into the lower levels.

He was told they had been experiencing intruders as of late.

While he spoke to them, he noticed some candles flickering.

Flickering?

They weren't. He reasoned calmly. Either that or some trigger happy witch was screwing around with some arcana somewhere.

AndyTeasey adjusted his tie, and took off his homburg in respect of church customs. He carried with him a specific walking stick: more of, a highly potent tool used by detectives as a means of wielding defensive and offensive capabilities. The stick, called an Arcanist's Stick, fittingly, was built without a Gu inside, but Andy had modified it with TrueGamer's help a while back to allow an Attack Gu to be stored in the pommel of the thing.

He was not a certified arcanist by any jurisdiction or moral force: but nonetheless he was no damned fool to walk into anywhere in his field without knowing how to use it to any degree.

Maybe it was because of this severe lack of anything that made him qualified in any sense he was the sole survivor of the job, outlasting any other detective, without luck. He never prayed. He knew what to do: diligence and discipline got him as far as it had, and it was all surrounded by the styling of experience, which made him a wild man, who sedated himself with time; memory, as it was for all who passed through it, either found fondness, nothing, or most commonly they found regret.

He was a man of regret because he had outdone those who had no need to fall back on regret to push them forward.

"My colleague has been here. Same call. Same circumstances. Anything you have to say?" he went around asking only himself. Why had Dark\_Synapse gone after TrueGamer before the report had been filed to begin with?

Eventually he gathered his cigarette fueled courage and entered the lower levels.

...

"Has NoMarionBerry come by?" A person of indistinguishable fashion tastes asked around. She had quickly become slightly concerned that the youth hadn't shown himself.

Going up the stairs, she quickly murmured "hullo" to a passing man in a white trenchcoat: another tenant, she remembered vaguely, a studying wizard: Thelightmaster7. Wonder what he was doing about.

Anyway she found CommissionJumpy tidying up the hallway for level seven.

"Ah. Well, Ms. Creepy\_Ebb, no one's gone up or down for the matter," was the answer she got. Creepy\_Ebb ran a hand through her hair and felt like groaning in exasperation. The boy was off of most likely some escapade. She thanked the young attendant and left for her apartment, gathering her coat, before heading outside in a hurry.

Although the older sister of NoMarionBerry, the two shared something more associated with parental relationships, as the two had been living alone for some time.

Only she really remembered the hands of their parents: in a sense she kept track of time, as best as she could, because to fall back on some long past joy was a good strength and gift of memory: they were far and between but she did indeed cherish NoMarionBerry, not because he was a living memory or an example of what was passed still living, but because he was her brother, simple as that. No doubt even if they were not she would still have found the youth a younger sibling, simply because he was and had somehow endeared himself as an adventurer not yet with renown. Ah, right. He always spoke of becoming the next aeronautical explorer, taking some airship to where the more unknown Gu lived.

Although not particularly religious or fond of believing in anything beyond practicality, she found that it was consolation to seek out hope, and be sought out by it. So praying was not beyond her.

...

AncientEnd caught up to the passing form of Altruistic5. He turned wearily and smiled slowly, before shaking her hand  politely.

"Good day arcanist," he said, in an older generic terminology for practitioners of the unnatural.

"Good day! Good greetings, and good morning," she said in quick succession, shaking his hand vigorously. She eyed his suitcase with keen interest. He followed her gaze and laughed a little.

"These are my findings on the positions of the recently arrived ships, Ms," said Altruistic, calmly. He spoke with a steward's cadence.

"I am afraid I am going to be very overzealous today, Mr. Altruistic," she announced warmly, clearly defining her mood as if it was set for the whole day. He nodded idly.

"Understood. Come, I ran into No\_Strawberry earlier. He left for LightningBench's bookshop," the man informed the young witch. He added: "And as for the reports, shouldn't ConversationSea be with us shortly?..."

"And you summon me," ConversationSea said with a dry sigh, although fond, slowly walking down the tunnel.

"Good day, studious writer," Altruistic5 said, shaking the man's hand politely.

While Altruistic5 was not a man naturally inclined to sturdy expectations, he had been surely molded by them: he let himself be molded, as it was the surest way of survival. It was expected and made sure not to become as though he was one without knowledge of expectation, hence his reliability. However, it was this service to molding and being shaped that made him almost entirely removed from the vigor of AncientEnd or the earnestess of ConversationSea: truly it made him someone who one could expect to laugh, no doubt of it, yet it would never light his eyes: for the fire, so far found in anyone who hoped without need, was gone, for he let it fail and be doused, because it was befitting of the day, and truly he was a speaker of the times. His heart, for all sources, from either god or any knowledge Gu or any knowing Arcanic method, was hidden by the tree of knowledge, which he pursued, and found that pursing it revealed only the misery that comes with chasing it: for truth, when pursued, seems to disappear.

...

Among the gathered parishioners, sat a man who watched AndyTeasey pass through the back-area doors. His fingers twitched, before he clasped his hands tightly.

He looked around: he was tired. Evidently. Sunken features. Not a face one would see without the expectation that person had been done in by their miserable luck or some other shit that had gone awry.

Which was correct. But he continued to watch the priests and parishioners move in a haze.

For the sleepless everything melded together into a blur.

ContinentalHater leaned down in the pew, before sitting up and adjusting his suit coat. He rubbed his hands together slowly: he had no vices yet was a living city of them, all packed together neatly in tight rows. He was of an order of heresy: not too long ago, but long enough he could sleep partly. There were cults, yes: for specific arcana or Gu, or both. The older methods of arcana, such as the Yellowed Robes, which he belonged to and now had disowned as himself, still claimed him entirely. He still had a sword from that order, tucked away.

He never slept, he dreamt of what he could and couldn't, and awoke feeling exhausted.

He never slept.

For him, the suddenly flickering candles sparked recognition: the old cultist had told him that whenever the Cicadas came, they blotted out everything: sound, scene, safety, sin. All was to become with the Cicada's discarded exoskeleton.

He left the tower without a word.

JohnKonami, who had been waiting for Altruistic to show himself again at an undisclosed time, saw Continental walking beside the shadowy visage cast by the tower's imposing heights. The two knew each other to a degree, but for JohnKonami, the man was beyond him.

So he let him pass without greeting.

Many things passed and pass in such fashion: we know of them but forget to greet them, or we do not feel the urge to greet them. Some things simply happen, become routine, and become entirely still-life to us until revitalized by some unknown.

To some, all that is around is only still-life.
