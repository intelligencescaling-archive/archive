NoMarionBerry stumbled slightly as he left the tunnel system, for a cicada had suddenly sprung into song beside him, hidden in some downtrodden foliage; although he knew about the tunnel city, and he passed through parts, it was entirely alien to him. Simply knowing a part of something does not merit the entirety, and the entirety when acknowledged does not allow one to see how far it truly goes: the rabbit's hole, as they say, will lead to wonderland.

He looked up at one of the many clock-heads placed upon oil lamp posts. It was getting late, and he'd been eavesdropping far too long. He began to hurry home. He headed to the usual store streets the folks from his sections would go to after work.

Consistent Ear had been thinking for the rest of the day about the Air-Dreadnoughts: even while he worked the ovens at Virtue\_Opposto's Bakery, taking out bread and putting in dough, he was tired beyond belief, as everyday merited. Although the heat did not kill him as he muttered to himself before: rather he had become accustomed to it, and it to him in a way. While it was drudgery he felt at ease with it: not because of how the hardiness had beaten him, but how he somehow circumvented it by believing in tomorrow, and staying in the present as best as he could: both looking at the way, and the sky above, as to guide each and every step with firmness.

Quickly calling at a work colleague, a man of greater age, to make sure the breads were not burnt, Consistent Ear adjused his pince-nez with a hand, while muttering incantations from a small book he always carried: standard issue for anyone in the fields of domestic arcana. He was repairing a small fissure in the side of the oven, which had suddenly appeared when his backpains began to pop and throb, and ended when his pains resided. It was rhythmic, and it was far too uniform to be coincidence, he noted. He was quietly considering taking a short leave with whatever time he had accumulated to somehow fix his pains, but now he could only repair the oven.

Once he had spun the golden light and egg upon the oven's side, he quickly thanked the colleague and returned to working as he could, before being told to handle the front desk, which he did. There were many customers as the time had begun to run into secondary lunches, and the main wizard's in each of the prominent towers of the city would extend the day as far as they could, usually about an hour, with ancient Time Gu.

"Aye, NoMarionBerry, what you doin' so late out ye?" Consistent Ear asked the younger boy as he walked up to the cashier's table. The youth shrugged. Ah. Consistent Ear smirked before ruffling the kid's hair, before he said, "Giving your sis a heart attack again?"

"I don't mean to! Besides, I'm getting us food,"

"With your own money this time?" Consistent Ear joked. The youth groaned and shoved some crumpled pay into the former's hand, before he was given the usual stuffed bread with cheese.

"Good kid, he is," Consistent Ear said, as he watched the youth shuffle his way out of the store. "I'll have to start visiting them again," he added absentmindedly, before busying himself. He wanted to fashion an automated suit for himself, so he could work and do leisure, but that would take time. Really it would be better for the whole country to have a much more robust system of agricultural production, his mind wandered; for the people and by the people. He did not enjoy being a part of any crowd, but the wizard's duty, he felt, was to be of service, and to be of service to himself: a win-win was always preferable.

Someone very cautious about himself within the crowd, but when alone inclined to recklessness.

AncientEnd was highly intrigued and to her quiet admittance very excited by the prospect of the Air-Dreadnoughts. Conversation Sea had been steering Altruistic to mention to the young witch the matters of "that", but the latter had avoided mentioning it at all.

Conversation Sea nodded, although troubled, "The Southern Kingdoms may send their forces: the telegraphs are a haze, friend. We can't make sense of what is what, and who is who. There is panic. But why? The why evades us."

"...The sky has changed," Altruistic said, at length. Conversation Sea laughed shortly, before he spoke, "I live almost always 'round here. Tell me, how has the sky changed?"

"It has deepened in hue. Simply, that is all, but it has left almost everything out of place within it. The clouds are stiller. The wind is reluctant," Altruistic said. "AncientEnd told me, actually, after she went up with that lad, No\_Strawberry. They go up frequently, mostly for the above ground business. Hm."

"She told you?"

"Yes."

"Observant," Conversation Sea said, with a small smile. He sighed, before running a hand through his hair.

"Aye. Here she comes," Altruistic quietly adjusted his cuirassier's helm.

No\_Strawberry had been somewhat late, as somehow Altruistic had evaded him in the tunnels, a jest most likely. He cursed the man and stepped back, retracing his steps to find everyone discussing.

No\_Strawberry stood as an unusual figure: he was a novice member for the Yellow Shorn Faith, a unique "cult" that had transitioned into an officially recognized religious sect of the Yellow State during some unknown period only defined as one large collection of religious conflict called, "The Time Of Sorrows,". Many cities had been built after the conflict had subsided, and most were constructed directly ontop of older structures, with the City Of Puddingwater one of these, having been built atop the older city of BattlerFan, which, according to whatever scant evidence remained and suggested, had been destroyed by an occult force associated with the Red Faith, a sibling cult to the Yellow Shorn Faith. While everything seemed to have lasted to a degree, and was certainly believed in, it all was fallen: as it was notable, every face that had seen these during their lifetime had since joined them in going. To note that although the face has seen, it is the face itsef that aso must go along, and all the things seen will pass along as well, for none are spared: even sorrow must leave. The remainders of what was are sharp until they are dulled by misuse, and the treachery of memory. Inevitably they are at a disservice by being borne of those who cannot remain permanently, or even intact beyond what is said, what was said, and what now remains of them, represented by perhaps only fragments.

The whole city of Puddingwater rested entirely on old foundations that were more or less intact by miracle. The tunnel system, and the overall buried Witch's Tower, were not deep enough that they were a part of these ancient structural features.

Whatever lay deeper? Well: currently there had been a great deal of archaeological sites slowly sprouting beneath and about. Deeper still, hidden, is the stirring. Cicadas slow crawl from the depths to the surface, and the one that cannot crawl but must escape dreams it has escaped beneath a clock. This dream, transmitted....the cicada will become free, and become the dream, as the dreamer becomes the wakened.

"AncientEnd," No\_Strawberry pulled her aside for a bit; they had both ascended upwards for the young witch's sake of acquiring some grimoires or something—he hadn't really heard after Altruistic5 had left for the surface again. He was concerned: something felt deeply off with the general area, not just a simple mistake but almost a constant dread. He felt it, and knew not how to explain it.

"Yes?"

"Nothing of importance. Dismiss my query," No\_Strawberry sighed. She looked at him intensely before shaking her head slightly.

"...I won't say much about it then," she said, after silence. She wanted to ask. But she saw it prudent not to ask: rather, she saw it from his eyes clearly defined that trouble lay in him. Troubled eyes, uncertain of something. For days she had felt somewhat left out of the more intense matters because of the factors of age, and not just simply age but the distinct attempt done unconsciously by the older fellows to shield her perhaps because they not only valued her character but also they believed, like many people, that youth should be peacefully transitioned out of, when in truth it is the most violent metamorphosis of a person, no matter the case. The mind changes, and the soul changes, and the body changes, and all together it is a great leaving of the previous body: like a cicada shedding it's exoskeleton, waiting for years to see the world, uncertain, but knowing of some things, believing, and perhaps having learned a handful of aspects along the way.

Mostly, any emotion refuses to be chained to any singularity, but rather it allows and concedes itself to existing in tandem with thousands more, blending into each other until one dominates, then returning, fading out. If any emotion can return to its original state, devoid of other emotions influence, that would be nearly impossible: even the deepest of sadness stems from many feelings that go without name, or possess only a term umbrella upon them.

And now, as many times before, the two walked in silence: AncientEnd regarded No\_Strawberry from the periphery of her eyes.

Feelings unspoken manifest themselves in disquiet silence. There was something meant to be asked: perhaps it should've been asked, and spoken for, but yet it wasn't, and remained in stillness. They were waiting for some opportune moment to speak, but neither knew when they could find such a moment, for these specific moments come and go as quickly as the breeze, and are almost like the breeze, as they are nearly invisible to the heart. We can go through life surrounded by wind, and be unable to make out when it thins out into a breeze, for whomever the breeze we believe we are now meant to share the unspoken with may as well be still surrounded by the wind still unbroken, and again the wind will pick up for us, and we must move along: to linger is to remain in what cannot be changed, and what cannot be changed may as well change us if we let it linger, even though nothing can be done of it, only deluding ourselves into thinking it can be altered.

There are times when we are faced with a particular situation, which can easily be passed through physically, but it is of damage to the spirit and the mind: there are times when such situations are so easy to run through, to expect, but never once to look at oneself and ask why one must pursue this path because it does not labor the body. We may realize it labors our mind and hearts, but this labor can be disguised: these particular situations can be of the most extreme pleasures to us, or of nature that is pleasant.

And there are such situations when there is an accumulation of discomfort, dislike, and despair, borne from an innumerable degree of factors that belong to not only a person but also the place with which they are made to live in. There, and not soon, but overtime, taken with the specific person with which this very agonies, silent, and shielded away because they are simply ignored, it becomes the conquering force inside the person: they begin to presume, they begin to assume, they begin to see only within a singular sense, usually morphing into negligence of empathy, sympathy: the understanding had fallen in them,  (though in some cases the person has never had the capacity of understanding) and because they were given factors that either refused understanding, deluded understanding, overwhelmed understanding with various grievances, or simply they were unable to understand themselves, for they feared to look deeper, and thus like the accumulation of difficulties they believed consoled and was of their character, they ignored it, or they simply never knew of it, as it lay so small as to avoid anything of inspection.

To summarize and finalize: the unsaid dominates our life; we cannot speak for all of it, only refer to it, as if we know of what it was that was passed, and we comfort ourselves with acknowledgment, or perhaps a subtle, lingering sense that goes without words itself, simply being there that of what the unsaid could have been. We cannot even summarize half of what has and what is. All that is, has been seen and spoken for in a journey of transitioning through thousands of this unsaid.

He knew he could not call for help: he knew he could not run, for if he did would forget.
