Chronicles of MinimumAd: The Thames Enigma

# Chapter 4: The Smoke Behind the Curtain

The hour was late, but London never truly slept. It merely shifted its tone—carriages grew fewer, the chatter dulled into murmurs, and the gaslamps flickered like weary sentries in the mist. MinimumAd walked swiftly, shoulders hunched beneath a grey workman’s coat, the kind one might see on a plumber, soot-checked and fraying at the edges. His gait was purposeful, steady, and entirely unbothered by the labyrinthine alleys he traversed.

He knew this part of town too well.

Clerkenwell, once an artisan's haven, had curdled into a borough of quiet vice and whispering windows. Tonight, the fog curled thick around his ankles, swallowing sound and scent alike. Only the occasional bark of a distant dog or the rattle of a windowpane disturbed the thick silence.

But even MinimumAd—calculating, dispassionate, unshakable—felt it.

The presence.

Somewhere above, the rooftops shivered under the weight of another presence.

High above him, leaping with effortless grace from one Victorian rooftop to another, was a figure clothed in the twilight of shadows and smoke. Leather boots struck chimney edges with the elegance of a dancer and the confidence of a cat. The long black coat she wore fluttered behind her like wings clipped by moonlight, and her hair—tied carelessly into a loose knot—bounced ever so slightly as she landed on the edge of a roof, crouched and perfectly balanced.

She grinned.

“My dear Minimum,” *Mynameisnotdark* whispered, watching from above. “Still wearing that ridiculous coat.”

Her eyes, sharp and amused, followed him as he approached the brick façade of *Smith Inc*. The building was tall, narrow, and deeply anonymous—no signs, no windows on the first floor. A discreet red door was tucked between two alleys, as though the building were trying to forget it had an entrance at all.

Minimum paused outside, glancing over his shoulder—not out of suspicion, but out of habit. Of course, he saw nothing. She had trained him better than that once.

From above, Dark smiled wider, lying flat against the cool tiles of the rooftop. Her breath left a faint cloud in the cold night air. She reached into her coat and pulled out a pair of opera glasses, gilded and absurdly elegant. She never went on a tail without some flourish.

Through them, she watched him retrieve a brass key from beneath the guttering, unlock the door with a satisfying click, and slip inside like a shadow returning home.

Dark chuckled softly. “And into the lion’s den you go.”

She didn’t follow—not yet.

She didn’t need to.

She’d already been inside *Smith Inc* three days prior.

Her message had been left behind in the ledger on the third floor, wedged precisely between two receipts: *“You always did walk into traps with your eyes wide open, darling. Let’s see how this one fits.”*

MinimumAd had yet to find it.

She closed her opera glasses with a crisp snap, stood up, and stretched like a cat on a windowsill. Then, with one last glance at the red door now quietly shut, she turned and melted into the fog-covered rooftops—gone as silently as she’d arrived.

The mansion sat on the edge of Belgravia, veiled in ivy and silence. To the untrained eye, it was nothing but another ancestral estate left to the whims of wealth and weather. But behind its doors, secrets paced.

The great oak double doors opened without sound as *Mynameisnotdark* stepped in, removing her coat in one fluid motion. A butler she never spoke to took it, as he always did, and she ascended the spiral staircase without waiting to be announced. Her boots tapped lightly across the marble hallway, each step carrying a blend of feline grace and calculated ease.

At the far end of the hall, a set of double doors were already slightly ajar.

He always knew when she’d return.

Inside, the study was steeped in warm, low light. Mahogany shelves curved around the room, stacked with leather-bound volumes that had never seen dust. A fireplace burned with a slow, dignified crackle, and from the armchair before it rose thin ribbons of smoke, coiling upward from the bowl of a pipe that glowed faintly in the dark.

*EnvironmentNo6525* sat in that chair, legs crossed, robed in the sort of dressing gown that suggested both leisure and power. The judge's wig was long gone; this was his real attire—the regalia of the mind.

“I assume he took the bait,” he said without turning.

“He bit hard,” Dark said, moving to the small table where a decanter sat. She poured herself a finger of scotch, no ice. “Didn’t even question it. Walked right into Smith Inc like it was preordained.”

Environment gave a soft chuckle, his eyes half-lidded, gazing into the fire as though reading from it. “Minimum always was fond of the theatrical. You give him a performance, he’ll chase it to curtain fall.”

She turned to him, one eyebrow raised. “But how’d you know he’d bite? You didn’t even nudge him. Just... dropped a whisper in the wind and waited.”

“That’s the thing about performers,” Environment said, drawing leisurely from the pipe. The smoke curled around his words. “They’re always hoping someone’s watching.”

He finally looked at her, eyes cool and gleaming with satisfaction. “He thinks he’s found a puzzle. A crime of precision. But *Smith Inc* is nothing but a detour. A very convincing one.”

Dark sipped her drink, her smirk returning. “You’re saying he won’t even realize?”

“Oh, he *will*,” Environment said, “but not until it’s too late to matter. By then, he’ll have lost something. Perhaps his temper. Perhaps the good doctor. Perhaps a little bit of himself.” He tapped ash into the tray beside him with delicate finality. “That’s the game. Not winning. *Tilting the board.*”

A pause.

“Tell me,” he added, glancing sidelong at her, “did he bring the surgeon?”

Dark nodded. “He did.”

“Good,” Environment murmured, puffing once more. “The doctor’s presence makes him... slower. More human. He doesn't realize it yet. But he will.”

Dark gave a small hum of amusement. “And here I thought I was the only one who could drag out the ghost in him.”

He raised his glass toward her. “You were the rehearsal, my dear. The doctor’s the stage.”

They sat in silence for a moment, fire crackling, smoke curling like secrets into the chandeliered air.

Outside, the fog deepened.

And the board tilted another inch.
