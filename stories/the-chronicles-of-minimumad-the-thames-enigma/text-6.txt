The Chronicles of MinimumAd: The Thames Enigma

# Chapter 7: The Calling Card

The clatter of London cabs and murmurs of the street faded as *MinimumAd* and *Sieben* stepped into the wide stone archway of the Scotland Yard. The building loomed with the same stiff-backed posture as its officers—cold, proud, and irritatingly confident.

Minimum adjusted the cuff of his coat as they climbed the stairs to *Inspector Morgan’s* office. He walked with a limp, courtesy of the cargo bay brawl, but carried himself like a man who didn’t plan to let pain keep him from his next chess move.

The door to Morgan’s office stood ajar. Inside, a voice chuckled.

“—well, if you’d seen the look on the dockworker’s face, you’d think he’d just been told Queen Victoria took a job as a lighthouse keeper—”

*Ifteaz.*

Minimum stepped inside without knocking.

Morgan sat at her desk, an amused look still lingering in her eyes. Ifteaz leaned casually against a shelf of case files. Her laughter died the moment she saw Minimum.

“Well, look who the Thames dragged in,” Ifteaz said.

“Morgan,” Minimum said curtly, ignoring her. “I need the Trueluck file. The updated one.”

Morgan raised a brow, but wordlessly turned and pulled a thick folder from her drawer. “We were just discussing the case, actually. You’ll find it thoroughly annotated.”

Minimum accepted it, flipping through the pages with quick flicks of his fingers. Sieben peered over his shoulder.

He stopped abruptly and tapped the section marked “Current Corporate Links.”

“Smith Incorporated,” Minimum said, tone flat. “A fake lead. Fraud. Someone wanted you busy while they did something else.”

Morgan leaned forward. “That’s impossible. Our informants—”

“Were misled,” Minimum interrupted, snapping the file shut. “Like me. Like all of us.”

Ifteaz frowned deeply. “That can’t be. I followed the trail myself—”

“And it led to a locked room and a man who nearly turned me into a blot on a crate,” Minimum said. “You don’t stage a golem at a dead end unless the hallway isn’t meant to lead anywhere.”

A beat of silence passed. The room grew colder.

Sieben cleared his throat gently. “Then… where *does* it lead?”

But Minimum was already turning toward the door. “To where no one’s been looking.”

The fog had begun to curl in ghost-like fingers along the edges of the cobblestones as they stood before *Trueluck’s apartment*—a brick building half-sunk into a weary alley, the gaslamps casting long shadows across its chipped facade.

Minimum knelt at the door. Sieben stood watch, hands in his pockets.

“Breaking and entering,” Sieben murmured. “That’s new.”

“*Trespassing*,” Minimum corrected. “Breaking implies damage. I’m simply being impolite.”

The lock clicked open with a soft clack.

They slipped inside.

The air was stale, but not untouched. Dust motes danced in the beam of Minimum’s small torch as he moved with quiet certainty. The apartment was small, the kind meant for someone who rarely returned to sleep.

Minimum checked the desk. Sieben opened drawers. Together, they rifled through papers, empty envelopes, and half-finished notes that whispered of debts, strained schedules, and something else—something deliberately buried.

Then, from the drawer beneath the floorboard—Minimum’s eyes narrowed as he pulled a card from the dark.

White. Clean. Immaculate print.

**Xamot113**
*Banker*
**Free Huckleberry & Co.**

Minimum held it up, his expression unreadable.

Sieben tilted his head. “Friend of Trueluck’s?”

Minimum stared at the name.

“No,” he said slowly. “I don't think so.”
