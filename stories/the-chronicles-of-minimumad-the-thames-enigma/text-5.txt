Chronicles of MinimumAd: The Thames Enigma

# Chapter 5: Where Shadows keep Watch

The door creaked open with the slow weight of exhaustion behind it. The dim gaslight in their flat on Piccadilly barely reached the threshold before it was filled by the disheveled figure of *MinimumAd*.

His coat was torn at the shoulder. A thin cut traced his cheekbone. His knuckles were scraped, and one eye was beginning to swell with the promise of a bruised tomorrow.

Sieben looked up from his chair, where he’d been meticulously cleaning his fountain pen. His brow furrowed as he stood.

“You look like you lost a duel to an entire staircase,” Sieben said, half-serious.

Minimum tossed the coat onto the rack with less grace than usual. He didn’t answer right away. He walked to the cabinet, poured himself a glass of water, drank it, and sat down hard in the armchair opposite Sieben.

“Cargo bay,” he said finally, breathing a little heavier than usual. “Smith Inc. Not quite what I expected.”

Sieben sat up straighter. “What happened?”

Minimum dabbed the cut near his eye with a handkerchief, wincing. “There was a shipment being moved. No documents, no names. Just crates, sealed tight. I was looking for something—anything—but instead, I found a man built like an ox with less charm.”

He chuckled bitterly. “Tried to flatten me with his palms like I was a housefly.”

Sieben blinked. “And you stayed for the fight?”

“Don’t be absurd. I ran like a proper gentleman with better things to live for,” Minimum said, wiping his bloodied hand on the inside of his coat. “But not before hearing two workers argue. One said, *'Why are we even keeping this running if he's not watching anymore?'* The other replied, *'Because it’s meant to keep him watching here, not there.'*”

Sieben frowned. “So Smith Inc. was a distraction?”

Minimum smiled grimly. “A rather expensive theatre act meant solely to waste my time. Someone went to a lot of trouble.”

He leaned back in the chair, grimacing from a bruised rib. Sieben crossed the room quietly, reached into the small wooden bowl on the windowsill, and picked up a card.

“A man came while you were gone. Asked for you. Said his name was *Far Transition*. Left this.”

He held the card out to Minimum.

Minimum took it lazily. Turned it over in his fingers. Read the letters:

**Far Transition**
*Physics Department, Oxford.*

He stared at it for a breath.

Then suddenly, with a flick of the wrist, he flung the card across the room. It hit the far wall with a whisper and fluttered to the floor like a broken feather.

And Minimum—

He *laughed*.

A deep, manic, spine-tingling laugh that echoed through the walls of the flat like thunder through a tunnel. It bent him forward in his chair, face in his hands, shoulders trembling from the sheer absurdity of it all.

Sieben stared, more confused than ever.

“What,” he asked slowly, “is so funny?”

Minimum lifted his head, eyes glinting with something that wasn’t quite madness—but close.

“My *arch nemesis*,” he said breathlessly, grin wide and wild. “*That’s* what’s funny.”

And he laughed again.

Because now, the game had begun. Not the false leads, not the theatre.

The real game.
