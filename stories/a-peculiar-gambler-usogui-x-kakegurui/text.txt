[Fanfic] A peculiar gambler—Usogui x kakegurui

Hyakkaou Private Academy.

Approaching its 122nd anniversary , the academy had a reputation.
Tradition. Prestige. Money. And above all, status.
The kind of place where even the school bell probably had a family name.

Children of CEOs, politicians, and people who make headlines but never read them — they all gathered here.

But grades? Medals? Honor roll?

Completely worthless.
Here, your GPA wouldn’t even get you a seat at the back of the room.

Because this school wasn’t about how fast you ran or how well you solved a quadratic.
It was about how well you read a face, masked your bluff, and turned your opponent into your puppet.

In other words — it was about gambling.

During class hours, it looked normal. Like any other elite institution with too much polish and too little soul.
But that illusion crumbled when the final bell rang.

Once school hours ended, the entire campus transformed.
The desks became game boards.
The students became gamblers.
And losing? That was the curriculum.

They didn’t all know how to win — but every one of them had learned how to lose.

---

It was just another day.

Except today, someone new arrived.

“I want you all to welcome your new classmate,” the teacher said, almost bored. “His name is Madarame Baku.”

He stepped forward. Bowed.

And smiled.

Not the polite, store-bought kind of smile.
No.
This one was sharp.
Predatory.
And if you stared at it too long, it almost felt like it was staring back.

“My name is Madarame Baku. It’s nice to meet you all.”

The classroom stirred like a disturbed ant nest.
A few girls leaned in, giggling.
Some eyes narrowed.
Others didn’t bother pretending to care.

But one girl — Satome — smiled differently.
Not with interest.
Not with kindness.

But with anticipation.

She tried to suppress it under a gentle demeanor, but her aura leaked like a cracked cup.
The class could feel it.
Like how animals sense earthquakes before they hit.

---

After class.

A boy approached Baku — hesitant, eyes nervous, voice soft.
He wore a badge.
One word printed on it:

Fido.

“Um… H-hi, Baku-san,” he said.

Baku turned, noticed the badge.
“Oh. Hi… Fido.”

“Ah, actually… that’s not my name…”

“Hmm?” Baku blinked.

The boy chuckled awkwardly, then began explaining — like someone introducing their own chains.

“In this school… when you lose badly in a gamble, you fall into debt. And if the debt crosses a certain limit, the student council declares you a ‘housepet’. Boys get called Fido, girls get Mittens… basically, we become the bottom rung. Like... slaves.”

Baku’s eyes widened.

But not in surprise.
Not in sympathy.
Not in fear.

In amusement.

Though you'd have to look closely to notice.

“Slaves, huh? Interesting. Go on.”

“I gambled... I lost... and now I’m a Fido. They take your money, your freedom. Some even make you act as their butler or dog, literally. That’s just how it is here.”

“Gambling, you say…”

“Yes, Baku-san. The school changes after classes. It turns into a—”

“Hello, Baku-san!”

A voice sliced clean through the conversation like a katana made of honey.

“I’m Satome. Nice to meet you.”

She leaned in. Smiling too brightly.

“Would you like to play a game with me?”

The Fido-boy’s voice died in his throat.
As if the words got caught in a noose made of old regrets.

Because she was the one who had wrapped that leash around his neck.

Baku didn’t flinch.
Didn’t hesitate.

“Oh? A game? Sounds fun, Satome.”

The boy’s eyes widened — flashing “Don’t do it!” in silent neon.
But warnings only matter when they aren’t obvious.

And Baku?

He walked forward.

Not because he didn’t understand the danger.

But because he did.

Exactly because he did.
******

The boy followed them like a housepet —
Living up to the title.

Satome led them into another room.
It wasn't just a room — it was a stage. A place specifically prepared for one thing:

Gambling.

Students filled the space, minding their own games — throwing chips, laying cards, raising stakes like their futures depended on it.

But the moment she entered, everything paused.

Like a sentence slamming into a full stop.

She was an upper-rank student, after all.
One of them — the kind people didn’t want to make eye contact with unless they were forced to.

She turned to Baku, twirling on her heel with almost unnatural charm.

“How about a simple game of rock-paper-scissors?” she asked, sweetly.

“Hmm~ I like gambling, but is that it?” Baku tilted his head, unimpressed.
“Just rock-paper-scissors?”

“Nope, silly,” she beamed. “There’s always a twist, right? Let’s add one.”

Baku gave a quiet nod, that casual "go on" expression pasted across his face.

“Okay then!”
She grinned like a cat cornering a bird.
“Ever heard of scissors cutting through rock?”

“Uh, no. Wh—?”

“Exactly!” she chirped. “Because we’re not playing regular rock-paper-scissors. We’re adding weight to it.”

“Weight?” Baku asked, eyes narrowing slightly. “Explain, Satome-san.”

“Each hand — rock, paper, scissors — will be assigned a point value,” she said, gesturing around.

“And guess what decides those points?”
She pointed to the crowd of students watching nearby.
“Them.”

“Each of these lovely spectators will vote — anonymously — for rock, paper, or scissors. The total number of votes for each becomes its point value. Then, you and I choose our hands.”

She held up her palm like she was already counting invisible votes.

“After that, we reveal. Whichever hand gets the higher point value wins. This way even scissors can beat the rock hand.”

Baku nodded slowly. That "Ah, I see" kind of nod.

“But what if we pick the same one?” he asked.

“Good question.” She winked.
“We’ll just call that a draw. No need to overcomplicate ties.”

“And how many rounds are we playing?”

“One per bet.”
She leaned in slightly. “So... do we have a deal?”

Baku nodded, calm and collected as ever.

But behind him, the boy — the one still wearing his ‘Fido’ title like a scar — felt a cold shiver crawl down his back.

Horror.

That kind of horror that comes not from jumpscares, but from foresight.
He didn’t want Baku to end up like him.
A collar, a number, a slave to the system.

*****

“Alright then. Let’s begin, shall we?” Satome said, spinning around briskly.

Within moments, a few students — clearly her henchmen — brought out a table and a set of blank cards, placing them at the center like they’d rehearsed it a hundred times.

“Each of us will get three blank cards per round,” she explained, her voice sugary sweet. “We’ll draw our hand on them. Meanwhile, our lovely audience will cast their votes on these special ballots.”

The thirty or so students in the room began to gather, circling Baku and Satome like vultures waiting for the corpse to blink.

Most of them looked like extras in a B-tier school drama.

But Fido stood out — mostly because he was the only one visibly panicking.

“Baku-san, I really don’t think this is a good idea. Ar—”

“It’s alright,” Baku said, cutting him off gently. “I can handle it. Don’t worry, Fido-chan.”
He gave a soft, disarming smile — the kind that could ruin empires and melt steel.
“I’ll win you something to help with your debt.”

Fido froze.
The fear didn’t vanish, but a warmth bloomed in his chest anyway.

He stepped aside.

Baku and Satome sat down at the table.
Facing each other.
Like war generals before the first cannon fire.

“Shall we?” Satome asked.

“Sure.”

“We’ll get two minutes per round. The audience votes during that time. We each pick one hand.”

“What’s the bet?” Baku asked, already fishing in his mind for odds and tells.

Satome smiled like she already won.
“Let’s start small — 20,000 yen?”

Baku leaned back, feigning surprise, then nodded.

They exchanged one last glance.

Then—

“Begin!” someone called.

The room shifted.
Conversations turned into murmurs. Students began scribbling their votes — some casually, some after intense argument, others not even caring.

Baku picked up a blank card and started sketching a symbol.

Satome did the same, humming quietly.

Fido stood stiff like a malfunctioning antenna.
He couldn't even fake composure.

Time ended.

Both players lifted their cards.

Baku: Scissors
Satome: Paper

All eyes turned to Fido, the vote counter.

He stepped forward, clearing his throat.

Results:

Rock: 10

Paper: 8

Scissors: 12

Baku blinked, then smiled.
“I won?”

“See?” Satome said with mock cheer. “Easy, right? But now... let’s get serious.”

“Oh? You’re saying that was the warm-up?”

“Obviously. Let’s raise the stakes.”
She leaned forward.
“2 million yen.”

Baku froze, eyes wide — then his lips curled into that devilish little grin.

She tapped her feet while waiting for his obvious reply.

“Fine”, he said with the same 'playfulness' as her.

Round Two.

Same setup.

Fido watched, horrified. This wasn’t going to be easy anymore — she wasn’t toying with Baku anymore.
She was testing him.

The audience buzzed again. This time, the murmuring was more intense.

Time ticked.
Votes cast.
Cards drawn.

“Fido, the results,” Satome ordered, her tone clipped — like a noblewoman addressing a servant.

He gulped and stepped forward.

Shakingly he showed the results.

Results:

Rock: 15

Paper: 7

Scissors: 8

They flipped their cards.

Baku: Scissors
Satome: Rock

“Yay! I win!” she exclaimed, clapping with theatrical innocence.

Her foot taping intensed.

The crowd gave a polite smattering of applause.
But some raised eyebrows. That voting gap was oddly convenient.

Fido’s fists clenched at his side.

Baku couldn’t prove anything, but…

Something smelled rigged.

********

Satome's lips curled into an obvious wife grin.
Not one of insult.
But of amusement.
The kind you show when the prey keeps walking into the trap, again and again.

“Let’s play again! Let’s play again!” she chirped like a spoiled toddler demanding candy.

Baku’s eyes flickered with something darker.
A flash of irritation.
A heat behind the cold.

“Sure,” he said, voice clipped.

“Hehe~ but this time…” She leaned forward, eyes glinting.
“The wager will be… 20 million yen.”

He blinked.
“Twenty million?”

She nodded, far too furiously. The kind of nod only someone with a safety net could afford to make.

Tsk…

“So is that a yes?” she asked, voice dipped in honey and arsenic.

Baku exhaled slowly, his face tightening.
Then gave a weak, almost reluctant nod.
It looked like surrender.

But it wasn’t.

“Baku-san!”
Fido leaned in, whispering sharply into his ear.
“This is not a good idea. Please… step down. You’ll los—”

“Oi, Fido!” Satome snapped, like cracking a whip.
“Mind your own business!”

Fido staggered back, her voice ringing in his ears.
But his face still screamed that familiar look:

“Danger! Don’t go!”

Baku, unfazed, gave Fido the faintest smile.
“It’s okay. I gave my word. I won’t back out.
Not until I free you, Fido-chan.”

Fido's heart sank.

Not from the kindness.

But from the fear that Baku was about to lose everything.

---

Round Three.

Baku sat, but his hand hovered.

He wasn’t drawing yet.
His eyes weren’t on his cards.

They were on Satome.

And then—

He noticed it.

Her foot.
Tapping.

Not rhythmically.
Not nervously.
Deliberately.

Tap. Tap. Pause. Tap. Tap. Tap—

Morse code.

Of course.
The students around her weren’t just spectators.
They were her pawns.
The one behind her? Watching her hand and transmitting it via foot taps.
Signaling the vote.
Skewing the entire game.

A well-calculated strike.
Clean. Quiet. Invisible.

Baku almost laughed.

Instead, he smiled.
That same grin.
The one she wore.

Their energies matched now.
The predator had found another predator.

Satome arched an eyebrow, feigning boredom.
But something twitched in her cheek.

He knew.
And she knew he knew.

Baku swayed his fingers playfully.

Tsk. Tsk. Tsk.

“Let me tell you something…” he said coolly.
“You’ll lose the next round.”

Then, he tapped his foot.
Loudly.
Mocking.

Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap.
(Pause)
Tap.
(Pause)
Tap. Pause. Tap. Tap.
(Beat)
Tap. Tap. Tap—drag. Tap.

It meant nothing.

But it meant everything.

A bluff.
A fake Morse code.
A poison pill tossed into her machinery.

No reaction.
Satome didn't flinch.
Not even a blink.
A master of poise.

Even Fido was confused.

But Baku?
He had already drawn his card.

Time’s up.

Fido, pale, stepped forward with trembling hands.

Baku: Scissors.

He smirked.
“I mirrored you,” he thought.
“If you played scissors too, neither of us win.
But if you switched… you’ll lose.”

He looked up at her.

She was smiling again.
Too hard.

Fido flipped the votes.

Rock – 12
Paper – 8
Scissors – 10

A pause.

“…The winner is…” Fido said hesitantly, “…Satome-san.”

Silence.

Baku's smile dropped.
His jaw slackened, lips parting in stunned disbelief.

“…What?” he whispered.

The world around him didn’t make sense for a second.

He had calculated.
He had baited.
He had disrupted the signal.

And yet—

He lost.

Again.
******

“Hehe, thanks for the free money, Baku-san. You’re the best,” Satome grinned as she scooped up the cash.
Her taunt was so thick, it hung in the air like smog choking a city.

“Wait!” Baku’s voice cracked out, raw with desperation.
“Let’s play again! Please!”

Satome froze mid-strut, and turned around slowly. Her expression exaggerated, theatrical.
“Why should I?”

“I have a deal,” Baku said, breathless.
“If I lose this round… you can make me a housepet. Alongside Fido.”

Her eyes gleamed.
Not because of the money.
Not even the offer.
But because breaking people like him was fun.

“Hmm…” She hummed, pretending to think, lips already twitching in delight.
Then: “Fiiiiiine,” she said with deliberate mockery.

Baku’s shoulders slumped, as though the weight of the entire room had collapsed on his back.
His mask of confidence now shattered.
All that was left was a broken boy.

“Baku-san!” Fido cried out.
He ran up to him, gripping his sleeve. “Please back out! Please!”

But Baku turned, gently resting a hand on Fido’s trembling shoulder.
“Don’t worry, Fido,” he said, eyes warm but voice calm—almost too calm.
“For me, gambling isn’t about winning or losing. It’s about experience.
But this time... it’s different.
This time, I’m gambling for someone.
Someone who placed their hope in me.
And I never break a promise.”

Fido’s eyes widened.
Something clenched inside him.
A tightness in his chest. Not fear.
Hope.

“…Okay, Baku-san.”

---

Round Four.

Same process. Same circle of vultures. Same blank cards.

But this time—

It wasn’t Baku who was falling apart.

It was Fido.

Because now he knew.
He knew it was him.
He was the reason Baku lost the previous rounds.

Was he a coward?
Was he just another puppet?

He didn’t need the answer.

All he knew now—was that Baku had to win.

---

Round Four Results:
Rock – 12
Paper – 11
Scissors – 7

Satome’s lips curved into that familiar victorious smirk.
Already reaching for the leash.

Baku took out a karume.

“Well then… ready to become the new Fido, Baku-s—”

“BAKU-SAN!!” Fido shouted, cutting her off with every ounce of his voice.

Satome blinked.

“…What?” she muttered.

A pause.

“…The winner is—Baku-san!” Fido announced.

A silence as sharp as broken glass fell over the room.

Satome staggered back like she’d been slapped.

“W-WHAT?!” she gasped, voice cracking.

It wasn’t just shock.
It was something else.

Fear.

“Oi! Fido! What are you saying?!”

“I-I’m sorry, Satome-san!” he stammered. “Please forgive me!”

“You IDIOT! You abso—”
She stopped.

Because of him.

Baku stood, calm no longer.

His hair curled with static, eyes dark with malice.
And his lips—
His lips stretched into a monstrous grin, wicked and wide.

A smile not born of joy.
But domination.

A grin that could humble demons.

At that moment—
Satome felt it.

Not just shock.

Not just panic.

Pure, undiluted terror.

---

“Oh… you thought I wouldn’t notice your cute little trap?” Baku said, his voice oozing with mockery.

“W-What… are y-you saying…”

“You are a very big liar, aren't you?“

She didn’t answer.
She couldn’t.

“You wanted me to see the Morse code trick. To think that’s how you manipulated the votes.
Your little foot-tapping signal so your pawns could vote in your favor.”

He stepped closer. Each word like a dagger.

“But mannequins don’t vote. Puppets don’t have choices.
They’re not ‘audience’, Satome.
They’re decor.”

Satome trembled.

“You used Fido,” he continued, voice calm again, but venomous.
“He’s the one who truly rigged the votes.
The guy behind you simply pointed out your hand.
And Fido… adjusted the numbers.”

Fido flinched, ashamed.

“I gave him a Morse code. He didn’t understand it.
But he knew I’d lose. Every time.
Because he was the one picking the winners.
A role you hid behind your fake Morse smokescreen.”

He turned slightly, locking eyes with Satome.

“You played a puppet show.”
He grinned.
“But you forgot one thing.”

“…W-What?”

“I don't gamble to win.I gamble… to break the game.”
*********

“You… you…”
Satome’s voice trembled, her throat constricting with rage and humiliation.
She couldn’t even choke out a full sentence.

“You bastard!!”

“Fufu… what happened, Satome-chan?” Baku tilted his head mockingly. “Lost your composure?”

“Shut up!! Oi, Fido! You trai—”

“Forgive me, Satome-san,” Fido said, stepping back. His voice was soft, but resolute.

“Wait,” Baku interjected, raising a hand casually. “Let’s play another round.”

Satome froze.
Her eyes widened—not from hope, but confusion.

Another round?
Why? Another setup?
Another slap in the face?

But Baku’s voice was calm. Too calm.
“I’m giving you one last chance to win your money back. Fair and square,” he said.
“No tricks. No audience votes. Just a normal game of rock, paper, scissors. One-on-one. But…tge draw results in the win of previous winner.”

She stared at him, searching for malice behind that relaxed face.
But saw none.

“…Fine,” she said after a pause, voice laced with tension.
She sat down with sudden seriousness.
She wouldn’t lose.
She couldn’t.

---

Two minutes.

That’s all they had to draw their hand.
She sketched furiously.
Paper? Rock? Scissors?

What if he used rock again?
But what if it was bait?

Her thoughts raced like a storm.

Then—

Baku raised his hand early.
With a calm smile, he revealed his choice: Rock.

“I’m using this,” he said.
“Draw paper and win back your cursed money.”

Her pen paused.
Her breath hitched.
She wanted to believe him.

But how could she?
This was Baku Madarame.
The liar.

Then she noticed something.
Just beside his foot—
A discarded hand from a previous round.

He picked it up.

It was… scissors.

And everything clicked.

“He’s bluffing.”
“He’s going to switch at the last moment.”
“This is the real trap. He’s baiting me with fake honesty.”

She smirked, confidence returning to her eyes.
“You’re not fooling me again, Baku,” she thought.
“I know your kind. Liars like you can’t change.”

---

Time’s up.

Fido stepped forward.

“Both show your hands,” he said.

The tension was palpable.
They revealed their hands slowly, deliberately, like two samurai drawing blades at dawn.

Satome: Rock
Baku: …Rock

Her pupils dilated.
Her brain stuttered.

“He… told the truth?”
“He didn’t switch?”
“He really used Rock…? That means draw…that meant…”

Baku chuckled darkly.
“Oh, you thought I’d swap it out last second with that fake hand?” he said.
“Come on, Satome… I’m not that big of a liar.”

Fido didn’t hesitate.
His voice was proud, loud, and thrilled.

“The winner is—BAKU-SAN!!”

---

Satome sat frozen.

Her breath uneven.
Her hand trembling as she passed him the money.

“You… you…”

He leaned in, smiling—not cruelly, but with razor wit.

“I am a very big liar,” he whispered.
“But today, I didn’t need to lie.”

And with that—

He turned, coat fluttering behind him, walking out the door.

Fido followed closely, eyes glowing with pride.

Rock. Paper. Scissors.
Winner: Baku Madarame.

******

Baku walked out of the room with Fido, 20 million yen in hand.

“Thanks for the help, Fido-chan.”

“Y-You're welcome. But… how did you know I was the one voting?”
