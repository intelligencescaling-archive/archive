CHAPTER 0: Reality or Fantasy

Corpse. Blood. Fire.

Not this dream again.

I have to think fast.

The sky is choked with smoke—black clouds heaving, streaked with orange and ash. On the horizon, flames lick the edges of crumbling towers like dying giants. Around me: mud, blood, the shattered remains of men. Bodies half-buried in muck, limbs twisted into grotesque shapes. Eyes locked open in frozen horror.

The air is hot and suffocating. Every breath tastes like metal and char. The stench hits me hard—burnt hair, spoiled meat, scorched leather. It clings to everything. It is everything.

I look down.

Still in my usual armor. It looks like the Elite Knight Set from Dark Souls, but different—scarred, soot-streaked, streaked with something red and fresh. I reek of death.

Look like "me" in this world hasn't taken care of myself in years.

In my right hand: a sword.

The blade is chipped, blackened at the edges—but solid. Steady.

My left—

Where’s my left hand?

My left arm is gone.

And it's not just the pain—it's the blood.

It doesn’t trickle. It pours.

Thick, hot, and dark—almost black in the dying light. It surges from the mangled stump in wild, uneven spurts, in time with my panicked heartbeat. Each pulse is like a scream from inside my chest, each beat a betrayal.

I can feel it running down my side, soaking into the ruined leather of my armor. The blood isn't smooth—it's clotted in places, sticky and warm, but already cooling at the edges. It pools under me, mixing with mud, with ash, with someone else’s blood already half-dried.

The edge of the wound is ugly, torn, and not clean. This wasn’t a blade. This was a force. Something ripped it off—explosion, crush, teeth? I don't know. I can’t remember. All I know is that the meat around the shoulder is flayed back like something chewed its way out.

I press my right hand against the stump.

Bad idea.

The moment I touch it, pain spikes again—fresh, feral, sharp as glass shoved under the skin. I press anyway, harder. I can feel the pulse thudding beneath my fingers, feel the heat, the slipperiness, the panic.

There’s so much blood. Too much. It’s leaking out of me, carrying time with it. Seconds. Minutes. Life.

I start to shiver, though the world is burning. My vision blurs at the edges. The roaring in my ears isn't fire—it’s the sound of my body shutting down.

I’m bleeding out. Fast.

*Whish-whish-whish.*

That sound.

Faint at first, but building fast.

Arrows. Slicing the air. A deadly rain.

Then—

A voice cuts through the chaos:

“Held!”

That’s me. Or… the name I go by in this dream.

I look up.

A man stands ahead of me, silhouetted against flame. His armor looks like mine, but newer, less worn. Less lived-in. His cheekbones are sharp, skin stretched tight over a gaunt face. His eyes are wide, terrified. His breath is shallow. Fast.

He’s new to war. I can see it in how he shakes.

His face twists—confusion, panic, pleading.

“Please…”

“…protect me. I don’t wanna die—!”

*Thwock!*

A sharp snap. Sudden. Final.

Blood splashes my cheek—warm. The man freezes. His mouth gapes. An arrow sticks out from his eye, the shaft jutting grotesquely forward through the socket. The arrow, its wicked tip skewered his eyeball, still fresh, the optic nerve dangling like a severed wire. Blood and viscous fluid oozed down the shaft in slow, sick drips. He crumples, armor clanking as he drops into the mud at my feet.

The arrow came from behind.

More are coming.

No time to mourn.

I grab his corpse and use it as a shield.

*Thwack! Thwack! Thwack!*

Arrows punch into the body—cold, indifferent.

I yank one out: standard arrow, peened chisel tip. It has enchantment, which looks like the increased power of the shot. The mana... 3x the power. The wound is 15 cm deep. This time is medieval, and the wielder hypothetically is a generally physically fit individual... 120 J, with enchantment... 360 J. The initial speed at 360 J is 141.4 m/s. And with velocity decay, no wind, or gravity, penetrates the steel plate, leaving a 15 cm deep wound... At least 300 meters. That was at least 300 meters, 3 football fields. Can I make it? I don’t know.

No. I must.

I sling my sword across my back, lift the corpse like a wall, and charge toward the source.

*Thwack! Thwack! Thwack!*

Arrows are still flying. 250 meters.

*Squelch! Squelch! Squelch!*

They're punching through now. I need to move faster. 198 meters.

Tink! Tink!

They’re glancing off the armor. Just barely. 100 meters.

*Crack! Thwack!*

Pain explodes in my right hand. One arrow didn’t just pierce the body I held—it punched through and impaled my hand behind it.

I scream inside. My grip nearly falters. It hurts badly.

I want to die.

No. Not yet.

Only 7 meters now.

I bite the shaft and yank it free. Blood fills my mouth—metallic, salty. But warm. Comforting, even.

Voices—panicked, shouting:

“What is this madman? He didn’t stop!”

“I didn’t come to war to meet this.”

“Keep shooting! He’s just a man!”

There they are. Two archers.

I use everything I have, throwing the corpse at one. He stumbles.

In the same instant, I draw my sword and stab through both of them. He screams, rises like smoke.

“AAAGH!”

The first archer collapses, impaled.

But my sword’s stuck. Too deep. I can't pull it free.

The second archer draws his bow, aiming—

“You bastard!”

Finally…

Death.

...

No.

Why can I still see?

Why is everything not black?

Not again.

My body moved on its own—dodged the shot.

F\*ck, I still have to live.

I charge. He’s reloading—two seconds.

No weapon left.

What do I have? My teeth.

Before he can finish knocking the arrow, I grab him. Pull his head back.

“NGHH—!”

He doesn’t finish. I tear into his throat with my mouth—rip his trachea clean.

Blood floods out. Salty. So much of it.

He’s on the ground now, hands on his neck, gasping.

He’ll die soon.

I turn.

The archer is still alive. Barely.

He’s impaled on my sword, blood bubbling from his gut. His mouth twitches as if trying to form words that won’t come.

Then finally—

A gasp.

A word.

“P–please…”

His voice is thin, broken glass underfoot.

“Don’t kill me… I—I didn’t mean to. I was just following—”

Of course, he was. Orders. Commands. Rules. Always someone else’s will. Never his own.

His hands shake as he reaches toward me. There’s no weapon in them—only open palms slick with blood and desperation.

“I have a family. A son. I didn’t choose this. You understand, right? Please…”

I stare.

He thinks I decide this.

He thinks mercy or death lives in my hands.

Fool.

I am not justice. Not a god. Not even a man, maybe. Not here. Not in this place. What I am—

Is will.

Not mine, though.

Something older. Colder. Unfeeling.

Nietzsche was wrong in only one thing: the will does not want power.

The will is power.

And it doesn’t need a reason. It doesn’t ask. It doesn’t justify. It acts.

I grip the hilt. His eyes widen.

“No—no—no, please! You don’t have to—!”

I do not have to.

I do it anyway.

I twist the blade.

Slowly.

His scream is sharp, sudden, and then—

Gone.

The light in his eyes snuffs out like a candle under boot.

Silence, again.

Warm blood coats my hand.

I do not pray. There is no sorrow in me. This isn’t vengeance. Not justice. Not even survival.

The line is broken.

I stand alone among corpses and flames, their blood soaking into the dirt, into my armor—into me.

The archers are dead.

Their outpost is shattered.

Their screams still echo somewhere in my skull.

Far behind, I hear the rumble—

Metal boots pounding through mud, a thousand strong.

My army.

We were waiting for this. For me. To tear a hole open in their wall.

Now they can charge through it.

I climb the ridge just beyond the archers’ bodies.

The wind hits me—thick with ash and smoke—and I raise my arm, the one that still works, and signal.

A single red flare burns across the sky.

Seconds later:

The warhorns sound.

A thunderous roar erupts behind me as our forces pour into the breach—blades drawn, banners torn, eyes hungry for blood.

A tide of steel and fury.

I drop to one knee.

Not from pain. Not yet.

From exhaustion. From silence.

From knowing I’ve done what I was created—no, programmed—to do.

Below, the enemy’s camp explodes into chaos.

Men scrambling, some half-dressed, others screaming orders no one obeys.

My soldiers crash into them like wolves into a pen of lambs.

“Held! Held!!”

Voices call my name as they run past.

Some with awe.

Some with fear.

Some like they’re chanting the name of a god they’ve only heard of in nightmares.

I don’t answer.

There’s nothing left to say.

Not in this dream.

I watch as one soldier drags a screaming man from a tent and stabs him again and again until he stops twitching.

Another lights a cart on fire just because he can.

This isn’t war anymore.

It’s slaughter.

A storm I unleashed.

And somehow, I’m the eye of it.

The fires are dying now.

What’s left of the enemy is either running or burning.

I remain still.

The blood of dozens cakes my armor, drying in thick, rust-red layers.

My left side feels… hollow. Not just from blood loss. From absence.

That’s when I heard her voice—

soft, cutting through the ruin like a silver blade:

“So… you really were alive in there, Held.”

I turn.

A woman walks toward me, her robes untouched by mud or ash.

White cloth flowing like mist, embroidered with glowing runes that hum as she moves.

She wears no armor—only conviction.

Her eyes, a pale violet, shimmer like stars barely held back by gravity.

Her presence is both warm and sharp, like remembering someone you’ve never met.

The Wizard. I know her. I’ve seen her before in other dreams—always watching from afar, hands folded like she was praying for something long dead. But now she’s here.

Real. Close.

She kneels in front of me.

“You shattered their line. Without you… They would’ve butchered us.”

“You saved them.”

She reaches toward me, but her fingers freeze mid-air.

Her smile fades.

She sees it.

The place where my left arm should be.

“Gods…”

Her breath catches. Something cracks in her expression. Pity? No, grief.

“You shouldn’t have survived this long like that.”

She closes her eyes. Murmurs words I can’t understand. Her hands begin to glow—soft white at first, then searing gold.

I recognize the runes.

Regeneration.

A high-level spell. Forbidden in some circles.

But this isn’t healing.

This is rewinding time—forcing flesh to remember what it once was.

And to feel every second of it returning.

Over, and over, and over, and over...

Her hand hovers inches from the wound.

Arcane syllables slip from her lips, glowing like molten gold in the air.

Pain hits.

Not like before.

Not like an arrow. Not like fire.

Worse.

Every nerve, every cell, every atom screams.

My knees almost give, but I stand.

Muscle twists. Bone groans. Tissue reweaves itself violently as if clawing its way back into existence.

And in my head—

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

――It was repeated endlessly.

But I don't scream. I don’t flinch. My face is stone.

The wizard watches me. Her lips still moving, voice steady—but her eyes tremble. She knows how much this hurts.

She knows it’s a miracle I’m still standing.

But she doesn’t stop. Neither do I.

The pain climbs higher, like knives dragging across nerves made of fire. My spine arches, jaw clenches hard enough to crack teeth.

But still—

No sound. No tears. Only breathe. In and out. Controlled. Buried.

The voice inside keeps screaming.

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

*Pain, red, scary, why, death.*

Then—

It’s done.

The magic fades.

Light dims.

And my arm… is whole.

Flesh restored. Skin clean, unscarred. Stronger, somehow.

Alive.

Voice is there, cutting through the storm:

“Let’s keep going.”

Her hands never leave me.
