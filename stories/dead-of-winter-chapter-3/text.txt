Chapter 3: Insight and Conflict

        The next parts of this narrative require some contribution from available sources that I managed to procure after the case itself was resolved. One of the sources in question was the casebook of Detective Portsman, which I managed to persuade him over a luncheon to allow me to incorporate into a more complete overview of the case and his investigative methods. As such, I shall be integrating excerpts from his rather dense notes into my descriptions. I will warn any readers that Detective Portsman’s descriptive abilities are discontinued past terse, clinical definitions, including the passage of time. Henceforth, I present the interview conducted by Detective Portsman of Mr. and Mrs. White:

December 16th, 7:55 AM - 119 Windsor Road

        I left the scene of Messrs. Carrow and Barnett feeling immensely dissatisfied. Although I think I had composed an interesting hypothesis of the second murder, it being that of an imitator, we still had yet to explain the pattern dictating the first and third. Damn it, I might have expected all the victims, and as such, members of the gang, to have something in common. They were all male, but that hardly narrows it down. Mr. Saxon, if unlikeable, was an influential figure! He was wealthy, he had connections. But Mr. Carrow was an extremely poor man who should not have had much of anything to do with the gang. Unless… perhaps he was some sort of intermediary? That theory could work. If he engaged with the enforcers of the gang who got their hands dirty, then the respectable gentlemen in the higher echelons of this society would naturally need a man like him. If he gained nothing from this exchange, maybe even coerced, it would be logical for him to betray the cult.

8:10 - Wright Building

        I went immediately to the Wright building, an offshoot of the Driskill hotel. Both of the Whites were staying there, reasonable given the resources at their disposal. I thought they might know something about the late Mr. Barnett. An embellished building, definitely heavily inspired by the romanesque design of the parent hotel, but those details are practically irrelevant to the case.
        The prologue and interview itself went as follows:
        “Mr. White?” I said, knocking on the door. I thought I may have heard a small noise in the room before it was opened by Mr. White wearing a scarlet smoking jacket and holding an unmistakable H. Uppman cigar.
        “What is it?” He said, cupping his hands in front of the cigar as he held it to his face. His hands were slightly pale compared to when I had seen him last.
        “May I come in? I have some questions.”
        He sighed and pouted for a split second.
        “Fine. Come inside. This better be quick after you forced me out like that before.”
        “Yes, my apologies, it was protocol.”
        I couldn’t quite make out what he responded under his breath.
        “What was that you said, Mr. White?”
        He sat in a large cushioned armchair made of mahogany wood and bit into the end of his cigar.
        “Nothing. So what happened? You must be here for some reason.”
        “Before I begin, do you know where Mrs. White is?”
        “She went out for some groceries. For how nice this place might be, the food selection isn’t very good. Breakfast should have happened half an hour ago!”
        “Hmm. She went out for groceries, you say? How long ago was that?”
        “Just a few minutes before you arrived,” he said, tossing the mangled remains of the cigar onto a silver ashtray with many other remnants.
        I took a step closer to Mr. White.
        “You will have to come up with a better lie than that. Where is she?”
        Mr. White froze.
        “What the hell are you talking about?”
        “Tobacco isn’t very good for one’s appetite. There aren’t any significant indents in the mat in front of the door. It seems like you might have been raising your hands from how pale they were when I stepped in. She is in this apartment, isn’t she? You just pushed her into the adjacent room.”
        Mr. White clenched his fist and loosened it.
        “Yes.”
        “You didn’t necessarily want anyone, not just me, to see what was happening inside this room. I advise you to choose your next words very carefully, Mr. White.”
        He gritted his teeth.
        “She is in the bedroom right now. It is the door to your left. How my wife and I handle our relationship is none of your business.”
        “It very well can be if you don’t cooperate, so I recommend you stay truthful.”
        He said nothing but bit his lip before nodding.
        “So. What do you know about Mr. Barnett?”
        He raised his eyebrows in surprise.
        “Mr. Barnett? He worked at Ward & Clews with Mr. Saxon. I barely know anything about him, I’ve already told you that Mr. Saxon practically never spoke of his job.”
        “Nothing?”
        “Nothing.”
        “Have you ever heard of anyone named Ronald Carrow?”
        He looked up to the ceiling.
        “He sounds vaguely familiar. Was he in construction at one point?”
        “Yes, he was a hod carrier.”
        “Then yes, I was acquainted with him. I invested in a construction company a few years ago and had to go downtown. He came up to me asking for a minor job, which I gave him. I don’t know anything else about him so don’t bother asking.”
        “I see. Has Saxon’s last will been put into effect?”
        “Not yet, but it isn’t as though I don’t know what I am getting. Saxon was quite clear when he was alive that I would be inheriting the ranch itself. He left everything else to his sister. You could say he was being generous to me, but I would like to think he only left me the ranch because he knew that the property tax and upkeep would be a thorn in my side.”
        Very interesting. Besides a grudge, we now have a physical motive for Mr. White to kill Mr. Saxon. It also explains his insistence on being the ‘de facto’ head of the ranch when I began this investigation. He might pretend that the ranch would be a burden, but no matter what, it would be a valuable piece of property he could sell. But this also opens a possibility for the murder to have been committed by Mrs. White. Extremely improbable, but possible. There is technically a motive, but it currently lacks psychological support. I initially believed that Mr. White might lack the needed will, but his behavior was concerning.
        I went to the side door and opened it to locate Mrs. White who was sitting on the linen bed, red-faced. She stood up.
        “Detective Portsman? What happened?”
        “Did you know Mr. Carl Barnett?”
        “He worked with my husband, no? He mentioned him to me occasionally, but I am afraid there is not very much information that I can share. He seemed to be a kind soul based on my husband’s description, but I never personally met or saw him.”
        “He’s dead.”
        Mrs. White blanched and put a hand to her mouth.
        “What?! Oh, his poor family.”
        She paced across the room with her shoulders hunched low.
        “Do you know someone, anyone, who might have had something to do with him?”
        She sat down and exhaled deeply.
        “I am not sure… I- another murder? I just need some time.”
        “Take your time.”
        She scratched her fingernails against the fabric and moved her lips ever so slightly.
        “W-well, I do not know anyone in particular who had something against him. Mr. Saxon would often mention those who he found unpleasant or unsuccessful to me. Perhaps one of those men became disillusioned and envious…”
        I took out my notepad.
        “Are there any names you can remember?”
        She sat ruminating for a moment.
        “There was Mr. Johnny Blake, who blundered on a major stock deal a few months ago. Nathan Montague and Francis Cantor, a duo who miswrote many transactional papers.”
        “Thank you, Mrs. White. Is there anything else you would like to share with me?”
        “Now that I think of it, I misplaced a few articles of jewelry, although that was over a month ago. I do not believe it will be relevant, but it may be worth noting. I believe that is all.”
        I carefully checked the door behind me before turning back to her.
        “I do not approve of Mr. White hitting you.”
        She cast her eyes downward before massaging her shoulders.
        “Detective, I think that is beyond the scope of this discussion. I appreciate your concern, but… sometimes he behaves like that. It is what it is. Please do not bring up this topic any further.”
        I sighed.
        “This is not a light-”
        “Stop,” she said firmly.
        “Very well. But do not hesitate to call the authorities if you feel unsafe. You may do what you want in your relationship with him, but when it leaks into legal territory, such as assault, it is my job to deal with it.”
        She said nothing, opting to stare at the sheets instead. I returned the favor and left the room without another word. Mr. White was waiting for me in the living room.
        “So how did that go, Detective?” he said caustically.
        I swept back my coat to reveal my holster to him.
        “Watch yourself, Mr. White. I could not care less about your sarcasm, but I will be waiting when you overstep.”
        Mr. White cast a grimace and went back to chewing on a cigar as I left the apartment. I have no patience for those men who think they are above the rest, and as much as I regretted dealing with him, I had possibly obtained invaluable information for the case. Upon returning to the station, I immediately called my assistants to find out more about this trio of men as it was our only real lead up to this point. At that moment, it also struck me that perhaps I might need to spread out my search. Based on my previous conclusion with the second authentic letter, it seemed that the killer or members of the group were present at the initial crime scene, as they knew the connection between Morris and I. Officer Lawrence was someone who was in attendance and more crucially, was the only confirmed individual who was around Carrow when he died. It did not constitute definitive evidence, but it was possible he was involved. He could have easily pricked Carrow. Despite his position as an officer, I could not let that eliminate him as a possibility and so I sent a man to watch him. I hope he will understand after this horrid matter is resolved.. Morris himself was included in this group, one of the reasons why I sent him back to his abode. Besides being a more defensible position if he would be attacked, it would make him easier to observe.
        But what to do now? There was nothing to do besides waiting, and as such, I was on standby for two days before an extremely unexpected incident.

December 18th, 9:10 AM - Austin Police Station
 It was the early morning of the 18th, perhaps an hour after I started my shift. I was in my office organizing my papers on a previous case I had been working on, as the culprit was still awaiting trial. My secretary, Mr. McKinnon knocked on my door with great haste and burst inside.
        “Detective! Officer Kant is on the receiver. It doesn’t sound very good.”
        I rushed to the police radio and lifted the tab to my ear.
        “This is Detective John Portsman.”
        I heard Officer Kant’s voice crackle on the end of the line.
        “Portsman! I’m calling from the phone box at the corner of seventh and Collins. Blake is dead. Stabbed in the heart, it seems like.”
        I bit my lip to hold back an oath. How was this even possible?
        “How in blazes did you let him die?”
        “There was nothing I could do. It seems like they got him at the shift rotation when I swapped with Freeman at dawn. In the past murders, they infiltrated the house through the front door. It seems like they’ve been doing a bit of observing themselves and analyzing our shifts. I found the back door to be broken in, very professional-like.”
        “Fine. I will be there within the next quarter hour.”

9:23 AM - 220 Harland Street

        I rushed downtown to the crime scene to see Officer Kant pacing up and down in front of the residence.
        “Status of the residents?”
        He flicked a cigarette from his hand.
        “He lived with his sister, who thankfully didn’t awaken during the murder. This case could have been much bloodier, although we might have caught him had she notified the neighbors.”
        I glanced around the front yard, taking careful note of the surroundings.
        “I doubt it. Assuming he entered and exited through the back door, he could have easily made an escape through the bushes onto another property. Given that the man was likely a professional, he probably had plain clothes on his person. He could easily stick the blade into the earth as he left, and as you may have noticed, his footprints are unnaturally flat and light. They are wider than one might expect, so as to disperse weight more effectively and leave an even more insignificant footprint. These are damning in of themselves, so I doubt he would have held onto them in escape. They were most likely covers worn on the soles of his actual shoes, which could have been easily disposed of.”
        I leaned closer to the ground to analyze the markings.
        “They are strangely indented. An average person makes an indentation with their back foot when stepping forward then another in the front when pushing off for another step, which creates the effect of a stride. Here, there is a more pronounced indent in the front, as if he was leaning on the front of his legs or bending his knees to simulate an awkward gait. He was trying to hide his height in the event someone would try to make such a calculation.”
        I stood up and scanned the ground ahead once again.
        “He doubled back to retrace his footprints in case they left a mark, but he slightly overcompensated. They lead back to the bushes bordering the house on the left, but that would be inaccurate. He went back the way he came, near the rear geraniums, but he pushed through the greenery. It’s possible, albeit very unlikely, that he was spotted by a neighbor.”
        Officer Kant raised the brim of his hat.
        “Never seen anyone like you, Detective Portsman, and I doubt I will. I took his sister out on the porch if you want to speak to her. I’ll ask the neighbors if they saw anyone.”
        “Good. There is no sense in setting up a perimeter at this point, as he is likely long gone.”
        I went up the yard to see a woman sitting on the porch who had a frozen expression on her face.
        “Ms. Blake?”
        She didn’t respond.
        “Ms. Blake?” I repeated. She seemed to become cognizant of my presence and turned her dark eyes to me.
        “Hello,” she said in a neutral tone.
        “I am Detective Portsman. I have some questions about your brother’s death.”
        She looked down at her feet.
        “Death?”
        “Ms. Blake, are you feeling quite alright?”
        She stood silent and swayed.
        “I… can’t believe it. How could I have not intervened? If… I could have stopped him-”
        “Sit down, Ms. Blake.”
        She acquiesced and sat with her arms folded.
        “I am extremely sorry that this occurred, but I need to know if you had observed anything abnormal before the murder?”
        Ms. Blake appeared to not hear me.
        “It doesn’t feel real.”
        “Yes, sometimes when a person experiences great loss, they have a surreal awareness for some time. It will pass.”
        “But… what if-”
        “I am afraid I must interrupt you, Ms. Blake. The man responsible was a consummate expert in his field. There was absolutely nothing you could have done to improve your brother’s situation. In fact, by staying asleep, I might argue you spared him any pain that would have resulted if he was awakened by you. You are not responsible for this tragedy, but with your assistance, we may be able to discover who it was.”
        She maintained her expression and nodded after a short period.
        “So, what do you have for me, Ms. Blake?”
        She opened her mouth but quickly shut it. Her lip quivered briefly before stiffening.
        “Last night, we were taking a short walk together. We used to do it every morning before Johnny got a job at Ward & Clews, which demanded he be there a bit earlier. Since then, we shifted the schedule to the evening. I didn’t notice anything out of the ordinary, whether physically or behaviorally. We went to bed in our respective rooms as usual. I… thought I may have heard something in the night, but I didn’t investigate the sound. I woke up at about dawn when I went to retrieve some water, as I was parched. I had an odd… intuition and looked into Johnny’s quarters…”
She abruptly cut herself off.
“After…after that, I went outside. I knew I required help even though I didn’t want to believe it. A man in plain clothing approached me and asked what had occurred, which I told him. He told me to wait here and he hastily ran down the sidewalk.”
        “What was the noise you heard? Could you describe it or where you heard it from.”
        “No, I don’t believe so.”
        “Hmm. Is there anyone who you believe may have been responsible for this?”
        “Regretfully, no. He was a tad bit envious of Mr. Barnett, so possibly they had a feud, but Mr. Barnett was murdered.”
        I sighed.
        “Thank you for your time, Ms. Blake. I will be analyzing the crime scene.”
        I navigated to two bedrooms next to each other and opened one of the askew doors to show the impression of a body under some linen sheets. It was Mr. Blake. A scarlet blot surrounded a small incision in the sheets where the blade had penetrated his body. I stripped the blankets covering his corpse and looked at the wound. It was an extremely clean cut and matched the triangular archetype of the original murder. I raised his hand and noticed a small paper which had been inserted below his pillow. I raised the paper and unfolded it.

        Using our title for one’s own crime is a despicable practice.

        Veritas Rubra

        Following these words was the alchemical motif that had thus far been characteristic of the cult. The letter seemed to imply that Mr. Blake had committed a crime in their name despite not having been involved. Could it be that Mr. Blake had Mr. Barnett killed? It would explain the inconsistency of the second crime scene. Clearly this group disliked those who might copy them and wish to be credited with those acts they have definitively committed.
        But back to the matter at hand. There was no bruising from strangling around Mr. Blake’s mouth and judging by the presence of rigor mortis in the extremities but not the larger muscles, he had been dead for anywhere from two to four hours. He had not been smothered then stabbed, so I would expect that his mouth was only covered to prevent outburst during the actual stabbing. It was possible that he had bitten into whatever the assassin used, so I peeled back his lips to examine his teeth. As it turned out, I discovered a black fiber, probably composed of some sort of organic collagen, most likely dark leather. Not many handkerchiefs are made of leather, so I would think that the culprit’s gloves were composed of this substance.
        Seemingly, Mr. Blake had purposely used the name of V.R. to misdirect investigative efforts, but it backfired horribly for him. However, how had Veritas Rubra actually determined that he was the impersonator? I highly doubt he went himself given that he is a broker by trade and given my perspective of his physical characteristics, I am unsure if he could have brutally overpowered another man. No, he had definitely sent an emissary. If he did indeed go about this course of action, there must have been a transaction that occurred. It would be very possible that he went in person but I think it would have been safer for him to send a telegram. If I could access the telegraph archive of Western Union, then there could be a wealth of information available for tracking down the mercenary who committed the second murder and even to establish a connection to V.R. given that we know they were aware of such a message.
        In addition, this development led to a unique opportunity. If V. R. values the proper use of their cult, what if this death was credited to another person? It seems that they might try to unearth his identity to kill him. Maybe I can construct a situation to my advantage given this information. I went outside to see Officer Kant once again.
        “I may need your assistance for a distinctive plan that could bear fruit.”
        He shrugged.
        “What do you plan to use me for?”
        “As bait, if possible.”
        “Excuse me?”
        “You will be perfectly safe, Officer Kant. All I require is for you to act as a resident of a specific building for a few days.”
        I called for the commissioner once I returned to the station and suggested that a certain tactic be executed using rented houses. I planned to give credit to an as of yet unknown killer who had left a note there. V. R. would clearly know that the man responsible for taking credit would have had to have been at the crime scene, which would have only been possible for a police officer who was there. This will lead them to the conclusion that either Officer Kant or I had planted the paper. But what of Officer Lawrence? If he was an informant of Veritas Rubra he might spoil my plans, so I decided to inform him that this plan was a false one meant to misdirect Veritas Rubra's efforts and possibly fool them into thinking that Officer Kant was the killer. After this, I would tell him that in truth there was significant circumstantial evidence pointing towards a subsequent break-in and that we suspected the real killer was a different man who we required he watch. Little would Officer Lawrence know that his own actions would be monitored. If there was an attack on the second man, then Officer Lawrence would most likely be an informant for V.R. as he was the only one informed of that aspect of the plan and would operate on that level of information. If he was not, then there was no considerable harm done.

11:20 AM - Western Union Station

        After organizing the logistics of the plan with the commissioner, I headed over to the Western Union telegraph office. I went to a desk with a telegraph operator behind it and sharply rapped the wooden surface to get his attention. The man, a decrepit being, looked up at me.
        “I am Detective Portsman of the Austin Police Department. I wish to look over some telegraphs that could aid in investigating a crime in progress.”
        “Detective, eh? I am sorry to inform you that there is a privacy policy here. I am not permitted to simply show you our archives, even given your affiliation.”
        I slid a twenty dollar bill over the desk.
        “What I am doing will not hurt anyone, and I will get the information I desire one way or another. I simply am offering you an easier path for both of us.”
        He hesitated and blinked his baggy eyes.
        “I-I want your word that I will not be prosecuted if this gets out.”
        “You have that as a given. This is between you and me.”
        He stood up and escorted me to a backroom full of cartons.
        “This is our storage room. It should have every sent telegram that went through Western Union within the last month. I will give you an hour.”
        I rapidly used the alphabetical sorting system to locate the carton with the telegrams I desired, flipping through the files within to find Mr. Blake’s name. Bingham, Binns, Blake. There it was. September 20th, a telegram sent to a coworker at Ward & Clews about a business deal. October 3rd, a telegram detailing a conflict of interests within the company sent to a low class journal. October 16th… a telegram sent to a man named Evan Taylor detailing how to dispose of a product and listing a payment method for the man’s consultation. It was sent at just the right time and the language was analogous to the crime that had been committed. The telegraphic address was incomprehensible to me, so I copied the text into my notebook and returned it to the clerk. He lifted a dusty monocle to an eye and squinted at the text.
        “It’s a shorthand for Pflugerville, an extremely small suburban area up to the northeast. It’s mostly populated by German families although there is not much there. The exact address is 126 Pecan Street.”
        “Much appreciated.”
        I immediately detailed what my investigation had yielded in my notebook before hailing a cab to the small neighborhood. I felt exhilarated that I had actually acquired a proper foothold on this case given how slippery it had seemed before. If I could locate this mercenary, then the only detail left would be the enigmatic Veritas Rubra themselves, but I had a suspicion that the conclusion may not be as simple as I think. I could not let even a limited success cloud my judgement before the case truly concluded.
        “Here we are sir.”
        “Much obliged.”

12:46 PM - Pflugerville

        I stepped out of the cab and strode down the dirt street until I found the correct house, a dilapidated old structure. I knocked on the door and waited for a response, only for a man to walk up to me. He was wearing a torn up coat and had a beard snaking down his chin.
        “Herr, what are you doing? That house is abandoned, nobody lives there.”
        “Hmm. I am looking for someone named Evan Taylor and thought this is where he might reside.”
        The man stroked his beard.
        “Herr Taylor did live here, but that was years ago. He was a Geschaftsmann, an entrepreneur of sorts. Some of his mail still goes here, though it’s usually gone by the time the next day comes.”
        “Where did Mr. Taylor go?”
        “I don’t know. He left without informing anyone. He was an unattached man. Why are you looking for him?”
        “I had a business proposition for him,” I said, going closer to the man. “If you happen to know where his mail goes, I would be delighted to enlist his services.”
        The man looked over at me with searching eyes.
        “As I said, I don’t know where the mail goes, if that is what you’re looking for. For all I know, some street rascal takes it every night. I haven’t bothered checking and neither does anyone else, it’s generally not safe at night.”
        I tipped my hat.
        “Thank you, sir.”
        “You are welcome.”
        How strange. From this development, I assume that Evan Taylor has been using his disappearance as a cover for acquiring information. It would be perfectly plausible for those in this poor neighborhood to take advantage of abandoned mail, nor would they have a real incentive to misdirect any messages. Presumably, Mr. Taylor has someone to pick up his messages, and we may be able to exploit that link.
        Nevertheless, I needed to return to the station to further my schemes. I managed to procure another cab before dusk and a quarter of an hour later, I was pacing in my office. As I have established, I needed to establish a trap for the agents of Veritas Rubra, but they had two valid targets: Officer Kant and I. It was essential that Officer Kant be their only suspect, but I theorized that they had come to that conclusion already. I had already been involved in all the previous murders involving the cult and knew that crossing them, especially during an active investigation, would be incredibly foolish as I could be persecuted by the state for tampering with evidence or murdered by Veritas Rubra, whose work I was intimately familiar with. Officer Kant, however, had the lucky circumstances of having access to the crime scene before anyone else was present. Barring a direct outside observer, once the facts of the case came out, it would be assumed that he was the only one who could have distorted the evidence.

December 20th, 10:20 PM - South 3rd Street

        Two nights later, the implementation of my strategy was at hand. The previous morning, the press had released an exclusive story regarding the new murder and I knew that Veritas Rubra could not ignore such prey dangling in front of them. Officer Kant had just arrived at the domicile late that night, although he was by no means alone. From our previous operations, I was aware of the assassin’s methods. He would first observe to calculate the correct time when his victim would be solitary, then strike. I watched, concealed from any windows, from the kitchen to see Officer Kant pick up a novel and flip through it casually. He took a bite of an apple and reclined on the couch in the living room.
This was our second night through this routine and I could only pray that our adversary would finally appear. I waited for perhaps an hour, wondering if this would be another failed attempt until I heard a subtle scraping noise coming from the front door. One of my officers behind me lifted his Smith & Wesson, but I raised the back of my hand. If we revealed ourselves now, he might be able to escape. Officer Kant hardly noticed anything and a figure slowly stepped forward through the door. It was a stout, lithe man in thick, jet black clothing wearing a cloth mask. He navigated prudently with moccasin-like footwear, his footfalls almost entirely silent. As Officer Kant turned around, He flattened himself against the wall bordering the two rooms with unnatural agility. I became motionless, awaiting any sort of movement. I saw the flash of a keen blade exit the man’s sleeve and his muscles tense as he prepared for the killing blow. Officer Kant would not have even had time to draw his gun if the man got a chance to move, so I waved my hand and exited the shadows.
        “Stop!”
        The man froze for a fleeting moment and rapidly turned to the door, which I shot before he could open it.
        “That was your only and final warning. If you persist, the next bullet will strike you.”
        The man seemed to hesitate and he froze.
        “Drop the blade!”
        He released the knife from his hand and kicked it towards me.
        “Very good. Turn around and walk backwards towards me slowly with your hands raised above your head.”
        The man did so and I firmly grasped his arms with another officer’s help, putting handcuffs on him. I directed the man to a chair and took his mask off carefully, revealing a clean shaven middle aged man with salt and pepper hair. He had a square jaw and fiery, intelligent eyes.
“Who are you?”
He cleared his throat.
“I get it, the game is up. It won’t help me to hide information, exactly, so I don’t lose much by telling you. I am Evan Taylor. I worked as a businessman about three years ago, but I moved into a much more lucrative business.”
“Hired murder?”
“Correct. Pays a lot better than trying to sell some new idea.”
“Were you hired by John Blake to murder Carl Barnett?”
Evan Taylor rapidly darted his eyes around the room.
“Hold it, I’m only going to answer any more of your questions given one guarantee: I refuse to get the chair or dock. I don’t plan on dying anytime soon.”
“I will see what I can do. It could be framed as a deal.”
“Good. Let the record show I’m only stating this information under that assumption. I did kill Carl Barnett.”
“But why would you do that if Veritas Rubra would not approve of such a thing?”
Evan Taylor began laughing.
“Veritas Rubra? You better prepare yourself for the truth. Veritas Rubra never existed. I’m not sure why she decided to create it like that, but the point was to misdirect police efforts and make seemingly random deaths connected so the authorities would overanalyze the case. Or at least that’s what I think, I’m not paid to ask for clarification.”
For perhaps the first time in years, I was dumbfounded by a statement. So this trap proved that my suspicions for Officer Lawrence were unfounded.
“But… who killed-”
“I was responsible for all the deaths,” he said, interrupting me. “I don’t know why the client wanted those people specifically dead, but I got the job done.”
“What of Carrow?
He held a blank expression on his face.
“Except that one. I did not kill Carrow, nor do I know how he was killed.”
I pondered the issue. Neither the key assassin nor Officer Lawrence had killed Carrow, yet he was undoubtedly a part of the entire conspiracy. Neither of those present were responsible, meaning…
“Suicide,” I said aloud. “But I digress. Your communication system involves your old address, no?”
Evan Taylor nodded.
“Ha, you stumbled across that? I moved to Pflugerville because I thought the underdeveloped community could be a gold mine. I was right, but only because of how out of the way it was, making it an ideal place for messages.”
“You had men pick up your dropped mail, didn’t you? People would assume it was stolen.”
Evan smiled.
“Use your imagination. I never had anyone pick up the messages, only see what would happen to them at night. If they were taken and kept, I laid low since someone might be looking for me. If they were just thrown away, then they would look at and transcribe the messages for me a few days later. I’m sure you know the qualities of lemon juice, don’t you, detective? The letter would be sent to me with the messages within, written in lemon juice.”
“I would assume they were sent to a pseudonym and that the papers had false messages written on them? It would be very odd if someone was just sending blank pages.”
Evan clicked his tongue.

If it was for self defense, she would have used it if someone pricked her. If it was not in self defense, I would have assumed she used it to carve the ‘VR’ symbols, but a pin was used instead. The blade was not dulled, so if it had been used, it was for something soft. Hypothetically, if she was murdered, she must have anticipated her own death, ergo she knew her body might have been searched, so surely she would not hide anything incriminating her murderer on her body. This could all be coincidental, but… possibly she had hid something in her room? But then why did she not cry out or seek help if she knew she was in danger? Well, if I was on the run, I would not alert others to my position. Uncomfortable questions, such as how I knew I would be in danger would abound.
        It could be that she hid something in her apartment, a failsafe of sorts in case she was in danger. This line of reasoning would hardly hold under the lightest scrutiny, but maybe… just maybe… there was something at play here. I handed my findings to Officer Kant.
        “I must stop by her apartment again. There may be a clue I missed there. Take Mr. White back to his holding cell.”
        “Affirmative.”

12:20 PM - Clark’s Estate

        Before I knew it, I was back in her room, overturning every available object. There was nothing of significance, not in any of the drawers or the cabinets. I ran my fingers through my short hair. Had I gone mad? Was I simply overthinking the case? I crouched close to the ground and analyzed the carved letters. If I were Ms. Carter and I were in this position, what would be my thought process? I would use the pin to scratch out the letters as a clue, then… what? What would be my insurance? I take out some item which can be used as my leverage and hide it. But where? They would notice a dulled razor. What is close to me and consists of a material which is not rough? Oh my Lord. The carpet.
        I lifted the corner of the carpet and noticed several stitches across the edge. They could have been nothing, or… something. I slit them open with my pocket knife and maneuvered through the stitching with a finger. I felt a small paper and pulled it out. The ‘VR’ lettering was a decoy! It could simply throw off anyone, even her enemies who attempted to interpret it.
        With shaking hands, I unfolded the letter.

        With this, I name the man who allowed me to take on the mantle of his group and advised me throughout this endeavor. He allowed me to connect with Mr. Taylor and certain unscrupulous circles, although I am concerned he may seek to leave no loose ends. Let the record show that the person who killed me in the event of my death is none other than Charles Morris, a blind man who built significant influence in this town.

Anna Carter

        This revelation staggered me, but then it all made perfect sense. The stranger who entered the scene conveniently, had incredible epiphanies, then exited. It was the perfect cover. He was one of the few who knew I associated with him, he was obviously poor, but that could be remedied! What did he do immediately upon arriving at the scene? He insinuated himself in the household. He was trying to make himself indispensable. If he organized the case from the start, Mrs. White would soon become divorced from her husband and probably turn towards the poor, pitiful, but brilliant mind who comforted her. He could efficiently monitor the investigation, and no matter what, he would end up benefiting.
        What of this group she spoke of? Well, Mr. Morris was likely involved with Mr. Wilson, as indicated by the letter. I already know of Mr. Wilson’s possibly illicit connections, so it was conceivable that they were involved. So, a trap must be set. Mr. Morris could have no fear that anyone would make such a connection without this letter, so he will not expect me to use it against him. His meeting on the 25th will be grossly interrupted by my own department, and I will capture him and his entire group at once. No one except the commissioner will know of this discovery until then so as to hide my plans.

Detective John Portsman, signing off for now.

Now, dear reader, we may return to my own, rather verbose narrative that continues two days after Detective Portsman’s dramatic finishing statement. The previous evening, Morris had invited me to attend a particular show that would be playing in an uptown theater the following morning and I could not decline his offer. He seemed strangely tense, but also excited, as if some great plan was coming to fruition.
        I had hardly arrived at his door when he burst through it to greet me, one arm raised, with a grin upon his face. His clothing was cleaner than usual, free of creases.
        “Ah, Mr. Hopkins! I am glad to see that you have made it,” he said, twirling his walking stick with a flourish.
        “Of course. I would not miss it for the world.”
        “Admirable. Now, I have sent for a cab which may be arriving within the next few minutes. Would you be opposed to waiting outside with me?”
        “Not at all.”
        We went through the front door, a bout of warm air touching my face as the vehicles made their way past us. The rhythm of pedestrians was ever-present, despite the lower population of Austin compared to its sister towns. The strong scent of tobacco inundated the environment, not entirely unpleasant, but simply adding a certain flavor to the whole scene.
        “Morris, you have not told me many of your thoughts regarding the murders. Did you hear that they caught Mr. White?”
        Morris’s lips tightened, but his smile remained, almost like he wanted to laugh.
        “I did, indeed. However, my good friend Detective Portsman took to refrain from bringing me along to any more scenes. For my own safety, or so he says.”
        “I know. But… you seem as though you suspect some alternative?”
        “Very shrewd of you, Bill. I believe he may suspect me, as ridiculous as that theory sounds.”
        I nearly snorted.
        “Nonsense.”
        Just as I finished speaking, a peeling yellowish cab slowed down next to us, the engine still firing. The driver, an oily looking man with a goatee leaned out the window with an arm folded over the side door.
        “Mistah Morris?” he asked.
        “Him and his very good companion.”
        The man rapped his fingers on the dashboard and beckoned for us to enter. I helped Morris inside before entering myself. The car was a trifle bit warmer than the great outdoors, likely having been heated in the sun. Almost immediately upon entering the cab, Morris screwed the top off his walking stick and replaced it with a curved wooden handle that he had mysteriously procured. He expeditiously inverted his jacket, showing a sort of strange green lace up the hem and a series of shiny buttons going towards his sternum.
        “Morris… what are you doing?”
        “Bill,” he whispered in a low tone. “I have reason to believe that we are being followed, and as such, I am altering my appearance.”
        He took a strange lump from his pocket and thrust it out, revealing a squashed top hat, which he perched on his head. He removed a pair of glasses with a squarish orange lens and replaced his own with them.
        “But… why?”
        All throughout this odd exercise, Morris was still smiling at me.
        “Don’t worry, you are not in any danger. Please take my walking stick and use it. We shall walk arm in arm. Make sure to create a large berth in crowds so as to hide my blindness.”
        “Are we not seeing a show?”
        “Yes, but a bit later than anticipated. We have some business to take care of. Oh, and please don this bowler hat. It may be needed to prevent identification.”
        “Morris… I am not sure about this.”
        Morris simply looked at me with an expectant grin.
        “Have I ever led you wrong?”
        That was all I required to hear.
        “Very well.”
        Our cab raced through the streets with considerable speed, and I watched the low lying buildings fly past us like some great urban kaleidoscope, varying colors and individuals of all ranks. As suddenly as we had begun, the cab halted and we were dispensed onto what could be called a sidewalk near the local theatre. Morris steered me to the center mass of a crowd, and I could barely make out the snatches of conversation in the groups over the din. The square ended with a series of cars waiting for various people. One of the drivers waved at us.
        “Mister Gruber?” He called, signaling at Morris.
        “Absolutely.”
        The man carefully stopped his automobile to allow us to break off from the crowd. We had entered when Morris began changing his appearance once again.
        “So… Morris. What have you done this time?”
        “Well, it could be that Detective Portsman is searching for me with great gusto. He expects me to be at the Harrington Warehouse in a short period of time. I have no doubt that he is tracking us right now.”
        “What?!”
        “Bill, I advise you to look back at the development of this case. A well planned crime, no doubt, with extreme intricacy and handled with considerable finesse. Do you think they would not consider the blind man and his friend who take a walk by the scene of the crime almost every day, like clockwork? If they performed a basic investigation, they might be able to infer my reputation as a conniving person. They would know that I crave stimulation and puzzles, and what about letting the greatest case of the decade grace my fingertips? I could not resist contributing my insight to the case. But what then? If they knew I was going to be involved, how suspicious it would seem if a stranger was implicated in the case they otherwise had no involvement in. Indeed! A man with underworld connections being accused. I had a feeling I would be suspected at some point in this entire spiderweb of intrigue, it is what any logical criminal would do. So, I made preparations. The case continued to have a theme of plateauing before a separate culprit was found, so I had to subvert that plateau. If this culprit knew of my underworld connections, then the obvious, most efficient move would have been to frame me as a member of the group which opposed them, in this case, Veritas Rubra. If we think about the progression of the case in these terms, it is a reasonable conclusion as the culprit knew of Detective Portsman’s trap and even anticipated it by adding another narrative unbeknownst to Evan Taylor. Therefore, I believed that they may have anticipated my move by placing Mr. Wilson’s gang against their own. If we look upon this situation from their perspective, then the most efficient course of action for Morris would be to take that move, so once again, it would be fair to conclude that we may frame Morris as a member of that group, so as to turn his attempt to stop us against him. What they may not realize is that I had sent a telegram to Mr. Wilson, requesting a meeting on the 25th. This may seem irrelevant, but if you also take into account that I have been sending Mr. Wilson anonymous tips about police activity to build trust, then another message I sent, one stating that I am the leader of Veritas Rubra, that the group is indeed real, although has had to back up due to general pressure, frames the telegram in an entirely different light. Now Mr. Wilson believes I am trying to eliminate him by luring him into a trap to alleviate my own risks. He will obviously see this as an opportunity to kill me by sending his own men, then defaming me with his tip, which will have been accurate, if not framing it as self defense. He will obviously disregard my contingency as he can simply dismiss it as defamation from a desperate murderer. What of Detective Portsman? I thought until the 25th may be enough time for the culprit to frame me, and I would not even have to worry about his wrath since then. Naturally, Detective Portsman would search through the Western Union Department, one way or another. Given his cautiousness, he would undoubtedly find my telegram and make the connection that I am meeting with my gang, but he will not reveal that information to the public as he will wish to set a trap for us, which would be most efficient by trapping all of us at once. He would not even use it as a preventative measure, as for all Detective Portsman knows, there will be no more murders as the case plateaued. He can only gain through this plan. Hence, we enter the endgame where Mr. Wilson will try to crush me at the warehouse and Detective Portsman will try to trap us there. Detective Portsman will arrest the men there, sparing me, and he will find a concealed docket there containing the information I had compiled against Mr. Wilson back in our previous adventure involving an interesting game of hangman. He will use that information against Mr. Wilson, who will have sent his vanguard to be captured and thus will be defenseless.”
        “Oh dear. What if Detective Portsman realizes this is a trap?”
        Morris delicately combed his hair.
        “He will not. I am sure he will be able to find out we had taken this cab, and he will then believe that we are attempting to flee. How foolish would Morris have to be to go to the very place that he deemed to meet Mr. Wilson! But there will be two cabs waiting for us on arrival at our destination. One will go to the edge of town, perfect for fleeing, but the other will go to the warehouse. Detective Portsman will be first misled to believe I went out of town, but then he will realize he has been fooled by a double bluff and attempt to follow us to the warehouse. Alternatively, if he tries to follow the one going to the warehouse, which will already take time to track down, he will end up right where I want him to be. As for Mr. Wilson’s group, I have had a locked room set up within the warehouse. They will assume I have concealed myself within it and it may delay them for some time. Unfortunately, the window of opportunity is very narrow, but it is what I can do.”
        “A masterpiece. But how did you manage these preparations whilst being observed?”
        “Ahh, what is man if not resourceful? There are beggars everywhere on the streets. Drop a coin with a folded note and they will do whatever you like, especially if it seems semantic on the outside.”
        I was captivated by the plan Morris had spun with only a few notes and a couple more fares.
        “The cab is slowing down. Prepare for our next stop, Bill.”
        We left the car behind and organized ourselves near a train station where there were a couple of cabs which honked at Morris. It occurred to me that Morris had likely altered his appearance each time to match a description he had given each one beforehand. We navigated to each one where he told the drivers to simply go off to their destination without any passengers. They were confused by his instructions, but finally agreed, leaving us in a cloud of fine dust.
        “What now?”
        Morris lit a cigarette and puffed on it once.
        “They cannot be far behind us. Go far to the left. We will walk about fifteen minutes to Cedar Springs Road. I have a car waiting for us there to take us to the wetlands.”
        “What are we going to do there? There is nothing but the old ranch there where this all began.”
        “The ranch, and one person who was released recently,” Morris said, releasing a small cloud of smoke.
        “You can’t mean-”
        “Mrs. White. But let us fly first.”
        I nervously walked alongside Morris, who was careful to obliterate our remaining footsteps before going on the sidewalk. The rural street next to us continued to wind until we came across a small taxi. The man inside it flicked out a cigarette stub and displayed a leathery hand on the side of the car. He had strikingly dark eyes and blinked them rapidly at both of us.
        “Better be one of y’all, I musta’ve been waitin’ all mornin’. Is one of you Villefort?”
        “That would be me.”
        “Get in.”
        He drove with blazing speed through the progressively narrow street until we finally arrived at the border of the wetlands. He nodded at me before allowing us to leave. Morris waited a minute or two for the man to depart then held out his hand for his walking stick, which I gave him. He brandished it like a sword and he followed me up the greenish mound to the ranch. The building was left just as it was near the beginning of my narrative, albeit with an additional air of unrest. The sycamore trees stood like large watchtowers, like some unseen figure was watching our progress from the shadows. I shrugged off these thoughts and knocked firmly against the door. At first, there was no answer. I knocked again, the vibrations resonating with my entire body. Something was at hand here.
        With that, the cold metallic handle turned sharply, and a vibrant woman, if somber, opened the door. Dolores White. She was wearing a loose fitting linen dress, as if symbolic of the freedom of her abusive husband, whose actions had been reported by Detective Portsman with much derision. She was only mildly surprised to see us.
        “Oh. Hello Mr. Hopkins. How are you doing, Mr. Morris? Please, come in for some tea.”
        Morris bowed respectfully.
        “Sure. It would be improper to decline such an offer.”
        She led us through the cavernous house to the dining room, where she had a china set sitting atop the wooden table, the light from the chandelier reflecting and refracting all over the room with the morning light. She stood over a pot of boiling water with several crushed herbs next to her in a golden tray.
        “What will it be?”
        Morris raised himself up and pursed his lips.
        “I’m afraid I have a matter of serious importance that needs to be discussed.”
        She laughed, an airy, light sound.
        “Oh, really? I never thought tea would be of such concern to you.”
        Morris gave a brief smirk.
        “Mrs. White… or rather Ms. Saxon, as will soon be your title… I know you killed your brother and husband.”
        Her radiant smile faltered.
        “I-I did not-”
        “I hate to correct you, but you did. You despised your brother, but at the same time, you appreciated his protectiveness. However, it was too much. Smothering even. You married Mr. White to escape him, but he became the antagonist of your life. Both of them grew bitter and pushed you over the edge. I know the truth as well. This… Ms. Carter never existed. She was a fabrication made by a desperate woman to test her husband’s loyalty. She was based off of a real person, whose identity I cannot attest to. You used a mortician's putty and your own acting skills to seduce your own husband. He failed your test, and so you came up with a plan to eliminate both of your problems. You pretended to have been robbed of your jewelry, which ended up in your alter ego’s hands. You used them to hire Mr. Taylor to commit multiple murders with your Veritas Rubra narrative. You purposely dropped the hint that the culprit was among those in the original investigation to frame your husband. You intercepted his letter to add a code, a breakable one at that, albeit extremely difficult. It was not a coincidence that Detective Portsman reported that Mr. White was abusing you. You set him up by provoking him. You faked your own death as your alter ego by inviting the woman who she was based off of here, then murdering her. Mr. Carrow? A poor, hopeless, but most of all, malleable man. You could convince him to kill himself. What would he say? He was a half step from bankruptcy, he may as well go out with a few more coins for whiskey. Now, you may have used a similar method for poor Ms. Carter, but I suspect you had someone else kill her. Someone… who had very little hope. A woman named Margaret Gutner, who had a short time on this earth anyway. It was probably easy to convince her by proxy to kill your model, then overdose on digitalis. I would guess you maybe gave her money for life insurance? Or better yet, gave her money for her children to receive in her will. It is amazing what people will do when they are hopeless. Oh, and what of Evan Taylor? You obviously planned for Detective Portsman to set a trap. You wanted him to become captured so you could frame yourself. At the end of the day, who would suspect the woman who had already been cleared of crime? Not to mention the false patterns you set. For a moment, I even believed I may have been mistaken. You opened so many possibilities for investigation, from Ward & Clews, to underground cults, to a fringe assassin. Sometimes I wonder how Detective Portsman came so far in his investigation. Maybe I shall ask him one day.”
        Mrs. White waited patiently throughout Morris's speech, her face becoming more and more scarlet.
        “Why-I have never heard such a bundle of offensive accusations before! I repeat that I did not kill my brother, or anyone else.”
        Morris put a hand on the table and exhaled.
        “You did. You did not want to, but you did.”
        “You don’t know how-”
        Morris raised a finger.
        “Yes, I do. I despise being so confrontational, especially towards a wronged woman, but I do know exactly how you feel. I have had no control over my life for nearly two years now, except a few unique circumstances. I know the suffocating feeling of being trapped, the pain of not being able to do what you wish to. That horrible sensation of having everything, all your potential, vanquished. Your radial artery betrayed you in our first meeting, Mrs. White. I already know that you are the culprit since it is not any of the others at the first crime scene. Your emotion was real, genuine, distress. The best acting is that of those who are not acting. Mr. White was a self-aware man, a coward who thinks he is able to dominate others. I knew from the outset that this family was dysfunctional. You were being truthful that you regretted Mr. Saxon’s death. In fact, if you could have had it any other way, I am sure you would have. He defended you, something you were clearly aware of, but it was too much. He treated his staff with tolerance, imagine how he treated the woman he grew up with!”
        Mrs. White sat with her eyes closed and cheeks rosy. When she lifted her head, there were tears streaming down her face.
        “It-it-it’s true. I am a monster.”
        Morris gazed with sightless eyes and a dignified expression.
        “No. You have done deplorable, horrific things, but you are not a monster. Not yet. It is not too late. You can confess your actions and detail everything to the police. There is a road to redemption, if long and hard. I will not say you are entirely vindicated by your circumstances, but it may contribute to leniency from both the court and God. I would be the monster here to prevent you from redeeming yourself, which would defeat the entire point of the agency which we both desire. The choice is yours.”
        Mrs. White waited as the boiling water hissed. She stood up and swallowed hard.
        “I will confess.”
        Morris grinned.
        “Then how about we enjoy some tea before the police realize what I have been up to? Be sure not to poison it, I will know.”
        A shadow of a smile appeared on her face.

        Our story resumes two weeks after this account, when Mrs. White, or rather Ms. Saxon entered a plea deal with Detective Portsman regarding her sentencing. It was agreed that she would not receive the death penalty in exchange for the expedition of her case, as there was virtually no evidence inculcating her involvement besides her confession. Parallel to the conclusion of the Rubedo case, Mr. Wilson was arrested shortly after his gang’s capture at the Harrington warehouse, where they were confronted by a dozen police officers.
        Although Charles Morris and I were detained upon being found at the ranch, we were released a few days later in light of the new evidence, along with a written apology from Detective Portsman. Mr. White was released, although he was charged with something relating to provocation. Morris took me to the theater, where our narrative finally approached its end.

        I sat next to Morris on the crimson cushions overlooking the show, an adaptation of Taming of the Shrew, which Morris was listening to with great interest. He occasionally asked a question of clarification to me before staying perfectly still and silent, as if he were an automaton. I could hardly focus on the narrative given the thoughts swirling around my head in the moment. The entire experience with the Rubedo case felt highly surreal, like a distant dream or a novel I read in my youth. Morris barely spoke of the case either, though he frequently sent letters to both Detective Portsman and, strangely enough, Ms. Saxon. I did not ask why and would not know why until a later adventure… but that is not within the ambit of this story, or at least this highly personal narrative.
