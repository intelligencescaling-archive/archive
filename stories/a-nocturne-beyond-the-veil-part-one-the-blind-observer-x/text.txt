**I present: A NOCTURNE BEYOND THE VEIL, PART ONE (TBO x Wildfire)**

As round as a coin, brighter than any lamp in the world and paler than the purest white, the luminous moon hovered above the mansion in the cool dark sky, outshining every one of the starry hosts competing against it. It stared down at the mansion eerily; Death had overtaken those inside, giving the moon a sufficient reason to weep bitterly. If only the transpired events were foreseen hours earlier…

—

Smatters of blinding light shot out of the never-ending rows of paneled windows, projecting yellow beams out into the darkening evening. Within the richly carpeted rooms, harmonious tunes floated through the air, pleasing the ears of the visitors; indeed, the magnificent sight of the grand orchestra amazed them, for who could afford to hire one in their own residence as the jewel of the building?

“It’s really nothing,” laughed the duke when one of the guests brought up the question.

The Duke of Hershire had long been known to throw extravagant parties, which were almost always held at his grandiose residence. Fitted with silk curtains at every arched window and filled to the brim with ornamentally carved furniture, one might’ve thought he was part of a royal family. However, while he usually invited guests of his own standing, his generosity stretched to the Director of the Revaltian Police Force this time and consequently, the men who made up the force as well as their families.

William Sigerson, a detective from Ecclesia, Revalty’s close ally, had been fortunate enough to attend this luxurious party through his connections with the Revaltian police, though he took part in the festivities with apparent half-heartedness. His rosy-haired cousin, however, her usually straight neat hair tied into an elegant knot, joined the others with great fervour, chatting away with the other ladies dressed in frilly clothing.

He sighed. Why, just why, did he allow himself to get dragged into this party by his eager cousin?

While he sat lamenting his wasted time, a curious sight met his eyes; two men, one donning a black greatcoat and another a brown suit wrapping around a clean vest, were glancing around, looking as if they too were wondering what they were doing there.

The calmer man from the pair, his coat-tails quivering and his tinted glasses gleaming, seemed to be blind, for the walking cane in his hands tapped around rhythmically while his companion in the brown suit led him around the room by the arm over to the empty seat opposite Sigerson.

“I hope you don’t mind, sir?” he asked tentatively, seeing Sigerson’s uninviting countenance.

“Not at all.”

While his companion went off to attain another chair, the blind observer smoothened his black hair and attempted to strike up a conversation.

“How are you, my good man?”

“Fine, thank you. What’s your name?”

“Charles. Charles Morris. You?”

“William Sigerson at your service,” replied Sigerson a little sarcastically, shaking the extended hand lightly.

“Ah, then this party isn’t much to your taste, is it, compared to something like, let’s say… puzzle-solving? I suspect your cousin must’ve dragged you here to unwind from your work.”

The detective sat up much straighter, his eyes brimming with intrigue.

“And how, sir,” asked the detective, a faint grin forming on his face, “did you know that?”

“Oh, such trivialities hardly need to be explained,” smiled Morris quite humbly, though it was clear that this reaction had pleased him.

“I had the privilege of having Bill read out the guest list for me with snippets of background information in between each entry. You, sir, are a hard-working detective, so when you shook my hand wearily, it showed me that you had laboured in something for the past few days, possibly policework. Then, your voice, tired as it sounded, also carried the weight of one who’s unwillingly present here—that, combined with the fact that I overheard your cousin trying to rope you into a conversation eagerly earlier, gave me the answer I provided.”

“That was certainly an interesting demonstration of your abilities, Mr Morris,” grinned Sigerson. “Yes, everything you mentioned was exactly right on target.”

William Hopkins, who’d just brought along a spare chair to the table and was frankly surprised at seeing the two men already conversing, set down three refreshing drinks and shook Sigerson’s hand warmly while introducing himself.

“So,” said Morris, clapping his hands together excitedly after thanking his friend for the drinks. “What do you say, Mr Sigerson? Are you up for a little gamble?”

A fresh deck of cards materialised out of seemingly thin air.

“Of course,” returned the detective, leaning forwards, a fire rekindled in his voice.
