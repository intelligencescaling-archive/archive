Chapter 10: And the Ground Quaked

The classes were sent reeling from the mass expulsion of both Hashimoto and some of the members of class C. Most of the teachers wore grim expressions on their faces for days, bewildered, shocked, and scrambling for answers. Even grimmer were the faces of my friends, who had all known and liked Hashimoto. In a way, I felt that it would be best to tell them, to explain to them what had happened, and why it happened: Why it had to happen. But I knew that it would be useless. They would just see me as someone who heartlessly went about his business, picking off friend and enemy alike. With this reasoning, I decided to not tell them.	How? How could you do that? Our classmate, our friend… 	Every time I pictured the scene of my confession, those words came out of my friend’s mouths, and I cringed at the shrill sound their voices would make. I could never tell them. Not if they didn't understand. 	Knowing the consequences, knowing what I was getting myself into, I choked. I rushed to the bathroom, and, lifting the toilet lid, vomited. Although sick to my stomach, I mustered up the resolve to press all that guilt downwards into the depths of my soul, and dial a number.
The phone rang three times, before a click.
“Hello?” A voice rang softly.
“Sakayanagi, it’s Light.”
Silence…
“After you stepped into Mashima-Sensei’s office… After you added on to my story about Hashimoto, in the hallway, do you remember what you said?”
“Yes. Yes I do.” She was smiling. Through the wiring and the trappings of the phone, I could tell. She had an impudent, cocky grin on her face. Realizing this, the dams of my guilt and frustration burst, giving rise to anger.
“Why then?! You knew what I was planning on doing, and yet you didn't stop me! And it doesn't seem like you’re going to blackmail me, so what’s all of this about?! You know, and you refuse to do anything? Why?!”
“You helped class A. And, by extension, me. I have no reason to betray you, after all, you’re valuable to us.”
I began to speak, only to be cut off by a sharp “Shh”.
“Hashimoto was a traitor, a liability, and would have caused problems down the line for us. It’s better that he’s gone, because Class A can remain at the top without any problem.”
As if she had read my mind, she responded with the utmost calm and summarized my exact thought process in such a concise way that it left me speechless.
“I must say, you well exceeded my expectations. Managing to expel that many students in such a short time, and with minimal leads back to you, is no small feat. I look forward to your contributions to class A. Good-bye.”
The line buzzed excitedly, and, limply, I put the phone back. I staggered over to my desk and put my hands to my face. She knows. She knows everything.
And me? I don’t know anything. I don’t know what she thinks, what she’ll do… It’s terrifying… 	All my life, I’ve been able to read the puzzle that human beings are, and put the pieces together effortlessly. But with her? There’s a supreme lack of pieces. And even if I had all of them, I would be hard-pressed to put them together.
Who are you, Sakayanagi? Just who are you?
