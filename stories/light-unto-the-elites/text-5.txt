Chapter 5: S-System

This boy must have been English. His looks and demeanor were seemingly out of touch with almost any culture I knew of. A faint smell of sweets emanated from around him whilst he vigorously bit his nails. His presence, in short, was distinctly alien from the rest of my classmates, and perhaps even the rest of the world. It was around the time that I had begun this train of thought that he began to slip off his shoes, and subsequently started twiddling his big toes. It was an odd sight for sure, and most uncomfortable to look at. To pry myself away from this rather conspicuous boy, I stood up, my classmates turning their attention to me almost immediately: Introductions were in place.	“Hi everyone! I’m Yagami Light! I was thinking that, since we’ll be spending the coming years with each other, it’s important that we get to know each other! If it’s okay, we could all go around introducing ourselves before the teacher arrives! Because you already know about my name, I guess I’d also like you guys to know that I’m really glad to have made it here, and hope to have a great high school experience with you all!”	What came next was a resounding amount of applause, especially from most of the girls. A delicate hand raised, and a tired, yet firm and happy voice enveloped the room.

“Hello. I’m Shirashi Asuka. It’s a pleasure to meet you all. As Yagami said, I’m glad to be here, and look forward to getting to know you guys.”

Once more, people clapped. I didn't particularly enjoy introductions, but I figured that I might do well to take some leadership with this class. Besides, all I needed to do was clap occasionally and listen when it interested me. Not much to it.

As per usual, there was at least one problematic figure. Don’t get the wrong idea, though. He didn't seem to be a delinquent or a punk; rather, he rubbed me as a sly fox. A tricky one who might cause trouble in the class in the long run. I decided I might try and befriend him to keep tabs on him. Mentally noting down his name, I thought it sounded a tad funny. “Masayoshi Hashimoto”. Didn't quite roll off the tongue too well.

It was just a bit after the last introduction was finished that our teacher walked in and stood behind his desk at the front. He had the demeanor of a true educator, both upright and caring. His brown hair contrasted with his older face, and so I thought he might have dyed it. With a firm tonality, he began:	“Hello, and welcome. My name is Tomonari Mashima, and I will be your homeroom instructor for the coming years. Firstly, I’d like you to have this. Please pass them back.”	Mashima-sensei lifted from the desk at the front a stack of informational booklets, likely the rulebook.	He then proceeded through an explanation of the basic rules. The main thing that I garnered from this explanation was the foundation of this school’s transactional side, that being the point system. Basically, points are to be divided into two separate types: 1. Private points, and 2. Class points. Private points are used for personal transactions on campus, and, with this school offering a variety of facilities, it’s not hard to see how these come in handy. When it came to private points, 1 point equaled 1 yen. The latter type of points, class points, are what determine the standing of classes.	While I went over these rules in my head, a thought occurred to me, one which I made sure to voice.	“Mashima-Sensei, I have a question.”	“Yes, Yagami?”	“I understand that as of now, we’ve been allotted 100,000 private points, but that won’t be enough to get us through the year. I was wondering how we receive private points throughout the year.”

A few students started whispering among themselves, but that was soon cut off by Mashima-Sensei’s stern voice.	“Excellent question. You students will be receiving private points based on your class points. Class points may be gained through positive contributions to the school community, class cooperation, good marks, good behavior, and scoring well on exams. That’s why it’s imperative you all strive to do your best.”

“Thank you.”	Sitting back down, I considered what this meant for our class. This type of system was one which would reward strong leadership, not chaos and disorderly action. 	“Any other questions?” Mashima-Sensei paused for a brief moment and then continued.	“I’ll be going, then. Good luck to you all.”

Chapter 6: The Following Days

Around a week had passed since the initial explanation of the school’s “S-System”, and a week had also passed since I had last shopped for groceries. As the bell rang, signaling the end of the school day, I quickly packed up and walked quickly in the direction of the school plaza. I entered the grocery store and decided that I might get some BBQ potato chips in addition to what I had bought last time. Oddly, the chip bag had a TV logo on it. In the past week, I had observed a plethora of punks and delinquents roaming the school, so it was weird to me that self-checkout was the norm for most stores. Looking at the roof of the building, I supposed that the security cameras took care of that.

On my way back from the store, I heard two familiar voices coming from a seemingly deserted alley. I paused, just about to turn the corner, and listened. 	“Y’know…” The first voice began slyly.	“You seem much more fun than class A– What I mean to say is: Let’s team up. I can help you if you can help me.”

I wasn’t shocked to hear that this first voice was Hashimoto’s. It only confirmed my suspicions that he was more treacherous than loyal. And with this confirmed, my blood began to boil.	“Tch. You don’t have anything yet, do you? Only come if you have something to give, then we'll talk. Got it?”	The second voice seemed to be the magenta-haired boy from the registration line.

“Heh. Yeah, yeah…” 	Hearing footsteps approach my location, I silently ran the other direction, taking the long route to my dorm. It was during this walk that I felt an excitement build up in my chest. This wasn’t something to be worried about: It was an opportunity. It was an opportunity to cleanse this school of one more piece of trash. To cleanse class A of a liability, of a traitor, of a filthy little liar… When, though… How? How?

This was the thought that dominated my mind for weeks… How? How to rid us of him? Even the announcement that Class A had beaten out all the others in terms of class points for the month seemed to pale in comparison to this thought. A trivial character, Hashimoto, was wrapped up in such an important matter. The problem of Hashimoto and his punishment was resolved with the announcement of the first exam: A purely academic exam which, if failed, would result in an expulsion.

Now was the time.

Chapter 7: The First Days of Light

	After class had ended, I stood up and mustering some courage, proceeded to ask the class if they could stay for just a few moments. Most of them agreed, including Hashimoto.	“Alright. Thank you so much for sparing a bit of your time for this. I think I know a way that we can all pass this exam. It may sound trivial, but I know it’s something that’s helped me in the past with tough exams, and that’s study groups. We could all help each other, and I think this would not only be a great way to score class points, but also a great way to get to know each other!”

	The class burst out into positive chatter, and most seemed to be enthusiastic about the idea. I let the talking naturally die down and continued with my announcement:	“So, it sounds like most of you guys are up to it. That’s great! But I think it would be best to establish groups right now so that we might be able to study sooner rather than later. To do that, we should have leaders for each group. Needless to say, I’d like to be one, but are there any other volunteers?”

	Sakayanagi stood up, along with Katsuragi.	Softly, Sakayanagi announced that she would take up a position as study group leader, and in a tough manner, Katsuragi did the same.

	“Great! Now, how about we choose who we’ll be managing? If it’s okay, I’d like to have Morishita, Shirasi, Kito, Takemoto, Totsuka, and Hashimoto.”	All six of them responded positively, and Sakayanagi went next.	After she was done, Katsuragi picked up the rest, and the study groups each individually confirmed when their first meeting would be. The first meeting of my group was scheduled to be in two days, right after school, and at the cafe nearby.

	As the leader, I arrived early and ordered some coffee for everyone. Almost immediately after I sat down, I saw Hashimoto walk in and waved to him. He came over with his signature smirk and sat across from me.	“That was a good idea of yours, Light. The study groups and all. It makes this boring thing all the more fun.”	“Thanks. But it’s really all because of you and my classmates that we’re able to do this in the first place, Hashimoto. I just don’t want to lose any friends to such a trivial thing.”	“Hm… Well, hopefully it’s not too hard an exam. I mean, it shouldn’t be that hard if it’s the first one they’re giving us.”	“Maybe, but I remember at my old school, our first test was the hardest by far. The only reason we knew that was because some upperclassmen told us, and then offered to give us juniors the test answers in exchange for 30,000 yen. I refused this offer, but some of my classmates went for it, and without studying, got a hundred out of a hundred on the test. In a way, it made me angry that I had gotten the same score but with much more effort.”	It was after I finished telling him this fabricated story that I saw his entire being light up.	“Really…? That’s pretty smart of them…” He muttered.	Suddenly, the rest of our group burst in through the door and crammed into the small booth that we would host this session in.	I passed out the coffees, and made sure to get some creamers and sugar. It seemed that Morishita really loved sugar, becaue she kept on making me get up and get just one pack at a time. After I had given her 10 packs of sugar, half of which she spilled, I gave up and started the session. All was well.

Chapter 8: Lunchtime Meeting

The next day, I sat down at lunch with a few friends and one enemy. As usual, Kito was keeping to himself, Machida was being serious, Hashimoto was teasing everyone, Morishita was arranging her food into a face (?), and Sakayanagi was gazing out into the distance with a slight smile. All of a sudden, Hashimoto was gone. As predicted.	My eyes scanned across the lunchroom, and I saw Hashimoto talking confidently to a bewildered upperclassman. I waited for about 5 minutes, at which point Hashimoto walked away with a smirk wider than usual. He stopped by the table, telling us he was gonna go and get some homework done in the library.

After making sure he was gone, I stood up.

“Hey, guys. I’ve gotta do something. Give me a second.” I said as I began walking towards the very upperclassman Hashimoto had just talked to.

As I sat next to her, she glanced away nervously. I realized that I might come off as rude, but in this situation, I figured it would be better to be forceful than graceful.	“I’m not gonna beat around the bush. He asked you for the test answers to the upcoming exam for the first years, didn't he?”	“Y-yeah… What about it?”	“And you gave them to him. In exchange for private points.”	“N-No!” She said a bit too loudly.	“Yes, you did. I know you did. It’s unfortunate your greed consumed you to the point it would be permissible to allow cheating on his end… I feel sorry for you. So that’s why I’m not ratting you out just yet. If you come with me and confess this, then I’ll let you off the hook. How does that sound?	“A-Are you blackmailing me? That’s illegal!"	A feeble attempt at getting out of this. “I’m not blackmailing you. I’m helping you. I’m making sure you don’t get the wrong end of the stick when all of this is revealed. Help me help you.”	“I… O-Okay…” She managed to mutter.	I stood up, and she did the same. We walked towards Mashima-Sensei’s office, and I was later able to get her to confess the whole situation to him. It was as she was finishing her story that I heard a cane lightly tapping across the floor outside the office door. Sakayanagi entered and sat down. After the girl was done with her explanation of the situation, Sakayanagi corroborated and, to my surprise, asked Mashima-Sensei to re-make the test. All of this was unexpected by me, as I hadn’t planned for her contribution, and yet she was playing her unintended role beautifully. Seeing where she was taking this, I agreed, asking him to change the questions for the sake of fairness. Mashima-Sensei eventually agreed, and I left the room satisfied until I heard Sakayanagi call out to me.	“I see what you’re doing. I see you.”	“What do you mean?”	“You know.”	I continued walking and ended up back at the lunch table with a few minutes to spare before our next class.

Chapter 9: Judgment Day

Ever since Hashimoto had talked to that girl, he had skipped all the study sessions. And when the fateful day of the test arrived, his face fell. It fell down an endless well, and that perpetual smirk of his was shattered into a million pieces. For the entirety of the day, he sat there, motionless. Unable to act, unable to betray. You see? This is what happens to people who step out of line, to insolent idiots who think they can parade around offering up their loyalty to the highest bidder. The next day, test scores were announced, and Hashimoto had failed. He was to be expelled. And although my face reflected deep sadness that my friend had been taken out of the picture, my true face, my soul reveled in his expulsion. So long, friend.
