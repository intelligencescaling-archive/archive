Light Yagami In COTE (Chapters 0 and 1)

Chapter 0: Light Yagami’s Soliloquy

Allow me to tell you of a world. A world where the evil are punished according to their misdeeds, and the good are rewarded according to their virtue. A world where karma is no longer a mystical thing, but rather a tangible justice system. A world of fairness, justice, and goodness. That is my dream.

Go on. I won’t be offended if you laugh at such a grand dream. It’s only natural, given the state of the world these days. I mean, look around you. People needlessly suffer at the hands of others every day, and most of society just turns a blind eye to it. Open up your laptop, and what are you greeted with? Suffering. Death. Cruelty. Women, men, and children are all equal in their suffering nowadays. And if you were to carefully look at these women, men, and children, you would realize that most of them are innocent, and that most of this pain was inflicted upon the innocent by evildoers.

From here, we have yet another problem. Whilst the innocent suffer needlessly, those perpetrators of the suffering are too often let off the hook. In essence: Those who do good are punished, while those who do evil are permitted. Most are able to grasp that at a certain point in their lives, and that’s why so many people, once good, commit horrible atrocities. “Hurt people hurt people”, and ultimately, too many people hurt others.

Society is backwards, and it needs correction.

This is what I’ve thought for a long time, and it becomes more and more evident to me that something needs to be done about this. No one else seems willing to do it, so why not me? That’s just it- I’m the only one who would do it- who *can* do it. If nobody else steps up, why can’t I? What’s stopping me? Nothing! Nothing is stopping me from realizing my dream! That’s right… I’ll change the world.

Chapter 1: Japan’s #1 Student	Although I had brought an umbrella, the bus stop was sunny, and the day was fine. I sat on the nearby bench, crossing my arms and looking around. The cherry blossoms were in full bloom this time of the year, and their petals rained down in the hundreds. Entrancingly, a few trees off in the distance swayed, all whilst cotton-like clouds swirled over my head. What a nice day. The normal worry of a student about the upcoming academic challenges of my high school were quelled by my past achievements, most notably, being considered the #1 student in Japan. As if to reaffirm this peace of mind, a breeze ruffled my hair calmingly. This meditative atmosphere was interrupted by the honking of a bus, which quickly snapped me out of my daze. Sheepishly, I walked into the bus, greeted the busdriver, and sat in a seat towards the back. I quickly surveyed the environment, a plethora of what looked to be office workers on the bus. Equal in number to the office workers were my fellow students, decked out in the school’s uniform. A few immediately caught my attention, most notably three boys who seemed to be huddling around each other. All of them looked to be thugs, people who didn't deserve their place in ANHS. Before I could consciously make any decision against it, my mind labeled them as vermin. Disgusted, I shifted my attention from the thugs to an old lady standing right next to them. She looked as if she were about to fall over, and, ultimately, I doubted that any of the coterie next to her would be willing to give up their seats for her. This act of indifference on their part only served to upset me more, and, with determination, I stood, and called out to the old lady, asking her if she’d like my seat. I regretted that I wasn’t able to walk over there and lead her to my seat first, but I feared that someone else might snatch the seat up before she could get there. My worries were dispelled when she walked over, thanked me, smiled graciously, and sat down.

I ended up moving away from the old lady, wanting to check out the bus’s landscape a bit more, until, not five steps later, I bumped into a girl. By the look of it, she was also a student at ANHS. She looked up at me, wide-eyed and smiling.
“Sorry”, She said apologetically.
“Ah. No need to be. It was my fault entirely, I wasn’t paying attention.”
“Well, sorry that I got your attention that way…”
“What do you mean?” I said in earnest.
“I mean that I wanted to talk to you, and I’m sorry that bumping into you was the way I went about it. I really hope this doesn't give you a bad impression of me.” She said pleadingly.

Somehow, her eyes reflected a different truth than the one she was trying to force on me. Personas are what we humans use to function effectively as a society, yet, oftentimes, they at least land closer to who that person is than not. I was getting a whole different vibe from this girl, though. Despite her smiles and polite demeanor, she seemed angry. Thinking it best to go along with the flow of this conversation, I asked her what she wanted to talk about.
“Mainly I wanted to commend you. It’s very noble what you did back there. In fact, I was thinking of the same thing, but you beat me to the punch... Oh! I forgot! My name's Kushida!” Her tone had switched from ashamed to hyper in just a second, and convincingly too.
“Thanks, but I’m sure that a lot of people would be willing to do it, so it’s nothing really. My name’s Yagami.”

“Nice to meet you Yagami-kun! Well, I hope we can be friends at school!”
“Likewise.”
It was then that she went on her way, and I was able to fully realize that, indeed, there was some malice coming from her. Her persona and who she really was seemed to be completely separate, something odd for a high schooler. After all, the younger you are, the less capable of putting up a front you are. In short, it would take something extreme to be able to disassociate to that level.

Shrugging that idea off, I continued my walk through the bus, taking care not to fall or slip as the bus went over rough patches or stopped. I eventually got to the end of the bus, where I noticed two peculiar figures.
