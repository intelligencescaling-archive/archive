Light Unto The Elites Chapter 4

Chapter 4: A Class

After a bit of waiting, the line started to move along steadily. Sakayanagi and I made conversation to pass the time. Just as she was in the middle of asking me something, a young man operating the desk tapped my shoulder.

	“Yer holdin’ up the line”, He said as he handed me a slip of paper.
I took it and promptly apologized.

	Looking at the paper, I first noticed the bold “A” that stood at the center. Underneath it, I saw a room number, as well as my name.

	“Would you mind?” Sakayanagi asked.

“Not at all”, I said as I held out the paper for her to see.

	“Oh! That’s delightful!” She, in turn, showed me her paper, which mirrored mine in the “A” placement.

	Once we had passed into the auditorium, it was made clear where we should go via the four groups that the student body was separated into. I assumed that the one with the “Class A” sign parallel to the students was where I was intended to go, and so I took my place next to a peculiar lot.

To my right, a tall boy who looked more like a man stood erect and serious. Although his physical stature was impressive, what seemed to set him apart was his bald head. To my left was Sakayanagi. I decided I might look behind me, and see who else I would spend the coming years with.

With obsidian-like eyes, the boy behind me was staring at my back. His posture was nothing to be impressed with, as he stood with a great slouch. His uniform looked to be hanging off of his rather skinny form, and he, instead of the school-issued dress pants, wore jeans. What caught my attention other than his awkward stance and odd dressing sense was his unkept hair as well as his eye bags. I averted my eyes, and trained my gaze on the podium, where a student, looking to be a year or two older than us walked to.

	With jet black hair, sharp facial features, and a stern atmosphere, I guessed he was the leader of the student counsel. As if in response to my observation, he began.

	“Welcome newcomers. I am the president of the student counsel, Manabu Horikita. As a representative of your upperclassmen, I extend to you a warm welcome. Because you have applied and gotten into this school, I assume that you are aware of its prestige and competitiveness, yet allow me to reiterate something. This school is a place of merit, one which boasts impeccable college acceptance and employment rates. With this in mind, we must work together to uphold this standard of achievement. The fact that you are here today is already a testament to your potential, and so I expect great things from each and every one of you. At this school, you alone control your path.”

	The rest of his speech consisted of mere formalities, and, eventually, we were led to our classrooms by a member of the faculty. The door to the classroom had both its number, as well as an “A” on it, while the classroom itself was laid out in an orderly fashion, with each of the desks boasting a nameplate on them. The desks were arranged in a five by seven layout, all of them facing a whiteboard and a larger desk, presumably for the teacher. Our chaperone had all of the sudden left us, and so, without much guidance, most of us got seated. My desk was on the farthest row from the door, five chairs up, and right next to a window. Two chairs behind me was the strange, unkept boy from earlier, who was now not just slouching, but pulling his knees up to his chest, seemingly to assume a fetal position. His nameplate read:

	Lind L. Taylor.
